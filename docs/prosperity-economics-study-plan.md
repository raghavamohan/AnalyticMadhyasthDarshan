## 2. Central thesis

Every order in nature has a balance proper to its existent state (MVD, p. 105).

- The material order keeps its balance through structural conformity and the
  plant order through seed conformity.
- The animal order keeps its balance through species conformity. "Every unit
  of nature, except humans, exhibits definite conduct" (JV, pp. 47–48).
- The human body is a composition of the biological order. Its needs for
  nourishment, protection and social participation are definite and can be
  quantified. Left to itself, the body comes to balance (*santulan*) with the
  rest of nature in the same way.

The human being, however, is a joint form of body and *jeevan*:

- *Jeevan* seeks **continuity of happiness**, which is fulfilled only through
  understanding (JV, pp. 162–163).
- In delusion, only four and a half of *jeevan*'s ten activities are
  effective: selection, taste, analysis, visualisation, and half of
  deliberation. They evaluate through the sensory perspectives of pleasure,
  health and profit. The remaining activities, those of *chitta*, *buddhi* and
  *atma*, lack the content of understanding, and the result is "continuous
  internal conflict" within *jeevan* (JV, pp. 72–73).
- The deluded person takes this conflict for a *lack* that more sensation and
  more possession can fill.

Sensory satisfaction is transient by design, "an alarm, an inspiration, and a
direction" towards awakening (JV, p. 163). The need it is asked to meet is not
material at all. Demand therefore loses its stopping point. Needs pursued as
indulgence ("comfort-accumulation") are "indefinite and unlimited" (MVD, p.
128). The lust for accumulation brings no satisfaction "because there is no end
to accumulation" (MVD, p. 170).

Accumulation settles on money because money escapes the constraint that
governs real goods:

- Grain rots and machines rust, so goods must be used rather than hoarded (JV,
  p. 152; MVD, p. 183).
- Only money, as symbolic value, can pile up without transformation (MVD, p.
  182).

The human economy therefore becomes an open spiral of accumulation rather than
a cycle. Scarcity then looks like a fact of nature. In fact it is the shadow
cast by indefinite want on finite means.

The darshan's alternative, **cyclical economics** (*avartansheel arthshastra*),
is the economic form of the human order's own balance. That balance is
resolution, prosperity, fearlessness and coexistence (MVD, p. 105). Economic
resolution is a consequence of resolution in *jeevan*, not a substitute for
it: "The economic system is just one part of this entire picture" (JV, p. 151).

The study's distinctive claim, to be tested in the critical review, is this:

> Mainstream economics **postulates** insatiability, through non-satiation and
> the definition of economics as allocation of scarce means. Madhyasth Darshan
> **explains** it as the product of a category error. A qualitative,
> continuous need of *jeevan* is pursued through quantitative, transient
> means.
>
> If the explanation is right, insatiability is contingent and alterable, not
> constitutive of human nature. That claim has empirical implications the study
> can examine.

### 2.1 Working abstract

This text replaces the stub's one-liner when the draft is submitted.

> Mainstream economics begins from scarcity: wants are taken to be unlimited
> and means limited, so economic life is the permanent allocation of the
> insufficient. Madhyasth Darshan locates the problem elsewhere. The human
> being is a joint form of body and *jeevan*. The body, a composition of the
> biological order, has definite needs for nourishment, protection and social
> participation, and like every other order it can come to balance with the
> rest of nature. *Jeevan* seeks continuity of happiness, which only
> understanding fulfils. In delusion it works through four and a half of its
> ten activities and evaluates by pleasure, health and profit; the unresolved
> conflict of its remaining activities is mistaken for a lack that sensation
> and possession can fill. Because sensory satisfaction is transient and the
> need it is asked to meet is not material, demand loses its stopping point,
> and accumulation — above all of money, which alone escapes use and decay —
> becomes the aim of production and exchange. The study reconstructs the
> darshan's alternative, cyclical economics (*avartansheel arthshastra*): body,
> mind and wealth deployed on natural abundance yield goods for use; use
> nourishes and protects the body and enables social participation; these
> renew the capacity to produce. Exchange rests on labour, production stays
> within nature's balance, need is assessed at the level of the family, and
> prosperity is the absence of the feeling of lack together with production in
> excess of need, the surplus flowing to right-use and purposeful-use. The
> account is compared with classical, neoclassical, Marxian, Gandhian,
> ecological and post-growth economics, with Indian and Advaita teaching on
> desire and non-possession, and with the sciences of homeostasis, hedonic
> adaptation and Earth-system limits. The study argues that the darshan
> explains the insatiability that mainstream theory postulates, and identifies
> what it must still take from economics: coordination beyond the family,
> measurable criteria of need and balance, and an account of money and credit
> as they actually operate.

## 3. *Avartansheelata*: what the sources establish

The term needs care. The study should present cyclicality in three nested
layers, plus a closure condition that connects them. The citations below are
working locators from the repository mirrors. Verify page numbers against the
PDFs before drafting (§9, stage A).

This map is built from MVD, JV, SB and KD only. *Avartansheel Arthshastra*
(AA) is the treatise in which Nagraj develops cyclical economics in full. Once
its working translation exists, AA becomes the leading source for this
section. The three-layer reading, the closure condition and the interpretive
questions in §3.6 must all be rechecked against it, and revised where AA
defines the terms differently.

### 3.1 Layer one: the cyclicality of nature

- **Composition and decomposition.** An excess of composition becomes the
  cause of decomposition, and the reverse. This cycle is visible in gross
  activity (MVD, p. 152).
- **The body's cycle.** Plants wither into soil; bodies form in the womb and
  return to soil, air and water at death (JV, pp. 65–66).
- **Earth's balances.** Seasonal and orbital recurrence, and the earth's heat
  balance, on which greenery and so the higher orders depend. Coal and
  petroleum are buried forests that once absorbed heat; burning them disturbs
  that balance (KD §3.7, pp. 73–81).
- **Time.** Time is reckoned from recurring activity: "every cyclicality
  happened before as well, is happening even now, and will keep happening"
  (KD §3.15). The KD glossary records *avartansheelta* (आवर्तनशीलता) as
  "cyclicality", attested for the water cycle and for recurrence as the basis
  of time.
- **Complementarity of the four orders.** Each animal unit is complementary to
  many plant units, and each plant unit to many material units. The human
  makes use and right-use of the other orders, or misuse in delusion (MVD, p.
  104).
- **Definite conduct.** The material order is regulated by structural
  conformity, the plant order by seed conformity and the animal order by
  species conformity. "Every unit of nature, except humans, exhibits definite
  conduct." Humans reach definite conduct only through *sanskar*-conformity,
  that is, through understanding (JV, pp. 47–48). This is the textual base for
  the claim that the body alone can reach *santulan* while the human being as
  a whole cannot do so without understanding.

### 3.2 Layer two: the cycle of human provision

This is the economic cycle proper.

- **The cycle.** "We deploy our resources in the form of body, mind, and wealth
  on natural abundance for production, and the produced goods become the basis
  of nourishment and protection of our body and social participation. We again
  become capable of producing on the basis of this social participation,
  nourishment, and protection. In this way, it is cyclical" (JV, p. 154).
- **Basic principle.** "Production by deployment of labour and exchange based
  on labour" (JV, pp. 154–155).
- **Resources and wealth.** Resources are body, mind and wealth. Economics that
  leaves any one out is incomplete. Wealth means produced objects of common
  aspirations (food, shelter, adornment) and special aspirations
  (telephony, broadcasting, transport). Money is a symbolic unit of exchange, not
  wealth (JV, pp. 151–153).
- **State policy.** State policy names the securities this layer requires:
  - *economic security* "through the principle of producing more than the
    needs of family by way of circularity";
  - *production security* "through the principle of maintaining nature's
    balance";
  - *exchange security* through labour (MVD, p. 264).
- **Economic resolution.** "Economic resolution comes from understanding the
  cyclicality of nature" (JV, p. 159). Layer two is nested in layer one.

### 3.3 Layer three: the human's own balance

- **The human order's balance.** Each order is balanced "in its existent
  state". For the knowledge order, that balance is "evidencing resolution,
  prosperity, fearlessness, and coexistence" (MVD, p. 105).
- **Prosperity.** "The lack of the sentiment of lacking itself is prosperity.
  Production in excess of needs is prosperity" (MVD, p. 71).
- **Need.** Needs are ascertained at the level of the family, not the
  individual (JV, p. 153). Upon awakening, common and special aspirations
  become definite, "satisfied with fewer resources" (JV, p. 136).
- **Duty and indulgence.** Duties are definite and limited, so they can be
  fulfilled. Indulgence is indefinite and unlimited, so it cannot. The first
  leads to peace and the second to unrest (MVD, p. 128).
- **Prosperity as a temporal need.** Work for prosperity is needed for the
  lifetime of the body. Only realisation has continuity (MVD, p. 96). This is
  the textual hinge of the body/*jeevan* asymmetry.

### 3.4 The closure condition

What turns provision into a *cycle* rather than a line is definite need
together with use rather than hoarding:

- Surplus beyond assessed family need goes to **right-use and purposeful-use**
  (JV, pp. 152, 157). *Use*, *right-use* and *purposeful-use* are the
  "natural essences of an awakened life" (JV, p. 157).
- **Non-accumulation** is "earning for expenditure", and its righteous
  expenditure is mandatory (MVD, p. 150).
- Accumulation, malice, false learning, conceit and fear are the five
  unrefined tendencies, and accumulation leads to greed. Non-accumulation
  (prosperity) heads the five refined tendencies (MVD, pp. 181–182; see also
  p. 213).

In delusion the closure fails twice:

1. Want has no definite measure, so there is never a surplus to release.
2. Money lets the surplus escape transformation.

The cycle opens into a spiral. This is the formal heart of the user's insight,
and the study should make it explicit.

The study should present the friction in the higher faculties carefully. The
texts call it "internal conflict within *jeevan*", where the remaining
activities "lack the substance for understanding". The Hindi term is to be
verified. The claim that this conflict
is *misread* as a material lack is a synthesis across JV pp. 72–76 and 162–163
and MVD p. 128. It is well supported, but it should be argued in the essay,
not presented as a single quoted statement (AGENTS.md §5, *Reviewing primary
support and synthesis*).

### 3.6 Interpretive questions for Editorial Notes

1. **Cycle of capacity or cycle of replenishment?** The JV text defines the
   cycle as capacity → production → nourishment and participation → renewed
   capacity. The Primer glosses *avartansheel* as "cyclical or replenishing
   production", which leans towards material circularity: returning nutrients
   and renewing resources. The two readings are compatible, since layer two is
   nested in layer one and production security requires nature's balance.
   The study should state the capacity cycle as the definition and the
   replenishment condition as its ecological constraint. *Avartansheel
   Arthshastra* itself should settle the question (§8).
2. **Do not conflate *avartansheelta* with *sahajavritti*.** KD's
   "natural-recurrence" (*anukshan-vikshan-vritti*, *sahajavritti*; KD 2, pp.
   43–45) is a *jeevan*-level continuity of contemplation, not economic or
   natural cyclicality. "The attainment of opulence gives rise to
   natural-recurrence" may still bear on prosperity. Investigate it, and cite
   it only after checking against the Hindi page images.
3. **English terms.** Fix one running English term for each of:
   - need (*avashyakta*, आवश्यकता); aspiration (*akanksha*, common and
     special); desire (*iccha*); indulgence (*bhog*);
   - prosperity (*samriddhi*); accumulation (*sangrah*); non-accumulation
     (*asangraha*);
   - right-use (*sadupayog*, सदुपयोग); purposeful-use (*prayojaniyata*,
     प्रयोजनीयता);
   - labour-value (*shram mulya*); exchange (*vinimay*, विनिमय); storage or
     reserve (*kosh*);
   - symbolic value (*prateek mulya*); natural abundance; profit-obsession
     (*labhonmad*).

   The terms marked with Devanagari are confirmed in the KD term mapping.
   Check the rest against `MD-Mapping.xlsx`. Align with Axiology and Undivided
   Society, which use "exchange-reserve".
4. **Scope of the critique of specialisation.** The critique (JV, p. 153) may
   target non-producing status-consumers rather than the division of labour as
   such. The texts accept telephony, broadcasting and transport, which need highly
   specialised production. Record this as an interpretive choice.

Read each tradition at its strongest. AGENTS.md §5 requires the same

### 5.2 What the darshan can learn from economics

The user asked for this explicitly. It belongs in §7.6 and should be argued,
not listed:

1. **Coordination beyond the family.** Prices carry dispersed information
   (Hayek). A labour-based exchange needs a theory of how heterogeneous labour
   is measured. This is Marx's reduction problem and the
   socialist-calculation debate in a new setting.
2. **Measurement.** National accounts, input–output analysis, material-flow
   accounting and life-cycle assessment can make "production in excess of
   family need" and "maintaining nature's balance" observable. Without them,
   prosperity and production security cannot be evidenced.
3. **Externalities and commons.** Pigou and Ostrom supply institutional design
   for production security and the exchange-reserve.
4. **Needs theory.** Doyal and Gough, Max-Neef and Sen sharpen "family-level
   assessment of need" against the charge of paternalism.
5. **Money as it actually works.** Credit creation, debt and interest. The
   darshan's "money is symbolic value" is correct as ontology but silent on
   macroeconomic mechanics such as growth dependence.
6. **Productivity and specialisation.** The division of labour underwrites
   the special aspirations the darshan itself endorses (telephony, broadcasting
   and transport). The critique of specialisation needs a precise scope.

## 6. Optional formal companion

Proposed: a technical note, `Technical-Note-A-Stock-Flow-Model-Of-Cyclical-Economy.md`,
placed beside the study and added with the `add-technical-note` skill. It would
model:

- **Household demand.** Body need *N* is fixed. In delusion, demand follows
  hedonic adaptation (*D*ₜ₊₁ = *D*ₜ + α·*C*ₜ), which has no fixed point. In
  awakening, *D* = *N*, and surplus *S* = *P* − *N* ≥ 0 flows to right-use.
- **Natural stock.** Stock *K* regenerates by *g*(*K*) and is drawn down by
  extraction *E*. The steady state requires *E* ≤ *g*(*K*), which is
  production security.
- **Money as a leak.** Goods depreciate at rate δ > 0; money does not. Show
  how a non-decaying store breaks closure, and compare with Gesell.

The note would state its status plainly. It is a formal reconstruction, not
proof of source claims, as the Our-approach statement requires.
