## Overall assessment

The central synthesis is substantially supported by the primary passages reviewed. Its strongest features are the distinction between actionless *satta* and active units; the inseparability of units and their mutuality; the distinction between bodily composition and constitutional completeness; the account of *jeevan* as the knower; and the connection of understanding, value, and conduct. The recent treatment of imaging, the four aspects, ten activities, and thirty values should largely be retained.

The manuscript nevertheless needs substantive corrections before it can be regarded as fully consistent. Several sentences are stronger than their sources, and some older formulations survive beside newer corrections. The clearest Madhyasth Darshan problems concern change between orders, the explicit value enumeration, contact and obligation, and individual versus social completeness. The clearest comparative problems concern Advaita's empirical continuity across death, the Gita quotation, source attribution, and citations. There are also important connections that need a short affirmative explanation rather than more repeated qualifications.

The prose is usually grammatical, but it is not consistently easy to read. The full Markdown contains about 37,100 whitespace-separated words; approximately 24,900 precede the glossary. Readers encounter the same distinctions in exposition, summary tables, further comparison tables, detailed comparisons, criticism, glossary, and notes. The remedy is selective consolidation and clearer sentences, not removal of source-supported relationships.

Textual support and philosophical truth remain different questions. Finding a doctrine in the primary texts establishes that the synthesis represents it fairly; it does not establish the doctrine against rival explanations or empirical evidence.

For review order, begin with the definite source and comparison repairs: R01–R03, R10–R14, R16, R20 and R25. Then consider the argument and cross-study clarifications, followed by the optional coverage additions and prose consolidation. This ordering indicates consequence and confidence; it does not imply that every proposed replacement is already accepted.

It is a map of the principal conceptual families relevant to these three studies, not a claim to exhaust every definition in Nagraj's complete corpus. MVD, SB, JV, KD, PS, AVD, and MAD retain the manuscripts' source abbreviations. Page-convention qualifications appear in the verification section.

The assembled direction is: coexistence and saturation ground active units; units have definite aspects and participate in mutuality; different orders express different kinds of activity; *jeevan* supplies knowing and the possibility of awakened evaluation; understood relationships, value and purpose guide conduct; conduct and transmission evidence that understanding. The difficult steps are the warrant for grounding, the transition to sentience, the connection between essentiality and obligation, and the independent assessment of claimed realisation. They should remain visible as questions about the assembled argument.

## Substantive comments on Madhyasth Darshan

### R01 — Narrow the prohibition on change between orders

**Location:** O §1.10, L278; related §§1.5–1.6 and the four-order glossary.

“Change of composition is therefore recognised as development or decline within an order, not as passage beyond it” is too broad. SB 260–265 supports the point that a body remains a pranic composition and does not become *jeevan* through aggregation. But SB 77 explicitly describes material nature becoming biological nature, result-conformance becoming seed-conformance, and a return to the material order. It distinguishes this reversible transformation from irreversible development.

**Proposed revision:** “Compositional change can produce pranic organisation from material constituents and can reverse through disintegration. It does not by itself produce the constitutionally complete sentient unit, jeevan. Even a human body remains a pranic composition through which jeevan operates.” Retain both developmental lines and distinguish reversibility from constitutional completeness. This is a correction of scope, not a rejection of the study's two-development synthesis.

### R02 — Remove the surviving denial of the explicit thirty-value enumeration

**Location:** O §1.4, L89, L95, L101; glossary L1131; Editorial Notes L1228; References L1313.

The text says the classifications are not supplied as one exhaustive taxonomy and that human values have no closed list, then correctly gives 4+6+9+9+2. PS printed 151 names the six human values as humane essential nature; printed 154 expressly gives the thirty-value enumeration. The absence of a closed list in one JV passage does not override that explicit source.

MAD's native reader p. 30 also supplies the principal nine established values and their paired expressions. JV's local “such as” wording does not make this separately enumerated group merely exemplary.

**Proposed revision:** “The primary texts enumerate thirty core values in five families. Their definitions, faculty positions, and expressions across human living are developed across several passages.” In the human-values row, state the six names directly. Keep the valid distinction between the explicit enumeration and the connected, multi-passage explanation of its relationships. In the notes, say that JV offers examples while PS supplies the enumeration adopted here.

### R03 — Do not make humane obligations optional in contacts

**Location:** O §1.4, L87; compare A §1.4, L107.

“Only the former carries the full relational expectation” suggests that voluntarily entered contacts fall outside the value account. MVD 61–62 distinguishes the manner in which expectations arise, while MVD 236 and 311–312 subjects contacts to humane conduct and duty. A already states this more accurately.

**Proposed revision:** “Relationships carry definite expectations towards completeness; contacts involve voluntarily undertaken expectations. Both are subject to justice and humane conduct.” The neighbour example can remain if it distinguishes the voluntary terms of a shared arrangement from the non-optional demand that conduct be just.

### R04 — Separate individual conduct completeness from achieved social completeness

**Location:** O §1.15, L372; related L168, L340 and the T3 summaries.

The requirement that T3 have an already available, reproducible tradition can make an individual's conduct completeness depend on completed institutions or multiple generations. MVD 15 explicitly lists activity and conduct completeness under completeness in an individual. MVD 16 separately lists completeness in society. KD's widening evidence and the passages on tradition connect these achievements without making them identical.

**Proposed revision:** “Conduct completeness is the individual's continuous expression of realised understanding. Its social fulfilment includes contributing to a tradition that makes understanding and humane living available across generations.” Preserve the demand for continuity; avoid implying that a person cannot attain T3 until universal society has already been achieved.

### R05 — Preserve the non-annihilation doctrine without adding a proof the source has not supplied

**Location:** O §1.2, L34; critical discussion §§6.1–6.2.

SB 57 does connect permeation, repeated division, and non-annihilation. The study's extra premise—annihilation would require a fragment separated from what saturates it—is not established merely by pervasive presence. Spatial inseparability establishes that division does not place a fragment outside the ground; it does not independently prove every form of persistence or the impossibility of cessation.

**Proposed revision:** “However a unit is divided, every fragment remains saturated in the pervasive reality. Madhyasth Darshan connects this indivisibility of coexistence with its claim that what exists is not annihilated.” Discuss the additional inferential burden in criticism. Do not call the doctrine unsupported by the texts: the doctrine is there; the issue is the strength of the argument attributed to it.

### R06 — Replace the blanket claim that the names of satta are unexplained

**Location:** O Editorial Notes, L1272–1282; related §1.1 and *gyan* glossary L1135.

The table treats ordinary English connotations of “God,” “energy,” and “consciousness” as internal tensions, then says the source does not settle how the names cohere. MVD 35 explicitly explains the names in relation to realisation, pervasiveness, regulation, sentient nature, activity in saturation, and non-transformation. O §1.1 already says the names indicate the same reality from different standpoints. The note currently weakens its own exposition through unacknowledged changes of meaning.

**Proposed revision:** Rename the note “Translation risks in the names of satta.” Explain the source's use first. Retain the separate philosophical question of how actionless grounding accounts for energetic and regulated activity; do not treat the everyday sense of “energy” or “God” as the source's admitted definition. Align O with E §1.1 and A's “Chetana, chaitanya and knowledge” note by distinguishing knowledge as pervasive ground, intelligible coexistence, and realised understanding.

### R07 — Add the missing short bridges on which epistemology and axiology rely

**Location:** O §§1.3, 1.7–1.8, 1.11–1.13.

The relevant definitions are mostly present, but several important connections remain scattered:

- SB 60–62 relates effort to *dharma/svabhav*, motion to *svabhav/guna*, and result to *roop* within the completeness context. A §1.1 already relies on this active account. Explain it briefly in O, preserving the overlap rather than forcing a one-to-one correspondence.
- MVD 170 connects *gyan*, *vivek*, and *vigyan*: understanding reality, discerning human purpose, and developing means. This is a useful bridge between the knower, evaluation, and work.
- The four dimensions of living—realisation, thought, behaviour, work—should be stated together before their value families are mapped. Their principal correspondences do not partition the person into exclusive compartments.
- Humane conduct brings together **value, character and ethics** (PS printed 151; E L149–155). Explain that connection briefly, and consistently distinguish the four dimensions of personal living from the five social systems in §1.15.
- *Sanskar* is named as a conformance mode but not sufficiently explained as acquired acceptances and tendencies that can support awakening or delusion. Two sentences would connect settled constitution with variable learning and conduct.

These are targeted additions. They do not require reproducing the whole epistemology or axiology study.

### R08 — Make the normative premise explicit instead of inferring obligation from coexistence alone

**Location:** O §6.1, L1006; §§1.12.1, 1.15; compare A §§5.2, 7.4.

“If existence is coexistence, human fulfilment must be evidenced as coexistence in behaviour” compresses the decisive normative step. The source's argument also invokes human *dharma*, essentiality, understood relationships, and mutual fulfilment. Physical coexistence alone is compatible with exploitation and conflict; it is not the complete premise.

**Proposed revision:** “Given the darshan's identification of human dharma with happiness and of value with essential participation, understood coexistence is fulfilled through just relationships and humane conduct.” Then retain A's critical question: what warrants those identifications, and how are disputed duties assessed? This preserves the affirmative synthesis while exposing its actual commitments.

### R09 — Broaden the critical discussion to physical claims already present in the source account

**Location:** O §§1.2–1.3, 6.2; Research Note: Physics of Satta–Unit Interaction §5, especially L107.

The open-problems section concentrates on detecting *jeevan*, survival, and evolutionary scope. SB also makes claims about atomic organisation, named physical forces, and imaging/radiance. SB 84–85 associates electromagnetic, gravitational, weak, strong, and mediative forces with the faculty structure; SB 251–252 distinguishes imaging/radiance from motion and pressure. These claims deserve a compact statement of their interpretive and empirical status.

The physics companion calls the fifth-force passage a structural analogy and says it is not a fifth force for physics to detect, yet immediately acknowledges that the source does not disambiguate literal and analogical readings. That categorical exclusion should be withdrawn.

**Proposed direction:** State the source claims, distinguish analogical reconstruction from a literal physical reading, and identify what each reading would require. Preserve the supported distinction between imaging and influence. Do not either assimilate the account to established physics or immunise every physical-looking assertion by calling it metaphorical.

### R10 — Explain the kosha classification without identifying it with five faculties

**Location:** O §5.7.2, L961; E §2.1, L251; MVD 49–50.

E says MD's *kosha* layers are activities and powers of an enduring *jeevan*. MVD's account applies annamaya/pranamaya already to the material order, adds manomaya in the pranic order, describes four including anandmaya in animal and deluded human expression, and five including vigyanmaya in awakened human expression. These are not simply atma, buddhi, chitta, vritti, and mun under other names. O correctly warns against a one-to-one Advaita match, but calls its second structure “developmental planes,” inviting confusion with the four *pad* classifications.

**Proposed revision:** “Madhyasth Darshan uses kosha terminology to describe manifestations across the orders of nature and human awakening. This classification is distinct from the five faculties of jeevan and from the four developmental planes.” Add the short source-based explanation near the orders discussion and cite it from the comparison. Do not assert an unverified exact identity between the appearance of *anandamaya* and T1 merely because both concern sentience.

## Corrections to the philosophical comparisons

### R11 — Compare ordinary death and ultimate individuality separately

**Location:** O §5.2, L805; compare its correct fuller explanation at L498.

The question “Does the individual survive bodily death?” receives the Advaita answer “Not as a separate individual; Atman was never born.” This answers an ultimate question about separateness, while the question concerns ordinary bodily death. Advaita accepts the empirical jiva's continuity through rebirth until liberation. The compressed answer therefore contradicts the fuller exposition and makes the comparison unfair.

**Suggested cell:** “At the empirical level, the jiva continues through rebirth until liberation; ultimately the Self is Brahman, never a separate individual.” Contrast this with MD's permanently distinct jeevan in a separate clause. BG 2.22 with Shankara's commentary directly supports embodied continuity: printed p. 45 / PDF p. 46 in the [cited Gita edition](https://analyticmadhyasthdarshan.org/References/Advaita-Vedanta/BG-Bhagavad-Gita-Shankara-Gambhirananda.pdf).

### R12 — Correct the Gita's field-and-knower passage

**Location:** O §2.7, L506; References L1349.

The text makes the knower itself “also the knowledge of the field.” The passage instead says to know Krishna as the knower in every field, and calls knowledge **of both the field and its knower** knowledge. Those are different claims. A general Advaita identity of Self and consciousness does not justify changing this verse.

**Suggested wording:** “The Gita distinguishes the body as field from its knower, identifies Krishna as the knower in every field, and calls knowledge of both field and knower true knowledge.” Explain Shankara's nondual interpretation separately, and repair the quotation in References as well as the prose. The [cited edition](https://analyticmadhyasthdarshan.org/References/Advaita-Vedanta/BG-Bhagavad-Gita-Shankara-Gambhirananda.pdf), printed pp. 502–504 / PDF pp. 503–505, includes Arjuna's opening question and numbers these verses 13.2–13.3; the alternative convention numbers them 13.1–13.2. State the convention once.

### R13 — Do not make bodily death complete liberating knowledge

**Location:** O §2.9, L522; glossary L1170.

“That recognition … is completed with the fall of the body” makes knowledge sound incomplete until death. The manuscript's explanation at L530 is better: already-begun karma explains bodily continuation after knowledge. The death of the body and the arising of liberating knowledge are distinct.

**Suggested wording:** “Liberating knowledge can arise while the body lives. The body's subsequent continuation is explained through already-begun karma; when that is exhausted, bodily existence ends, conventionally described as videhamukti.” This allows discussion of later differences over embodied liberation without implying that death produces freedom. BSB 1.1.4 distinguishes liberation from a produced result; 4.1.15 explains continuing bodily momentum. See printed p. 28 / PDF p. 52 and printed pp. 839–840 / PDF pp. 859–860 in the [BSB translation](https://analyticmadhyasthdarshan.org/References/Advaita-Vedanta/BSB-Brahma-Sutra-Bhashya-Gambhirananda.pdf).

### R14 — Define sublation as correction of a reality-claim

**Location:** O §2.10, L542; §5.7.4, L978; related shorthand L930, L969, L1151.

“What changes at paramartha is ontological status” and “removes the world's independent being” can imply that a previously independent world is changed by knowledge. The intended claim is that knowledge removes the **attribution** of independent reality. Brahman does not change; empirical appearance and conduct need not immediately cease.

**Suggested wording:** “Knowledge changes the understanding of the world's status: the attribution of independent reality is sublated, while empirical appearance and conduct may continue.” Define *badha* once in §2.4, then use the shorter formulation consistently. [BSB](https://analyticmadhyasthdarshan.org/References/Advaita-Vedanta/BSB-Brahma-Sutra-Bhashya-Gambhirananda.pdf) 2.1.14, printed pp. 330–334 / PDF pp. 352–356, and 4.1.15 support the distinction.

### R15 — Qualify the historical division over indefinability

**Location:** O §2.4, L476; Editorial Notes L1290, L1298; References L1346.

The distinction between Shankara and later systematic theories is useful, but the wording separates them too neatly. Shankara already uses *tattvanyatvabhyam anirvacaniye* for name and form in BSB 1.1.5: indeterminable as identical with or different from Brahman. Related language occurs at 2.1.14, translated in the cited edition as non-determinable as real or unreal. This does not establish that Shankara held every feature of later theories of positive avidya.

**Suggested wording:** “Later Advaita systematises indefinability in theories of mithyatva and error. Shankara already describes name and form as indeterminable as identical with or different from Brahman; later accounts develop this language in different ways.” The exact first occurrence of the compound *sad-asad-anirvacaniya* was **not established** in this review. Keep claims about its chronology only with a precise source. See [Sanskrit BSB 1.1.5](https://advaitabharati.sanatanasampatti.in/display/bhashya/BS) and [BSB 2.1.14 in translation](https://analyticmadhyasthdarshan.org/References/Advaita-Vedanta/BSB-Brahma-Sutra-Bhashya-Gambhirananda.pdf), printed p. 334 / PDF p. 356.

### R16 — Repair the Advaita citation and attribution cluster

**Location:** O L520, L526, L961, L965, L992; References L1343, L1351 and the §5 VC entry.

- **VC verse 639 does not exist in the cited edition**, which ends at verse 580. Verse 210 is relevant to sheath discrimination; verses 149–153 and 209–211 also address it. Remove 639 or identify the actual intended text; do not guess a replacement number.
- **“Shankara's Vivekachudamani”** contradicts the manuscript's own authorship qualification. Use the title alone or “traditionally attributed to Shankara.”
- The reference note saying **Paramatman is sublated together with Ishvara and jiva** is incorrect. VC 244 resolves the adjunct-based distinction between Ishvara and jiva; it does not sublate the Supreme Self. The main prose at L423 and L959 is already more accurate.
- **BS 1.1.2** is an imprecise pinpoint for systematic treatment of *tat tvam asi*. It defines Brahman through origin, continuance and dissolution. Prefer CU 6.8.7, BSB 1.1.4 or BSB 1.4.22 for the identity claim, while retaining 1.1.2 for cosmological definition.

These are repairs to the apparatus, not reasons to discard the exposition. Primary checks: [VC](https://analyticmadhyasthdarshan.org/References/Advaita-Vedanta/VC-Vivekachudamani-Madhavananda.pdf), PDF pp. 17, 20, 47; [BSB](https://analyticmadhyasthdarshan.org/References/Advaita-Vedanta/BSB-Brahma-Sutra-Bhashya-Gambhirananda.pdf), 1.4.22 at printed pp. 286–289 / PDF pp. 311–314.

### R17 — Distinguish Nagraj's objection from the later avidya-locus controversy

**Location:** O §6.3, L1043–1054.

The quoted objection concerns how jiva and world originate from Brahman while being illusory. Where ignorance is located is a related but distinct question. Saying that Nagraj thereby “engages that crux” makes the study's analytical connection sound like evidence of his precise historical target.

**Suggested handling:** “This objection can be related to a further Advaita problem: where ignorance is located.” Supply original passages for the Bhāmatī/Vivaraṇa attributions or shorten that excursus. A beginningless dependence is not simply an assertion that one member must temporally precede the other. The original later-school passages were not retrieved in this review, so their complete accounts should not be treated as verified here.

Also separate an external MD objection from an internal Advaita inconsistency. Failure to preserve MD's ultimate plurality is a disagreement, not by itself a defect in Advaita. The fair critical question is whether Advaita adequately explains dependence, ignorance and sublation. The manuscript's concluding qualification at L1054 should govern the entire discussion. [BSB 2.1.14](https://analyticmadhyasthdarshan.org/References/Advaita-Vedanta/BSB-Brahma-Sutra-Bhashya-Gambhirananda.pdf), printed pp. 333–334, explicitly defends empirical creator–creature instruction within nonduality.

### R18 — Remove the exclusive claim about resistance to reduction

**Location:** O §5.6, L934; compare the better formulation at L789.

The claim that the MD–Advaita agreement “does not extend to panpsychism, dualism, hylomorphism, or other non-reductive alternatives” contradicts the study's accurate recognition that several of these also resist reduction. The limited agreement is real; their positive ontologies differ.

**Suggested wording:** “Several other non-reductive positions share this resistance to reduction, but they differ over what bears experience and whether that bearer persists.” Retain the decisive difference between Advaita's nondual Self and MD's many permanently distinct jeevans.

### R19 — Compare hylomorphism through substantial unity

**Location:** O §3.6.1, L651.

The earlier description of Aquinas's rational soul as subsistent and the body's substantial form is sound. The later contrast with “prime matter plus a single rational soul per body” is misleading: hylomorphism is not the addition of two independently constituted objects, and one soul per body does not distinguish it from this study's MD account.

**Suggested wording:** “Aquinas explains the living human as one substance whose rational soul is its form; MD describes joint living of a pranic body and a constitutionally complete jeevan.” Distinguish a soul's subsistence after death from the completeness of a human person. Remove the resemblance to “substantial completion” unless that Aristotelian concept is defined and supported. See [Summa Theologiae I, q.76 a.1](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1076.htm), especially replies 5–6, and [q.75 aa.2, 4, 6](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1075.htm). Do not transfer Aquinas's complete account of survival to Aristotle without qualification.

### R20 — Replace the Leibniz link and identify the constitutional difference

**Location:** O §3.6.3, L659; References L1376.

Gutenberg item 43758 opens **The Blizzard in the West**, not *Monadology*. Replace it with the verified [Latta translation revised by Donald Rutherford](https://dss-sites.ucsd.edu/drutherford/Leibniz/translations/Monadology.pdf), accurately labelled, or [Latta's text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Monadology_(Leibniz,_tr._Latta)). A working HTTP link is not sufficient reference validation.

The windowlessness/pre-established-harmony contrast is correct. Add the more basic difference: monads are **simple and partless**, whereas the study's jeevan has a nucleus and orbital particles/faculties. Perception also belongs to monads generally; MD locates sentience in jeevan rather than every material unit. The analogy concerns plurality and persistence, not identical constitution or a common threshold of consciousness. Primary anchors: *Monadology* §§1–3, 6–7, 14, 18–19, 78–81.

### R21 — Clarify Samkhya's count and the location of mental functions

**Location:** O §5.4, L873.

Use “two irreducible kinds of principle: prakriti and many purushas,” rather than “two ultimates,” which sounds like two individual entities. The twenty-four include prakriti; its evolutes number twenty-three. “Prakriti and its twenty-three evolutes” removes the ambiguity.

Retain inactive witness versus active jeevan, and add the point most relevant to epistemology: buddhi and manas belong to non-conscious prakriti in Samkhya, while MD's five faculties belong to sentient jeevan. Similar words do not identify the same ontological bearers. The primary anchors are Karikas 3, 18–22, 27 and 62–68 in the [Samkhya Karika with Gaudapada's commentary](https://sites.rutgers.edu/edwin-bryant/wp-content/uploads/sites/169/2020/11/Samkhya-Karika-Gaudapada1.pdf).

### R22 — Tighten the entropy statement

**Location:** O §4.4, L734.

“Isolated or appropriately closed” leaves the relevant condition unspecified. A closed system can exchange heat and lose entropy; being closed excludes matter exchange, not entropy exchange. The later account of local organisation is sound.

**Suggested wording:** “For an isolated macroscopic system, entropy does not decrease, apart from statistical fluctuations. A subsystem can become more organised while exporting entropy to its surroundings.” See David Tong's [Statistical Physics §4.3.3](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/statphys/statmechhtml/S4.html) and [§1.2.1](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/statphys/statmechhtml/S1.html). This clarification need not expand the section.

## Remaining connections to epistemology and axiology

## Interpretations that should be retained

A close review should protect the improvements already made. In particular:

- The saturation triad describes overlapping relations and consequences; it need not be forced into exclusive one-to-one functions.
- Form, property, essential nature and dharma are real and relational. This is not a choice between isolated substances and observer-created attributes.
- Imaging, causal influence and completed knowledge are distinct. This supports E's account of a knowing process without making the body the knower.
- All ten activities belonging to jeevan and only four-and-a-half being effectively organised in delusion are explicitly connected in JV PDF p. 73. The distinction between constitution and effective organisation is supported.
- The thirty-value enumeration and the principal dimensional map are both supported, although their full explanation connects several passages. A single source table containing every relationship is not required.
- The correct established/expressed pairings include glory/simplicity and gratitude/humility. The two classified appearances of *udarta* do not invalidate the count: the enumeration counts roles, not thirty unique spellings. Align the ordering of O L96–97 with its correct pair list at L101.
- Named faculty positions and adjacent-faculty harmonies give compatible accounts. Do not discard one to retain the other.
- Distinguishing paramanand at realised atma from anand at buddhi is useful, while allowing contextual uses of bliss rather than imposing an exclusive gloss on every occurrence.
- Equal constitutional capacities of jeevans do not mean identical current bodily capabilities. Families and institutions do not become a collective jeevan.
- The comparisons rightly distinguish Advaita's dependent appearance from absolute non-being, and empirical ethics from ultimate non-agency. Preserve these against overcompressed tables.
