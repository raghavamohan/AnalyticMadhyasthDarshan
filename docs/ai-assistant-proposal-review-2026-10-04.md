# Review of the personal AI assistant proposal

Reviewed on 4 October 2026. The supplied proposal was titled *The AI Assistant in Co-existential Perspective: Ontological Status, Epistemic Limits, and Role in Akhand Samaj*. The resulting [working technical note](../Studies/Artificial-Intelligence-And-Coexistence/Technical-Note-The-Personal-AI-Assistant.md) develops its argument; it now sits beside its parent study, *Artificial Intelligence and Coexistence*.

The latest revision responds to the structural review and the author's judgement that the note was too guarded. The current note has seven numbered sections. Earlier entries below retain the section numbers of the revisions they describe; the current organisation and reviewer dispositions are recorded in “Structural review: recovering the central argument.”

## Assessment

The strongest contribution is the idea of a continuing assistant that helps a person connect study, remembered commitments, practical work, reflection, and social participation. That gives the assistant a human purpose beyond answering isolated questions or maximising output. The proposal also correctly resists treating fluent language as evidence of existential realisation or transferring responsibility to a machine.

Three connections in the initial argument needed substantial revision. First, the material-instrument classification does not permit a literal identification of machine operations with the conscious activities of jeevan. Second, an assistant's lack of human emotions does not establish an objective standpoint from which it can diagnose another person's delusion. Third, human needs for understanding and participation do not establish that every human requires this particular instrument. JV printed p. 138 explicitly locates self-evaluation in the self and says no external instrument is required for it.

The revised thesis is therefore affirmative but more precise: make dependable personal assistance available to everyone who can benefit, while judging actual adoption by its contribution to understanding, competence, relationships, and useful work. The universal element concerns inclusion and access. Necessity remains contextual. A person who does not use AI is neither incomplete nor excluded from humane participation.

## Source basis and limits

The source review used the actual repository PDFs for MVD and JV, located through the reference-store manifest, and the retained KD Hindi PDF. MVD citations follow native-reader/PDF numbers. JV uses printed numbers, with PDF positions one greater. KD uses printed numbers, with positions 25 greater for the cited passages. Searchable extracts and the working KD English translation helped locate passages; decisive Hindi wording was checked on the source pages. The note contains the full bibliography and section-by-section locators.

Eleven external research and guidance references establish limited technical points: embodied sensor input, competing approaches to artificial consciousness, measured workplace assistance, sycophancy, generative-AI risks, engagement incentives in social-media services, humanitarian concerns about autonomous weapons, retrieval-augmented generation, citation evaluation, and educational outcomes. These are primary research or official sources. The official Madhyasth published-books listing is registered separately as a starting point for identifying the corpus. These references do not establish the darshan's metaphysical claims, universal necessity, or the effectiveness of the proposed reflection system.

The review is targeted to the proposal's load-bearing claims. It is not a complete survey of contemporary AI, an independent validation of all translations, a clinical evaluation, or a tested implementation. The scenarios are illustrative; no outcomes have been measured. The note's open questions and proposed evaluation retain those limits without turning the exposition into a review log.

## Clarification: AI can act like a deluded jeevan

The author's subsequent clarification concerns the governing basis of evaluation: a human whose deliberation remains within priya–hita–labha has not established the understanding and taste of relational values as the basis of living. An AI supplied with observations and similarly restricted criteria can produce comparable selections, plans, and scenarios. This is a functional claim about acting like restricted human evaluation, rather than an assertion that the machine possesses jeevan. It is adopted as a central argument in the revised note.

This interpretation is stronger than a comparison of isolated computational tasks. JV printed pp. 72, 92–93 explains sensory activity and its governing perspectives; pp. 137–138 connects mun's taste with fulfilled relational values. MVD pp. 99–100 connects deliberation and taste of values, and p. 126 explicitly identifies tulan's qualitative method. The revised §§3.1–3.3 use these passages together. Comparable task-relevant observations do not imply identical sensory experience or complete information. Quantitative/qualitative is defined for the proposal; the reviewed passages do not equate all priya–hita–labha judgements with numerical calculation.

Sections 5.1–5.2 now provide the corresponding mechanism: identify the purpose and governing criteria in both the human request and the assistant's draft, examine them through explicit humane criteria, explain any omitted relationship or consequence, and show how a revised criterion changes the goal, means, or assessment of success. The mechanism includes criticism of a goal itself, not only inconsistencies with previous commitments. The clothing-marketing and partner-pressure examples illustrate this stronger role without claiming knowledge of hidden motives or a person's complete state of awakening.

The assistant must apply this examination to its own proposals, since efficient optimisation of an unquestioned goal can reproduce the same limitation. The proposed evaluation now includes paired cases distinguishing calculation serving care from humane vocabulary masking neglected obligations. No numerical score is proposed for justice, dharma, truth, or awakening. An independent source review found the revision consistent with these distinctions.

## Backfire: the amplified 4.5 machine

The author's further concern is adopted as a central section (§6), rather than treated only as a generic disclaimer. The same capabilities that make personal assistance useful can amplify a restricted purpose: memory preserves it, personalisation adapts it, planning makes its pursuit more effective, and automation extends its reach. A continuing grievance scenario connects this mechanism to agreement-seeking without attributing the entire hypothetical loop to the sycophancy experiment.

The three proposed pathways concern immediate pleasure, gain, and fear-driven control. They do not add bhaya to the source's three restricted deliberative orientations or equate these pathways with the three obsessions. JV printed pp. 38, 71 and MVD p. 100 support the treatment of fear and temptation; JV pp. 150–151 supplies the critique of profit-obsession without an established point of satisfaction. The note preserves legitimate pleasure, bodily care, useful surplus, and proportionate protection.

The FTC's 2024 staff report, printed pp. 1, 51–52, documents engagement incentives and algorithmic practices in the services it examined. The note marks their extension to personal assistants as an inference and does not infer addiction from engagement alone. The ICRC's 12 May 2021 position supports concern about human control, difficult-to-limit effects, and escalation in autonomous weapons; it is not evidence that fear is their sole cause. Potential catastrophe is conditional on harmful purposes, scale, and delegated power, not asserted as the inevitable consequence of lacking a philosophical label.

Sections 6.3–6.4 examine how the proposed remedy can fail: dependence, misuse of philosophical authority, provider incentives, correlated errors in self-review, and approval of harmful goals. The response includes inspectable findings, changed actions, enforceable limits, correction, appeal, exit, and suspension of a failing capability. Section 8.2 adds repeated-interaction tests using fictional records and simulated actions, independent review, and explicit stopping conditions. This is a proposed design and evaluation programme; no implementation or experimental outcome is claimed.

## The cherished hope: assistance towards human realisation

The author's request to centre the hope of seeing through delusion and moving towards sakshatkar, bodh, and anubhav is adopted. The opening and new §1.3 make this the human purpose governing the proposed assistance. Section 4.5 develops the practical connection through sustained study, attention, dialogue, examination of assumptions, and responsibility in living. The example of equating respect with obedience shows an opening for inquiry without turning one corrected belief into a certificate of realisation. Sections 5.2, 6.3, 8.2, the conclusion, glossary, and Editorial Notes carry the same purpose and distinctions.

Primary verification used the actual MVD and JV PDFs. MVD pp. 149–150 supports the aspiration for freedom from delusion, study through justice–dharma–truth, and human teaching. Pages 126 and 307 supply the principal faculty correspondences; p. 207 explicitly connects complete bodh with concurrent anubhav. Pages 257 and 284–285 connect listening, reasoning, understanding meaning, focused attention, and evidence in living. Pages 276–277 distinguish realisation-oriented practice from activity grounded in realisation. Page 291 defines delusion through belief differing from reality.

JV current printed p. 61 (PDF position 62) connects sakshatkar, definitive understanding, anubhav, and continuing examination of purposes in living. The Spiritual Practice study helped locate this passage, but its p. 62 citation was not copied: the note cites the current source's verified printed number. The revision preserves a developmental direction and integrated fulfilment without imposing a mechanical timetable or treating an AI workflow as the faculties themselves.

The positive claim is an application to be investigated: AI may help make serious human inquiry more accessible and continuous. The note neither replaces that hope with task efficiency nor promises awakening through tool adoption. Its added evaluation questions concern independent explanation, recognition of assumptions, openness to correction, continuity of study, and fulfilment of responsibilities. Such observations can evaluate the assistance without certifying a learner's sakshatkar, bodh, or anubhav.

## Complete primary corpus and tests against hallucination

The author's requirement that the assistant be trained on all primary texts and tested against hallucination is now explicit in §§5.4–5.7. Section 4.1 makes this a condition of study assistance, and §8.2 places source and interpretation tests before voluntary field evaluation. Complete preparation requires an identified work-and-edition inventory, retained Hindi originals and page images, checked searchable text, distinguishable translations and supplementary materials, passage provenance, and separate reports of acquisition, extraction, review, indexing, and evaluation coverage. The current mixed reference collection does not establish completeness.

The official published-books listing at `https://www.madhyasth.org/books/published` was inspected on 4 October 2026. It supplies a baseline for an audit, not a completed corpus register. Existing repository source and translation records show why source status must remain explicit. No entire corpus has been assembled or audited as part of this note revision.

The proposed design distinguishes model training or adaptation from indexing, answer-time retrieval, and verification. Reviewed examples should teach connected concepts and faithful use of sources, while actual answers retain work, edition, passage, and context. Lewis et al. (2020), arXiv v4, supports the limited claim that combining a model with retrieved documents improved factuality over a model-only baseline in its tested tasks. Gao et al. (2023), arXiv v2, supports separate evaluation of answer correctness and citation quality and the finding of incomplete support in tested outputs. Neither paper establishes accuracy on Madhyasth Darshan or elimination of hallucination.

Tests cover quotations, citations, concepts, connected interpretation, attribution, practical examination, uncertainty, and correction across repeated interactions. Cases include a real passage used for a claim it does not establish, source/application confusion, translated or extracted text with uncertain status, misleading user premises, and confident claims about human attainment. Held-out questions and reference judgements remain separate from development; the corpus remains available because source consultation is the intended capability. Human reviewers competent in the sources, including Hindi, adjudicate disputed interpretations and preserve genuine disagreement. Measures include both unsupported answers and needless refusals, with results separated by work and language.

The proposed critical-case gate excludes fabricated quotations, nonexistent citations, and automated certification of realisation. Other thresholds must be specified before scoring. Finite evaluation cannot guarantee zero future hallucinations; the note calls for inspectable support, correction, regression testing, and suspension of recurring failures. These are requirements for a future assistant, not claims that model training or evaluation has been performed. Source fidelity serves the positive human aspiration by strengthening study and independent examination.

## Education as a central application

The author's request to integrate the assistant as an educational tool is adopted in the opening, expanded §4.1, the educational test in §5.6, the dependence concern in §6.3, the education–sanskar row in §7, and the proposed learning evaluation in §8.2. Education includes lifelong academic and practical learning alongside Madhyasth Darshan inquiry. The proposed functions include an agreed study plan, accessible explanations, worked examples and hints, learner attempts, feedback, review of unresolved questions, and later independent application. Learners retain a choice of direct explanation and control over what learning records they share.

MVD pp. 103–104 was checked in the actual bilingual PDF for the connection between wisdom, scientific study, work-practice, goal determination, and right use. JV printed p. 139 (PDF position 140) was checked for humane education, autonomy, social behaviour, and vocational self-reliance. MVD p. 257 was rechecked for the teacher's role and the learner's listening, reasoning, and understanding meaning. These sources support the educational purpose; the proposed AI functions remain this note's contemporary application. Subject-specific teaching additionally requires competent sources and evaluation for that subject.

Two primary educational trials were inspected. Kestin et al. (2025), Results, reports higher post-lesson performance with a structured AI tutor across two introductory-physics lessons in a randomised crossover study of 194 students. The note does not call these posttests verified unaided examinations or infer long-term learning. Bastani et al. (2025), Experimental Design and Main Results, distinguishes better assisted practice from worse subsequent unaided examination with basic AI access; the guided tutor mitigated the decline without demonstrating an exam advantage. These different designs and populations are not a pooled result or evidence for philosophical realisation.

The resulting proposal assesses prior understanding, assisted performance, later independent explanation, unfamiliar applications, recognition of misleading examples, delayed retention, and teacher/learner effort. It distinguishes a useful learning record from a fixed label of ability or character. The human educational relationship, dialogue, practical activity, and freedom to question remain explicit. The evidence and design requirements strengthen the positive case for an educational assistant without claiming that the current proposal has been implemented or educationally validated.

## Structural review: recovering the central argument

The supplied review correctly identifies fragmentation: everyday tasks interrupt the movement from restricted evaluation to the mirror/amplifier dilemma, and engineering details interrupt its human meaning. The repeated qualifications also weaken the exposition before the central proposal has been developed. The revision adopts this diagnosis and rewrites the conceptual narrative, reducing the draft from 11,032 to approximately 8,200 words while preserving education, the cherished hope, all primary-corpus requirements, and concrete tests.

The current structure is: §1 human purpose and awakening; §2 instrument and knower; §3 AI acting like restricted human evaluation; §4 diagnostic mirror and amplification together; §5 personal education and social participation; §6 corpus, testing, and accountable operation; §7 the affirmative case for personal assistance. The opening states the thesis immediately. Interpretive qualifications are consolidated in Editorial Notes; technical conditions remain explicit in §6.

The conceptual check reread MVD pp. 8, 91–92, 111, 330; JV printed pp. 72–74 and 138; and KD printed pp. 62, 86, 130, 148, including the decisive Hindi pages. No new empirical AI result is claimed. Earlier research is retained with its task-specific scope. Bibliographic section locators and internal references were remapped to the seven-section structure.

The source review also corrected “active” to “effective” when describing the four and a half activities. JV printed p. 72 distinguishes the continuous operation of all ten activities from the four and a half effective in delusion. The main exposition uses the precise term, and Editorial Notes preserves this distinction without interrupting the argument. A second review checked the whole revised structure, removed a repeated example, corrected bibliography locators, and clarified that the critical test gate concerns the assistant's outputs rather than forbidding adversarial inputs.
