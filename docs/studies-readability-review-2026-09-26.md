# Studies readability and comparative structure: review of the supplied feedback

Review date: 26 September 2026. Repository baseline: `master`, `8473da46c196dcf59c52c3a71a923c9f4ea17d65`.

The feedback is substantially right about the difficulty of entry. The strongest improvement is to make the question, the competing answers, and the argument's limits intelligible while helping readers progressively understand the full classification system. Preserve the scholarship and the connected Madhyasth Darshan account; improve how its detail is explained and connected.

**Scope clarified by the author on 26 September 2026:** Every study must retain its full substantive information and detail. This principle applies throughout the collection, including topical, formal, and applied work, rather than only to Ontology or to released studies. Length and difficulty are reasons to improve the teaching, not grounds for cutting content. Revisions should preserve each study's claims, distinctions, classifications, relationships, examples, qualifications, and supporting evidence. Clearer wording, local reordering, well-explained tables and figures, and additional examples or connecting prose are appropriate; a revised study may become longer. The earlier proposals to shorten expositions or transfer their detail to appendices or companions merely as a readability remedy are withdrawn for all studies. Existing appendices and formal treatments keep their legitimate roles. Success means better understanding of the complete account, with no loss of information; it does not require preserving every original sentence or repeated phrase.

Three qualifications matter. The denser studies contain substantial comparison, rather than merely token rival positions. Axiology already identifies the philosophical problem behind essentiality and value. And the proposed sample rewrite improves the rhythm while loosening several defined meanings. We should adopt the reader's experience as evidence of a communication problem, without treating every proposed remedy as conceptually interchangeable with the present text.

## 3. What the structure actually shows

Approximate counts show the scale of the reader's task and help locate where sustained explanation is especially important. They do not establish that the information should be reduced. These are whitespace counts after removing Markdown link destinations and HTML tags, rounded to the nearest hundred. They include headings, citations, tables, and some Markdown syntax, and exclude material from the first Appendix, Editorial Notes, or References heading onward. They are structural indicators, not linguistic complexity scores, reduction targets, or estimates of reading time.

| Released topical study | Main-body words, approximately | Initial MD exposition | Share of body | First dedicated rival section |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Ontology | 23,100 | 8,700 in §1 | 38% | Advaita, §2 |
| Epistemology | 13,700 | 4,900 in §1 | 36% | Advaita, §2 |
| Axiology | 13,300 | 4,200 in §1 | 31% | Advaita, §2 |
| Why Humans | 9,000 | 2,100 in §1 | 23% | Scientific evidence and physicalism, §2 |
| Nature of Time | 4,500 | 1,900 in §1 | 43% | Advaita, §2 |
| Undivided Society | 5,500 | 900 in §2 | 16% | Alternatives, §4; earlier evaluative framing in §1 |
| Self-Sustaining Organizations | 5,900 | Distributed across thematic sections | Not comparable | Modern forms, §7; Advaita, §11 |

The named sections are landmarks, not the first occurrence of a rival's name. The papers often mention competing views earlier. *Undivided Society* also has substantive diagnosis before §4, so its 900-word foundation must not be mistaken for the entire wait before comparison.

Ontology's Advaita, modern philosophy, science, and comparison sections together contain approximately 12,200 words. Calling those merely sidebars would be inaccurate. Nonetheless, a reader must traverse an unusually long system exposition before encountering them in depth. Epistemology's dedicated comparison arrives after four substantial expositions; a reader needs an earlier reason to care about their differences.

The figures also show why percentage alone is misleading. *Nature of Time* allocates a larger proportion to §1 than Ontology but asks for fewer than a quarter as many words before Advaita. *Why Humans* is not especially short: its advantage is that familiar experience and a clearly stated disagreement organise the exposition.

There are three different costs to reduce:

1. **Entry cost:** how much the reader must already understand to grasp the first paragraph.
2. **Dependency cost:** how many distinctions must be remembered before they are used in an argument.
3. **Delayed payoff:** how long the reader waits to see why one account differs from another and what the difference explains.

Clearer sentences and plain-language lead-ins help the first cost. Introducing the organising distinction, explaining a complete taxonomy in stages, and using it in an example help the second. A concise early statement of disagreement helps the third. Across all studies, these are ways to support the reader through all the information while preserving it in the main exposition. A shorter paper is not the success criterion.

Introduce that example earlier, retaining its full distinctions and the later argument it supports. Food can nourish; money can represent a price; a caregiver can recognise a responsibility; and actual care can succeed or fail. Return to the same situation as further concepts are explained, so it helps the reader connect the whole account rather than substituting for its detail.

## 6. A writing practice that preserves meaning

### Explain the point, then name it, then use it

A gloss such as “essentiality (*maulikta*)” translates a label but may still explain little. A stronger paragraph establishes a distinction the reader can recognise, gives its technical name, and shows how the argument depends on it. The source citation supports that statement.

For Axiology, an editorial prototype could begin:

> Feeding a child brings several kinds of value into one activity. Food contributes to nourishment; care directs the adult's participation; money records a price. A higher price does not by itself mean better nourishment or better care. Madhyasth Darshan connects value with the definite character and participation of the beings involved, whether or not a person evaluates them correctly. Understanding the responsibility, having the means, and actually fulfilling it remain distinguishable.

This adapts the existing §§1.1, 1.4, and 1.5 rather than offering a new proof of essentiality. Its narrow example would be followed by the broader definition and its primary citations.

As an editing heuristic, look closely at any introductory paragraph that adds more than two or three unfamiliar terms. This is a prompt to explain and connect the terms, not to omit any of them or enforce a numeric publication rule. An enumeration can be appropriate after its organising distinction is understood. Preserve the complete terminology wherever its precise identities matter, and use consistent English explanations rather than switching between loose synonyms.

### Distinguish textual warrant, argument, and evidence in ordinary prose

“Settled” is ambiguous. A claim can be explicit in the texts, internally connected to other claims, philosophically contested, and empirically unestablished at the same time. The studies need clear sentences about those relations, rather than a single status label.

| Situation | Useful formulation |
|---|---|
| Stating the philosophy's commitment | “Madhyasth Darshan holds that…” followed by the affirmative account. |
| Explaining the study's conclusion | State the inference and the premises it depends on. |
| Preserving an empirical boundary | “This distinction does not establish the proposed constitution or persistence of jeevan.” |
| Rejecting a particular inference | Name the inference and explain why the premise does not establish it. |
| Identifying an unresolved issue | State what additional argument, observation, or public criterion would resolve it. |

Remove verbal padding such as repeated “may perhaps suggest.” Preserve uncertainty that changes what a reader is entitled to conclude. Keep translation variants in Editorial Notes, serious criticism in the critical discussion, and any material ambiguity visible where it affects the argument. “Released” remains a publication status, not a certification of philosophical truth.

### Borrow the strongest form of each opposing position

The principle is already well expressed in *Why Humans*: physicalism can recognise mental life and moral commitment. Similar care should govern all comparative papers. Distinguish scientific findings from metaphysical interpretations; distinguish Advaita's empirical and ultimate claims; and distinguish competing Western theories rather than giving “the West” one answer.

A three-column snapshot fits *Why Humans* more naturally than Ontology, Epistemology, or Axiology, which separate modern philosophy from science and discuss disagreements within those groups. A table with one “science” cell can erase the distinction the study needs to preserve. Choose comparison dimensions around a shared question, not a forced number of columns.

### Make openings communicate a finding and its limit

A brief orientation paragraph, often 120–180 words where useful, can help a reader recover the question, MD's answer, the central disagreement, and the most important unresolved burden. This is an optional guide to the scale of orientation, not a cap on the introduction or substantive exposition. In every study, retain all unique information from the existing introduction and full account; the orientation helps readers approach that information and does not replace it. Use additional explanatory prose wherever needed.

For example, the following Ontology opening is an editorial prototype based on its current conclusion:

> What must an account of reality explain about things, consciousness, and their relationships? Madhyasth Darshan answers that existence is the coexistence of an all-pervading, actionless ground and countless real, active units. It treats the world and distinct sentient selves as enduring realities, and connects understanding that reality with the fulfilment of relationships. Advaita Vedanta gives ultimate reality a different status: the deepest Self is non-dual consciousness. Modern philosophy and science offer several further accounts of matter, mind, and explanation. These positions sometimes share vocabulary without making the same claims. This study develops their differences and asks what would justify preferring one account. The primary texts support a connected Madhyasth Darshan ontology, but that support does not establish its claims against alternatives. The constitution and persistence of jeevan, the interpretation of physical terms, and the connection between coexistence and obligation still require further argument and evidence.

This is not a proposed universal preface. Each paper needs its own intellectual result and limitation. An opening must not claim that a short summary contains all the qualifications needed to evaluate the argument. All such prototypes are entries into complete studies, not abridged replacements or permission to remove what they omit.

## 8. Relationship to the repository's editorial rules

## 9. Order of implementation and evidence of improvement

For every study revision, begin with a working content inventory of the substantive claims, distinctions, classifications, relationships, examples, qualifications, and source support in each affected subsection. Include formal assumptions, notation, model rules, and derivations where present. Compare that inventory with the revision to ensure that every item remains in the study and that connected explanations have survived. This inventory is a check for the edit, not a new project backlog or material for the published essay. The success measure is whether readers understand the complete account more accurately; its length may stay the same or increase.

Begin implementation with a small, assessable prose pilot: the six-perspectives passage in *Why Humans* and the opening/first-example sequence in Axiology. Preserve their full information while testing clearer explanations, connections, and examples. Compare the originals and revisions with readers before propagating the technique.

Then improve the full Ontology and Epistemology expositions through the teaching changes in §4, using what the pilot shows about comprehension. Both retain all detailed classifications and their connected arguments. Explain the conceptual dependencies and maintain stable identifiers where possible; repair incoming references when local reordering changes them. Adding headings alone is insufficient unless the explanation within and between them helps the reader. Apply the same retention and comprehension checks to the other studies as their revisions proceed.

The homepage action can be improved independently. Persistent reader orientation belongs with the existing `UX-07` argument-route work. Extend the successful prose pattern to the other released studies, including the formal study, while respecting their different scopes. This order ranks the present editorial work; it does not reorder unrelated authentication, audio, infrastructure, or reference priorities in the project's existing backlog.

Use a small formative reader exercise, for example four to six readers spanning newcomers and readers of comparative philosophy. That size can reveal misunderstandings; it cannot establish a population-wide effect. Give readers the opening and one representative section, then ask them to explain:

- the question and MD's proposed answer in their own words;
- a rival's strongest relevant answer;
- what a central technical term means in the example;
- what the study concludes and what it has not established;
- where to read next or find the detailed support.

Record wrong inferences as well as hesitation. In particular, watch for “physicalists deny values,” “dharma just means purpose,” “satya means social harmony,” “a source citation proves the ontology,” and “the example proves a separate jeevan.” Those would show that fluency had been purchased at the expense of accuracy. Counterbalance which version readers see first where practical; do not interpret preference alone as comprehension.

For the homepage, ask a newcomer to find the recommended first reading and a returning reader to locate a known study or passage. For the reader, test direct links, search arrivals, and path arrivals. Include narrow-screen, keyboard, and screen-reader checks when implementing the navigation. An initial screen should offer a clear next action without obscuring exploration.

Useful editorial acceptance questions are concrete: Can a reader explain the organising distinction and then use the full taxonomy correctly? Does each list have an explanation and an argumentative use nearby? Can a critic recognise the rival position? Does the closing qualification survive the summary? Can a specialist recover every classification and its evidence? For every revised study, check that all substantive items in the content inventory remain, detailed passages are explained where they belong in the argument, and examples preserve the distinctions they illustrate. In formal work, also check that the prose and illustrations match the retained definitions, assumptions, and model rules. Word-count reduction is not an acceptance criterion; extra explanation is worthwhile when it improves understanding without changing the claim.

## 10. Implementation safeguards and limits of this report

Canonical study changes require the usual feature branch, a real current `Edited on` timestamp, synchronized catalogs, and affected HTML/PDF regeneration through the repository pipeline. Every study requires the information-retention check described in §9: a fluent or shorter revision is insufficient if it loses substantive information, a connection, or its support. Any new or changed quotations need source verification, and changed citations need reference checks. Section movement requires checking references both within the paper and from other studies and companions. A substantial restructure can also require deck, presenter, and audio alignment; inspect those dependencies rather than silently leaving contradictory teaching material.

Homepage edits belong in `_build_studies_index.py`, with matching shared wording in README and a rebuilt/verified index. Reader-only navigation changes need the reader tests, glossary consistency, and offline build checks; they do not require blanket study timestamp changes or PDF regeneration. Use the established study finalizer and applicable lifecycle checks for actual study edits. Publication remains with the protected workflow after merge.

For this report, validation consists of reading the named studies, selected direct primary-text checks, the structural counts, repository/source inspection, and one live desktop homepage inspection. It does not include a complete citation audit, fresh scientific literature review, reader testing, mobile visual verification, or rendered-output verification. No study, homepage, reader code, catalog, rule, or published artifact was changed. The report and its linked backlog entries record proposed improvements, with implementation still to follow.
