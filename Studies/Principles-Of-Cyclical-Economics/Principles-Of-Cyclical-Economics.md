Cyclical production, non-depletive consumption, and genuine wealth (samriddhi) in Madhyasth Darshan: a critical analysis of Avartansheel Arthashastra against extractive capitalism and socialism

## Study proposal

This study undertakes a systematic philosophical and structural analysis of Shri A. Nagraj's economic treatise, Avartansheel Arthashastra (Cyclical Economics). Modern economics is fundamentally structured around profit maximization, scarcity, debt-driven monetary expansion, and linear extraction of natural resources leading to ecological crisis and inequality.

Key areas of inquiry:
1. Cyclical production (avartansheelta) harmonized with the regenerative cycles of nature's material and plant orders.
2. Labor (shram), right-use (sadupyog), and the rejection of profit-maximization and interest-bearing accumulation as the basis of exchange.
3. Defining prosperity (samriddhi) as the assured availability of physical means in excess of properly assessed bodily needs, rooted in family self-reliance.
4. Comparative analysis with free-market capitalism, state-controlled socialism, and modern ecological/steady-state economic models.
5. Primary texts: Avartansheel Arthashastra, Samadhanatmak Bhautikvad, and Manav Vyavahar Darshan.
