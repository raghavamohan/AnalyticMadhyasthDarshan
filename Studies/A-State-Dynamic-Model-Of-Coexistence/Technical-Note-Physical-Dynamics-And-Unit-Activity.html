          Abstract

          The state-dynamic model of coexistence describes existence through units whose activities establish configurations, interactions, and continuing order. Quantitative development can begin by adopting established physical laws for the insentient, or jada, domain. This note proposes a bridge between Lagrangian and Hamiltonian descriptions of material dynamics and the four aspects of the active unit: form (roop), relative power (guna), essential nature (svabhav), and innateness (). It also examines law (niyam), regulation (niyantran), and balance (santulan) as connected questions about definite conduct, the conditions that sustain it, and participation in a larger order. A damped, driven oscillator illustrates configuration, interaction, persistence, and dynamic balance. The approach then extends to material composition, pranic organisation, and the body associated with . Jeevan’s constitution remains invariant, while understanding, evaluation, and dispositions require a distinct account of refinement. Saturation remains an ontological premise, and bodily association requires an explicit interface for coupled predictions. The note establishes an initial research specification: conceptual correspondences, a worked physical example, a proposed architecture, validation requirements, and unresolved questions. Mathematical formulations are collected in the appendices.

          1. The question and intended achievement

          How can the qualitative account of unit activity become a model with explicit laws of change? The State-Dynamic Model of Coexistence already distinguishes constitution, changing conditions, mutuality, disposition, and evidence. Its occurrence relations identify what a coherent account must include. A quantitative extension must additionally specify variables, durations, interactions, and rules that determine particular changes.

          This note adopts established physics for material activity at the scale being studied. Jada includes material and pranic units, including the human body. The new theoretical work principally concerns the interpretation of physical descriptions within the ontology, ’s refinable state, and the interface through which sentient activity participates bodily. This commitment supplies a practical starting point; the correspondence between particular physical entities and the source’s atomic account remains a question for investigation (§9).

          The intended achievement is a usable specification for the next stage of the study. It should identify which descriptions can be supplied by physics, explain how they answer the four-aspect and orderliness questions, and state what additional structure is required for Jeevan. A successful next version will reproduce an independently understood material example, extend it to a bounded case of human participation, and make clear which predictions depend on measured inputs and which depend on a proposed learning or interface rule.

          This initial version develops the conceptual bridge and an analytical example. It supplies no fitted Jeevan dynamics or experimental validation. The main study remains the qualitative synthesis; this companion develops a possible quantitative extension.

          2. The active unit and its continuing organisation

          A unit is an active bearer with definite constitution and form. Its strength in state and power in motion are inseparable; effort, motion, and result describe connected aspects of its continuing activity. Form, property, essential nature, and describe that same unit through its configuration, effective participation, characteristic conduct, and innateness (SB, pp. 58–62, 248–252, 256–257; MVD, pp. 47, 50–51).

          The bridge begins with this continuity. A physical state describes selected conditions of a unit at a considered time. An evolution law relates those conditions to further conditions through actual interactions. The time interval marks a duration within activity. It does not separate effort, motion, and result into three successive processes. The parent study’s occurrence index can therefore be related to observation times or numerical integration intervals without assuming that nature proceeds through discrete pauses.

          Constitution, configuration, and environment have different roles. A mechanical assembly can retain its parts while their positions change. A compound can form or disintegrate. A body can maintain its organisation while taking in and releasing matter. An adopted physical model must specify which of these changes it represents and when its original description ceases to apply. The distinction between a containing whole and interacting participants is especially important: a model boundary identifies a system for study, while a claim that a further unit exists requires an account of its definite organisation and conduct (MVD, p. 42; SB, p. 260).

          Saturation grounds the intrinsic activeness of units within the coexistence account. In this extension it remains a standing ontological premise. The physical equations account for the finite energies, forces, and exchanges of the selected material system. A future physical explanation of saturation would require additional definitions and evidence (§9; parent study, Appendix A.2).

          3. Lagrangian and Hamiltonian descriptions

          3.1 Describing structure and change

          A Lagrangian description starts with suitable coordinates, their rates of change, and a function specifying the dynamics. For a familiar mechanical system this function is kinetic energy minus potential energy. The corresponding action is accumulated over a time interval, and stationary action yields the equations of motion. The principle concerns stationarity, which need not be a minimum (Tong-L, §2.1).

          A Hamiltonian description replaces velocities with conjugate momenta, where this change of variables is locally invertible. It presents evolution through first-order equations on the space of possible states. The two descriptions then give the same physical trajectories from corresponding initial conditions. The Hamiltonian is particularly useful for examining symmetries and conserved quantities (SICM-H, §3.1).

          Neither formulation selects a physical model by name alone. Each application needs a specified constitution, interaction law, initial state, and surrounding conditions. Mechanical action, measured in energy multiplied by time, also has a different meaning from purposeful human action. The mathematical construction is set out in Appendix A.

          3.2 Exchanges and levels of description

          Forces supplied by the surroundings and dissipative effects can be included explicitly in the Lagrange equations. A spring, moving mass, and damper provide a simple example. Its mechanical energy changes through work supplied to the assembly and energy transferred to its surroundings (MIT-D, pp. 1–4).

          The broader jada programme will choose models appropriate to the phenomenon: mechanical motion, chemical transformation, heat and material transport, or bodily regulation. Hamiltonian and Lagrangian methods provide an entry into this programme. Atomic and molecular applications require the relevant quantum descriptions; a pranic model requires the processes that sustain bodily organisation. These applications have their own state variables and empirical parameters. A single classical particle trajectory is insufficient to specify all of them.

          4. A bridge to roop, guna, svabhav, and dharma

          The four aspects are co-present descriptions of one active bearer. Their physical representation will consequently overlap. Coordinates, interaction laws, and persistence conditions jointly help answer the four questions; there is no requirement to assign each aspect to one mathematical quantity.

          Aspect Meaning in the unit account Proposed use in a physical model
          Roop — form Definite configuration, dimensions, density, and boundary-framework Specify geometry, composition, spatial distributions, and the configuration that is present. Include internal conditions needed to explain how that form can change.
          Guna — relative power The unit’s operative influence in actual mutuality Describe responses, interactions, transfer rates, and the resulting effects on participating units. State the conditions under which a capacity is expressed.
          Svabhav — essential nature The functional character and usefulness of the unit’s powers within its order Identify whether the modelled participation integrates or disintegrates material organisation, or vitalises or devitalises a pranic organisation.
          Dharma — innateness What is borne according to order: existence in the material order, with growth included in the pranic order State the order being represented and examine the continuity of its organisation and participation. Distinguish that organisation’s persistence from the continued existence of its constituents after dissolution.

          The definitions follow the four-aspect account (MVD, pp. 42, 47, 50–51, 77, 115; SB, pp. 179–180, 248–252, 256–257). The physical representations in the final column are this note’s proposal.

          For roop, position alone can be inadequate: two bodies with the same outline can differ in temperature, composition, or internal stress and therefore evolve differently. These conditions belong in the physical state, even where their relation to outward form is indirect. For guna, an interaction term and the response it produces are more informative than an isolated material constant. Elastic stiffness, for example, contributes to a definite response only within a particular configuration and encounter.

          Svabhav gives the effect its order-specific significance. Formation and dissolution both occur within material participation. In a living organisation, the further question concerns nourishment, growth, repair, and decline. states the innateness whose manifestation is being considered. A conserved physical quantity can help explain a particular continuity, but dharma has no established identity with energy, momentum, or any other conserved scalar.

          The same care applies to the effort–motion–result account. A physical trajectory provides a succession of configurations and interactions through which the three aspects can be examined together. The source terms bal, shakti, and shram need their defined meanings; translating them as strength, power, and effort does not assign them units of newtons, watts, or joules. Such a numerical correspondence would require a separate operational definition.

          5. Niyam, niyantran, and santulan

          Law, regulation, and balance express connected orderliness. Niyam is the basis for regulating conduct and activity and the way in which activity is preserved and disciplined. Characteristic conduct expresses svabhav and is itself connected with niyam. Niyantran concerns the lawful mutual recognition through which units participate in definite relations; in material units, recognition means constitution-conformant response. Santulan includes the mediative maintenance of balance between generative and dissolutive activities (MVD, pp. 34, 70, 77, 154; SB, pp. 56–57, 62).

          A unit maintains its definite character and participates in mutual relations. Material conduct is described through result-conformance; pranic regulation through seed-conformance; animal balance through species-conformance. Humans establish conscious order through understanding and . These characteristic emphases belong to a connected account in which law, regulation, and balance apply across existence (SB, pp. 235–236; MVD, pp. 174–175).

          The proposed bridge distinguishes three questions within each physical application:

          Concept Question to make explicit Physical description to investigate
          Niyam — law What makes the conduct definite under the stated conditions? Evolution equations, admissible states, interaction laws, and their domain of validity.
          Niyantran — regulation Through what organisation and mutual conditions is that conduct maintained or altered? Constraints, internal couplings, surrounding exchanges, and, where present, feedback that maintains an operating range.
          Santulan — balance How does the unit’s activity remain coherent with the participating whole? Specified balance relations, bounded operation, persistence, and stability or recovery under stated disturbances, together with effects on other participants.

          These are complementary analytical questions. A constraint can contribute to law and regulation; a maintained exchange can contribute to regulation and balance. The table does not divide natural orderliness into three independent mechanisms or require a separate controller for every unit.

          Physical balance also needs a declared meaning. An equality between incoming and outgoing flows, a stationary configuration, and recovery after disturbance answer different questions. Stability analysis can establish a particular form of persistence or return under explicit conditions (LYAP, local stability and LaSalle’s invariance principle). The wider meaning of santulan concerns participation as well as the persistence of a selected subsystem. A device may operate steadily while exhausting the resource that sustains its surroundings. Its steady operation alone would leave the wider question unanswered.

          For human living, balance requires evaluation of relationships, values, responsibilities, and consequences. Balanced living is connected with justice, and participation in universal orderliness with resolution (MVD, p. 174). The physical description supplies facts that this evaluation must take into account.

          6. A worked material example

          Consider a mass attached to a spring and damper, with an optional external drive. The assembly is a system chosen for analysis; this choice does not by itself classify it as a new compound. Its material components have definite dimensions and constitution. The mass moves, the spring responds to displacement, the damper transfers mechanical energy into heat, and the drive can supply work. Appendix A derives its motion and energy balance.

          Roop is represented through the assembly’s geometry and its changing configuration. Guna becomes evident in the spring’s restoring response, the damper’s resistance, and the drive’s effect in their actual mutual arrangement. Material svabhav is examined through the maintained organisation and, in an expanded model, its formation or breakdown. Material remains existence: termination of this assembly’s organisation leaves its constituents participating in subsequent material configurations. An ideal spring equation describes only a limited part of that account; it contains no fracture or chemical transformation law.

          Niyam is expressed through the definite response equations and their assumptions. Niyantran is examined through the attachment constraints, restoring interaction, damping, and specified drive. Santulan is investigated through an explicitly chosen scope: continued motion within the assembly’s operating range, its exchange with the surroundings, and its persistence under disturbances.

          Three cases expose the distinctions. An ideal undamped assembly continues oscillating after displacement. With damping and no drive, the model approaches its resting configuration. With a periodic drive and positive damping, it can approach a repeating motion in which average supplied power equals average dissipated power. Balance can thus accompany continuing motion and exchange. These statements follow for the specified oscillator; they are not a derivation of universal natural balance (MIT-D, pp. 2–4; Appendix A.3).

          A resting coordinate also describes only the variable selected by the model. It does not establish that all activity of the material bearer has ceased. This is how a reduced mechanical description can coexist with the ontology’s broader account of continuous unit activity.

          7. Extending the approach to Jeevan and human participation

          7.1 Invariant constitution and refinable disposition

          has a constitutionally complete organisation with atma at the centre and , , , and in successive orbits. Its constitution remains complete while its sentient functioning develops. The main study places this fixed organisation in the constitutional description and the changing disposition in a separate persistent profile (MVD, pp. 78, 91; parent study, §§6–7 and Appendix A.3).

          The extension should preserve that distinction. The variable content concerns understanding, accepted meanings, evaluation references, purposes, , and the coherence of faculty activity. Faculty existence is invariant. Reports and observations about these states belong in an evidence record; a person’s report of realisation does not, by itself, determine the underlying state.

          This suggests a structured transition account for Jeevan: an encountered situation is recognised through a present understanding; evaluation informs resolve and selection; consequences become available for further examination; study and recognition can revise accepted meanings and dispositions. Such an account may initially use qualitative states and event-based changes. A numerical rate of refinement becomes meaningful only after its variables and observation procedures have been defined.

          7.2 What can be carried over from mechanics

          Mechanics offers a disciplined way to separate state, invariant structure, evolution, coupling, and observation. Those distinctions can organise a Jeevan model. The concentric account supplies faculty roles, but it does not supply measured orbital coordinates, conjugate momenta, or a Hamiltonian for semantic change.

          A future variational proposal for Jeevan would therefore need to define the admissible states, their meaningful differences, allowed trajectories, and the functional whose stationarity yields the proposed change. It would then need to show why this construction fits the source account and improves prediction. The first version can proceed with a specified revision relation; a Hamiltonian form is a research question, rather than an entry requirement.

          7.3 Bodily expression and the interface

          The body remains a pranic organisation described through its material processes. Its association with Jeevan manifests the human being. The model must connect bodily presentation to sentient recognition and connect resolve and selection to bodily expression while retaining the two units (parent study, §6.4 and Appendix A.3).

          For a conditional model, observed bodily activity can be supplied as an input to the material model, and reported experience together with observed conduct can inform the proposed refinement model. For a joint prediction, the interface must itself be specified through a functional relation or an empirically estimated rule. Its underlying mechanism can remain unexplained while its input–output behaviour is investigated. Any proposed coupling must remain compatible with the physical equations and exchanges of the chosen system; an assertion of zero added energy alone does not settle that compatibility.

          7.4 Values, fulfilment, and continuity

          The knowledge order brings evaluation into conscious participation. Its fulfilment includes happiness, and the highest goal is paramanand with continuity of realisation and fulfilment. Justice concerns recognising and fulfilling relationships through values and their evaluation (MVD, pp. 101, 174, 327; parent study, §§7.8–11; Axiology: Value Theory, §§1.1–1.8).

          The extension therefore retains the study’s value distinctions: Jeevan values, humane qualities, established and expressed relationship values, and object values. A resource quantity or successful bodily action supplies only part of the evidence. Evaluation must also consider the actual relationship, recognised responsibility, people affected, and continuing consequences. A numerical implementation should use a structured assessment of these requirements before considering any scalar summary.

          Continuity has three connected scopes: continuing realisation in Jeevan, sustained humane conduct through changing situations, and the continuation of understanding and fulfilment through successive participants. A stable behaviour pattern can be examined mathematically, but its stability does not establish its justice or the realisation of paramanand. Families and institutions extend this inquiry through education, shared arrangements, and the transmission of understanding. They remain organisations of participating persons; the extension assigns them no additional Jeevan. Their contribution to akhand samaj, universal human order, and awakened tradition is examined through the continuity of that participation (MVD, pp. 338–339; parent study, Appendix A.11).

          8. Development and validation approach

          The research should advance through bounded applications whose results can be checked independently. The oscillator establishes the mathematical method. A subsequent shared-water example can connect a physical process to evaluation without requiring a complete theory of society.

          In that example, the material account follows stored water, inflow, withdrawal, and the conditions of delivery. The human account follows needs, actual relationships, agreed responsibilities, reasons for allocation, action, and subsequent evaluation. Water delivered is measurable. Whether the allocation fulfils responsibility requires the relationship and value account as well. If a valve is actuated, its force and energy requirements belong to the physical description; the choice of allocation belongs to the proposed evaluation and interface description.

          Work package Concrete output Requirement for accepting the result
          Material specification State dictionary, system boundary, interactions, initial conditions, and adopted equations Reproduce the known example; check dimensions, energy or material accounting, and sensitivity to numerical resolution.
          Ontological bridge A description of all four aspects and the three orderliness questions for the same example Every correspondence has a clear meaning, scope, and source basis; physical variables retain their scientific definitions.
          Composite or pranic extension One model of maintained organisation with explicit exchanges Account for the constituents, containing organisation, and changes that invalidate the chosen model.
          Human participation pilot Separate records for observations, accepted meanings, responsibilities, decisions, and consequences Use observed interface inputs first; state what would be required to predict them.
          Refinement and continuity Competing revision rules and criteria for continuing fulfilment Compare predictions with later observations; distinguish bodily limitations, circumstances, and changes in understanding.

          Validation has three distinct tasks. Source review checks the meanings and connections being represented. Mathematical analysis checks whether the proposed equations entail the stated behaviour. Empirical comparison checks whether that behaviour fits observations. Agreement in one task supplies no automatic result in the others.

          For the pilot, repeated cases must help distinguish competing explanations. The same outward action can arise from different accepted meanings; a person can understand a responsibility while lacking the bodily or material capacity to fulfil it. Observations of reasons, action, consequences, and later revision should therefore be examined together. Finite observations can support evidence of maintained fulfilment over the tested situations; a claim of indefinite continuity needs an additional argument.

          9. Issues requiring further work

          Issue What remains to be established
          Physical identification of units Define the scale and map the selected physical system to the ontological description. Specific claims about atomic development and constitutional completeness need their own comparison with contemporary atomic physics.
          Saturation Clarify whether a physical interpretation is proposed, and what observation could distinguish it. The present equations contain no additional saturation force or energy supply.
          Boundaries and composition Establish when a changing aggregate counts as a maintained whole and when it dissolves. Match constituent and whole descriptions without counting the same matter or energy twice.
          Open and pranic dynamics Specify exchanges, reactions, transport, and the operating conditions required for continuing bodily organisation.
          Meaning of balance Choose the quantities, participants, durations, and disturbances being assessed. Separate local equilibrium, boundedness, recovery, and wider complementary participation.
          Jeevan’s mutable state Define what a change in understanding, evaluation, or sanskar consists of, and which distinctions can be inferred from evidence. Keep its constitutional architecture invariant.
          Body–Jeevan coupling Specify both directions of the interface, its timing, and its compatibility with the physical model. Distinguish conditional forecasts from coupled predictions.
          Variational Jeevan dynamics Justify any proposed state geometry, action functional, or conjugate variables. The existence of a highest value does not select a physical Hamiltonian.
          Values and measurement Preserve the distinction between material provision, relational fulfilment, and Jeevan’s realisation. Determine which indicators support which claims and where evidence remains incomplete.
          Refinement and stable fulfilment State the conditions under which correction is retained and conduct remains coherent through new situations. Include conflicting evidence and failures of transfer.
          Necessity and emergence Preserve the philosophical argument that awakening is the necessary fulfilment of human capacity. A quantitative inevitability claim additionally needs a specified transition law and a proof that its stated conditions ensure the result.

          The last issue is particularly relevant to Hamiltonian language. An unforced canonical Hamiltonian flow preserves phase-space volume. It cannot simply be assigned the attracting behaviour of a dissipative learning process on that same space (Tong-H, §4.2). An account of irreversible refinement needs explicit assumptions about what changes, what is retained, and how interaction or the chosen description makes that change possible. The present note establishes the framework in which those assumptions can be investigated.
