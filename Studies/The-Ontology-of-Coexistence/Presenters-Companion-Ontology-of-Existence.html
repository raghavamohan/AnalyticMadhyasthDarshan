          The Ontology of Existence in Madhyasth Darshan

          Slide-by-slide delivery notes and primary-text background for Q&A.

          Companion to the 29-slide presentation based on §1 of The Ontology of Coexistence (AnalyticMadhyasthDarshan.org). Primary texts cited: (MVD), Manav Karm Darshan (KD), Samadhanatmak Bhautikvad (SB), Vidya – An Introduction (JV), and Adhyatmvad (AVD), and the Hindi Paribhasha Samhita (PS). Translation and edition conventions follow the study; KD is used through identified working-English paraphrases, not a published English translation. PS citations use printed pages, eight below the PDF position, in the third edition (2012), printing dated 14 January 2016. The official source collection and page-level references are linked in the study bibliography.

          How to use this guide: each slide has three parts. “Delivering the slide” is a complete read-aloud script for live or video-conference delivery, written for a smart audience with no prior Madhyasth Darshan or formal-philosophy background — English first, source terms second; speak it as written (or nearly so). It is mirrored in the PowerPoint notes pane. “Primary-text background” is for pre-session study, not for reading aloud. “Likely questions” is for Q&A preparation.

          House conventions: the English translation often prints “Omnipotence” for ; the study, this guide, and the slides use “Omnipresence” in prose and preserve the translation’s word inside quotations only. Both names point to the same satta. Hindi shunya is rendered “Space.” Where the study flags an open problem, the suggested answers say so plainly — candour about what is and is not established is part of the study’s method. The study’s Editorial Notes explain the contextual uses of . The epistemic payoff is the epistemic ladder (slide 24) and realisation (slide 25); presentation, communication, expression, and art connect understanding with conduct (slide 26); method and evidence close the “how known” question (slides 28 and 29).

          Three movements: the deck is signposted in the kicker line of each movement’s opening slide — Movement 1 at slide 5 (what is real), Movement 2 at slide 12 (how development proceeds), Movement 3 at slide 15 (from sentience to awakened conduct).

          Slide 1

          Title — The Ontology of Existence

          Delivering the slide

          Welcome, and thank you for joining.

          You do not need any prior familiarity with , or with formal philosophy, to follow this session. I will use ordinary English first, and introduce source terms only when they help. Our aim is not persuasion or devotion. It is to examine one coherent account of reality — testing its definitions and internal consistency.

          Madhyasth Darshan — also called Co-existentialism — was propounded by Shri A. Nagraj. The name points to a thesis we will unpack later: order in nature is mediative and inherent, not imposed by an external governor.

          Here is the one claim to hold for the whole talk: existence is coexistence. Reality is the ever-present togetherness of a formless, all-pervasive ground and countless real, bounded units of nature, each saturated in that ground. Everything else — sentience, values, justice, society — unfolds from this picture.

          One word about vocabulary. The published translations often print “Omnipotence” for the ground; the slides and I will say Omnipresence. Same reality, different English label — you will see the translation’s word only inside quotations.

          Primary-text background

          Coverage claims are scoped to the translated corpus (MVD, SB, JV, AVD), KD as cited, and the selected Hindi PS definitions identified in this guide. Other Hindi works may develop these subjects further.

          “What is evident is that consciousness and matter are inseparably present. Upon examining their fundamental nature, we learn that all of existence is essentially nature (matter) saturated in Omnipotence (consciousness)… existence itself is eternally manifest in the form of coexistence.” — SB, p. 48

          Translation convention: the English translation prints “Omnipotence” for and “Omnipresence” for vyapak; the study uses “Omnipresence” in analytical prose. Hindi shunya is rendered “Space” — void/emptiness, not physical akasha.

          MD’s own title for SB — Samadhanatmak Bhautikvad, Resolution Centred Materialism — signals that the darshan reframes materialism rather than abandoning it (SB, pp. 8, 44).

          Likely questions from the audience

          Q: What does “Madhyasth” mean, and why is the darshan named after it? Madhyasth means mediative. MVD p. 26: “Omnipotence is mediative, therefore the nature saturated in it is regulated and conserved…” Orderliness at every level — atomic nucleus, ’s atma, cosmic regulation — is the same mediative role; that inherent self-regulation gives the darshan its name (slide 11 develops this).

          Q: Is this a religion or a philosophy? It is presented as a darshan — a way of seeing — offered for study, experiment, and verification in living, not for belief. Its final test is : lived evidence in humane conduct. There is no scriptural authority claim, no worship, and no cosmic governor.

          Q: Which texts should I read first? JV is the accessible entry point; SB states the ontology of matter and coexistence most directly; MVD is the systematic darshan text; KD supplies karma, evaluation, and related structure. The study provides page-level citations throughout.

          Slide 2

          Orientation — The Argument in Three Movements

          Delivering the slide

          Before the details, here is the map of the argument. One claim — existence is coexistence — moves through three parts.

          First: what is real, and how it is ordered. There is an all-pervasive ground and countable units. Each unit is inseparable from the ground and stands in definite relations to other units. We examine saturation, the four aspects of a unit, the perpetual world, and the four orders of nature with their modes of conformance.

          Second: how development proceeds along two lines. Composition forms molecules, cells and bodies. Development in an atom reaches constitutional completeness as the sentient unit, . Across both lines, activity includes effort, motion and result together.

          Third: how sentient living becomes awakened conduct. The body supplies a medium for jeevan’s faculties. We examine their harmonies, the difference between sensitivity and understanding, intentional action, and completeness in understanding and conduct. That prepares us to examine values, justice, realisation, expression and humane tradition.

          The body line and the sentient-unit line meet in animal and human living. Their meeting, and the different contribution of each, is the connection we will keep returning to.

          Primary-text background

          The three-movement framing is a teaching scaffold for §1, not a separate textual division. Compositional vs atomic development: study §1.7; KD 3.2–3.3. Joint form without identity of process: §1.8; MVD pp. 13, 115.

          Likely questions from the audience

          Q: Why separate the body line from the line so early? Because the deck will repeatedly use that fork. Bodies compose and decompose; jeevan is constitutional completeness at the atomic level. Collapsing them makes T1 sound like neural complexity and makes death sound like annihilation of the knower.

          Q: Is this the whole study? No — this deck is §1 (’s ontology stated on its own terms). Comparison with Advaita, Western philosophy, and science belongs to later sections and a companion session.

          Slide 3

          Questions Ontology Must Answer

          Delivering the slide

          Any serious account of reality has to face a small set of questions. Let us name them before we give this tradition’s answers.

          What is existence? Is there one fundamental reality, or many — and how are they related?

          What exists? What are the basic constituents, and what is each of them?

          Does what exists begin? Did reality originate at some time — or do only configurations begin and end?

          Does the self begin or end with the body?

          Is the plural world finally real, or only an appearance?

          And sixth: how can what exists be known — what must be understood, and at what scope?

          Different traditions answer these differently. does not answer them with six separate doctrines. One picture — coexistence — is meant to answer all six at once.

          We will return to this list at the end with the answers filled in. Along the way you will also see how knowing widens, where the developmental drive comes to rest, and how different kinds of evidence are kept distinct.

          Primary-text background

          The study’s framing questions open the paper. Standpoint: from a physics-trained background, consciousness usually appears as something the brain does, yet the hard problem, the status of the self, and the reality of value remain contested — that open space motivates the inquiry.

          “Humans inherently seek to live with evidence through experimentation, behaviour, and experience…” — SB, p. 44

          JV, p. 120: justice understood on the basis of relationships; on the basis of orderliness; truth on the basis of existence.

          Likely questions from the audience

          Q: Why is ‘how can it be known?’ an ontological question? Because in MD the way of knowing follows from the way of being: each aspect of a unit demands a definite scope of understanding, and knowledge has an ontological base in orderliness already present in saturated units.

          Q: Why should we care about ontology — isn’t this abstract? In MD’s reading, complementarity and value are built into existence; justice, family, and undivided society express how reality is — not conventions imposed on a value-neutral world. Slide 27 shows the human goals of resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, and coexistence.

          Q: How is this different from Vedanta’s answers? Preview only: Advaita answers with alone and treats the world as at the highest truth. MD keeps both poles real: and units are co-eternal, and the world is perpetual (jagat satat). Full contrast is a separate session.

          Slide 4

          Conceptual Map of §1

          Delivering the slide

          This map is the organising picture for what follows. You do not need to memorise every node. Use it as a place to return when later slides feel dense.

          At the top: coexistence — the ground and countless units, inseparable without losing their distinction. Then, on the unit side: four aspects of every unit; relationships; four orders of nature; and two developmental lines.

          Notice the fork. Bodies belong to compositional building. The sentient unit is the result of a different line — development in an atom. The two meet in animal and human living.

          Later we will name three milestones on the sentient path: the arrival of sentience, then settlement of inner seeking, then authentic conduct. Those are not further material stages.

          Most of the deck is a zoom into one region of this map.

          Primary-text background

          Companion conceptual map materials under this study folder (SVG/PDF) mirror §1. Primary pointers: MVD, KD, SB, JV as footered on the slide. Two developments and T1–T3: §§1.7, 1.11; joint form: §1.8; the four classification vocabularies: §1.12.

          Likely questions from the audience

          Q: Is the map itself primary text? No — it is a teaching synthesis of §1. Every node should be checkable against MVD/SB/JV/KD citations developed on later slides.

          Q: Where do karma and justice sit on this map? Regulation and conformance belong beside the four orders (slides 10–11). Karma (slide 19) structures desire, doing and result in human living. Values and justice (slides 22–23) then explain how human understanding fulfils relationships. The map connects these regions; the teaching sequence introduces each after its prerequisites.

          Slide 5

          Existence Is Coexistence — Two Inseparable Aspects

          Delivering the slide

          We now enter the foundations — what this account says exists.

          Existence has two inseparable aspects.

          First, the ground — Omnipresence. Formless, all-pervasive, transparent, permeating, non-transforming, immeasurable. Actionless energy: it acts not at all, yet every unit is energised and regulated in it. There is no place where it is absent. The same pervasive reality is also named jyoti, light; realisation in it is described as enduring prakash.

          Two words make this relation clearer. Permeating concerns the unit: the pervasive reality passes through every unit, which remains saturated in it. Transparent concerns mutuality: units become manifest to one another through imaging, which provides a basis of recognition. Transparency, permeability, and pervasiveness together evidence one indivisible reality.

          Second, units of nature. Formful, active, countable. Each is bounded and saturated in the ground. Each has form, capacities, a way of participating, and an inseparable innateness — we will walk those four shortly.

          Do not collapse levels. At the root, nature is analysed as atomic units — a centre with orbiting parts. Compounds and bodies are composite units. Body and sentient unit together are a joint form. Families and societies are relational assemblies.

          These two poles were never separate. Separation of a unit from the saturated whole never happens. The ground does not change. Nature saturated in it is dynamic. Therefore all change is unit-activity.

          One naming caution. The texts list many English names for the ground — including Knowledge and Consciousness. Those names do not mean a mind that knows. Active knowing belongs to the sentient unit, never to the ground.

          Primary-text background

          “Omnipotence is formless and omnipresent. Within Omnipotence, nature is endowed with form, properties, essential nature, and , and is inseparable from it…” — SB, p. 48

          “Omnipotence remains in a state free from motion and pressure, whereas the whole of nature existing in Omnipotence is present with motion and pressure.” — SB (following p. 48)

          “The Omnipotence eternally present as omnipresence is an indivisible entity, because its reality is apparent as transparence, permeability and omnipresence.” — SB, p. 46; restated as omnipresent, eternal, ever-existent, transparent, and permeating at SB, p. 160.

          Source-defined names and translation risks: MVD p. 35 and Editorial Notes in the study. PS p. 137 names Brahm as pervasive reality, uniform , knowledge and jyoti; p. 80 defines jyoti through realisation in the pervasive as enduring prakash. This is the source basis of the light vocabulary for the ground. These are connected passages, rather than a verbatim definition equating satta and prakash. Inter-unit distance held in satta as Space: SB, pp. 57, 59, 79. Never-separated: SB, p. 70; JV, p. 18.

          MVD p. 32 locates permeation in insentient and sentient units and transparency in their mutuality. PS p. 114 connects this with images and recognition. The ground remains actionless; imaging and recognition do not make it a knowing subject.

          Likely questions from the audience

          Q: Is God? “God” is one of the names SB lists — but satta is actionless, formless, and non-willing. MVD’s Reality list (p. 13): “God is omnipresent, deities are many.” There is no cosmic governor; orderliness is self-regulation.

          Q: If satta is called Consciousness, is it aware of us? No. “Consciousness” in SB p. 48 labels the all-pervasive reality, not a knowing mind. Sentience as the activity of a knower (chaitanya) is the status of only.

          Q: How is satta different from a physical field or the quantum vacuum? A dynamical field carries energy-momentum and propagates waves — unlike actionless, non-transforming satta. Comparisons stay at “consonant with,” never “proved by.”

          Q: If units are bounded, what is between them? Not empty in MD’s sense: the distance between bounded wholes is held in formless existence — satta as Space (shunya).

          Slide 6

          Saturation — Soaked, Submerged, Surrounded

          Delivering the slide

          Saturation names the inseparability of units and Omnipresence. Every formful unit is soaked, submerged, and surrounded in the same pervasive reality.

          Picture a cloth immersed in water. Water is within the cloth, the cloth is submerged, and water surrounds it on every side. This illustration helps us picture the three relations. Its limit is equally simple: cloth can be removed from water, whereas a unit is always present in formless Omnipresence.

          Soakedness means that the unit is endowed with energy and force. Submersion means that it is active and stands in mutual recognition with other units. Surroundedness means that it remains regulated in definite relations. We will meet this regulation again alongside the four orders of nature.

          These are connected aspects of one saturation. Energy becomes evident in activity, recognition in mutuality, and regulation in definite conduct. The ground is named jyoti, light; each unit saturated in it is prakashman, manifest. The texts explicitly connect this manifestness with permeating the unit and its being endowed with force. Manifestness belongs to all units, including sentient .

          Because the pervasive reality permeates every unit, dividing a unit leaves every fragment saturated. The texts connect this continuing presence with their doctrine that what exists is not annihilated. Particular arrangements can end while their constituents remain. We will return to that distinction when we consider perpetuity and time.

          The next slide follows the relation between units: how they become manifest to one another and participate in complementarity.

          Primary-text background

          The cloth-in-water illustration distinguishes soakedness, submersion and surroundedness. It is a teaching illustration, not a claim that Omnipresence is a material fluid; unlike cloth in water, a unit cannot leave the pervasive reality.

          The source connects permeability with non-annihilation: “Being soaked itself is forcefulness. It becomes clear that units are soaked because Omnipotence is permeating. No matter how many times we divide a unit, each fragment remains submerged, surrounded, and soaked. It also becomes clear that a unit cannot be annihilated.” — SB, p. 57. Division leaves each fragment saturated; whether this establishes every form of persistence is a further philosophical question. The three aspects taken together, with permeativeness inferred from soakedness: SB, p. 243.

          Surroundedness as what establishes regulation: “This very surroundedness itself clarifies the regulation of every unit, because every unit … remains regulated within a definite distance from one another, which itself is formless existence.” — SB, p. 79

          “Every unit in its atomic state is active as orderliness, because it has inherent energy due to being saturated in Omnipotence.” — SB, p. 69

          “Unit + Energy fullness = Activeness.” — SB, p. 69. The Hindi term is ikaitva, unit-ness — the abstract property of being a bounded unit at all — which the published translation renders as a bare “Unit.” The study’s glossary carries “unit-ness” for that reason.

          “Thus, without basic impulsion, the energy remains unmanifest, and without the energy, there is no basic impulsion.” — SB, p. 62

          Saturation endows forcefulness, basic impulsion, and regulatory order (SB, p. 57). Relative energy does not become apparent without mutuality (MVD, p. 40). Saturation itself as knowledge: MVD, pp. 32–33.

          SB p. 57 associates soakedness with forcefulness, submersion with activeness, and surroundedness with regulation. PS p. 114 associates soakedness with energy-endowment, submersion with mutual recognition, and surroundedness with regulation. These overlap rather than establishing exclusive compartments. AVD pp. 115–116 derives units’ force-endowment, activity and manifestness from permeating them; SB p. 248 independently grounds manifestness in force-endowment under saturation. PS pp. 80, 137 supply the connected jyoti/prakash vocabulary for the ground. PS p. 121 identifies the image as a basis of mutual recognition. See study §§1.1–1.3 and Editorial Notes.

          Likely questions from the audience

          Q: Does cause unit-activity? Not as an efficient cause. Satta is mahakaran — supreme cause in the sustaining sense (MVD, pp. 288–289; SB, pp. 49, 62). Particular changes occur through unit-activity in mutuality. Slide 9 develops this.

          Q: Is “inherent energy” measurable physical energy? The texts treat it as ontological endowment. Relative energy appears in unit-mutuality and includes named phenomena such as heat, sound, and electricity. Comparing those terms with measured physical quantities requires a further account; satta’s uniform energy is not thereby identified with a physical field.

          Q: If complementarity is “given,” why do conflicts exist? Complementarity is perpetual; recognition and fulfilment can fail. At the human order, mis-evaluation under body-identification leaves complementarity incompletely expressed (MVD, p. 112).

          Q: Isn’t “a unit cannot be annihilated” contradicted by particle physics? The claim is about what exists, not about the persistence of a particular configuration. Reactions transform arrangements. The study is explicit that this ontological claim is not the same as, and is not proved by, conservation of quantity (§1.4, §6.2).

          Q: Does each saturation term have exactly one consequence? The primary descriptions overlap. SB p. 57 connects submersion with activeness, while PS p. 114 connects it with recognition in mutuality. The study preserves both connections and the shared regulation account without assigning one exclusive consequence to each term.

          Slide 7

          Nature Is Complementary — The Second Bond

          Delivering the slide

          Each unit is saturated in the ground. Units also stand in mutuality with one another. Complementarity names their definite participation in the wider order of coexistence.

          Recognition begins with units becoming manifest in mutual facing. Their image, called pratibimb, provides a basis of mutual recognition. In insentient nature, recognition means definite responsiveness: heat is accepted and redistributed, particles maintain definite relations, and atoms combine under definite conditions. A unit’s relative powers exert effects in these interactions. Human understanding involves the further knowing activities of .

          Every unit is a whole along with its environment. It retains its own constitution while participating in definite relations. Value, in the universal sense, is essentiality and participation in orderliness — what a unit contributes through its conduct. Usefulness is that essentiality as it appears in objects.

          Fulfilment is the participation that follows recognition, according to constitution and order. Below the human order it follows definite conformance. In human living, knowing and believing must guide recognition and fulfilment, and this can succeed or fail.

          This gives development its direction in the account: fuller participation in complementarity. Later we will examine how that direction becomes conscious responsibility in human relationships.

          Primary-text background

          Bounded plurality provisions recognition: “Nature is recognisable in the state-complete Omnipotence with its countless unitness. Consequently, a provision for one unit to recognise another got manifest in existence.” — SB, p. 50; also SB pp. 57, 62.

          Complementarity given in the bond rather than created: SB pp. 49–50, 53. “Nothing is isolated — that is the principle” — JV, p. 43. “Every entity of nature recognises another; that is why it fulfils…” — JV, p. 69.

          The equipment of recognition — capacity (kshamata), ability (yogyata), receptivity (patrata): SB pp. 57, 62, 79; MVD p. 62. “Transparent in mutuality”: MVD p. 32.

          Recognition below the human order is not reflective cognition; the heat, particle-distance, and atomic-combination examples are the study’s own (§1.2). Value as essentiality and participation in orderliness: §1.13.1.

          The completeness drive is grounded in these endowments: SB pp. 50–51 — this is the citation behind the closing line of the script. Composition as natural inclination toward coexistence: JV p. 67 (§1.5). Integration at T1 through hungry/undigested (overfull) complementarity: MVD p. 8; SB pp. 58, 71; KD 3.3 (§1.7.1). Justice as recognising relationships, fulfilling values, evaluating the fulfilment, and achieving mutual satisfaction: §1.13.3.

          PS p. 121 defines image and imaging through mutual recognition. SB pp. 248–252 distinguishes imaging from the influences of relative powers. Its account extends from insentient mutuality to animal recognition of essential nature and human exchange of values and evaluation of . These are the source connections behind the revised study §§1.2–1.3.

          Likely questions from the audience

          Q: Isn’t “complementarity” just cooperation with a nicer name? No — cooperation is something agents choose. Complementarity here is a structural feature of bounded plurality in one ground: it holds between particles that choose nothing. Cooperation, at the human order, is one way of fulfilling it knowingly.

          Q: If value is built into existence, why do people disagree about values? The claim is that essentiality is objective, not that recognition and evaluation are automatic. Cultures differ in what they recognise and how they evaluate; on this account they are not differing about whether a unit is complementary to others, but about whether they have seen it correctly. The study is explicit that human evaluation can fail (§1.13.1, §1.13.2).

          Q: Isn’t “recognition” in a particle just a metaphor? The texts give an account of mutual imaging as well as definite responsiveness. PS p. 121 names the image as a basis of recognition, and SB pp. 248–252 distinguishes imaging from influence. Neither attributes human understanding to a particle. Knowing and believing belong to sentient nature (§§1.2–1.3).

          Q: How is this different from saying everything is interconnected? “Interconnected” is compatible with the connections being arbitrary or merely causal. The claim here is stronger and more specific: each unit has a definite usefulness to others, given its order and constitution, and that definite usefulness is what value means. Vague holism does not entail an account of justice; this is meant to.

          Slide 8

          Form, Properties, Nature, and Dharma

          Delivering the slide

          What is every unit? Four inseparable aspects — not a static checklist pasted onto things, but how a definite bearer shows up in the world.

          Form is the real configuration of the unit: shape, volume, and density. The constituted unit is bimb. Its image in mutual facing is pratibimb, and being imaged is pratibimban. Take the peepal tree: one bounded living form with trunk, branches, leaves, and roots.

          Properties are relative powers inseparable from their bearer. In the peepal, nutrition, repair, seed production, and degeneration become effective under definite conditions.

          Essential nature is usefulness in mutuality. The peepal’s effects are vitalising or devitalising according to the affected body and conditions. Its being alive or decomposing does not alone decide which effect it has on another body.

          means inseparable innateness: what belongs to the unit and is fulfilled in its activity. For the peepal: existence plus growth. Seed-conformance is how it conducts itself, not an extra label piled on top.

          Slide 14

          Sentience — The Constitutional Threshold

          Delivering the slide

          How does sentience enter this account? The first threshold is constitutional completeness, which we will call T1.

          According to the primary texts, sentience appears when one developing atom reaches constitutional completeness — not through neural complexity, molecular size, or the material-to-biological order change.

          The described process begins with particle exchange. Hungry atoms can incorporate particles; overfull or undigested atoms seek to expel them. Under suitable mutuality, a developing atom integrates the full count required by its constitution. When that requirement is satisfied, the constitution closes and particle count no longer increases or decreases.

          The slide shows the before and after. Before sentience, the constitution remains open: particles are absorbed or expelled, molecular and weight bondage continue, and kind, capacity, and qualitative change track particle count. At the sentience threshold, all required particles are integrated. The count becomes fixed, molecular and weight bondage end, and qualitative activity continues. The texts classify that closed unit as sentient .

          That separation of qualitative from quantitative change is the stated criterion. MVD adds the visible signs of release from the molecular group, liberation from molecular- and weight-bondage, and establishment in hope-bondage.

          The transition to sentience is therefore a claim of reclassification at atomic closure: one atom becomes jeevan; a body does not become conscious by becoming more complex. The texts give a process and a criterion, but not an accepted physical measurement of this threshold or a causal explanation of why closure is sentience. We will return to that open bridge under method and evidence.

          Slide 21

          One Drive, Four Vocabularies

          Delivering the slide

          Four vocabularies now help us distinguish the aspects of order and development we have examined.

          There is one drive: nature saturated in state-complete Omnipresence, oriented toward development and awakening until realisation. Everything on this table is a way of describing where something stands in that drive.

          Orders — material, bio, animal, knowledge — say what kind of thing a unit is: its organisation, its essential nature, its innateness. Four orders.

          Order and development are described by four terms: existential progression, way of existence, development progression, and awakening progression. The first names the definite order in which the orders become manifest; the second, the conformance by which each order keeps its conduct; the third, physicochemical and atomic development up to T1; the fourth, qualitative development inside a complete sentient unit toward T2 and T3.

          Planes — physicochemical, delusional, deific, divine — say how far something has come. These are the four we just saw across the top of the last slide. Before T1; T1 only; T2; T3.

          Human types — animalistic, demonic, humane or discerning, deific, divine — say how a human life is oriented. Five of them, and they classify modes of consciousness and conduct. Not biological species. Not social classes. Not castes.

The knowledge order spans more than one plane — most human beings are in the knowledge order and on the delusional plane at the same time, and there is nothing insulting in that; it just means realisation has not happened yet. T1 separates physicochemical from sentient standing. T2 and T3 are completeness within an awakened sentient unit, and no fifth plane is added beyond them.

          Understanding in Expression

          Delivering the slide

          Every unit is prakashman, manifest through its form, properties, essential nature and . Presentation, communication and expression describe this participation throughout nature.           Method and Evidence

          Delivering the slide

          The account we have examined uses several modes of inquiry and evidence. Each answers a different question.

          Logical exposition tests whether definitions and relationships form a coherent account. Study and self-examination test whether the proposal and the activities of knowing can be understood and recognised.

          Experiment and practice in work and behaviour test whether understanding regulates action. Conduct-evidence makes relational consequences — fulfilled values, mutual satisfaction and participation — visible to others.

          Realisation is ’s claimed completion of knowing coexistence, and humane conduct. Its status as first-person completion differs from publicly measurable physical evidence.

          The sentience threshold, the jeevan–body interface, and post-body reassociation raise physical questions. No accepted measurement currently identifies them. The survival and reassociation account introduced with the joint form remains a claim to examine under that further standard.

          These methods can support one another while retaining their different scope. Evidence of humane conduct does not by itself identify the sentient atom, just as internal coherence alone does not establish that a physical claim is true.
