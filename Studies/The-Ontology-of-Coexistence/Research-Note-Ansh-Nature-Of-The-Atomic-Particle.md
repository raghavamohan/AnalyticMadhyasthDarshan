# Research Note: The Nature of *Ansh* — the Atom's Constituent Particles

**Author:** Raghava Mohan Madhwapathi ([analyticmadhyasthdarshan.org](https://analyticmadhyasthdarshan.org))

**Edited on:** August 5, 2026, 12:04 AM IST

**Status:** Internal research note (not a catalog entry). Compiled as a companion to [Research-Note-Physics-Of-Satta-Unit-Interaction.md](Research-Note-Physics-Of-Satta-Unit-Interaction.md) (hereafter "the Physics note"), which establishes the atom's overall architecture (nucleus, orbits, growth law, species). This note narrows the lens to the *ansh* itself — the particle as a thing in its own right, prior to and apart from the constitution it joins.

**Scope:** Every explicit or directly inferable statement in the primary texts (SB, MVD, JV, AVD, JVD, KD) that characterises the constituent particle (*ansh*) — its intrinsic nature, its behaviour in isolation, its functional role by position, and the limits of what the corpus attributes to it as a standalone entity. ## 1. Two functional classes of *ansh*, not two kinds of particle

The darshan does not divide *ansh* into different species of stuff (nothing resembling proton vs. electron). Instead, the same particle-concept carries two different **functional roles** depending on where it sits in the constitution:

- **Nucleus particle(s)** (*madhyansh*) — described as more potent than the rest, and specifically charged with *mediative* (*madhyastha*) activity: holding the other particles at their regulated distance and normalising their imbalances (MVD, p. 174).
- **Dependent / orbital particles** (*āshrit ansh*, also *parivesh ansh*) — the particles that revolve at a distance from the nucleus, whose behaviour is *sam-viṣam* (generative–degenerative), i.e. the charge-bearing dynamic already covered in the Physics note §8 (MVD, p. 174).

The differentiation is therefore positional and functional, not compositional. This is worth stressing against the modern-physics comparison already flagged in the Physics note's open tensions (§15): the corpus never identifies *ansh* with named particles, but it does give the nucleus/orbit distinction a *behavioural* asymmetry (regulating vs. being regulated) that has no single-word equivalent for "electron" or "proton" — it is closer to a role than a kind.

**A subtlety gradient in the one fully-named case.** Where the atom in question is *jeevan* (the constitutionally complete, sentient atom), this functional split is carried further into a hierarchy of refinement. The outermost orbit, *mun*, is the most sense-bound; moving inward through *vritti* and *chitta* to *buddhi*, and finally to the nucleus, *atma* — named directly as the most developed *ansh* of the sentient unit (MVD, p. 317). Position toward the centre tracks degree of subtlety, not just function.

---

## 2. Every *ansh* is itself a "unit" (*ikai*), saturated in Omnipotence

The Physics note (§4) establishes that every unit in existence — atom, molecule, planet — is described as saturated in *satta* (Omnipotence) and therefore inherently force-endowed, not inert stuff requiring an external push. The texts extend this baseline explicitly down to the particle level: the capacity for activeness is present in the particles of every atom precisely because every particle, like every other unit, is saturated in Omnipotence (MVD, p. 89). KD states the same endowment chain from the particle's own side — it is by virtue of being energy-endowed, and thereby force-endowed (specifically magnetic-force-endowed), that the particles of an atom are able to recognise one another in mutuality at all (KD, p. 54).

**Reading:** an *ansh*'s activeness is not acquired from the atom it eventually joins — it is already there, as the same standing endowment every unit in the darshan's ontology carries by virtue of being *samprikt* (saturated) in the ground. The atom does not animate its particles; the particles' pre-existing forcefulness is what makes an atom's formation and regulation possible in the first place.

---

## 3. The isolated *ansh*: restlessness and incompleteness outside a constitution

This is the corpus's clearest direct statement on a lone particle's own condition. KD describes a particle that remains separate within the omnipresent substance as failing to evidence the orderliness that is its natural intent; consequently it persists in a whirling motion, in search of another particle, until it is ultimately absorbed into an existing atom or joins with another particle to form a new one (KD, p. 56). Only once absorbed does the receiving and the donating atom settle into natural-state motion (KD, p. 57; see Physics note §6.4b).

**Reading:** a standalone *ansh* is not pictured as a stable, self-sufficient little sphere sitting inertly in space. Its natural condition in isolation is restlessness and incompleteness — motion without settlement — and this restlessness is itself what drives combination. Combination is not something done *to* an *ansh* from outside; it is what an *ansh*'s own nature is already oriented toward. This connects directly to KD's "hungry" vs. "undigested" vs. "satisfied" vocabulary for atoms overall (KD, pp. 61–62; Physics note §6.4b) — the same restlessness-until-completion logic that operates at the atom level (development toward *gathanpurnata*) is already present, in miniature, at the level of the single particle.

---

## 4. Inside the atom: definite mutual distance and the particle's "own existence"

Once part of a constitution, SB describes every *ansh* contained in an atom as clarifying its own existence by holding a definite, fixed distance from the other particles in their mutuality — and it is this discipline of fixed mutual spacing, taken together across all of an atom's particles, that constitutes the atom's boundary and its "motion-path" (SB, ch. 4, approx. pp. 60–61 — page pin approximate; verify against PDF before citing in a Study). This dovetails with the separate, already-verified claim that the gap between any two particles or units is not empty space but is formless existence (*satta*) itself (SB, p. 79; Physics note §4).

**Reading:** the phrase "own existence" (*apna astitva*) is doing real work here — each *ansh* is treated as a distinct existent, not a mere subdivision of the atom that could equally be described as an undifferentiated blob. The atom's unity is a *regulated plurality* of such existents, each individually recognisable, held at fixed mutual distance by the mediative nucleus (§1 above) — not a single continuous substance. And the "distance" that keeps them apart, and thereby distinct, is the same formless ground that saturates each of them (§2 above) — separation and saturation are two descriptions of the same relation.

---

## 5. Sentience belongs to the completed atom, not to any single *ansh*

Nothing in the corpus attributes sentience or insentience to a particle in isolation. Sentience/insentience is a status of the atom as a completed whole. When an atom crosses into constitutional completeness, every particle it comprises participates in that transition together and equally — the texts describe every unit within the completed status as equal in strength and power (SB; see also Physics note's *gathanpurnata* glossary entry). There is no suggestion that some particles are "already" sentient ahead of the others, or that sentience accrues particle-by-particle as the constitution fills up. It is a threshold property of the whole, not a summable property of the parts.

---

## 6. What the texts do not attribute to a lone *ansh*

No intrinsic, standalone, measurable property — shape, size, a charge magnitude, a rest quantity — is ever assigned to a particle considered by itself. Every property the corpus does state (weight, charge, an atom's "hungry"/"undigested"/"satisfied" condition, species) is explicitly relational: a function of an *ansh*'s position and count relative to the other particles in the same constitution (Physics note §§6, 8, 10). This is continuous with the darshan's general doctrine that named physical quantities are *relative energy*, apparent only in mutuality and never as a property one unit carries alone (Physics note §3). A single *ansh*, considered outside all mutuality, is therefore close to a philosophical limit-case in the texts: they assert it is restless and incomplete (§3), but they do not — and by their own "relative energy" doctrine, arguably cannot — assign it any further quantified property until it is in relation to at least one other particle.

---

## 7. Synthesis

Putting §§1–6 together, the darshan's *ansh* is:

- **the same "unit" concept as everything else in existence**, recursively applied at the smallest scale the corpus names — saturated in Omnipotence, therefore inherently force-endowed, therefore able to recognise other particles, exactly as an atom recognises another atom or a planet its neighbours;
- **positionally, not compositionally, differentiated** — nucleus vs. orbit is a division of function (mediative vs. generative-degenerative), not of substance, though in the one fully-named case (*jeevan*) this positional split doubles as a gradient of subtlety from *mun* to *atma*;
- **restless and incomplete when alone**, with combination-seeking as its natural condition rather than an externally imposed process;
- **individually existent but only relationally measurable** — each particle "clarifies its own existence" by holding a fixed mutual distance from the others, yet every quantity the texts attach to particles (weight, charge, condition) is a fact about that mutuality, never a standalone property.

The overall picture is closer to a **relational ontology of parts** than to a classical-mechanics picture of discrete little bodies with fixed, particle-intrinsic properties: an *ansh* is a real, distinct existent, but almost everything sayable about it is sayable only once it is in relation to another *ansh*.

---

## 8. Open questions (flag for the paper)

- **Is restlessness (§3) unique to sub-threshold (insentient/incomplete) particles, or does it describe every *ansh* universally, including those already inside a constitutionally complete (*jeevan*) atom?** KD's "whirling in the void" passage (p. 56) is stated in the context of atoms still under development; whether the same restlessness-in-isolation claim would apply to, say, a *buddhi*-particle hypothetically separated from a completed *jeevan* atom is not addressed anywhere in the corpus.
- **Does "own existence" (§4) imply particle-level indestructibility**, on the same footing as unit-level indestructibility elsewhere in the corpus (Physics note §11), or is it a weaker claim about recognisability alone? The texts do not distinguish these readings explicitly.
- **The nucleus/orbit functional split (§1) and the subtlety gradient (*mun*→*atma*) are stated together only for *jeevan*.** Whether ordinary (insentient) atoms' nucleus particles are also, in some lesser sense, "more developed" than their orbital particles — or whether the subtlety-gradient reading is a *jeevan*-specific feature that does not generalise — is not settled by the texts consulted here.
- **Page pin for §4's SB passage is approximate** (SB, ch. 4, "Existence and Development of Atom in Existence," roughly pp. 60–61) and should be confirmed against the PDF directly before use in a catalog Study, per the Method note below.
