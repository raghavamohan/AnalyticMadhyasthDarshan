Computational architecture versus conscious selfhood: examining AI, neural complexity, and functionalism through Madhyasth Darshan's distinction between the brain (medhas) and jeevan

## Study proposal

This study addresses the contemporary challenge of Artificial Intelligence and computational cognitivism from the perspective of Madhyasth Darshan. As Large Language Models and autonomous systems demonstrate increasingly sophisticated verbal and analytical capacities, functionalist and physicalist philosophies of mind argue that consciousness is merely computational information processing.

Key areas of inquiry:
1. The brain (medhas) as a sophisticated physicochemical organ of signal reception, motor control, and biological memory, distinct from the conscious self (jeevan).
2. Computational algorithmic simulation versus the ten conscious activities of jeevan (specifically anubhav, bodh, and chintan).
3. Intentionality, the search for continuous happiness (dharma), intrinsic valuation (mulya), and natural acceptance (sahaj swikriti) which insentient machines lack.
4. Ethical boundaries and the right use of technology in service of humane social order.
5. Primary texts: Manav Karm Darshan (§3.16), Samadhanatmak Bhautikvad, and The Epistemology of Coexistence.
