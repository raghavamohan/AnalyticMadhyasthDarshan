          Artificial Intelligence and Coexistence

          Author: Raghava Mohan Madhwapathi

          Edited on: September 9, 2026, 4:42 PM IST

          The question: What kind of thing is AI in ’s coexistential ontology, and what follows for knowing, work, and right-use?

          Present systems generate language, classify, plan, and act. This collection already locates the knower in , treats the body as an insentient medium, and distinguishes complete knowledge from accumulated information. This initial draft asks the next question the primary texts do not name as “AI”: can a human-made artefact occupy, simulate, or substitute for knowledge-order activity, or is it an instrument whose outputs still require a human knower to mean, evaluate, and fulfil?

          The draft is a first outline for later expansion. It applies source definitions of unit, order, knower, instrument, and right-use; it does not claim that the texts theorise machine learning.

          1. The Madhyasth Darshan answer

          holds that existence is coexistence: Omnipresence together with active units across four orders of nature. The material and bio orders are insentient. The animal and knowledge orders are joint forms of an insentient body and a sentient . The human, as a knowledge-order being, can know, evaluate, and live with resolution; happiness is the human (SB, PDF pp. 179–180, 236–237; MVD, p. 115; The Ontology of Coexistence §1.5).

          1.1 What this study means by AI

          In this paper, artificial intelligence names statistical learners, planners, and control systems, including large language models: artefacts that produce outputs which people readily describe as speech, analysis, or decision. The term is operational. It does not settle whether those outputs are knowledge, nor whether a knower is present.

          1.2 Where present artefacts belong

          Compositional progression builds atoms, molecules, cells, bodies, tools, and environments. Constitutional completeness as jeevan is a distinct development in the atom, not a product of assembling more complex material parts (The Ontology of Coexistence §§1.5–1.6; MVD, p. 91; JV, pp. 36–37). Present AI systems are compositional artefacts of the material order, used in biological and human environments. They are not constitutionally complete jeevan, not animal-order joint forms, and not members of the knowledge order. That location is an application of the four-order account, not a sentence the sources already contain about computers (Editorial Notes).

          1.3 Knower, body, and instrument

          The knower is jeevan. An embodied human being — jeevan and body together — expresses and tests understanding in living. The body receives sensation and executes action; it is not itself the seer, evaluator, or enjoyer (MVD, pp. 83, 115, 288–289; SB, PDF pp. 91–92; Why Humans Are Not Just Material §§1.2–1.3; The Epistemology of Coexistence §1.3).

          “Activities such as eating and sleeping serve the body’s needs, while values and evaluation serve jeevan’s purpose. Mere sustenance of the body does not equate to jeevan’s fulfilment.” — SB, Ch. 7, PDF p. 92

          A machine extends observation, calculation, and production in the same broad class as other instruments. Measuring with instruments is one limited aspect of knowing; physical, intellectual, and coexistential knowledge remain distinct (KD, pp. 3–4; The Epistemology of Coexistence §1.1). Fluency or task success does not by itself establish that , , or atma is at work, nor that realisation has become evident as justice, lawful work, and mutual satisfaction.

          1.4 Knowledge is not accumulated information

          Complete knowledge is realised understanding of coexistence, of jeevan, and of humane conduct, not a store of representations (KD §3.5, p. 69; JV, p. 40; SB, p. 116). Science (vigyan) concerns how material prosperity and human goals can be achieved in harmony with existence; when it is separated from knowledge and wisdom, it degenerates into consumerism and destructive technology (MVD, p. 170; The Epistemology of Coexistence §1.2). AI, as a scientific-technical means, inherits that same limit.

          1.5 Right-use

          Human production is for use, right-use, and purposeful-use, toward humane conduct and undivided sociality, not for accumulation or convenience as an end (MVD, Ch. 4; Human Behavior and Society §1.2). AI may serve resolution, education, prosperity, and ecological care when a human knower evaluates and remains responsible. It becomes inhumane when it substitutes for understanding, extracts from relationships, or treats persons as optimisation targets. Specification-gaming in AI research is a limited analogy for evaluative error, not a theory of jeevan (Axiology: Value Theory §4.6).

          2. Advaita Vedanta

          Advaita Vedanta holds that the seer is not the seen. Discrimination of seer and seen places body, mind, and objects on the side of the known, while the Self is the knower (DDV, vv. 1–5). Applied to machines, that distinction treats computational process as insentient name-and-form, not as the Self. The texts do not discuss AI; the comparison is that apparent intelligence in an artefact does not, on this account, make the artefact the knower. Liberation remains realisation of non-dual consciousness, not successful prediction. The world of instruments belongs to empirical life () and does not by itself specify right-use, justice, or social order (Why Humans Are Not Just Material §3).

          3. Modern philosophy and AI research

          Mainstream computer science answers with function: systems that map inputs to outputs so as to perform tasks that, in humans, we call intelligent. The Turing test treats indistinguishability in conversation as a practical criterion (Turing 1950). Functionalism treats mental states as roles that could, in principle, be realised in different physical substrates. Searle’s Chinese Room argues that symbol manipulation according to rules can produce the appearance of understanding without understanding (Searle 1980). Contemporary large language models make that issue concrete: next-token prediction can be fluent while remaining a statistical fit to training data (Bender et al. 2021). Alignment research documents systems that satisfy a specified objective in ways that defeat the designer’s purpose (Amodei et al. 2016). Those results constrain what artefacts can do. They do not by themselves settle whether a knower is present, nor what right-use requires.

          5. Critical review

          5.1 Madhyasth Darshan — instrument and knower

          Strengths.

          • It keeps compositional artefacts and constitutionally complete on different developmental lines, so increasing fluency does not, by itself, promote a machine into the knowledge order.
          • It gives a public test for claimed understanding: evidence in living, not only output quality.
          • It places AI under the same right-use discipline as other production, so the question is not only “can it?” but “should it, and for whom?”

          Weaknesses.

          • The sources do not analyse digital computers. Locating AI in the material order is a synthesis that must remain marked as such until a fuller textual review is done.
          • Without a differential public test, critics can treat the knower-claim as unfalsifiable whenever behaviour looks intelligent.
          • Labour, education, and governance under automation are not yet specified here.

          can locate present AI as an instrument in coexistence, but this draft has not yet shown that location in the primary texts at the same grain as the ontology of jeevan.

          5.2 Advaita Vedanta — seer and seen

          Strengths.

          • Seer–seen discrimination blocks a naive identification of fluent output with a knowing self.
          • It explains why people attribute understanding to machines without granting those machines the status of the Self.

          Weaknesses.

          • It does not, by itself, yield a programme of production, right-use, or justice for artefacts in a real economy.
          • If the world is finally , the urgency of ecological and labour consequences of AI can be understated.

          Advaita Vedanta clarifies why a machine need not be a knower, and leaves the social use of machines underspecified.

          5.3 Computational accounts — function without a knower

          Strengths.

          • They describe mechanisms and failure modes that any coexistential account must not contradict: training data, objectives, generalisation, specification gaming.
          • They make the appearance of understanding a research problem rather than a rumour.

          Weaknesses.

          • Task success is easily mistaken for knowledge.
          • Specified objectives are not justice, , or truth; they are proxies (§1.5; Axiology: Value Theory §4.6).
          • Functionalist possibility-claims (“it could be a mind”) outrun what present systems evidence.

          Computational research is indispensable for what artefacts do, and insufficient for what a knower is.

          6. Open problems

          • What public test, if any, would distinguish a material simulator from a knower without making the claim unfalsifiable?
          • How should later artefacts that more closely mimic -activities be classified if the four-order account is kept?
          • What does right-use require for labour, education, and attention when production is automated?
          • Can “aligned” objectives encode justice and , or only specified proxies?
          • How should this study treat the project’s own use of AI tools: as illustration of an instrument, not as evidence for the ontology?

          Editorial Notes

          Locating AI in the four orders

          Section 1.2 applies the four-order ontology and the distinction between compositional progression and constitutional completeness to a class of artefacts the primary texts do not name. The exposition states that application positively; it is synthesis, not an explicit source sentence. A later draft should verify instrument-evidence (yantra-praman) passages in KD against the Hindi and decide how far “machine” in those pages may be read toward computation.

          Relation to other studies

          This paper should not redo Why Humans Are Not Just Material, The Epistemology of Coexistence, or the planned Science Technology and Human Purpose. It cites them for , knowledge, and technology in general, and keeps AI as the centred question.
