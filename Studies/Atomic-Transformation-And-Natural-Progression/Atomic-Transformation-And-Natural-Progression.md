The transformation of physicochemical atoms into the constitutionally complete conscious atom (jeevan), and the unfolding of the four orders within coexistence (niyati-kram)

## Study proposal

This study investigates Madhyasth Darshan's evolutionary cosmology and philosophy of nature, focusing on the constitutional transformation of the atom (parmanu vikas and gathan-purnata) and the orderly progression of the four natural orders (niyati-kram). While modern physicalism treats consciousness as an emergent epiphenomenon of complex molecular biology, Madhyasth Darshan posits a specific physical-to-conscious threshold: an atom undergoing constitutional completion such that it neither gains nor loses constituent particles, becoming a conscious, non-decaying unit (jeevan).

Key areas of inquiry:
1. The physical structure of the atom, atomic saturation in omnipresent space (vyapak me sampriktata), and constitutional completion (gathan-purnata).
2. The orderly progression of the four natural orders: material (padarth), bio/plant (pran), animal (jeev), and human/knowledge (gyan avastha).
3. Comparing Madhyasth Darshan's teleological and co-existential natural progression (niyati-kram) with neo-Darwinian evolutionary theory and physicalist emergence.
4. Primary texts: Samadhanatmak Bhautikvad, Jeevan Vidya: Ek Parichay (Ch. 5–7), and Manav Vyavahar Darshan.
