## Study proposal

This study examines the legal philosophy, theory of justice, and constitutional architecture set forth in Shri A. Nagraj's Manaviya Samvidhan (Humane Constitution) and Vyavaharatmak Janvad. Contemporary legal systems typically rely on coercive deterrence, positive law, and state-enforced rights. In contrast, Madhyasth Darshan grounds jurisprudence in natural human purpose, right understanding (samadhan), and the preservation of universal orderliness.

Key areas of inquiry:
1. The nature of law (niyam, nyaya, neeti) as inherent order rather than arbitrary sovereign command.
2. The constitutional policies: religious policy (dharma neeti), economic policy (artha neeti), and state policy (rajya neeti).
3. The ten-tiered institutional structure (das-sopaniya vyavastha) from the family assembly (parivar sabha) to the world family council (vishwa parivar sabha).
4. Restorative versus retributive justice, focusing on restitution, awakening, and mutual satisfaction.
5. Primary texts: Manaviya Samvidhan, Vyavaharatmak Janvad, and Manav Vyavahar Darshan.
