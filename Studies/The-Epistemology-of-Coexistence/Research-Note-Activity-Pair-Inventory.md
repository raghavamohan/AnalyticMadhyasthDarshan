# Research Note: The Sixty-One Activity Pairs of *Jeevan*

**Author:** Raghava Mohan Madhwapathi ([analyticmadhyasthdarshan.org](https://analyticmadhyasthdarshan.org))

**Edited on:** August 14, 2026, 12:02 PM IST

**Status:** Internal research note (not a catalog entry). Compiled as the source inventory behind §6 of [*Jeevan Architecture*](Research-Note-Jeevan-Architecture.md).

**Scope:** This note sets out all sixty-one activity pairs of *jeevan* as the primary texts give them, pair by pair, with the *bal* and *shakti* member of each named from the tabulated enumeration and defined from the detailed definitions. It records what the two sources agree on, where the definitional language cuts against the tabulated assignment, and which variants or pair-relations the texts leave unsettled.

The inventory is documentary. It does not propose a functional interpretation of the pairs; that interpretation is in [the technical companion](Research-Note-Jeevan-Functional-Model.md), and the questions this inventory leaves open are collected in §8.

## 1. Why the inventory exists

The 122 activities are the finest-grained account the sources give of what *jeevan* does. They are stated twice: once as a table that assigns every activity to a column, and once as definitions that describe each activity in a sentence or two. Both are needed, and neither is sufficient alone.

The table fixes which member of a pair is the *bal* and which the *shakti*. The definitions fix what each activity is about. The two are consistent in their ordering throughout, but the definitions cannot be used to recover the assignment, because in several places the language of a definition points the opposite way from the column it sits in (§6). A reading that classified the activities by what their definitions sound like would therefore disagree with the sources at identifiable points. Setting both out together is the only way to keep the assignment source-governed.

## 2. The two source presentations

**The tabulated enumeration** appears as Table 1, "122 conducts of awakened *jeevan*", with the decomposition $2 \times (1 + 2 + 8 + 18 + 32) = 2 \times 61 = 122$ printed as its subtitle (AVD, pp. 91–94). It has five columns:

| Column | Hindi | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *akshay bal ki kriyayen (pratyavartan)* | The activities of inexhaustible *bal*, under reflection |
| 2 | *akshay bal ka naam* | The name of the inexhaustible *bal* — the faculty |
| 3 | *jeevan ka avayav* | The constituent of *jeevan* — nucleus or orbit |
| 4 | *akshay shakti ka naam* | The name of the inexhaustible *shakti* — the orientation |
| 5 | *akshay shakti ki kriyayen (paravartan)* | The activities of inexhaustible *shakti*, under projection |

The middle three columns carry the faculty-level naming and the outer two the activity-level partition, which is why one table states both grains at which strength and power are used. The English rendering in the source translates *bal* and *shakti* inconsistently, using "power" for both in the column heads and "force" for *shakti* in its name column; the Hindi *बल* and *शक्ति* is unambiguous and is what this inventory follows.

**The detailed definitions** run under the heading "The Definitions of 122 activities of *Jeevan*" (MVD, pp. 328–348). Each faculty's block opens with its two modes, and the activities beneath are set out as two-name headings followed by a definition of each member. The block headings are realisation and authenticity for *atma*, enlightenment and resolve for *buddhi*, contemplation and visualisation for *chitta*, deliberation and analysis for *vritti*, and selection and taste for *mun* (MVD, pp. 328, 329, 332, 339).

## 3. The five loci

| Constituent | Faculty (*bal*) | Orientation (*shakti*) | *Bal* activity | *Shakti* activity | Pairs | Activities |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| Nucleus | *Atma* | *Praman*, evidence | *Anubhav*, realisation | *Pramanikta*, authenticity | 1 | 2 |
| First orbit | *Buddhi* | *Ritambhara*, resoluteness | *Bodh*, enlightenment | *Sankalp*, resolve | 2 | 4 |
| Second orbit | *Chitta* | *Ichha*, desire | *Chintan*, contemplation | *Chitran*, visualisation | 8 | 16 |
| Third orbit | *Vritti* | *Vichar*, thought | *Tulan*, deliberation | *Vishleshan*, analysis | 18 | 36 |
| Fourth orbit | *Mun* | *Asha*, hope | *Asvadan*, taste | *Chayan*, selection | 32 | 64 |
| | | | | | **61** | **122** |

Every pair below occupies one position at one of these loci. The numbering within each faculty is the source's own (AVD, pp. 91–94). The numbered rows are contents of the generic pair named in this table, not a second set of faculties and not dimensions of it. The sources use “eight dimensions” for visualisation in *chitta*, and they do not map those dimensions onto the eight *chitta* pairs (§4.3). For *mun*, *asha* names the orientation—hope toward tasting—while *asvadan* and *chayan* are the two activities; the thirty-two pairs are what that tasting and selecting are over.

## 4. The sixty-one pairs

The Hindi activity names and their *bal–shakti* column assignments follow the AVD table. English glosses follow AVD where its rendering is stable and MVD where the detailed definition supplies the clearer term; §8 records material differences. The two definition columns condense MVD, retaining only the governing clauses where a definition runs longer.

### 4.1 *Atma* — one pair

| # | *Bal* entry | Condensed MVD definition | *Shakti* entry | Condensed MVD definition |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *anubhav* — realisation, in *astitva* | Eternal presence in the form of nature saturated in Omnipotence | *pramanikta* — authenticity, of *paramanand* | Realisation-based expression, communication and exposition |

*Atma*'s pair is named twice over. The tabulated enumeration gives the modes, realisation and authenticity; the definitions give their contents, existence and ultimate bliss, under the sub-heading "Realisation in existence / Authenticity of ultimate bliss" (MVD, p. 328).

### 4.2 *Buddhi* — two pairs

| # | *Bal* entry | Condensed MVD definition | *Shakti* entry | Condensed MVD definition |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *anand* — bliss | Endless, uninterrupted celebration activity | *dhee* | The inclination activity of celebration, readiness for projection |
| 2 | *astitva* — existence | Acceptance free from delusion of state, motion, development, awakening, and the present | *dhruti* | Dedication in truth as coexistence and tendency for projection; absence of fear, trust in the present |

*Buddhi*'s block carries the one sentence in the whole enumeration that names the partition of a pair outright: the pinnacle of acceptance of the state and motion of awakening is bliss and *dhee* (MVD, p. 328). Both of its pairs place an acceptance against a readiness for projection.

The purpose of the sensory group is stated with it: every awakened human recognises that the purpose of sensations is maintaining the health of the body, and nourishing is defined as unit plus conducive unit against depleting as unit lacking a conducive unit or joined with an unconducive unit (MVD, pp. 347–348). In delusion, taste and selection already operate over this sensory and instinctual field; the thirty-two pairs as defined are the awakened inventory of *mun*'s two generic activities, not a further set that remains unused from lack of need ([the architecture note](Research-Note-Jeevan-Architecture.md) §4.2, §6).

The repeated *poshan* label follows the *shakti* column in the AVD table (pp. 93–94). The fuller MVD definitions render cold, heat, and each taste through nourishing/depleting and then define both terms (p. 348). The table preserves the enumerated activity name while its final column records the fuller semantic contrast.

## 5. What the two presentations agree on

The left–right order is the same in both. Each detailed heading places the member corresponding to the *bal* column first and the member corresponding to the *shakti* column second. The agreement extends to compound entries, where *mridu-kathor* stands against *vahan-samvahan* in the definitions exactly as the columns place them (MVD, p. 347; AVD, p. 93). Three closing entries differ in their actual names, so their agreement is positional rather than lexical (§8).

That agreement is worth stating because the two presentations are otherwise independent: one is a table of names with no definitions, the other a run of definitions with no columns. Their concurrence on ordering is what allows either to be read against the other, and it means the *bal* member of any pair can be identified from the definitions alone by position, though not by content (§6).

Naming differs in places without affecting the assignment. *Shruti* is rendered "revelation" in the table and "realised articulation" in the definitions; *pushti* is "validation" against "confirmation"; *jati* is "species" against "kind"; *bhav* is "value" against "sentiment"; *ananyata* is "non-dualness" against "non-otherness". These are translation variants of one Hindi term in each case.

## 6. Where the definitions cut against the column

The tabulated assignment cannot be recovered from the definitions by reading which of the two sounds more like a state and which more like a motion. In the following pairs that procedure gives the wrong answer or no answer, which is why the table governs.

| Pair | Tabulated assignment | What the definition says | Why the reading misleads |
|---|---|---|---|
| *veerta* / *dheerta* | *veerta* is the *bal* | *Veerta* is "utilising one's powers to ensure delivery of justice to others"; *dheerta* is "dedication and firmness towards justice" | The *bal* member is defined by outward deployment and the *shakti* member by settled dedication, the reverse of the expected sense |
| *sweekruti* / *swagat* | *sweekruti* is the *bal* | The definition places evidencing in projection of what has been received within *sweekruti* | A *bal* activity defined with projection and evidencing in its own clause |
| *santosh* / *shree* | *santosh* is the *bal* | *Santosh* is "the lack of the sentiment of lacking" and also "readiness for evidencing along with production more than needs" | Readiness-for-evidencing language inside a state activity |
| *keerti* / *vichar* | *keerti* is the *bal* | *Keerti* is "the activeness for awakening" | Activeness is motion language; both members read as motion |
| *shraddha* / *pujyata* | *shraddha* is the *bal* | *Shraddha* is "movement towards righteousness"; *pujyata* is "activeness for qualitative development" | Both members read as movement; neither reads as a resting state |
| *nireekshan* / *guna* | *nireekshan* is the *bal* | *Nireekshan* is an inspecting activity; *guna* is "relative motions", what gets inspected | The pair divides as act and object, not as state and motion |

Two general points follow. First, several definitions carry both an established clause and a readiness-or-evidencing clause within one activity, on both sides of the partition; the internal grammar of a definition is therefore not a reliable classification criterion. Second, *bal* and *shakti* here are not "static" and "dynamic". A *bal* activity is an activity, and the sources say so; what distinguishes it is that it is what the faculty has established in itself, which may well be established as an activeness or a movement.

## 7. What the sources do not settle

Neither presentation states one general rule for how a *shakti* member stands to its *bal* member. The definitions variously concern accepted conditions, readiness for projection, evidence, relationships, responsibilities, acts, objects, sensory qualities, and bodily correlates. The tabulated columns therefore establish assignment without establishing a uniform pair semantics.

This inventory does not sort those relations into a functional taxonomy. A proposed grouping and pair-level schema are kept in the [technical companion](Research-Note-Jeevan-Functional-Model.md) §8, where their interpretive status is explicit.

## 8. Variants and unresolved points

**A second, unpaired list.** Before the definitions begin, a shorter run of activity definitions appears without pairing, laid out as a flat glossary (MVD, pp. 323–326). Most of its entries recur in the paired enumeration, sometimes worded differently — *medha* is "the activity that bears *kala*" there and "the carrier-bearer activity of memory" in the paired list, and *shanti* is "behaviour based on resolved thoughts" against "consequence of resolved thoughts". Two entries in it have no counterpart in the paired enumeration at all: *ichchha*, desire, as "awareness about one's wishes and aspirations", and *kalpana*, imagination, as "prior form of belief" (MVD, pp. 325–326). Neither appears among the 122. *Ichchha* is separately the name of *chitta*'s *shakti* in the tabulated enumeration, which may account for its absence from the activity list, but nothing consulted accounts for *kalpana*.

**Closing names differ between the table and definitions.** The AVD table gives *chirchira*, *shvasan–nihshvasan*, and *swagat–aswagat* (pp. 93–94). The detailed MVD definitions instead give *charpara*, *prashvasan–vishvasan*, and *apanapan–parayapan* (p. 348). The inventory rows retain the AVD activity names because that table governs column assignment; their condensed definitions follow MVD. The variants are recorded rather than silently normalised.

***Shruti* is mistranslated once.** The Hindi definition of *sauhardta* includes *smriti aur shruti*, memory and *shruti* (MVD, p. 340). The accompanying English renders the latter “direct recognition,” a term used elsewhere for *sakshatkar*. The inventory retains *shruti* and uses its definition as realised articulation from the same activity list (MVD, p. 329).

**Saujanyata twice.** In the detailed definitions the twelfth *vritti* heading reads "*Nyaya* (*saujanyata*), *Samvedna*", giving *saujanyata* parenthetically at *nyaya* although it is already the *shakti* member of the tenth pair (MVD, pp. 335–336). The tabulated enumeration has *nyaya* alone at position twelve.

**Sound has no pair.** The sensory group covers touch, temperature, the six tastes, smell, and visible form. Sound appears inside the definition of *priya*, among the five organs whose conduciveness it names, but not as a pair of its own (MVD, p. 343). Whether that follows from the enumeration's principle is open.

**The counts are stated, not derived.** The decomposition into one, two, eight, eighteen, and thirty-two positions is printed with the table but nowhere argued, and the general account of atomic constitution says only that each orbit holds one or more particles (MVD, p. 78). The four orbital terms are the shell capacities $2n^2$ for $n = 1$ to $4$; no consulted passage states that correspondence.

**What a pair-level specification would need.** The enumeration supplies a uniform two-slot form but not uniform fields. Some definitions state a governing criterion or an evidential result and others a single clause, and the tabulated version has five columns with none of the three. A specification giving each pair its content, criterion, relevant relation, and evidential result would therefore have to be constructed rather than read off.
