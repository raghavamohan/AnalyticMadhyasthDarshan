Research Note: The Sixty-One Activity Pairs of Jeevan

Author: Raghava Mohan Madhwapathi (analyticmadhyasthdarshan.org)

Edited on: August 16, 2026, 7:08 AM IST

Status: Internal research note (not a catalog entry). Compiled as the source inventory behind §6 of Jeevan Architecture.

Scope: This note sets out all sixty-one activity pairs of as the primary texts give them, pair by pair, with the bal and shakti member of each named from the tabulated enumeration and defined from the detailed definitions. It records what the two sources agree on, where the definitional language cuts against the tabulated assignment, and which variants or pair-relations the texts leave unsettled.

The inventory is documentary. It does not propose a functional interpretation of the pairs; that interpretation is in the technical companion, and the questions this inventory leaves open are collected in §8.

1. Why the inventory exists

The 122 activities are the finest-grained account the sources give of what does. They are stated twice: once as a table that assigns every activity to a column, and once as definitions that describe each activity in a sentence or two. Both are needed, and neither is sufficient alone.

The table fixes which member of a pair is the bal and which the shakti. The definitions fix what each activity is about. The two are consistent in their ordering throughout, but the definitions cannot be used to recover the assignment, because in several places the language of a definition points the opposite way from the column it sits in (§6). A reading that classified the activities by what their definitions sound like would therefore disagree with the sources at identifiable points. Setting both out together is the only way to keep the assignment source-governed.

2. The two source presentations

The tabulated enumeration appears as Table 1, “122 conducts of awakened ”, with the decomposition 2×(1+2+8+18+32)=2×61=1222 \times (1 + 2 + 8 + 18 + 32) = 2 \times 61 = 1222×(1+2+8+18+32)=2×61=122 printed as its subtitle (AVD, pp. 91–94). It has five columns:

Column Hindi Content
1 akshay bal ki kriyayen (pratyavartan) The activities of inexhaustible bal, under reflection
2 akshay bal ka naam The name of the inexhaustible bal — the faculty
3 jeevan ka avayav The constituent of jeevan — nucleus or orbit
4 akshay shakti ka naam The name of the inexhaustible shakti — the orientation
5 akshay shakti ki kriyayen (paravartan) The activities of inexhaustible shakti, under projection

The middle three columns carry the faculty-level naming and the outer two the activity-level partition, which is why one table states both grains at which strength and power are used. The English rendering in the source translates bal and shakti inconsistently, using “power” for both in the column heads and “force” for shakti in its name column; the Hindi बल and शक्ति is unambiguous and is what this inventory follows.

The detailed definitions run under the heading “The Definitions of 122 activities of Jeevan” (MVD, pp. 328–348). Each faculty’s block opens with its two modes, and the activities beneath are set out as two-name headings followed by a definition of each member. The block headings are realisation and authenticity for atma, enlightenment and resolve for , contemplation and visualisation for , deliberation and analysis for , and selection and taste for (MVD, pp. 328, 329, 332, 339).

Every pair below occupies one position at one of these loci. The numbering within each faculty is the source’s own (AVD, pp. 91–94). The numbered rows are contents of the generic pair named in this table, not a second set of faculties and not dimensions of it. The sources use “eight dimensions” for visualisation in , and they do not map those dimensions onto the eight chitta pairs (§4.3). For , asha names the orientation—hope toward tasting—while asvadan and chayan are the two activities; the thirty-two pairs are what that tasting and selecting are over.

4. The sixty-one pairs

The Hindi activity names and their bal–shakti column assignments follow the AVD table. English glosses follow AVD where its rendering is stable and MVD where the detailed definition supplies the clearer term; §8 records material differences. The two definition columns condense MVD, retaining only the governing clauses where a definition runs longer. Each cell gives that clause first in Hindi as printed, then in the published English, so the translation can be checked against the source wording rather than inferred from the English alone. Temperature and the six tastes require two parallel documentary views because the AVD assignments and MVD definition headings do not divide the content in the same way (§4.5).

4.5 Mun — thirty-two pairs

The left-hand column belongs to asvadan; the right-hand column to chayan. A row places both members at one position in mun. It does not state that the shakti member is the selection of that taste. The first twenty-two are relational and dispositional; the last ten are the activities of the cognitive and work organs, set out under their own sub-heading (MVD, p. 347).

The purpose of the sensory group is stated with it: every awakened human recognises that the purpose of sensations is maintaining the health of the body (MVD, pp. 347–348). In delusion, taste and selection already operate over this sensory and instinctual field; the thirty-two pairs as defined are the awakened inventory of mun’s two generic activities, not a further set that remains unused from lack of need (the architecture note §4.2, §6).

5. What the two presentations agree on

The left–right order is the same where both presentations supply two names. Each detailed heading places the member corresponding to the bal column first and the member corresponding to the shakti column second. The agreement extends to compound entries, where mridu-kathor stands against vahan-samvahan in the definitions exactly as the columns place them (MVD, p. 347; AVD, p. 93). Temperature and the six tastes are the exception: AVD supplies two columns and repeats poshan, whereas MVD heads each sensory quality and defines it through poshan/shoshan without a separately defined second member (§4.5, §8). Three closing entries also differ in their actual names, so their agreement is positional rather than lexical (§8).

That agreement is worth stating because the two presentations are otherwise independent: one is a table of names with no definitions, the other a run of definitions with no columns. Their concurrence on ordering is what allows either to be read against the other, and it means the bal member of any pair can be identified from the definitions alone by position, though not by content (§6).

Naming differs in places without affecting the assignment. Shruti is rendered “revelation” in the table and “realised articulation” in the definitions; pushti is “validation” against “confirmation”; jati is “species” against “kind”; bhav is “value” against “sentiment”; ananyata is “non-dualness” against “non-otherness”. These are translation variants of one Hindi term in each case.

6. Where the definitions cut against the column

The tabulated assignment cannot be recovered from the definitions by reading which of the two sounds more like a state and which more like a motion. In the following pairs that procedure gives the wrong answer or no answer, which is why the table governs.

sweekruti / swagat sweekruti is the bal The definition places evidencing in projection of what has been received within sweekruti A bal activity defined with projection and evidencing in its own clause
santosh / shree santosh is the bal Santosh is “the lack of the sentiment of lacking” and also “readiness for evidencing along with production more than needs” Readiness-for-evidencing language inside a state activity

Two general points follow. First, several definitions carry both an established clause and a readiness-or-evidencing clause within one activity, on both sides of the partition; the internal grammar of a definition is therefore not a reliable classification criterion. Second, bal and shakti here are not “static” and “dynamic”. A bal activity is an activity, and the sources say so; what distinguishes it is that it is what the faculty has established in itself, which may well be established as an activeness or a movement.

7. What the sources do not settle

Neither presentation states one general rule for how a shakti member stands to its bal member. The definitions variously concern accepted conditions, readiness for projection, evidence, relationships, responsibilities, acts, objects, sensory qualities, and bodily correlates. The tabulated columns therefore establish assignment without establishing a uniform pair semantics.

This inventory does not sort those relations into a functional taxonomy. A proposed grouping and pair-level schema are kept in the technical companion §8, where their interpretive status is explicit.

8. Variants and unresolved points

A second, unpaired list. Before the definitions begin, a shorter run of activity definitions appears without pairing, laid out as a flat glossary (MVD, pp. 323–326). Most of its entries recur in the paired enumeration, sometimes worded differently — medha is “the activity that bears kala” there and “the carrier-bearer activity of memory” in the paired list, and shanti is “behaviour based on resolved thoughts” against “consequence of resolved thoughts”. Two entries in it have no counterpart in the paired enumeration at all: ichchha, desire, as “awareness about one’s wishes and aspirations”, and kalpana, imagination, as “prior form of belief” (MVD, pp. 325–326). Neither appears among the 122. Ichchha is separately the name of chitta’s shakti in the tabulated enumeration, which may account for its absence from the activity list, but nothing consulted accounts for kalpana.

Closing names differ between the table and definitions. The AVD table gives chirchira, shvasan–nihshvasan, and swagat–aswagat (pp. 93–94). The detailed MVD definitions instead give charpara, prashvasan–vishvasan, and apanapan–parayapan (p. 348). The inventory rows retain the AVD activity names because that table governs column assignment; their condensed definitions follow MVD. The variants are recorded rather than silently normalised.

AVD repeats poshan against temperature and the six tastes. Table 1 prints poshan (nourishing) as the shakti member of rows 24–30, and the English WIP copies the gloss “(nourishing)” onto row 31, whose Hindi is shvasan/nihshvasan, inhalation and exhalation (pp. 93–94). MVD does not name a second activity in rows 24–30. It defines cold, heat, and each taste through poshan/shoshan, then defines nourishing as unit plus conducive unit and depleting as unit minus a conducive unit or plus an unconducive unit (p. 348). The inventory retains the AVD pair assignments and presents the MVD definition view beside them. It does not decide whether repeated poshan denotes seven distinct activities, one recurrent activity, or tabular shorthand. Named poshan also occurs independently at row 21 with mata.

Shruti is mistranslated once. The Hindi definition of sauhardta includes smriti aur shruti, memory and shruti (MVD, p. 340). The accompanying English renders the latter “direct recognition,” a term used elsewhere for sakshatkar. The inventory retains shruti and uses its definition as realised articulation from the same activity list (MVD, p. 329).

Saujanyata twice. In the detailed definitions the twelfth vritti heading reads “Nyaya (saujanyata), Samvedna”, giving saujanyata parenthetically at nyaya although it is already the shakti member of the tenth pair (MVD, pp. 335–336). The tabulated enumeration has nyaya alone at position twelve.

Sound has no pair. The sensory group covers touch, temperature, the six tastes, smell, and visible form. Sound appears inside the definition of priya, among the five organs whose conduciveness it names, but not as a pair of its own (MVD, p. 343). Whether that follows from the enumeration’s principle is open.

The counts are stated, not derived. The decomposition into one, two, eight, eighteen, and thirty-two positions is printed with the table but nowhere argued, and the general account of atomic constitution says only that each orbit holds one or more particles (MVD, p. 78). The four orbital terms are the shell capacities 2n22n^22n2 for n=1n = 1n=1 to 444; no consulted passage states that correspondence.

What a pair-level specification would need. The enumeration supplies a uniform two-slot form but not uniform fields. Some definitions state a governing criterion or an evidential result and others a single clause, and the tabulated version has five columns with none of the three. A specification giving each pair its content, criterion, relevant relation, and evidential result would therefore have to be constructed rather than read off.
