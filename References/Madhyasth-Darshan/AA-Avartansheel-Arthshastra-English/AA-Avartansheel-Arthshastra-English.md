## Alternative (*vikalp*)

1. The human being could not be studied through thought based on instability and uncertainty and centred on physicochemical objects, that is, through the method of science. Nor could the human being be studied through the mystery-based idealistic method of contemplation. Both kinds of ideology have called the human being a living being (*jeeva*).

    As an alternative, Madhyasth Darshan, Co-existentialism, through the method of existence-based human-centric contemplation, has recognised the human being as being in the knowledge order, and has enabled this to be recognised.

    According to Madhyasth Darshan, the human being alone is the knower (the one who knows), and existence in the form of coexistence is the reality worth knowing and believing, that is, the whole of reality to be known. This is the knowledge of the holistic view. Along with it, the method by which coexistence is evidenced, together with the knowledge of *jeevan* and the knowledge of humane conduct, has become accessible to study.

    I have presented this knowledge of existence-based human-centric contemplation, Madhyasth Darshan, Co-existentialism, before humankind in the form of treatises (*shastra*) for study.

2. Before existence-based human-centric contemplation, my (A. Nagraj, Agrahar Nagaraj, District Hassan, Karnataka State, India) initiation took place through spiritualist (*adhyatmavadi*) knowledge and the worship and action (*upasana*–*karma*) natural to Vedic thought.

3. According to Vedanta, knowledge is "Brahma is truth, the world is false (*mithya*)", whereas the origin of the world of living beings was attributed to Brahma.

    - *Upasana* :- in relation to gods and goddesses.
    - *Karma* :- all actions rewarded in heaven (in the form of language).
    - In the *Manu Dharma Shastra* :- the daily duties (*nitya karma*) of the four classes (*varnas*) and four stages of life (*ashramas*) are prescribed.
    - In ritual practice (*karmakanda*) :- sixteen kinds of rites, from the conception rite (*garbha sanskar*) to the death rite (*mrityu sanskar*), are accepted, together with their programmes.

    From studying all these, a question arose in my mind -

4. How can the world of living beings, born of Brahma, who is *satyam jnanam anantam* (truth, knowledge, infinite), be false? In the course of inquiring with the Vedic experts and scholars of that time, I received:-

    the assurance that in *samadhi* the unknown becomes known. On the basis of the support of the scriptures, I accepted the undertaking of *sadhana*, *samadhi* and *samyama*. In the state of *sadhana*, *samadhi* and *samyama* I attained complete understanding, by the method of study and realisation, of the whole of existence as being and abiding as coexistence. As a result, the alternative emerged in the form of the body of thought (*vangmay*) of Madhyasth Darshan, Co-existentialism.

5. According to idealistic scriptures and to mystery-based, God-centred contemplation, knowledge and tradition: knowledge is unmanifest and ineffable.

    According to Madhyasth Darshan: knowledge has become clear as manifest and expressible, understandable through the method of study, and, through the method of behaviour, as evidence accessible to all.

6. According to materialism, which is based on instability and uncertainty, science was taken to be knowledge in object-centred thought, in which it is said that laws are made by humans. As its alternative, according to the knowledge of existence-based human-centric contemplation in the form of coexistence, it is propounded that existence is stable, that development and awakening are definite, and that all laws are natural, being and abiding so.

7. Existence is not merely physicochemical: physicochemical and *jeevan* entities are inseparably present in the omnipresent reality. This is the formula (*sutra*) of the treatise "Madhyasth Darshan Co-existentialism".

### Testimony (*satyapan*)

8. Where I began this bodily journey, my ancestors were known as Vedic scholars (*vedamurti*). In home and village, only the Vedas, and the Vedanta, Upanishads and Darshan related to Vedic thought, were heard as language, sound and melody. In the family tradition, worship (*upasana*), adoration (*aradhana*), offering (*archana*) and hymns of glory (*stavan*) in accordance with the Vedas went on being performed.

9. In our family tradition, the scholars of the highest rank were naturally service-minded and industrious in their practice of behaviour and practice of work; of these, I accepted industriousness and the tendency to serve. Question marks remained on the side of scholarship.

10. The first question arose:-

    How can the origin of the world and of living beings from Brahma, the truth, be false?

    Second question:-

    How can Brahma alone be the cause of bondage and of liberation (*moksha*)?

    Third question:-

    Is the word the evidence, or the one who holds and carries the word?

    Is the authoritative statement (*apta vakya*) the evidence, or the one who utters it?

    Is the scripture the evidence, or its propounder?

In the circumstances of the time, one more question arose.

Fourth question:-

After independence in India, a Constituent Assembly was formed. Without giving the essence and elaboration (*sutra vyakhya*) of nation, nationality and national character, the constitution accepts people as eligible to be people's representatives.

What kind of public mandate and people's representative comes from an alliance of vote and note (money)?

Secularism in the constitution: one sentence, and with it the mention of many castes, sects and communities.

Equality in the constitution: one sentence, and with it the mention of reservation and its procedure in the constitution.

Democracy: an alliance of vote and note in the process of electing people's representatives to government.

What kind of democracy is this?

11. To free myself from the tangle of these questions, at the suggestion of the scholars, Vedic scholars (*vedamurti*) and honourable sages (*rishi*, *maharshi*) of that time -

    (1) They pointed to *samadhi* as the only path for making the unknown known, and I accepted it.

    (2) I accepted Amarkantak as a favourable place for *sadhana*.

    (3) I began the work of *sadhana* in 1950. In the 1960s, *sadhana* matured.

    (4) In 1970 I came to accept that the state of *samadhi* had been accomplished. In the state of *samadhi* my hopes, thoughts and desires fell silent. I also came to understand that in such a state the event of the unknown becoming known was nil. This state of natural *sadhana* went on for twelve (12) to eighteen (18) hours every day.

    I accepted *samyama*, in the sequence of *samadhi*, *dhyana* and *dharana*, as a spontaneous system. After two years, with *samyama* accomplished, I accepted it as evidence that *samadhi* had taken place. The process of accomplishing *samyama* from *samadhi* also went on taking 12 to 18 hours. As a result, I realised that the whole of existence is and abides naturally as coexistence. Its body of thought (*vangmay*) has been presented in the form of the treatise "Madhyasth Darshan Co-existentialism".

    Within coexistence itself:- It was understood that the constitutionally complete atom is a sentient unit, in the form of "*jeevan*".

    In, from and for coexistence:- It was understood that coexistence is eternally effective. This itself was understood to be the natural progression (*niyatikram*).

## Chapter 1: The Definition/Acceptance of Resources (*Artha*), in Sequence from the Beginning of Their Recognition

We have received the auspicious opportunity to present cyclical economics before the public mind.

Before this, in the human tradition that has passed until now in the form of individualistic communities, the methods of those times gave a general clarification of the form of resources as accepted by humans, of allotment or sharing in the family, of exchange in communities, and of need, possibility, source, and the tendencies and works carried out by humans. Considering it appropriate, we have presented that too.

Fundamentally, it is natural that the tradition of primitive humans is connected with the present. In other words, it is in keeping with destiny (*niyati sahaj*). "Natural" means that in every time and place humans have lived on this earth accepting the "self" (*sva*) only within their own limits. Its evidence is what is found, in sequence, from generation to generation. Destiny means development progression, development, and awakening progression, awakening. Awakening means precisely the human being who knows, believes, recognises and fulfils.

Whenever more than one human being exists in proximity (proximity means living together), it is natural for them to recognise one another. In the human being, who is naturally endowed with awareness, the activity of recognising is itself the evidence of minimum awareness, and the recognition of relationships is also indicative of minimum awareness. Its evidence is that even today every healthy human being, at the minimum, recognises the things around them. "Around" means the limit of the reach of the senses of sound, touch, form, taste and smell. Awareness, too, means knowing, believing, recognising and fulfilling. Thus, on this earth, it was natural for the body formation of primitive humans to be composed within the bodies of some animals such as the monkey or the bear, and to become established on the earth. For on this earth, before the human body formation, it is seen that once the body formation of animals of one species and its tradition had become established, the body formation of another species developed, together with its tradition, by these methods. Among bodies with a brain, the sequence of bodies with a richer brain can be seen on this earth. In this same sequence the human body formation also emerged; in the sense of the sequence of brain formation and its completion, the supremely rich brain formation, and a bodily working system corresponding to it, have become clear in the human body alone. Along with this it is also clear that all the kinds of body formation that preceded the human body formation are also seen to be impressed by nearby

[p. 2]
<!-- source: _page-images/p012_print002.png -->

objects and events. To be impressed means to direct one's tendency at once towards that (the sound, touch, form, taste or smell at hand). Therefore it is naturally established that primitive humans, too, were impressed by the states, motions and events at hand.

For more than one human being, it has been natural first of all to recognise relationships on the basis of form; as a result, needs arose, that is, they were set in motion as hope, thought and desire. In this sequence arose the first need, for food and drink, the need for a place to sit and sleep, and the needs for protection from cold, wind and rain. In this sequence the human being, making use of leaves, bark and grass, went on carrying out the activity of recognising, as far as tubers and roots. At the same time, they kept searching for ways to protect themselves from the attacks of fierce animals. Reproduction, too, went on taking place. In the rearing, nourishment and care of offspring, a difference from the animals that preceded humans continued to be recognised.

It is also natural that the differences of climate between different geographical conditions existed then and exist now. Accordingly, the human traditions that began in these various climates and geographical conditions developed, each with its own form (race) and colour. This is fundamentally why, whenever humans raised in one climate met humans raised in another, they went on fighting one another out of malice or fear, owing to differences of race and colour. Through this sequence, from forests to the tribe and to the village cluster, tendency and endeavour went on being inspired by the magnificence of the imaginativeness and freedom of action found in human beings. For imaginativeness and freedom of action have always been evident in every human being. This is also what is meant by the natural emergence of tendency and imaginativeness. This has been the cause of change from generation to generation. Change reached from the age of forest, vegetation and wood to the stone age and the metal age. It reached the point of living as villages and tribes. Humans living in some environments accepted animals as their food; humans who lived in other environments learned to fill their stomachs from plants. Most of the humans who have obtained their food from plants took up agriculture in the course of the changing ages. It is worth remembering here that humans also began to recognise things from the forest fit for human consumption as grain. At the same time, humans naturally gave certain kinds of animals found in the forest a place in their own natural setting, and went on making meaningful the purpose of using them. Their usefulness was considered in terms of helping agriculture and helping to provide food. Up to this stage, needs fundamentally extended to food and drink, covering and clothing, together with a fixed place to sleep and live, and its form. Fear and malice concerning race and colour persisted among the various tribal groups

[p. 3]
<!-- source: _page-images/p013_print003.png -->

and villagers. In the course of strengthening the groups of each one's own species, change went on continually. With every change, the form, quantity and quality of needs changed of their own accord. This became clear of its own accord in two areas. First, change in the area of food, shelter and adornment; second, in the course of acts of attack and resistance driven by fear and malice, a wave of fighting that moved from punches, slaps and strangling to fighting with stones and sticks. From fighting with stones and sticks, the use of tools made of metals was a further step in itself. It was another form of change in fighting, in attack and resistance. Up to this period, this programme of attack and resistance passed through two forms. The first was resistance to the attacks of fierce beasts, and the second was the attacks made upon frightened humans and the resistance to them. Up to this period, tools (that is, tools used for fighting) and the objects obtained for food, shelter and adornment went on being exchanged voluntarily between people. Second, one person kept seizing another's objects, or one community kept seizing the objects of another community by attack, or kept attacking in order to seize them. Against this, the programme of resisting it continued without break. In this way, whatever objects were obtained for these two purposes we can, in today's situation, give the name "resources" (*artha*).

Along with the tribal age, the calamities of race and colour hovered again and again. Repeated preparation for attack and resistance; food, shelter and adornment; and repeated changes in the system of reproduction — first, reproductive relations among offspring of the same mother; second, reproductive relations between those not of the same mother, which in today's language are called marriage relations — continued as natural. Tribes and village communities, tormented by such fear and malice, gave themselves over to the seat of state and the seat of religion on the assurances of the king and the guru. In the course of accepting the king and the guru, they surrendered to the seat of state on assurances of the security of whatever they had acquired as food, shelter and adornment, and of body and family (the recognised limit of a definite number of humans), that is, on assurances of freedom from seizure and attacks. On assurances of making good sense and good tendencies accessible, they surrendered to the seats of religion. Along with this, the intent to be free from suffering remained present in all humans of that time. Declarations of assurance of its success went on pleasing everyone. On this basis, faith in the seat of state and the seat of religion was formed. Faith means accepting the existence of something even without knowing it. In this way came the doctrine of faith (*asthavad*), the establishment of the seat of state and the seat of religion, and their acceptance in the public mindset. The noteworthy and memorable event here, that is, the event worth keeping in memory, is that dharma remained not understood by the public mind, and at the same time a seat of religion capable of evidencing dharma in its own form was

[p. 4]
<!-- source: _page-images/p014_print004.png -->

not established; moreover, while the state generally signifies the grandeur natural to humans, the state was established as rulership without humans being understood. Fundamentally, at the time rulership began, it was dominated by religion: on the basis that the whole country's inhabitants, that is, all persons, were followers of whatever religion the king belonged to, a religious state policy held sway. Such various communities, too, have kept succeeding in establishing the assurances of state and religion. Contradictions arose among religious beliefs (faiths); as a result, the grip of religion, of religious policy, or of religious rule began to loosen. Meanwhile, after prospering through the metal age, humans reached explosive systems: gunpowder, guns and cannon. Along with this, together with the use of animals achieved by the tribal and village age, elephants and horses had come into use; now these were accumulated in very large numbers for war between states. The sword was there from before; cannon and guns were added to it. Along with the metal age, the system of arrows had also come into use. In this way the warfare system and its quality advanced towards horror, and the food, shelter and adornment objects of the various regions became plentiful. In this way, state and religion, engaged in conflict, now advanced fully into the work of accumulation through methods of taxation, in order to establish the dominance of the state. This took the form of unbalanced accumulation, that is, not everyone could accumulate in this way; such accumulation took place in the seats of state or in reserves. To console the public mind over such events, the seat of state and the seat of religion together kept drawing attention to national security, to the integrity of religion and to expansionism, and kept offering consolation.

Invigorated in these forms, state, religion and people, that is, the communities living in the state, also kept applying their own imaginativeness and freedom of action. In the course of all these kinds of production, village industries and cottage industries were established. In their early period, the barter system prevailed. **Along with the programme of taxation by the throne, the barter system too turned to profit-earning. This can be regarded as the first stage of profit-obsession.** After this, in various states, the circulation of currency was established in place of the various kinds of barter. That metal currency came into circulation in the early period is also recorded in history. Exchange of objects began on the basis of numerical values written on metals. The noteworthy event here is this: "Before this, when the barter system was established, and then even with the circulation of metal currency, the evaluation of the value of labour, and its system, method and policy, could not be established."

Passing through these circumstances and systems, owing to states and religions, and owing to humans' own freedom of action and imaginativeness, the scientific age was found to begin on this earth. Whatever

[p. 5]
<!-- source: _page-images/p015_print005.png -->

difficulties or obstacles arose in its becoming established came about chiefly from the side of the seats of religion. The evidence of scientific events has been accepted on this earth by a method that follows the sequence of destiny; therefore science became established by its own methods. It arose in the form of laws and principles. All the laws came from methods of taking parts apart and looking at them separately. **That is, the procedure of seeing any one thing by dividing it into many parts has been called a law. The system and method of joining all these parts together and evidencing one working event has been called a principle.** What this sequence has made available, or put within reach, as benevolence to the human race is in three forms: transport, telephony and broadcasting. In the second area, the explosive element, which was there from before, was prepared, with a destructive mentality, for use as hand grenades and bombs and for destruction. War and resistance were there from before. As it became more modern and ultra-modern, it established its application in long-range strikes or projectiles and in destructive works. In this sequence, of food, shelter and adornment, change took place in the areas of shelter and adornment. At present, buildings of up to a hundred storeys have been seen. Automated mechanical systems developed; cloth, paper and metalwork began to be produced in abundance by centralised methods, and unemployment grew from generation to generation. One or two metals became the basis for evaluating all other objects; the more of such basic metals, that is, metals basic to evaluation, a country's treasury held, the more the value of the paper currency based on them rose. In this way metal became the symbol of objects, and paper the symbol of metal. Meanwhile, one also hears that leather currency came into circulation. In this way, according to the modern age, economics has emerged only as the study of resources. The study of resources took the form of the basic metal that symbolises objects, the accumulating of it, paper currency based on its quantity, and objects evaluated on the basis of paper currencies, or of values in the form of paper currency. In all these endeavours, profit alone became the principal element. **Ever since the hope of profit began in humans, the form of profit has always been the tendency to give less and get more. How much less one should give and how much more one should get: no fixed limit (*dhruv*) for this has yet come into use on the basis of the prevailing economics.** The economics prevailing so far has been thought out by the unrestrained method and by the regulated method. Both kinds of economic thought have looked to profit. **And in the method of gaining such profit, artificial scarcity was regarded as a principal technique.** This was found to rest on capital in the form of currency. The expectation of maximum profit through artificial scarcity was in fact realised in most cases. The noteworthy point here is what was thought in the past: both kinds of economics have also gone on believing that for one unit to profit, another unit suffers loss. Even after this, economics thought out in both ways has been unable to devise an economics and a system free of profit and loss. This is the point. As a result of the suffering that arises from the method indicated

[p. 6]
<!-- source: _page-images/p016_print006.png -->

by this point, a rethinking of economics was seen as a necessity. As the resolution of this very problem, the opportunity arose to propose cyclical economics and to establish its propounding source of inspiration.

At present, on this earth, the activity of transaction (giving and taking) in the human tradition is found to be based on paper currency and kept in motion by it. Paper currency is based on some metal currency or on the quantity of a metal. This accumulating of metal, or metal treasury, is considered to be, and is seen to be, under each nation or state. Every object produced by humans is seen as evidence of human creativity. The accumulating of metals (gold and silver) does not in itself appear as anything useful to humans. Perhaps one person in hundreds of thousands may have used it as medicine. Accepting such a useless object as the standard among useful objects became the basis of going astray. The paper currencies that symbolise it opened the way to many distortions. In whichever country the metal reserve declined, the value of its paper currency fell; in whichever countries the accumulation of metal grew stronger, that is, increased, the value of their paper currency rose. Yet to the eye, all of these look like nothing but paper. **Symbolic currency: on this paper, printed as a symbol of currency, one bears some name and number, another some other name and number, and the numbers written on them run from one to thousands. When objects are unavailable, such papers, or even all the many metal reserves that lie at their root, taken together, cannot serve in place of grain for one person or quench one person's thirst.** An ordinary person can internalise this; what need is there to speak of scholars and thinkers? Thus, in this form, we can count symbolic currencies in two ways: (1) metal reserve, (2) paper reserve. As for their mutual relation: to give a regulating form to the quantity of the paper reserve on the basis of the metal reserve, humans draw a line, such as one tola of gold for paper currency numbered 100, or one tola of gold in exchange for paper currency numbered in the tens of thousands. This too has been a programme based on belief and faith; no practical or meaningful basis is visible in it. Such a sequence went on expanding a mysterious situation. This itself has become the cause of artificial scarcities, because all objects are seen to be in the grip of paper currency. Its clear result is that wherever the most paper currencies are accumulated or created, there objects are found to be imprisoned. The organisation of persons able to bring about such events is called a trade association. In keeping with the profit mentality of such a trade association, imprisoning useful objects takes them out of the motion by which they become accessible to all; the imprisonment of objects amounts to their being cut off from their own motion. In such a mentality, the movement is found to be from profit, and after profit,

[p. 7]
<!-- source: _page-images/p017_print007.png -->

towards still more profit. Until now, no point of contentment for profit has been found anywhere, in any place or time. In this way, all trade experts are found to carry an endless process of profit, or a profit mentality. **From this, one more assessment follows: the producer of objects has always been seen to remain poor.**

The trade system based on profit is found to remain confined to objects and object value. Along with this, that is, with the trade association, families, associations, nations or communities free of enmity could not even come into being; as a result, a mindset resistant or opposed to community consciousness and societal consciousness took hold. Produced objects have excellence in quality and in aesthetic values (the shape and type of the produced object, its decoration and its method of protection), by which they come to be accepted in accordance with the natural activity in humans of finding something pleasing. Quality means the functionality of any object, in the sense of its usefulness. Humans naturally expect continuity of functionality. This is seen especially in all kinds of machines and in the areas of shelter. As for adornment objects and food objects, they appear the same from the earliest times until now. Thus the food system has been prosperous since before humans appeared on this earth; the human race certainly took time to recognise it. The human race has gone on adopting changes in adornment and shelter. Along with this, once all the machines were made, all this became caught in the snare of trade. Trade hangs in the noose of profit. Profit keeps sinking, through exploitation, into accumulation, indulgence, over-indulgence and multi-indulgence. In keeping with this, a distinct, recognisable community of arts, artistry and art experts has also come into being. The grandeur of realisation and authenticity is found to be the activity of understanding, and enabling others to understand, the evidence natural to justice, dharma and truth as eternally present in coexistence, and communication and expression-exposition in mutuality. As an example, someone asks me: is it necessary to review trust with regard to the human being? Is it also necessary to evidence it? In answer I say: yes, it is necessary. Then I am asked: how do you trust? For this I understand that:

2. Imaginativeness and freedom of action are present in the human being.

Imaginations have already been made clear earlier. Imagination is carried out in accordance with hope, thought and desires. In the four and a half activities — selection, tasting, analysis, deliberation and visualisation — that is, within the limit of deluded imaginativeness, and, among the five activities natural to awakening that are to be reviewed again and again, in the course of reviewing justice, trust has been seen to exist as a uniform value (*samya mulya*). One breathes in the environment precisely on account of trust, walks on the earth with trust, and drinks water. Forests and minerals are found to be trusted continually, on the basis of the essential nature of all these. In the second state, we all carry out work, behaviour and thought with all these only with trust. The method of trust of human with human, too, we carry out only on the basis of humane essential nature. Among the humane essential natures, trust comes to be reviewed in the sense of justice. Such a thing as trust does not come before the eyes and is not touched by the hands, but it is understood. All understanding remains glorious as contemplation, enlightenment, resolve, realisation and authenticity. Of these, contemplation, enlightenment and realisation are the dignity of jeevan, and are its state. Authenticity and resoluteness are its magnificence; they are its motion. By this method the conclusion for us about ourselves is this: complete awakening in understanding, that is, in knowing, believing, recognising and fulfilling, is itself the eternal source of our satisfaction. Fulfilling, that is, fulfilling in accordance with what has been understood, is itself the definition of honesty. Whatever is fulfilled without understanding turns out to be delusion.

In knowing and believing, all value is understood. At its satiation point, the acceptance of its completeness certainly occurs. With this very authority, that is, through realisation, it is evidenced as expressed in the sense of comprehensive resolution (*abhyudaya*). **The form of all values counts as jeevan values, human values, established values, civic values and object values.** Jeevan values are understood as happiness, peace, contentment and bliss. This can be evidenced only when the human being understands jeevan, which is the ten activities stated above. The form of happiness comes about in awakening, in the state where tasting and selection are balanced together with deliberation and analysis. This, every

Human values are understood to be in the form of fortitude, courage, generosity, kindness, grace and compassion. Fortitude is evidenced through the method of recognising relationships and fulfilling values, or we evidence fortitude. This is indicative of balance in mutual relationships. The name of the reality present uniformly in all relationships is itself trust. Trust as a reality is understood only as relationship balance. It was also mentioned earlier that the human being has become capable of maintaining relationship balance with the earth, water, air, forests, minerals, vegetation and living beings on the basis of their respective essential nature and dharma. In maintaining relationship balance between human and human, the need still awaits fulfilment. Therefore **the working system of the cyclical economic system becomes meaningful only after balance in human relationships. As an example, we can see that to the extent that one keeps one's relationships balanced — that is, with one individual and with many individuals as well — all of one's objects come to be used by a method free of profit and loss;** and wherever there is no relationship balance, there, through delusion, profit-obsession remains spread over everything. Every human being can observe this with the humans in one's surroundings, and it can be evidenced. We understand courage as the tendencies, activities and conduct able to make justice accessible to all. An example of this can also be seen: wherever two individuals are caught in a dispute, such a dispute has been seen to arise only from being deluded on some issue. At the root of such a dispute both are wrong, or one of the two is certainly wrong; only then, analysis shows, does dispute arise. In such a state the two do not remain recognising their relationships with each other, and therefore they become bent on disputing. After understanding the relationship, the capability to resolve the differences of opinion that have arisen between them certainly dawns. On the basis of this very principle, if a third person makes efforts to establish relationship balance in the mutuality of the two, the dispute goes away. Relationship with fellow humans does remain established; knowing, believing, recognising and fulfilling it is itself what awakening means. Only through awakening does the human being evidence

Of the human values stated above, observing fortitude, courage and generosity in oneself has been seen as a natural task and achievement. For this, a brief and clear glimpse of the working mechanism (*tantrana*) of jeevan has already been presented before humankind, in which the ten activities of jeevan have been made clear. Here the basic form of jeevan and the elements relating to its formation have been indicated in subtle form; its detailed study has been made in "The Development of the Atom in Existence" (*Astitva mein Parmanu ka Vikas*).

The basic form of jeevan is a sentient unit; the constitutionally complete atom is itself jeevan. The sentient atom has been understood as a constitutionally complete atom, because in existence the basis of all kinds of planes and orderliness is the atom alone. Existence is eternally present only in undivided and divided forms. The undivided form is itself Omnipotence (*satta*), filled with uniform energy. In such energy-filled Omnipotence, all divided existence (appearing as infinite units, one by one) is seen submerged, surrounded and soaked. The testimony of being soaked is energy-fullness; the testimony of being surrounded is regulation; and the testimony of being submerged is activity. Activity is clear as labour, motion and result. Formful and formless existence is eternally present inseparably. In formful existence, fundamentally, the basic form of orderliness has been seen as the atom. More than one atom, organising together, becomes clear as a molecule, and molecules, being formed together, as many bodies (*pind*). In this very course, physicochemical formation and decomposition are found to occur continually by a complementary method. In the course of excellence of this very chemical formation, the plant world, the various animal bodies and human body formations have been understood. This is known by the name of development progression. In this, it is the course of excellence in formation alone that is evaluated. In the course of development in the atom, all these kinds of chemical-physical expressions come to be accomplished. Some atoms, attaining all the necessary conditions, become endowed with constitutional completeness. These constitutionally complete atoms make the irreversible transition into the sentient plane. This itself has been addressed by the name "jeevan". Jeevan, on the basis of being free of weight-bondage and molecular bondage, itself forms the path of its work and motion, together with the whole formation of the atom. The basis of this is jeevan's
