### Angle (कोण)

In the context of angle: after recognizing some one unit in the form of a center point, it has become quite clear that very many degrees are contained in the point. However many degrees we can conceive of, more degrees than that remain joined in one point. Upon establishing, at such a joined definite point, the target line that joins the parallel line (the base line), angles are able to become clear. Upon joining every two degrees, an angle of definite degrees does get formed. In this way, in each one, from one up to infinite angles are contained. The recognition of degrees happens only on the basis of straight, concentric lines. Concentric line means a line joined to one point and spread out on all sides; this has been recognized by the name of the degree-dividing line. By this very method — concentric lines, a great many joined in one point — every one of us persons can make this figure. As the distance of such mutual lines from the center goes on increasing, so the mutual distance between the lines also goes on increasing. In this very sequence, the span of vision gets recognized. It becomes clear as well, accepted as well. Degree, angle, extension, distance — all these are facts within the reach of human sight. That is why, keeping human sight in the basis, the method of recognizing all facts is formed. In the same way, by a second method: after recognizing two poles upon some flat composition, and — on the basis of this same understanding — after recognizing three poles, upon joining poles with poles the recognition of angles occurs. Every angle is found to be a figure of some definite numerical degrees. In this way, both direction and angle become clear. Along with this, distance too becomes clear.

### Dimension (आयाम)

[p. 132]

We have always been using dimension-related language. Any one thing, while being bounded from six sides, we speak of only three dimensions. In this too, the human's sight alone is the principal basis. For the six sides of any thing to be reflected in the human's eyes, the need remains of seeing from at least three manners (sides). This itself is what we call three dimensions. The unit, in its form, is soaked, submerged, encircled in the pervasive entity. On the basis of the pervasive entity being transparent, in the human's span of vision the position and circumstance of a thing, and the reflection of things occurring in the eye, is being seen in the form of sight-points (*drig bindu*). The things reflected in the form of sight-points can be reflected upon the human eye up to 180 degrees of their complete shape and volume; more than that they are not able to be. All things reflected upon the eye — that only a partialness out of themselves comes to be in the form of the reflection — [every human] can [ascertain] this with testing and observation. Every human can experiment with this. For experiment, various things are placed right before the human. In this: upon one human standing before a human and looking — regarding length, breadth, thickness, and build, upon looking from a definite good distance — 180 degrees, that is, only half the portion of the human-formed thing, is visible. The remaining half portion remains hidden. In this way, what is seen with the eyes is incomplete. The decisions that are made only on the basis of seeing with the eyes are also incomplete. However many programmes are based upon this, it is natural for them too to be incomplete. The human, in his sequence of gaining satisfaction, does not accept incompleteness. On this issue it has already been clarified in a general form that mathematics is more than the eyes, less than understanding. That is, the awareness that comes from mathematical language is more than from the eyes, while the matter of awareness occurring on the basis of the eyes also comes into understanding, or the matter of being depicted comes in. A picture too remains a thinking-and-considering formed on the basis of the gaze; it too remains incomplete. In a picture, only 180 degrees of shape and volume is able to be seen. In the gaze too, only 180 degrees is able to be contained. Whereas the awareness that comes from mathematical language conveys the complete awareness of shape and volume. It comes into understanding that there is simply no reflection of the solid [volume] in the eyes. Beyond this, it has also been clarified that whatever comes into our understanding by the mathematical method, more than that comes into understanding in qualitative and causal language. Because the completeness of every thing is the aggregate of form, quality, innate-nature, and dharma. The interpretation related to form, quality, innate-nature, and dharma has been made before. In, from, and for the human, the awareness of every thing, only together with form, quality, innate-nature, and dharma, is able to become the cause of complete awareness and satisfaction. From any part of these remaining hidden, satisfaction for the human is not possible. A decision taken on the basis of complete understanding,

[p. 133]

and work-behaviour, will be resolution-producing. This matter comes into understanding. The human does not accept problem. He accepts resolution. Resolution emanates together with decisions taken on the basis of completeness in every direction, angle, and dimension. Every human can examine this quite well.

In the aforesaid way, however many experiments and decisions humankind has made remaining dependent on the basis of the eyes, all that, in result, has become the form of countless problems. Instrument evidence cannot be more than the eyes. This itself is the biggest evidence. The endeavour of science — using the eye, making tools for seeing in an even more subtlest form than the eyes — has come into practice. That is, the powerful lens, showing magnified hundreds of thousands and tens of millions of times; for showing billions and trillions of times, the electronic lens too has come into use. Even after all these, what happens in the eye — make the eye billions of times more effective, only that much meaning comes out. That is why: recognizing the whole from the coexistence-ist outlook, by the combined method of knowledge, science, and discretion; being able to communicate it in causal, qualitative, and mathematical language; being able to have awareness of it; and, as a result, attaining the goal in the form of human-aspiration and jeevan-aspiration — this alone is the sole necessity. For weighing upon this, direction and sight are very necessary activity. Let us understand direction in this way — by geographical methods we know direction anyway; by the astronomical method too we know direction. Now, along with this, two more parts which are hidden — the direction of development, and direction for awakening — need to be recognized. Direction for development [means]: by the method of complementarity and usefulness with the chemical, physical, and animal order, [the human] deciding his own work-behaviour — that is, the human deciding his own work-behaviour and bringing it into conduct, and attaining the fruit and result — is the meaning of the direction of development. The import of the awakening direction too is just this: to evidence, with understanding, honesty, responsibility, and participation, harmony (*sangeet*) and concord (*samarasyata*) in the human; to evidence jeevan-aspiration and human-aspiration. On both these issues the human needs to succeed. For this, the coexistence-ist outlook alone is the perpetual resolution.

From the viewpoint of human contentment: only after taking to heart with clarity the dimensions in the form of form, quality, innate-nature, and dharma does every human accomplish resolution-oriented decision in the context of development and awakening, and, as a result, become able to be meaningful in the direction of awakening and development. This itself is the summary.

## 3.15 Time (काल)

[p. 134]

The definition of time in the form of 'the duration of activity' is clear. Activity, in itself, does keep being accomplished continuously. Along with it, some activities keep recurring. The continuity of such recurrence remains formed. It never stops at all. On the basis of one recurrence of such continuously accomplished activity, the basis for recognizing time was accepted. In one rotational motion of the earth, day and night are found to be accomplished over the whole earth. This itself has been given the designation of one day. Such day-and-night activity has come on from ever and ever, and will keep going on. Dividing this duration by 24 parts, by 60 parts, we recognize it by names such as hour, minute, *ghati*, *pal*, and so on. In this manner we went on dividing time, and forgot the activity. We made ourselves ready to evaluate every activity on the basis of time. Time became primary; the thing was forgotten. From all the decisions taken on the basis of this mistake, problems arose. The need remained for evaluation to occur on the basis of activity and its fruition.

It is clear that time-reckoning is in the form of the duration of activity. Activity, in its form, is continuous, perpetual. Time-reckoning is in the meaning of being, of continuing to be. Coexistence, in itself, is in the meaning of being eternally continuous. Eternal continuity being is clear. It is this continuity that comes under the designation of time, and perpetuity too comes under the designation of time. On the basis of the whole of nature being perpetual, on the basis of its being imperishable, perpetuity is evidenced. Because the entire material order is present in the form of existence dharma; this very material order, having developed, on the basis of flourishing into the biological order, has come into recognition as being of growth dharma together with existence. In all animals, or in the animal order, being of hope dharma together with existence and growth has come into understanding. In this very sequence, it is also clear that the human is of happiness dharma together with existence, growth, and hope. In coexistence, realisation = resolution = happiness; that happiness is only from realisation — this matter too has become quite clear. By this method it comes into understanding that existence is imperishable. That is why time is only the ever-present. In the form of the present thing there is coexistence alone. It is the present that we have given the name time. In the word time (*kaal*), the meaning is the present alone. In the form of the present there is coexistence alone. In this way it comes into understanding that time, in the meaning of coexistence, is the ever-present.

Those who recognize time in the meaning of past and future — they too recognize it only on the basis of the duration of activity. Because every cyclicality happened before as well, is happening even now, and will keep happening ahead as well. By this method, on the basis of the event,

[p. 135]

we go on fashioning past, future, and present. On the basis of being imaginative and free in action, the human, dividing the duration of a natural activity into many parts, arrives at the place of time-zero. This one small event is extremely easy for the human. Keep dividing one hour by 60; divide 1 out of that again; after doing this several times, upon being asked how much remains, they say: equal to nothing, or nothing. But this very thing became the basis of the human's going astray. That is why, in the age of science, recognizing time became most complicated. It is not possible — so it seems.

If the present becomes zero, where is human conduct to be recognized, where is behaviour to be recognized, where is the place for recognizing human knowledge, science, and discretion? That is why the human, beset with problems, began to bring problems about. He regarded this as his bravery; in this very thing, human tradition has sunk quite far. The way of escape ahead is this alone — coexistence as the ever-present, the eternal manifestation of development and awakening, and, on the basis of recognizing that the human alone is the one who evidences development and awakening — only on this basis will human-aspiration and jeevan-aspiration be able to be fulfilled.
