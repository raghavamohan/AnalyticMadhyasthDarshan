## 3.9 Quantity (मात्रा)

[p. 88]

Whatever substance exists in the form of a single unit — what is meant by substance is precisely the expression of actuality. Actuality, in every substance, is orderliness-with-inherence (*tva*). Actualities remain present, mutually with one another, in an indivisible form all the way from form, property, disposition, to dharma. That is, form, property, disposition, and dharma are found expressed in an indivisible form together with quantity. At the root of being expressed is the expression, communication, and manifestation of coexistence. It has already become clear that, in coexistence, every single unit is found submerged, saturated, and enclosed within the pervasive substance. It has also become clear that the pervasive substance is permeative and transparent throughout each and every single unit. It is precisely as a result of remaining saturated within the pervasive that each single unit is found to be endowed with energy and endowed with magnetic force.

Each and every atom alone is the root quantity. Such numerous atoms, in the form of molecules; numerous molecules, in the form of molecular composition; such compositions, in the form of matter and the prana-state — this too has already been made clear before. In every species, atoms constituted from two particles up to many particles have made their own respective forms of conduct clear.

That the atom is the root quantity has become clear for this reason: that the unit which comes to be defined is one that is, in itself, an orderliness, and participates in the overall orderliness. Even in units that are not themselves in orderliness, a tendency for participation in orderliness is nonetheless found to occur — as with an atomic particle, or a deluded human being. An atomic particle, in itself, is not able to evidence orderliness — that is, it is not able to express a determinate necessity — and therefore every atomic particle is propensive in the sense of participating in an atom and, as a result, evidencing orderliness. The evidence of this is that every atomic particle does become absorbed into one atom or another; or, more than one atomic particle, recognising one another, come to function in the form of an atom, and as a result determinate conduct alone comes to be known as orderliness. From this it becomes known that atomic particles recognise one another and carry each other out; determinate conduct in the atom is clear.

Just as an atomic particle does not exist in orderliness without recognising other particles, so too the human being cannot live in orderliness at all without recognising other human beings. There is one and only one thread for recognising the human being — human-ness. Human-ness is nothing but human consciousness, by which all humankind can be recognised in the form of the human being. Human-ness, in its own nature, is nothing but the nature of the definition of the human being. In the sense of mental well-being

[p. 89]

itself, it is by the coexistence-ist method, even through the discipline of science, that the thread-form of mental well-being comes to be analysed, determined, and elaborated. Such determination is necessary only in the sense of the human goal. In this same sequence we have already understood, by the coexistence-ist method, that it is indeed necessary for the human being to recognise his own necessity in a firm form. The certainty of this necessity — that is, the certainty of humane necessity — is found to occur in the form of evidencing human-ness as resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, and coexistence. This means precisely that we human beings are able to make humane conduct clear only in the process of evidencing the human goal. The determination of human necessity is indicative of knowing-believing. Knowing-believing alone is the conduct of being evidenced; this itself is the thread and the explanation.

It is natural that the principle of quantity too, at its root, remains animated by these very meanings. Therefore, as more than one particle gathers together and functions — that is, conducts itself — the many species of atoms remain recognisable on the basis of their conduct. The human being recognises this; this becomes the grand, dignified condition of the human being's own natural fitness. At the root of this remains simply the tinge of knowing and believing. In the same way, in the compositions constituted from prana-cells too, the composition of the prana-cells remains determinate. The evidence of this is precisely the composition of the plants of many species — in all of these, remaining animated from seed to tree, and from tree to seed — that is, impulses from one seed to the tree, and impulses from the tree to the seed, remain contained in that very seed-tree. In these two kinds of impulse together, the necessity of the trees of that species has been made clear. In this way we human beings recognise any substance in a firm form; the basis of this remains precisely firm necessity. In the same way, the human being has become assured about the conduct of the ant up to the elephant, of the dog up to the cat, and of the cow, the tiger, the hen, the insect, and so on. Along with these, the human being has also adopted the necessary method. This necessary method appears, by way of indirect statement, to have been accomplished all the way to the world other than the human. When, in that same sequence, human beings come to the position of recognising other human beings, we then feel doubtfulness — whether we are managing to recognise them or not. For this doubtfulness, disputes remain formed in human tradition by methods relating to caste, ideology, sect, position, and money. As a result, treachery, rebellion, exploitation, and war are found effective. What is meant by being effective is that these accidents keep on occurring.

Even if the human being were to consider himself as mere quantity, would the human being's necessity thereby become clear? Even on thinking of oneself as mere quantity, it still seems that we human beings have not been able to determine our own necessity, and it is for this very reason that necessity has remained uncertain,

[p. 90]

and even on the basis of necessity we have not been able to recognise the universality of orderliness, its poles. One pole of human-ness is precisely the human being's own necessity, which comes to be evidenced in behaviour, in the work of production, as participation in orderliness with resolution.

Human necessity is, in itself, entirely clear: resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, coexistence. The evidence of this is that human conduct in the form of value, character, and ethics becomes successful. This has been seen tested by experiment. This does not come to be evidenced by the idealist or the materialist method. Therefore it is precisely this — that is, by the coexistence-ist method — that the human goal and conduct become easy to study. To succeed at this universally, or to succeed and be so in all humankind, is itself the glory of awakened human tradition. In the form of evidencing value, character, and ethics, the human being does keep on doing something or other from within himself. Why should it not be thought that, in the sense of human necessity, all this be done? Any individual can make such a resolve. It is also to be seen that the necessity for such a resolve to occur is felt after a certain age. Most human beings, most predominantly from the age of 12–14 to around 20 years, retain, in the form of the most predominant mentality, the expectation that there be justice in society, orderliness favourable to all, and happiness for all. But, by getting attached to some faction or other, this mentality is gradually killed off — that is, these expectations get stunted. After this, what remains is that whichever sect or class-mentality people remain committed to, or continue to hold — that alone we take to be correct, take to be proper. As a result, we get weighed down into matters of treachery, rebellion, exploitation, and war in mutuality.

That all humankind, at some age, should wish well for the greatest number of people — that is, that all humankind should wish for universal wellbeing — for the confirmation of this, it is coexistence-ist study alone that has made this possibility accessible. At present, one and the same human being, up to a certain age, wishes for the happiness of all or of most people, and after a somewhat greater age is seen weighed down toward gathering convenience for his own family and himself. To establish balance between these, it is necessary, according to Madhyasth Darshan (coexistence-ism), to make available the education of value that is consciousness-development — that is, it is necessary to universalise coexistence-ist education. To bring all four states to a balanced point, the coexistence-ist method alone will have to be adopted.

[p. 91]

All substances, in their subtle form, in the form of the atom, are the root quantity. As composition, they are available to us in the form of large and small units, and available in the form of minerals. Together with these attainments, the thread of our future remains bound. Whatever conduct we have carried out today, whatever work-behaviour we have carried out, whatever conduct in disorderliness we have carried out — the result and consequence of this, even if not today, will have to be undergone by us even after years. From this endeavour it also becomes understood that, in the present, we have to be understanding for ever and ever. To remain understanding, it is necessary also to remain diligent in order to keep the tradition of understanding intact. This itself is what is meant by the human being's state-motion.

State-motion, in every quantity, is a natural activity. It is only in the combined form of state and motion that every activity is recognised. The conduct of a substance is recognised; in motion, conduct remains as fruition in state. This itself is the form of the actual condition. The thread and the explanation of the actual condition remain contained in this very thing. For example, hardness in iron is a physical necessity. It is only in its state-motion that the evidence of this comes about. To make use of iron is precisely what is meant by its capacity for motion. It is only when the human being, bringing his own power and force to bear, tests iron, that trust in iron comes about. In this the human being has already succeeded considerably.

Testing and inspection have already been carried out with a great many substances. Certainty has been attained, and on the basis of this certainty, many technologies are seen to remain functional. It is out of the desire to participate in some one or other of these technologies that we ourselves undertake technical education. Meaningfulness is also seen in this. The evidence of this is that many people do participate in technology. Even while many people succeed in the discipline of technology, being evidenced in mental well-being has still not come about. In order to be evidenced in the discipline of mental well-being, it is recognising the human being that becomes the primary matter. Without recognising the human being, the evidence of mental well-being does not occur. Recognising the human being comes about only through living with resolution and prosperity, and through evidencing justice. Justice, in itself, is found to become clear in the forms of relational value, evaluation, and two-sided satiation.

Without oneself carrying out humane conduct, recognising humane conduct in another human being simply does not come about. This can be tested in the following way. One person carries out humane conduct, and another also does so; if, in the mutuality of these two, work-behaviour is assessed, and experimental research is also assessed, then of humane conduct

[p. 92]

evaluation comes to be found in mutuality; it becomes clear that evaluation occurs only in mutuality. In this same sequence, further: one person carries out humane conduct, another does not; in that situation, the one who does has trust in himself, while the one who does not has no trust in himself. The third situation is just this: neither of the two carries out humane conduct; then, in the discourse of vices, the matter arises of agreeing on vices; in criminal acts, in criminal thoughts, a situation of partly agreeing and partly disagreeing arises; no conclusion emerges from examining things in this way.

From the above happenings we come to know that every awakened human being keeps his own identity intact only through conduct in the form of orderliness within himself. This itself is the ascertainment of the actual condition. We have already carried out this study by the method of the ascertainment of actual conditions. The ascertainment of the material state, and the methods for the ascertainment of the prana state and the jeevan state, have already become trustworthy. Up to now the human being has trusted in these three states, and, on that very basis, has also attained success in his own manner. What is meant by his own manner is that he has attained success in the sense of the convenience of accumulation — even though not everyone has obtained the convenience of accumulation. In this way, it is only in the process of we human beings evidencing our own necessity in a firm form, by the method of maturity, that there is the possibility of reaching undivided society. It is only after the thread of undivided society becomes meaningful that universal orderliness comes to be explained. This alone will be the meaningful, effective form of wisdom and science. In this sequence, the matter for our attention is the study of the human being, human-ness, humane conduct, undivided society, and the thread and explanation of universal orderliness. In these studies, one principal matter is this: what is the purpose of every human being? The purpose of every human being is just this: firmness in conduct. After this, the purpose is evidence of conduct within orderliness. After this, the final height of purpose is participation in universal orderliness.

At present, while recognising everyone else, the human being is most backward in the discipline of recognising the human being. This deficiency can be made up through knowledge-, wisdom-, and science-replete work-behaviour. This means that, in the units of the three states, participation, together with their own inherence, in orderliness and in the overall orderliness, has already come to be evidenced. It is precisely as an outcome of this that the human being has succeeded in giving concrete form to ideas. Iron's remaining unfailing in its own orderliness; soil, stone, gem, metal — all remain unfailing, each in its own conduct. On this very basis the human being has been able to enrich, together with these, the technological system relating to his social ambitions, or production. If all these were to become mutually quarrelsome, given to disagreement, opposition, and rebellion, like the human being, what would happen then? If, in the same way,

[p. 93]

the rest of nature too were not to maintain stability and certainty in its conduct, what would happen? No human being would have the possibility of giving concrete form to ideas. It became a meaningful form for the human being that the three states, by the sequential method, each remain manifest in the form of determinate conduct according to their own actual condition. But the human being, in order to be meaningful while living together with the rest of nature, has simply not managed to evidence mental well-being. Without evidencing this, it is not at all possible for the human being to be complementary with all of non-human nature. Not only this — without the human being being complementary with the human being, being complementary with the rest of nature does not come about at all.

From the coexistence-ist viewpoint, the activity, process, and meaningfulness of science become assured, because, by the coexistence-ist method, the purpose of science is to determine the direction for the human goal to become meaningful. Since the human goal is resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, and coexistence, it is precisely the determining of the direction, and of the methods of teaching, for this to become meaningful in all humankind that is the purpose of science and wisdom.

Science has its importance by way of this method of determining such a goal. By this method both the viewpoint and the work of wisdom and science became clear. This also makes clear the intent that, in favour of human conduct becoming determinate —

- science should become meaningful in the sense of wisdom;
- wisdom and science should become meaningful in the sense of knowledge;
- knowledge, science, and wisdom should become meaningful in the sense of coexistence;
- knowledge should come to be in the sense of coexistence.

This itself is the meaning of realisation-based coexistence-ist knowledge, science, and wisdom.

Quantity, in its root form, is recognised as a unit that is itself an orderliness, and that participates in orderliness. Such a unit begins in the form of the constitution of more than one particle, whose shape (consequence) is determinate. On the side of the determinacy of shape (consequence), a great many examples have already been presented. Now here it is necessary to become clear about form, property, disposition, and dharma. Every form is clear in the forms of shape, volume, and bulk. What is meant by shape is however it remains constituted, together with however much

[p. 94]

space it remains spread out over, and functions — that is, remains in state. As one might think about a tradition, into however much space it remains active. In the same way, further, the compositions constructed from numerous molecules, whether very large or small — the shape they remain formed in, however much space they occupy — it is on this basis that the recognition of form comes about. Of this, what shape is, how the shape is, is a matter of construction; the meaning of volume is enclosure (spread). What is meant by enclosure is remaining bounded on every side within the pervasive substance; by another method it is also in terms of length, breadth, and height. By these methods we are able to recognise shape; every such shape, or unit, remains reflected on every side. The unit remains reflected in the units situated on every side of it, which are illumined by it. In this way the method of mutual recognition remains formed. There is no need to construct the method of recognition. By this method, every substance remains reflected upon every other substance. Every substance is luminous.

On thinking by the above method, this question can arise: is light not needed in order to recognise one another? — as sunlight is available upon the earth. The answer to this is just this: whatever light we human beings recognise at present, it is only on the basis of recognising light through the eyes. Whereas every substance is indeed luminous. The reason for this is just this: in every atom, activity in the form of effort, motion, and consequence remains present. In the fruition of motion, sound, heat, electricity, and wave remain manifest. At the root of activity, being endowed with energy and being endowed with magnetic force remain self-established. The glory of the unit endowed with these four kinds of force keeps itself illumined in the forms of existing-ness and luminousness — that is, they remain illumined. From this we come to understand that every substance is luminous. The testimony of luminousness has already become entirely clear as being, in the form of reflection, present on every side of the unit. That reflection alone is the basis for recognising one another — this fact has already become understood. For this: place any substance in any one room; if we sit down naturally on every side, that substance does indeed come within the sight of all of us. By another method: our eyes, according to the construction of the visual organ, cannot see anything in a dark room without more light. There is no motion at all in the reflection of substances placed in the room. This is understood by this method: owls, which are active by night, and animals with similar eyes, do indeed recognise the substances they need. Just as owls and bats do recognise fruits, and the deer recognises grass. Whereas the human being does not recognise. Merely from the human being's not recognising, the substance does not itself

[p. 95]

become an error in presenting itself, nor is it the case that there is no one else who recognises it either. It can be considered that the elephant, the ant, and the owl too do see it. By the method of investigation the conclusion emerges that each of their eyes remain formed according to their own respective necessities. In the same way the basis of the five sensations remains formed, because the arrangement for recognising something through the eye, something through the nose, something through the tongue, something through the skin, something through the ear, is found formed in the living human being. It can be thought that a similar arrangement will surely be present in all living creatures. Just as the frog's skin remains formed as it is, the feathers of many birds remain formed in different ways, and the cow's hide remains formed of its own kind — in all of these, tactility remains formed differently according to each one's respective necessity. In this way it remained natural for the tactility of the human body to remain distinct. We can accept, from bodily knowledge, that the composition of the human body can express all five kinds of sensation with excellence, and can communicate tendencies. This good fortune makes clear the sense of equality present in every kind of human body. By way of indirect statement, sensation is found to be present in all living beings. In this way it became clear what activity occurs, together with quantity, in the jeevan state and the knowledge state. Every composition is one quantity; in these quantities, diversity of form is a matter for study in the forms of shape and volume; diversity of property in the forms of motion, work, and tendency; diversity of disposition in being different across the four states; diversity of dharma in being distinct across all four states. This matter has already been made clear before as well. The material state is endowed with the dharma of existence, whose destruction is not possible. The prana state is endowed, together with existence, with the dharma of growth (*pushti*) — the evidence of this is the tradition of one seed transforming into many. It is by the logic of seed-and-tree alone that the whole of the prana state exists. The jeevan state, together with existence and growth, is endowed with the dharma of hope. Every animal wants to live; to live means to express sensations. There is nothing more than this. In the knowledge state, the human being, together with existence and growth, is endowed with the dharma of happiness — this too has been made clear along with this. Resolution alone is the evidential form of happiness. All-round resolution is what the human being expresses in the process of evidencing his own understanding. This expression, communicated, reaches, in, from, and for every human being, unto study, comprehension, realisation, and evidence, and comes to be accepted. From this it also becomes known that the human being, in coexistence, expresses all-round resolution only by the realisation-based method, from which it is natural for the human family to be benefited. What is meant by the benefit of the human family is being within human awakened tradition, human universal orderliness, human undivided society, and, at the very least, human family orderliness. In family orderliness too, the human being, for the human being, or for the family,

[p. 96]

is also motivational, and by this method resolution remains bound up with benefit. Or, by the method of benefit, resolution remains bound up. In this way, in the human being, it is precisely understanding that is meant by seeing; understanding alone is seeing.

If human quantity — the human being — is taken to be one quantity, what the purpose of this is becomes understood in and of itself, in that situation. In the same way, animals too, on the basis of quantity, are found studied as being complementary to the human being, in the same sense as their being complementary to the plant order and the earth. It is found that, through the excreta and through the respiration of animals, complementariness to the earth and to the prana-state occurs, because, in this, fertility in the earth naturally increases. The animal order is understood to remove many diseases of the plant order. The evidence of this is that in forests where there are no animals, many diseases are found to occur among the trees and plants; in forests where many animals live, disease and affliction are found to be minimal. Any person can test this. In this same sequence, in the world of crops too, weeds, insects, and the coming-and-going of birds — especially the combination of honeybees — are found to be beneficial to all crops. Wherever there is a lack of all these, the crop diminishes, and there the insects that destroy crops increase, and new diseases keep getting prepared every year. For this very reason, however the earth is adorned, there is need to pay attention to it. This adornment has come about, in itself, only by the method of complementariness and usefulness; the evidence of this is that the prana state manifested only after the material state. Whatever species are contained within the prana state, all of them are found to be complementary to, and useful for, the material state. It has remained natural that the manifestation of the prana state occurred only after the material state, with all its various species of atoms, became accomplished on this earth. From this it becomes known that the material state is complementary to the prana state, and the prana state is complementary to the material state. The prana state is useful in this manner: that when the prana state comes to be constituted, fertility begins to increase in the earth. As fertility increases in the earth, so too does the advancement of the prana state proceed on this earth. What is meant by advancement is becoming advanced. Its form is such that, as fertility went on increasing, excellence in all the shrubs and plants also went on increasing — which we can also call qualitative change (*gunatmak parivartan*) or development. The prana state is useful for the animal order, and the jeevan state is found to be complementary to the prana state. The usefulness of the prana state for the jeevan state is seen in this way: that it is in the prana state itself that the composition of the male cell and the female cell has already begun. In the jeevan state, the composition of the female and male body became clear as mutually distinct forms. What this means is that, in the prana state, cell-compositions, to whatever extent

[p. 97]

advanced, was not sufficient for the jeevan state, and there remained a need for further development, whatever the developmental sequence might be. From this the knowledge becomes clear that, in the sequence of the body-composition of animals, the distinctly clear form of male-female body-composition manifested itself. Food for animals has come to be accomplished from the prana state — in this manner complementariness and usefulness are entirely clear. In this way the material state, the prana state, and the jeevan state have proven to be mutually complementary and useful. It remained natural that the very first use of the prana state, in the body-composition of one or another animal tradition, emerged in the form of a festival in the prana-cells only as a further composition-method after the satiation of the prana-cells. On this very basis, in the bodies of some animals, the incorporation of the seven metals (elements) and the most enriched medhas-composition came to be accomplished. It was in such a body alone that the reproductive system, occurring through the conjunction of prana-cells, and the composition of the human body, remained a natural system. It is also clear that such body-composition has the possibility of being accomplished from various animal bodies in different environments — that is, in different geographical environments. The evidence of this is the existence, on this earth, of the body-composition of humans of many races. From this too it can be accepted that the fixed point (*dhruv bindu*) of one's own race is the accomplishment of the human body from a basic animal body. It is natural for some colour or other to accompany every race. In this way we human beings can make conjectures regarding the beginning of the human lineage. In this tradition, through which thousands, crores of generations have passed, it was indeed a necessity to find consolation by tracing root relations together with animals — for the sake of gaining recognition as a link in the developmental sequence. In this sequence, after the human being, in every geographical circumstance, descended with various kinds of racial colour, humans achieved success in the endeavour of securing their own tradition. Because the number of humans on this earth has already increased considerably, it is also to be observed that other animal traditions too remain secure in their own colour and form. On the basis of having the most developed body-composition, it is clear that the human being is the most useful, or entirely useful, for jeevan. It is precisely in the process of being evidenced that the human being is indeed desirous of giving a sharpened form to cognisance, or to cognisance-ness. It is by this very method that the human being's study coming to be evidenced in the forms of knowledge, wisdom, and science is an inevitable event. From this coexistence-ist viewpoint, being evidenced in knowledge-fullness, wisdom- and science-fullness, and, together with human-ness, in orderliness and in the overall orderliness, remained both necessary and inevitable. In this same sequence, from the coexistence-ist viewpoint, it became possible for all the matters that the human being already wished to know, that had remained unknown, to be explained.

By the above method it became clear that, of the whole of quantities found in the four states in existence, each single quantity is itself an orderliness together with its inherence, and is a participation in the overall orderliness. This study has been presented for the human being to be meaningful precisely in the sense of participation, together with human-ness, in orderliness and in the overall orderliness.

In the discipline of quantity, the study of form has already become clear for all four states, in relation to form, property, disposition, and dharma. Now it is necessary to become clear about property and disposition. Disposition is seen to be different across the four states. In the material state, disposition is seen in the form of the activity of combination-decomposition, or combination with one thing and decomposition from another. In the prana state, disposition is found in the forms of essentiality (*sarakta*) and destructiveness (*marakta*). Essentiality is in the sense of being abundant in oneself, and, secondly, in the sense of being useful for the animal order. Destructiveness means the activity of creating obstruction in making one's own seed-arrangement abundant, and the condition of being disease- and death-causing for the animal order. In the animal order, disposition has been recognised in the forms of the cruel and the non-cruel. Non-cruel means not killing any other living beings, not eating flesh, and continuing to live on plant foods. In this way both the cruel and the non-cruel become clear. In another language these are known as the carnivorous and the herbivorous; in a third language they can also be called the violent and the non-violent. The disposition of the knowledge state is found to be fortitude, valour, generosity, compassion, grace, and mercy. These themselves become clear as the human disposition. Human-opposing disposition is reckoned in the forms of baseness, wretchedness, and cruelty, together with covetousness of others' wealth, others' wife/husband, and inflicting pain on others. Those who remain in these tendencies are reckoned among the animalistic human and the demonic human. Both of these are found to be human-opposing. There remains the possibility that those with human-opposing disposition may gradually be transformed into humane disposition. It is on this very basis that meaningful education is proposed — in other language, coexistence-ist education is proposed.

What is meant by fortitude is remaining entirely clear about, and evidenced in respect of, human justice. Study and tradition alone are the sole basis for becoming entirely clear. Tradition is the bearer and carrier of the work of study; study keeps on occurring in the present. What is meant by valour is precisely this: remaining committed in faith and in behaviour to justice oneself, and, while remaining evidenced, dedicating and surrendering one's own body, mind, and wealth so that justice becomes accessible to people. This too is found to become meaningful only by the method of education, and by the method of education for the populace. Generosity's

[p. 99]

meaning is precisely making use, by the method of usefulness, well-usefulness, and purposefulness, of the wealth that is body, mind, and money obtained from within oneself. What is meant by the method of usefulness is deploying it in bodily nourishment, protection, and the movement of society. What is meant by well-usefulness is dedicating and surrendering objects while participating in undivided society. What is meant by purposefulness is participating in universal orderliness, that too by the self-motivated, self-governing method.

What is meant by compassion is the activity of making substances available in accordance with worthiness (*patrata*). What is meant by grace is making worthiness available according to the substance available. This is found to occur in a situation of the absence of worthiness commensurate with the substance. What is meant by mercy is the activity of establishing both of these when neither substance nor worthiness is present. It is found that the human being, through compassion, grace, and mercy, comes to be made glorious in the forms of the human, the god-human, and the divine human. Such glory is found to be extremely useful for the country, the earth, and the human being.

The principle of quantity, in summary form, comes to be understood in this way: the substance existing in the form of each single unit is a unit-quantity. In the unit, form, property, disposition, and dharma remain present in an indivisible form. Each and every atom alone is the root quantity. That the root quantity is the atom has become clear for this reason: that the unit which comes to be defined is one that is itself an orderliness and participates in the overall orderliness. Where a unit exists but does not participate in orderliness, even in such units a tendency for participation in orderliness is nonetheless found — as with an atomic particle, or a deluded human being. A particle, in itself, is not able to evidence orderliness — that is, it is not able to express a determinate necessity — and therefore every atomic particle is propensive in the sense of participating in an atom and, as a result, evidencing orderliness. It is precisely determinate conduct that comes to be known as orderliness. From this it becomes known that atomic particles recognise one another and carry each other out. The human being's recognising the human being is, even now, a necessity. There is one and only one thread for recognising the human being — human-ness; it is necessary only in the sense of the human goal. The human goal is resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, coexistence. It is only in the process of evidencing the human goal that we are able to make humane conduct clear. The evidence of this is in the forms of value, character, and ethics. To succeed at this universally, or in all humankind — or for it to be so — is the glory of awakened human tradition. On this very basis, every human being becomes, in himself, an orderliness together with human-ness, and comes to participate in the overall

## 3.10 Property, Disposition, Dharma (गुण, स्वभाव, धर्म)

Property, in its own nature, is generative, degenerative, and mediating activity and tendencies. It is assessed on the basis of the result and consequence of these. Creative, combinative activity is indicated by the name generative tendency. Decomposition contrary to the work of manifestation, and destructive activity and tendency, is called degenerative. The tendency and activities carried out in maintaining whatever compositions are formed by whatever method of union-combination has been given the name mediating. Every activity does indeed manifest itself in the form of state and motion. It is natural for tendencies to be present at the root of every activity. Every propensity, in every unit, is dynamic and manifest on the basis of mutual recognition. This can be recognised in every state, in every category, in every actual condition. In every unit there is a tendency to recognise. On the basis of recognising and carrying out, propensity, development progression, development, the awakening sequence, and, on the basis of knowing, the occurrence of awakening, are found to come about. On this very basis, every unit's determinate necessity becomes clear in the form of inherence (*tva*). It is natural for necessity to become clear on the basis of propensity alone. In this way necessary propensity, necessary recognition — in this sequence mutual recognition occurs. Recognition occurs only in the sequence of orderliness together with inherence. In this sequence of mutual recognition of one another, becoming free from mystery is found to occur. Regarding these units, nothing remains by the name of the unknown. In the time of study, doubt can arise on this issue: to what extent should each and every single thing be tested? For example, how long should we keep testing the sea's water drop by drop?

How long should we keep testing atom after atom? In the same way, how long should we keep testing unit after unit of the prana state, the jeevan state, the knowledge state? Will one ever get to the far shore by this method? In answer to this, these facts have already been understood in this way: that every actual condition, state, and category is linked one to another like a staircase. Just as, in the material state's developmental sequence, passing through many actual conditions, elaborating form, property, disposition, and dharma, orderliness has come to be accomplished for physical substances from the very smallest to the very largest. Namely: shape, volume, and bulk, for form; generative, degenerative, and mediating, for property; combination-decomposition,

[p. 101]

for disposition; and existence, for dharma — we have already understood these. From this it becomes known that merely from testing one drop of the ocean we come to understand the water of the entire ocean. It is in this very way that the method of study is able to become meaningful.

By the above method, the study of the prana state too has already been understood: namely, shape, volume, and bulk, for form; generative, degenerative, and mediating, for property; essential and destructive, for disposition; and, together with existence, growth, for dharma. Beyond this, in the jeevan state, this has been evidenced in some animals as jeevan and body being in a combined form. In the elaboration of this: the presence of jeevan is evidenced in those animals endowed with a body composed of the seven metals (elements), an enriched medhas-system, and the capacity to receive the human being's directive signals. In such animals, jeevan too has shape, volume, and bulk; the body too has these — however exceedingly subtle jeevan may be. In jeevan too, the generative, degenerative, and mediating property remains at work. In the human being, jeevan predominates. For this very reason, the body's generative, degenerative, and mediating property becomes secondary. In animals, disposition and dharma are in a combined form. Cruelty and non-cruelty come to be made clear in the combined form of body and jeevan. The hope of living, too — dharma — becomes clear in combined form. The whole of the animal order is explained by the hereditary method. In the same way, further, the elaboration of the human being's generative, degenerative, and mediating properties too becomes possible. In the human being too, property is most predominantly on the side of the mediating, which is expressed in the form of one's own glory. So long as the human being remains deluded, he regards the being of the self as paramount, and regards others as secondary. It is precisely the manifestation of this very tendency that has, from the distant past, become clear in the forms of treachery, rebellion, exploitation, and war. In this too, it is only the acceptance of the glory of the self that remains. The glory of the self is held to be good. Only with awakening does one come to understand the fact of the glory of the self existing together with the good of all; through delusion one remains unaware of this. When faith comes to be placed in this, one becomes awakened, and then one becomes free from degenerative properties. So long as one is not free from degenerative properties, frustration, despair, and torment remain in effect. This itself is what is called suffering in human tradition. The awakened human being's property is found to be, for ever and ever, mediating. Its universal form is: keeping awakened tradition intact, keeping universal orderliness intact, and, through study, keeping the thread of undivided society evidenced. This becomes manifest in the form of keeping justice evidenced within the family. In studying all these facts, an entirely clear outlook of goodness, resolution, and truth becomes evidenced for the whole of humankind. This alone is what is necessary. It is by this very traditional method that the whole of study becomes intelligible. It is through humane properties alone that the human being's fundamental distinctiveness comes to be mutually understood. In the same way, the units present in every state, in every category, keep on being evidenced in expressed form together with their respective property, disposition, and dharma-form

[p. 102]

itself. Among these, in every state and category other than the human being, every unit is indeed itself an orderliness together with its inherence. The human being too, being a unit, has need to become awakened up to the point where control in sensitivity is evidenced through cognisance. What is meant by the human being is precisely this: knowing, believing, recognising, and carrying out the material-conscious (*jad-chaitanya*) nature situated in the whole of the categories and states within coexistence. By this method, wholeness and universality in the human being, communality in animals, genus-ness and species-ness in plants, and, in the material state, numerous actual conditions according to the numerical differences of particles in the developmental sequence, are found to be evidenced. Even while there is diversity in existence, the arrangement of quantity, through the differences of these states and categories, for recognising the human family, is a matter for study through the explanation of the thread of coexistence.
