Chapter Two

Upasana (*upāsanā*) – Wisdom

Knowledge: — knowledge of the holistic view of existence, jeevan knowledge, knowledge of conduct filled with humaneness

Wisdom: — the immortality of jeevan, the perishability (*nashwaratva*) of the body, the rules of behaviour — together with knowledge — being evidenced in the human goal and the jeevan goal

Human goal: — resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, the natural evidencing of coexistence

Jeevan goal: — happiness, peace, contentment, joy

Upasana is an important part of human jeevan. Upasana itself is the process of refining and transforming the root tendencies. This very study is also the transformation of sanskar and disposition.

Attaining togetherness by proper means is itself upasana's natural conception, for which there is diligence (refined effort) and practice. It is through practice and diligence alone that the veracity of the gross, the subtle, and the causal becomes clear through study. Through this, research concerning the relevant matter, the course of destiny, power, magnificence, and special powers, and the rules connected with them — conceived by way of *anugaman* (the path to realisation) — becomes well established. Research differs by the physical, the intellectual, and the spiritual. The process of research is directly manifest in the form of ratiocination, contemplation, resolve, and experiencing.

The meaning of the gross, the subtle, and the causal (the Seer) is: being endowed with the capacity to see, understand, apply, behave, and experience.

[p. 31]

Upasanas occur by the distinction of *kutastha* (abiding in the immutable), *rupastha* (abiding in form), and *atmastha* (abiding in the self). The process undertaken for complete realisation is *kutastha upasana*. The process undertaken for proximity to a form together with magnificence is *rupastha upasana*. The process of reflection itself is *atmastha upasana*, to which the human being is bound. Therefore, the refinement of tendencies is itself the programme of human jeevan. The expectation of completeness and of refinement-capacity is found in every state of the human being. Refinement-capacity itself is skill, proficiency, and scholarliness. Completeness itself is scholarliness. There is no knowledge and skill greater than scholarliness, and no behaviour and production greater than proficiency. Refinement-capacity is found in production and behaviour. Scholarliness itself is enlightenment; enlightenment itself is education and orderliness. Until one becomes fully complete with enlightenment, upasana is an extremely helpful aid.

Gods and goddesses (*devi-devata*) are to be reckoned of three kinds: earth-located, space-located, and divine-located. This is based on their range of movement. The conscious unit is the combined form of subtle and causal activity. This itself is the jeevan-cluster (*jeevan-punj*). This itself is gods and goddesses. The gross body is found controlled by the life-breath (*pran vayu*); subtle activity, by chitta; and causal activity, by atma. There is no separation of the subtle and the causal — this itself is the witness of immortality. The indication of the subtle and the causal becomes evident only in operating the gross body, and this is also indispensable. In the state without a gross body, subtle activities are accomplished only according to the indication of buddhi.

The three kinds of deity are: heaven-located (moving in unlimited space), space-located (moving in the space of a single cosmos), and earth-located (moving only within the environment of a single land). These, in order, are the divine soul, the deific soul, and the elemental soul.

Because of conduct-completeness and being endowed with complete awakening, [there is] the divine soul; because of being endowed with alertness accompanied by activity-completeness, [there is] the deific soul; and because of intense attachment to objects together with apprehension and fear, [there is] the elemental soul. It is the demonic human and the animalistic human who, upon a change of body, are reckoned as elemental souls; it is the humaneness-filled human and the deific human who, upon a change of body, are reckoned as deific souls; and it is the divine human who, after relinquishing the body, is reckoned in the category of the divine soul.

Elemental souls are in a state of absolute-oneness (*tadatmya*) with adhibhautik elements, deific souls with adhidaivik facts, and divine souls with the Spirit (*adhyatma*). Therefore, three categories are found to be established.

[p. 32]

In the adhibhautik domain, production-predominant behaviour [prevails]; in the adhidaivik domain, behaviour-predominant production; in a life filled with the Spirit, realisation-based thought, behaviour, and production occur in direct sequence. Therefore, behaviour and enterprise are found to be restrained and controlled in a life filled with the Spirit; orderly and disciplined in an adhidaivik life; and undisciplined and disorderly in a physical life. Therefore, it is only in a life filled with the Spirit that the integrality of the human being's four dimensions is found.

For the sake of the "instincts-quad" (*vishaya-chatushtaya*), [absolute-oneness is found] in adhibhautik elements; for the sake of the "triad of desires" (*eshana-traya*), in adhidaivik (social and behavioural) values; and the divine human is found in a state of absolute-oneness with the Spirit — for which, respectively, attachment, upasana, and dedication are employed.

The whole of insentient-sentient nature is saturated in Paramatma (Omnipotence); therefore these [three kinds of soul] are bound to awakening, culminating in realisation, in "this" [existence].

The deluded human is afflicted, excited, or bound by relativity — this is directly evident. This relativity remains observable only until the unit becomes free from delusion, because the free-from-delusion state is itself its supreme excellence.

Control itself is rule, inspiration, coordination, relativity, effort, motion, consequence, activity, conduct, union, separation, combination, status, state, and control — all of which is observable.

Upasana of the divine souls is itself learning (*vidya*); upasana of the deific souls is itself sub-learning (*upavidya*); and upasana of the elemental souls is itself base learning (*kshudra vidya*). It is well known that upasana of divine souls is undertaken, with awakening, for freedom-from-delusion together with intellectual resolution and material prosperity; upasana of deific souls is for intellectual resolution and material prosperity; and upasana of elemental souls is only for materiality.

All jeevans are endowed with their own respective disposition. This itself is their standing. Beyond this, no further manifestation of disposition is possible from them.

[p. 33]

Materiality alone has not proven sufficient for the human being, because the four dimensions found in the human being are not satiated by material substances alone. Therefore, until the human being becomes fully complete with the integrality of production, behaviour, thought, and experiencing, he remains bound to experimentation, endeavour, and practice.

In the upasana of divine souls, wisdom-filled science, austerity (firm dedication), determination, decision, and the capacity for resolve become available — this itself is the direct result of their upasana. From the upasana of deific souls, half-wisdom, science, patience, courage, affection, trust, renunciation, and control and restraint in the activities of jeevan are found — these are the direct attainments of deific upasana. From the upasana of elemental souls, unwise science, impatience, recklessness, attachment and aversion, apprehension, and uncontrolled activities of jeevan are found. It is as the result of these three upasanas, respectively, that one becomes observable in states of alertness-filled watchfulness, awakening-filled watchfulness, and apprehension-filled non-awakening. These are, respectively, the delusion-free, the semi-deluded, and the deluded states.

Rasayanik (chemical) substances' constituted bodily-formation, in which medhas is contained, is itself the meat substance, and among plants, the vegetable substance is available. In both of these, chemical substance is found to be predominantly similar. Even while such similarity of chemical substance remains, there is a fundamental difference between meat-body-formation-mechanisation and its use, and the formation-process and use of the plant-image. This is as follows: —

| **2. Purpose** — medium for the conscious unit's natural tasting and welcoming process | medium of medicine and food (for animals and humans) |
| **3. Mechanisation** — bodily formation with medhas | form-formation without medhas |

In meat-eating decorum, forceful slaughter and exploitation are inherently embedded events.

Within the domain of inhumaneness, even while a state of diversity persists in decorum, conduct, behaviour, and tendency between the vegetarian and the non-vegetarian human being, predominantly a similarity is found in the state of purpose — this is also evident among animals. Therefore this fact is clear:

[p. 35]

when humaneness becomes studiable and comes to be understood, both the non-vegetarian and the vegetarian come to agree in favour of humaneness. When they become established-in-dedication, even the non-vegetarian becomes vegetarian. In awakened human decorum, trust and expectancy are also alike. This itself is the one point of truth where every kind of decorum stands in abiding wait for the maintenance of human decorum. This itself is a site of possibility, which is the interval for the continuous excellence of awareness.

In humaneness and higher-humaneness, the originality of vegetarianism becomes clear. The oneness (freedom from diversity) of decorum in the human being is found to be established only through humaneness. Therefore, the chief utility in upasana lies in the immersion in universal values.

Those upasana methods through which resulting decorums establish class-sentiment in the human being are not fully helpful for well-being, because class-sentiment, in the final analysis, is oriented only toward conflict. For the state of being free from conflict, universality is an indispensable fact.

At the root of all upasanas there is a similarity of aim: it is undivided society, universal orderliness. It is only universal welfare. Because, without the aspiration for universal welfare, one's own welfare cannot be accomplished.

Other desirable desires only give the appearance of a state of welfare, not realisation. Therefore, the human being is bound to make effort toward a programme in conformity with the aspiration for universal welfare, and up to the capacity worthy of following it.

It is only through the universal, humaneness-filled method of conducting the "triad of rule" — intellectual, social, and natural — that there is the possibility of, and actual freedom from, economic and communal class-sentiments. In this alone does all class-sentiment dissolve. Therefore, upasana becomes meaningful only through the desired understanding, together with awakening — which is itself awakening.

In the human being, the powers are activity-power, desire-power, and knowledge-power alone — these are its qualifications. Qualifications are found to be based on each unit's capacity-for-awakening and on [its] awakening.

[p. 36]

Upasanas exist only for capacity-for-awakening, not for decline.

There is no pain in the progress of the process of awakening-progression. This itself is the music (integrality) of jeevan. In contrast, it is only in [wrong] activity-process and decline that every kind of pain exists — these are sorrow.

There are intellectual, behavioural, and physical pains. These pains are observable, respectively, in the form of problem, offence, incapacity, and wastage. This itself is the direct form of non-awakening.

It is only deficiency in watchfulness and alertness that is the cause of all affliction. Watchfulness and alertness are seen to manifest only on the basis of the capacity, competence, and worthiness found in the human unit. Therefore —

For powers not to be wasted through the sense-organs of sound, touch, form, taste, and smell, but rather to be well spent — this itself is the awakening of activity-power. The determination of good-use and wastage lies, within the domain of humaneness, in the form of the "triad of rule".

The non-wasting of the *antahkarana*'s (the inner instrument's) root tendencies — namely hope, thought, desire, and resoluteness (*ṛtambharā*) — occurs only by the method of human consciousness and higher-human consciousness, and this itself is good-use. This itself is the awakening of desire-power.

Right-enlightenment together with the completeness of experiencing is itself the capacity for the manifestation of knowledge. This itself is the awakening — or the complete awakening — of knowledge-power. This "triad of awakening" is directly evident in humaneness and higher-humaneness. This itself is the supreme-culmination attainment of human jeevan. This itself is the utility of empowered upasana; this itself is the aspiration of the whole of humankind. This itself is universal welfare.

The direct form of jeevan-awakening is itself the application of wisdom-filled science. This itself is watchfulness, undivided sociality, enlightenment, freedom-from-disparity, coexistence, education, method, orderliness, civilisation, culture, intellectual resolution, material prosperity, and the continuity of jeevan-awakening.

[p. 37]

Every human unit is bound to effort and upasana up to complete awakening. As a result of this, the refinement of root tendencies occurs, on account of which distinctive and cultured mentality, and the capacity for thought-contemplation-enlightenment, and the completeness of realisation, become manifest. These are the attainments of the best upasana. This itself is study-completeness.

Whoever is a complete knower of a discipline, and is practised in its work, behaviour, and realisation, is found to possess continuous activeness in it. Hence, the completeness of an activity is the continuity of that activity. Therefore, it does not prove to be a cause for indifference, or for making another indifferent — because activeness exists for the sake of completeness. Completeness is the continuity of activity.

Whatever is active for the sake of completeness has continuity after becoming complete, as with activity-completeness and conduct-completeness. Therefore, the measurement of completeness by the yardstick of incompleteness, or the measurement of the awakened by the unawakened, is not possible, because the lesser value is contained within the greater value, not the greater value within the lesser. This is directly evident in the human being. For example, it is directly evident that every human being has succeeded in measuring what is less developed than himself. Concomitant with this, it is evident that the human being produces along with less-developed nature, and has also gained knowledge of what is more [developed]. Therefore he is bound to behave with his equal. This itself is the imperativeness of sociality. This itself is the natural glory and dignity of human jeevan. This dignity itself is the impetus for behaving with one's equal, and for practising toward greater awakening. This itself is real upasana.

Wisdom — that is, the human goal — and jeevan-value — that is, resolution [and] prosperity — is itself the direct result of upasana, in which sociality remains naturally contained.

The projection of realisation-enlightenment is itself non-accumulation (prosperity), generosity, and compassion. It is the enrichment of the natural foundational values of generosity and compassion that is the life-essence of sociality. This itself is social music. It is for this alone that the human being thirsts. Wisdom alone manifests intellectual resolution and social values through their maintenance.

Wisdom and science are themselves the chief characteristic of indirect knowledge (good-behavioural knowledge). Realisation alone is the final state of indirect knowledge. Prior to this, only the authority of inference is established. Realisation exists only in the truth of the factual condition, the objective, and the state.

[p. 38]

Without indirect knowledge, [the discernment of] the eternal and the non-eternal, the proper and improper, the just and unjust, the righteous and unrighteous, the true and false, the desirable and undesirable, the seen and unseen, and the authority of indirect knowledge itself, is not established.

Without the authority-of-knowledge of the eternal-and-non-eternal, dedication toward one's own-dharma is not found in the human being. Human dharma is itself happiness; happiness is itself just conduct; just conduct is itself the observance of humaneness-filled [conduct] and of the "triad of rule". This itself is the human being's own-dharma. The human being is happiness-natured.

There is no unit without propriety. Just as, in animals, disposition-propriety; in plants, property-propriety; and in substances, form-propriety, is not violated. This itself is their dignity. In the same way, in the human being, resolution-happiness is itself dharma; dharma is itself propriety. This itself is their dignity and trust. The direct form of propriety is itself trust.

"There is no happiness in relationship and connection devoid of trust." There is no human being devoid of relationship and connection. This itself is the imperativeness for one's own-dharma. Whatever incapacity, incompetence, and unworthiness there is in its observance — that itself is sorrow, affliction, problem, and non-awakening.

Until one becomes fully complete with fullness in one's own-dharma and with the capacity, competence, and worthiness fit to observe it, there is no absence of upasana in the form of study, for the purpose of acquiring knowledge.

It is in the human being's own-dharma alone that creed, sect, and class disappear. This itself is the direct dignity of capable upasana. "This itself is auspicious." It is indispensable for all three of these — the aim, the seeker, and the means — to remain contained within upasana. The integrality of these alone is the success of upasana; otherwise, it is failure. The proper use of whatever power and means are obtained in every state — that itself is its advance awakening. This itself is upasana.

There is no such power or means which has no deployment or use, because the accumulation of power or means is not practical.

[p. 39]

In self-delight: wisdom; in wisdom-filled buddhi: rooted science; in science-filled art, composition, discernment, and thought; in discernment-based tasting and welcoming; in emotion-filled mind — [all of these] are continuously balanced and resolved. As a result of this, it becomes well established that there is an inundation of supreme-bliss in atma, an inundation of joy in buddhi, an inundation of contentment in chitta, an inundation of peace in vritti, and an inundation of happiness in mun.

The activities of the sense-organs, and the field of operation of the sense-organs — this itself is the boundary of direct knowledge. In this transaction, the four-object domain's attainments are its outer limits. Beyond this, there are no further attainments in it.

The human being is not confined within the boundary of objects, because the four dimensions are well established in the human being.

The human being alone is bound to become endowed with production, behaviour, thought, and experiencing. This itself is necessity, occasion, possibility, and orderliness.

It is only with restraint that dignity-filled grandeur manifests from the activities carried out by the human being in every context. As follows —

From practising the speaking of truth together with truth-enlightenment, there arises the cessation of fear and distrust, and the arising of delight and enthusiasm.

From gaining mastery over non-violence, there arises freedom from the fear of opposition, and the arising of affection and generosity. Not participating in violence by the distinctions of done, caused, and intended, [and by] the physical, vocal, and mental — [this equals] non-violence.

From dedication to non-possession, there is the destruction of pitiableness and baseness, and the arising of patience, fortitude, and generosity.

From the establishment of non-stealing, there is the destruction of cunning and distress, and the arising of satisfaction and contentment. The meaning of non-stealing is not committing theft.

[p. 40]

From the establishment of value (sentiment) toward the world, there is the absence of disparity between the desired [deity] and the seeker. Along with this, the disposition of that deity enters [the seeker].

From the practice of remembering, in identical form, the meaning of a word — that is, the meaning of a mantra — its meaning and disposition become attainable. Every meaningful word is a mantra.

From devoting oneself more in awakening, there is the eradication of pride and ego, and the arising of learning and simplicity.

From bodily sensation remaining restrained, [there is] purity of mind; from purity of mind, the gain of mental strength.

From the destruction of attachment to one's own body, attachment toward the world is removed. It is only through an intellect filled with the trustworthy aspiration in the form of universal-welfare that there occurs the outpouring of generosity, compassion, grace, and mercy toward the world, and knowledge and realisation of coexistence in the world's foundational Omnipotence.

Without restraint, the refinement of intellectual root-tendencies, intellect-strength, the realisation of social values, the vision of conscious activity, resolution, and restraint [itself] are not accomplished.

"Gratitude alone is the beginning-to-end basis of restraint."

In ingratitude, lack-of-restraint is directly evident. It is necessary to plant gratitude precisely in infancy and adolescence.

Through the continuity of compassion-filled thought, the fault of miserliness is remedied, and the experience of supreme delight occurs.

In whatever aim one makes the *antahkarana* (mind, vritti, chitta, buddhi) take-the-form-of, one becomes intense in that very thing. As, by taking-the-form-of water, the destruction of distress and the experience of satiation occurs.

By taking-the-form-of fire, the destruction of disease and the gain of health occurs.

By taking-the-form-of air, the destruction of instability and the gain of stability occurs.

[p. 41]

By taking-the-form-of light, the destruction of ignorance and the arising of knowledge occurs.

From contemplation on others'-welfare, the increase of enthusiasm and delight occurs.

From benevolence toward others, fame occurs.

From resolving problems, glory occurs.

From contemplation on the welfare of all, the development of talent occurs.

From the practice of counting others' virtues, the destruction of one's own vices occurs.

A biased intellect is incapable of grasping the virtues of the opposing side, because it is not free from envy, aversion, hatred, neglect, and delusion.

In the progress of awakening, the condition of self-versus-other faction is not found; therefore, in it, orderly sequence, rule, and justice are accomplished. The very cause of problem is non-enlightenment. Its remedy is enlightenment alone, which is itself awakening. This itself is the direct dignity of upasana.

For upasana, the importance of environment is unavoidable, among which the human-made environment is foremost — which exists only in the form of education and orderliness.

Education fully complete with enlightenment is itself the direction-giving magnificence for personality. The orderliness fit for its due protection and promotion is itself sovereignty.

The process of generalisation of enlightenment is itself the direct form of qualitative change. One person who becomes enlightened through upasana becomes common through education and orderliness.

[p. 42]

In the state of not having the perspective of qualitative change, the deluded human being becomes intent on evaluating on the basis of quantity. This itself is class-struggle and conflict.

The conduct of the "triad of rule," natural to humaneness-filled behaviour, is itself personality. Active contribution to the making of such a personality is itself duty. This itself is human effort. This itself is enlightenment.

Upasana grants the individual the direct form of personality and duty, because upasana is itself, in its own nature, education and orderliness. Therefore, this itself is enlightenment. Enlightenment itself is education and orderliness in the human being.

There is no diversity in truth and truthfulness.

In symbol-ness, there is indeed multiplicity. Through it, only delusion is indicated.

In the intellectual condition that mistakes the symbol itself for the goal, communal excitation, pride, and ego are found.

The symbol-goal-oriented upasana process has proven secondary and unsociable to the extent of [its] incompleteness, because in it there is no clarification and attainment of the human goal. Therefore there are disparities. Such symbols are reckoned by the distinctions of place, caste, language, art, image, composition, statue, memorial, and word.

Intellectuality alone is the direct form of the human being's personality, not the body, because it is precisely the intellectual capacity found in each individual that receives respect or neglect, honour or contempt, esteem. Hence, intellectuality alone is [the seat of] root tendencies, the capacity to receive and transmit signals; it is itself necessity, desire, thought, attachment or non-attachment, refinement, effect, readiness, and intellectuality.

For the human being, natural, capable upasana is an indispensable programme, which establishes the standing from inhumaneness to humaneness, and from humaneness to higher-humaneness.

[p. 43]

In coexistence, natural-recurrence occurs through the moment-to-moment unbroken tendency (*anukshan-vikshan-vritti*).

The very disappearance of the moment-to-moment mediating obstruction is itself the moment-to-moment unbroken tendency.

The meaning of *anukshan* [each-moment] is that, continuously, in every moment, contemplation on coexistence, and the natural evidencing of authenticity in the sequence of thought, becomes presented — this itself is natural-recurrence.

The knowledge of the saturated-ness of nature saturated in Omnipotence — [that is,] the complete holistic view — is itself the disappearance of moment-to-moment mediating obstruction. This itself is the disappearance of deluded presence-and-absence. This itself is natural standing and state.

From the absence of natural knowledge of nature saturated in Omnipotence, and from the absence of realisation-rooted knowledge, [the human being], out of fear and temptation, sustains all delusion-rooted activity-behaviour and thinking. This itself is the cause of all affliction.

Time is the duration of activity. Within this very duration, imposed thought and desire are unnatural, and non-imposed thought and desire are natural.

It is in the human unit alone that, even in the progress of awakening, the capacity for non-imposition is found. Imposition occurs out of delusion.

Whatever is as it is — [regarding it] as more, less, or not-understanding — this itself is imposition. This itself is ignorance. This itself is incapacity. This itself is delusion.

It is only in relation to the state-fulness of insentient-sentient nature saturated in Omnipotence, and to the natural completeness of Omnipotence, that the activity of imposition or non-imposition is found to occur.

In every unit, form, property, disposition, and dharma are contained. This itself is its functionality. It is only the knowledge of the count, measure, and purpose of this that is the indicator of the capacity, free-from-delusion, toward nature. This itself is proof. This itself is naturalness.

All activities are, at root, observable in the distinction of form and word.

[p. 44]

Without complementarity, there is no advancement in a unit.

Up to completeness, the unit is inclined toward complementarity [and] usefulness.

Complementarity alone manifests the characteristics of decline and development in the unit. This itself is also the chief utility.

The properties beginning with sound that manifest in a unit are themselves relative powers. There is no unit devoid of property. Therefore —

In the human being, there is no absence of natural aspiration. Naturalness itself is dharma. This itself is happiness, peace, contentment, and joy. This itself clarifies conception, which is directly evident.

Every karma-result has proven to be both the nourisher and the exploiter of human happiness.

There is no obstruction in the realisation-sequence of truth and truthfulness, because the realisation-sequence-orderliness is dense. The links of awakening are dense. Therefore —

Naturalness is free from imposition. Imposition itself is deficient-or-excessive evaluation. The activity of deficient-or-excessive self-evaluation is itself unnaturalness.

Self-evaluation occurs within the domain of the "triad of *-tā*" [likely capacity, competence, and worthiness — see note below].

There is no human being on this earth who does not fall within the domain of the "triad of *-tā*".

There is no separation from dharma, because this is [itself its] conception. Its direct form is itself conduct filled with humaneness and higher-humaneness, which is the chief characteristic of naturalness. Therefore, conduct that advocates inhumaneness is itself unnatural.

Nature is present within itself with completeness. This itself is duration. Therefore, it is contained within the whole. This itself is coexistence, natural to completeness within the whole. This itself is the ever-present-ness of completeness, and the emergence of completeness in the human being in the state of knowledge. This itself is the imperativeness for awakening.

[p. 45]

The removal of unnecessary imaginings found in the human being's intellectual domain is itself qualitative refinement in the capacity for the holistic view. This itself is the sum-total of qualitative sanskar-transformation, education, and the programme of jeevan.

The excellence of the capacity for the holistic view is itself the moment-to-moment [unbroken tendency]. This itself is the capacity of mediating activity. Mediating activity itself is the Seer.

The supreme-culmination of mediating activity is itself the complete control of generative and degenerative activity. This very capacity is freedom from moment-to-moment mediating obstruction.

It is only through sanskar that the human being is found engaged, concomitantly with the capacity of intellectual orderliness-process, by way of activity and cessation, within the domain of natural desire and objects — this is directly evident.

In the intellectual process and orderliness concomitant with atma (mediating activity), the state of truth-resolve and a mentality filled with truth-imagination is found, which is renowned as complete with knowledge of karma [and] upasana. This very capacity manifests deific and divine humaneness. This itself is complete awakening.

In desire-attached intellectual orderliness, humane and deific-human disposition manifests. In accordance with this, there is the state-fulness of mental environment. Such capacity — that is, social consciousness — is found to become fully complete with alertness and watchfulness.

In object-attached tendency and process, inhumane conduct occurs, which is observable in the form of bestiality and demonic-ness. In these, a mentality fit for that very [state] is found. This itself is the cause of latent-and-dormant imagination. This itself is the indicator of non-awakening and incomplete watchfulness.

Only when one becomes inquisitive toward the good (awakening) do latent-dormant imaginings become refined. As a result, indifference toward demonic and bestial tendencies becomes established. Along with this, the arising of wisdom occurs.

The arising of inquisitiveness toward the good occurs in one's own sanskar, in method-prescribed study, and in an environment favourable to it.

[p. 46]

Method-prescribed study is itself skill, proficiency, and scholarliness.

Study and environment alone are the orderliness capable of sanskar-transformation, whose qualitative change is itself the inquisitiveness for self-enlightenment.

Self-enlightenment alone is the chief characteristic of the inquisitiveness for truth. Therefore —

Conception alone is the inclination toward *anugaman* and cultivation, which becomes manifest in the form of decorum.

For progress, conception is indispensable.

Conception is well established for awakening, and attachment for decline. These are, respectively, cessation and unnecessary tendency.

Conception itself is right-wisdom. Right-wisdom, in itself, is the discernment of truthfulness, which is clear. At root, this itself is universal-goodness and welfare.

The conception of realisation, in the form of truth-enlightenment; conception (right-enlightenment) is itself truth-resolve. This itself, having been projected, becomes directly manifest in the fruited form of good-karma, upasana, and conduct. The projected value of this very thing is directly manifest in the forms of courage, valour, generosity, compassion, grace, and mercy.

Right-enlightenment occurs only in truth. Falsehood alone gives rise to imagination and [mere] appearance.

Karma joined with baseness, wretchedness, and cruelty is inauspicious.

Self-value alone is the satisfaction of activity and cessation. Therefore —

[p. 47]

Karma and upasana that are free from falsehood, pride, and arrogance, [and] joined with truth, simplicity, naturalness, and courtesy, are causes of the good.

From the continuity of the aspiration for truth, the conception of the goal occurs. It is in knowledge alone that production, behaviour, thought, and experiencing are directly manifest.

Knowledge is itself wisdom-sanctioned science, which is complete.

Firmness in truth and truthfulness is itself a jeevan filled with the good.

The experiencing-of-truth alone is everyone's desire (*abhīṣṭa*).

It is the mind alone that operates, through the medhas, all the activities accomplished by the body. All the nerves are controlled by the medhas.

The root form of word is mind alone. The mind, upon the medhas, carries out the transmission, operation, and control of tasting- and welcoming-sentiment-filled waves; at the root of this is word alone.

A controlling word for the sake of motion toward awakening is itself a mantra. The process of the goal-attainment-worthy sequence is itself control. Whatever sentiment (value) is in a word — that itself is its meaning. The meaning of meaningful words is itself motion toward awakening, because the meaning of a word is an entity in existence.

Whatever usefulness-filled indispensability is in sentiment — that itself is its importance. Whatever definite direction is in usefulness-filled indispensability — that itself is its firmness. This itself is right resolve. Whatever completeness is in right resolve — that itself is realisation, which, respectively, are the well-cultured fundamental activities found in mind, vritti, chitta, buddhi, and atma. The meaning of sentiment lies in being [itself].

Activity-power [as] *kāsā*, desire-power [as] *ākūti*, and knowledge-power [as] *medhā* — [these are] the divine intellects that are directly manifest, from whose arising alone mental-strength, intellect-strength, and atma-strength are evidenced.

[p. 48]

Without the awakening of the triad-of-powers — [that is,] the awakening of desire-power, activity-power, and knowledge-power — renunciation (freedom-from-delusion) and love are not evidenced.

For body-centred jeevan, there is the creation of beings other than human; and for mind-centred, intellect-centred, and atma-centred jeevan, there is the creation of the human being alone.

Without freedom-from-delusion (renunciation) and a love-filled jeevan, self-and-other welfare does not occur.

The meaning of welfare is the natural continuity of awakening.

Without karma, upasana, and knowledge that are beneficial to self and other, peace does not become complete.

In a dispassion-filled jeevan, the absence of absence occurs. Dispassion alone is the cause of the atma-goal.

From the influence of the atma-goal, self-enlightenment occurs.

From self-enlightenment occurring, self-illumination occurs.

From self-illumination occurring, self-trust occurs.

From self-trust occurring, the decay of ignorance occurs.

From the decay of ignorance, self-standing occurs.

From self-standing occurring, dispassion-toward-other occurs.

From dispassion-toward-other occurring, the accomplishment of dominion occurs.

From the accomplishment of dominion, supreme opulence and complete satiation occur.

[p. 49]

From the attainment of opulence, natural-recurrence occurs.

From natural-recurrence, the realisation of freedom-from-delusion occurs.

The realisation of freedom-from-delusion is itself, for the human being, the ultimate human effort.

This itself is complete awakening and the standing of the Seer's plane.

"May there be eternal good, may there be good for all."
