## 3.16 The Human Being as the Combined Form of the Prana-State, the Human Body, and Jeevan (प्राणावस्था, मानव शरीर और जीवन के संयुक्त रूप में मानव)

At the root of the prana state, combinative (*yaugik*) method and tendency are found to occur by the self-motivated method. If study is made regarding the favourable circumstance for this, then it appears, in background, that it lies simply in being enriched, in itself, from cosmic rays and from atoms of many species on the earth. If one thinks about the combinative background on this earth, there is no cause greater than this. In maintaining cosmic favourableness, cosmic ray-radiation alone is the one source. Even today this thing is presented right up to this earth's atmosphere. Whatever the ray manages to reach, all of that comes to be more evidenced in the earth's own light. Along with this, heat's relation too is formed with the earth. The earth, and every atom in the earth, is affected; in such a condition, whatever other sources there are, those radiant sources keep up a circulation of radiativeness produced from, or spread from, many earths as an effect of cosmic activities. This is still ongoing even now. Along with this, this earth, on the basis of being enriched with atoms of many species, also has, within itself, a great many radiant substances at work. In this way it has been the background for the whole of combinative activity to occur, through the combination of ray, radiation, and heat. Combinative events, proceeding from the event of water, into acid and

[p. 136]

alkaline forms — the background of the possibility of such combinative events occurring may be accepted. Because even now, in another form, acid, alkali, and water combining, in various ratio-sequences, various kinds of chemical substance remain ready before us. Whatever became helpful, in these ratios, for change has always been the help of organic and other species' molecule-atoms. In this way acid, alkali, and water, together with these molecule-atoms, are found to have been helpful in increasing the number of species of chemical substances.

In this way the activity of chemical substances becoming enriched has been occurring on this earth. Out of the union-combination of these chemical substances, the formation of nourishment-elements and composition-elements — this event, being brought forth from the continued combination of radiant rays and heat, is found to be manifest to this very day. It is seen that in the sum of nourishment-element and composition-element, the composition, presentation, and functionality of prana-sutras comes about. It is this very nourishment-element and composition-element which, becoming enriched in themselves, in the form of the prana-sutra and the composition of the prana-sutra — through this same combination of heat, through the combination of radiation, by force of vibration — bring about, in the prana-sutras, the manifestation of the respiration activity together with the composition-method. Immediately after this, the acceptance of the composition-method in the prana-sutras, for the nature of prana-cells to be a composition composed from cells, and for such compositions to be diverse, becomes intelligible. After one kind of composition becomes enriched in itself, acceptance in the prana-sutras for a second kind of composition occurs; as a result, compositions of the second kind, and, in this same sequence, other compositions, have already come to be evidenced upon this earth. All of these are together with the seed-tree method. In this same sequence, the sequence of the glory of plants, the sequence of the composition and de-composition of plants, and its continuity, are in the form of the actual condition.

The residue of decomposed plants alone became the basis of the sweat-born world. Even today this matter can be tested. Gather leaves together before the rainy season; those very leaves, at the time of rotting, produce a great many worms and insects. These worms and insects prepare the egg-born world. In such an egg-born tradition, it has remained natural for excellence to occur in the prana-cells, or there kept being talk of further excellence, and of further species. The egg-born world, becoming abundant, kept establishing itself in the forms of the water-dweller, the land-dweller, and the sky-dweller. From the prana-cells of the egg-born world, the prana-cells of the womb-born world developed. In this womb-born world, the majority became land-dwellers, whereas in the egg-born world all three — land-dweller, water-dweller, sky-dweller — occurred. Of these, in the egg-born world the sky-dwellers were more numerous; after this, just as

[p. 137]

the womb-born world too came, land-dwellers became more numerous. In the sky-dweller and water-dweller, the womb-born world obtained fewer species. After this, in this sequence, as many species developed in the womb-born world, the tradition of the human body-composition developed as the final species.

In human tradition, jeevan and body being in a combined form, activities different from those of animals began to be accomplished by the human being, by force of imaginativeness and freedom-of-action. Such as felling trees, breaking stone, digging soil; and, starting from this, building houses, making clothes, adopting methods different from those of animals in raising children — these kept coming about by the self-motivated method. Of these, in identifiable form, building houses, making clothes, and producing crops came within the affirmative portion. These three kinds of act became entirely distinct from those of animals.

In this same sequence, the evidence for obtaining knowledge of body-composition also proceeded alongside. In the sequence of the composition-method within the prana-sutra, the prana-cell, and the prana-sutra as described at the root, whatever degree of excellence there was to be, kept coming about, with the species remaining as it actually is (*yathavat*) from the primordial human up to the present principal form. It is possible that the contribution of human tendency too remains contained in this. By this method the human being's ascending sequence became a chain. In today's condition, the knowledge of body-composition, its fundamental process, its measurement, the method of testing diseases, the combination of medicines — all of this, taken together, has become bound up with commerce, or has become caught in the clutches of commerce. By this method this entire affirmative portion has come into the grip of a few people's hands. From this too, the greatest inconveniences have come to be formed for the human being. Thought and consideration for becoming free from this problem are indeed under way.

The principal matter is: the tendency of prana-cells in body-composition; in body-composition, the coming-together of the prana-cells of two species as a festival; as a result, passing through the embryonic state, body-composition — that is, the composition of limbs and organs, hands, feet, and so on; the completion of the infant's composition within the womb by the time five months are complete; the enriched medhas-system being present when the infant's composition is complete, with a more enriched, more complete medhas-system than in the animal order becoming ready — this becomes intelligible. In this same period, jeevan's becoming ready to operate the body — the indication of this becoming understood by the woman who is to become a mother; after the birth of the infant, for its nurturing, the attention that used to be given a hundred years ago, the attention given fifty years ago, and being able to give even more precise attention today — in today's condition all of this comes within the human being's study.

In this, curiosity is formed regarding the tendency in the prana-sutras for diversity in composition — why does it happen, how does it happen? The need to think about this issue does indeed arise in the human being. The principal matter is precisely colour and form, in which diversity comes about. No particular diversity is possible in the limbs and organs. This fact has already been understood through testing and inspection. Today's human being has already come to recognise many kinds of fluid substance with precision. On the basis of their proportional, various deployment, the colour of the body is found to occur. Taken altogether, colour comes to be known from the condition of the prana-cell, half-dried and dried, in the upper part of the body. The blood that is under the skin is red in everybody's body. It is from the upper part that colour is known. What the prana-cells display after becoming dry is what we come to know as colour. For this reason, recognising the human being on the basis of colour has not been precise in the sense of recognising the human being. It can also be thought that, from a very long time ago, colour remained predominant for mutual recognition, and other bases too kept being sought. Along with this, race too remained very predominant. ## 3.17 Knowledge, Knower, Known (ज्ञान, ज्ञाता, ज्ञेय)

Darshan [seeing/philosophy] (justice, dharma, truth); *drish* [the seeing] (human jeevan); *drishya* [the seen] (coexistence);

*Dhyan* [attention] (awakening); *dhyata* [the attender] (the human being); *dhyeya* [the object of attention] (freedom from delusion, jeevan-value, the natural evidence of the human goal);

*Karan* [cause] (the knowledge-state); *karta* [the doer] (the human being); *karya* [the effect] (wholeness, participation in universality);

[p. 146]

aim (*sadhya*) [the seer-status, awakening]; seeker (*sadhak*) [the human being]; means (*sadhan*) [body and jeevan].

On the issue of knowledge, knower, and known, it has been well-known, or it has been on record, in human tradition, from the distant past, that thinking took place only from the viewpoints of spiritualism (*adhyatmavad*), god-centred-ism (*adhidaivikvad*), and materialism-of-the-beyond (*adhibhautikvad*). The collective naming of these three disciplines has been idealism (*adarshvad*). The point of commonality among these three is precisely mystery. Even what is more than the physical is a mystery. Divinity is, in itself, a mystery; spirituality is, in itself, a mystery. Idealism's root basis-site, and its destination-site, are both mystery. It has been necessary for differences of opinion to arise precisely on the basis of mystery being the basis, and mystery being the destination. Along with this, effort at practice and exercise by many methods was also made. In whatever ways effort was made in these three disciplines — from exercise, means, yoga, meditation, all the way to worship, recitation, prayer. As the outcome of all these acts, the assurance that was obtained was moksha and heaven; moksha too was a mystery, heaven too was a mystery, and this itself kept being called the destination.

The basis too came to be a mystery in this way. It is from mystery alone, in mystery alone — from the void, from Brahma — that all this creation, sustenance, and dissolution occurred; or, from some substance beyond physical activity, creation, sustenance, and dissolution occurred. In this way the beginning was from mystery, and the end too was from mystery. The end, an auspicious mystery, was from heaven and moksha. Auspicious, because heaven and moksha bring joy; mystery, because its ending occurs in mystery. According to idealism, which is the combined intent of these three kinds — regarding the seen, the seer, and seeing: according to the Brahma-doctrinaires, Brahma alone is the seer, Brahma alone, according to his own resolve, is the seen, and Brahma alone is the seeing-sight that beholds. In the same way the god-centred ones too say — as one gets to read according to the documents — that, according to them, three are propounded: Shakti-knowledge, Shiva-knowledge, and Vishnu-knowledge. In Shakti alone is the entirety of the seen, creation, sustenance, dissolution. In the same way, in all three, this is god-dependent. All that is seen exists precisely because it is, in the form of the seen, the resolve of the gods; the gods alone are the seer, and the one who beholds too is the god. In the same way, according to materialism-of-the-beyond too, it has been said that there is some mysterious seer, seen, and seeing more powerful than the physical. In this same sequence, a statement has also been made regarding knowledge, knower, and known. Brahma alone, or the god, is himself, in the forms of knowledge, knower, and known, attention, attender, and object of attention — all of these. In the forms of effect, cause, and doer too, they are ready to accept God or the god. This has been the tenor of the voice up to now.

[p. 147]

According to materialist thought, difference in consciousness comes to be produced from difference in composition; no evidence of this is to be found. The physical substance alone was taken to be the source of consciousness. In this sequence, nothing is seen from testing stone and soil.

From the coexistence-ist viewpoint, while carrying out the study of the human being's being the combined form of jeevan and body, the situation of jeevan being established in the status of the conscious becomes intelligible. Together with awakening from the awakening-sequence of jeevan, which is of the nature of conscious nature, evidencing awakening within human tradition has to be done. The proximity of body and jeevan is necessary in human tradition, or coexistence is necessary. On the basis of the ten activities occurring in jeevan — namely, realisation, evidence, the enlightenment of realisation and resolve, the direct recognition and forming-of-images of resolve, the comparison and analysis of the forming of images, the tasting and selection of analysis — these ten activities are accomplished in jeevan. It is these very ten activities that come to be evidenced after jeevan-awakening. So long as the body is taken to be jeevan, only four and a half of the jeevan activities are able to be accomplished; the remaining five and a half activities are not able to be evidenced. It is precisely because of this crisis that the human being becomes tormented. Because of self-contradiction within himself, he is tormented by the crisis of jeevan's powers not being able to be evidenced. In the same way, the delusion of taking the body to be jeevan becomes the cause of problems, and, because of external torment, the human being remains agitated and beset with crisis. The effect of this — treachery, exploitation, war — has forever kept dancing, dominating over the head of the human family. Or, the human being has already come to regard this as unavoidable. What worse picture the human family could present than this is a point worth pondering. Whereas the human being has kept regarding this itself as glory.

By the coexistence-ist method, the study of jeevan and body, and the study of coexistence — the study of jeevan remaining indivisible within coexistence, the entire study of the whole of physical and chemical activities being accomplished within coexistence — has come within grasp. The study of the activities of chemical and physical substances, of the whole of the plant order, the animal order, and human body-compositions being accomplished, becomes accessible. On the basis of the medhas-system in the human body being the most enriched composition of all, jeevan's freedom-of-action and imaginativeness being manifested, thought and reflection, activity, and, on the basis of result and consequence, thought and reflection continuing to occur — now, toward the point of satiation of imaginativeness and freedom-of-action, presented themselves for research and investigation. Because we do indeed want to gain deliverance from treachery, rebellion, exploitation, and war. This is possibly most accepted among the best of men. For this, the need for coexistence-ist study remained.

According to coexistence-ist thinking, knowledge, knower, and known can be studied precisely. Jeevan was recognised as the knower; as knowledge, the knowledge of the holistic view of coexistence, the knowledge of jeevan, and the knowledge of humane conduct were recognised. What is meant by the known is precisely making human-aspiration and jeevan-aspiration meaningful with awakening; it is evidencing the human goal and the jeevan goal through undivided society and universal orderliness — this itself is the evidence of the known becoming meaningful.

In the same way, the meaningful explanation of seer, seen, and seeing also comes about. In the seer-status is jeevan; in the seen-status is existence-as-coexistence; in the sense of seeing, becoming a meaningful explanation in the form of the philosophy of existence comes about.

Attention, attender, and object of attention also become clear in this same sequence. With the object of attention in the form of awakening, attention being accomplished with understanding — in the sense of the sequence for evidencing understanding, the attender is the arrangement for recognising jeevan.

Doer, effect, and cause too are clear. In the doer-status is jeevan; in the effect-status is the human being; the god-human, the divine-human status-establishment; as cause, existence-as-coexistence is the ever-present establishment.

Regarding what used to be thought of in the forms of aim, means, and seeker: from the coexistence-ist viewpoint, the method of recognising the seeker is found to be very easy, and bound up with purpose. From the coexistence-ist viewpoint, the seeker comes to be recognised in the form of the human being. As the aim, jeevan-awakening becomes accessible. It is awakening alone that comes to be recognised in the forms of the human being's innateness, freedom, and right. Such innateness, freedom, and right, in the form of understanding, and such understanding, in the form of knowledge, science, and wisdom — again, innateness, freedom, and right are found to be made glorious. This itself is the evidence of awakening. In this way, how meaningful the aim is — that is, how meaningful awakening as the aim is — every individual can consider. As the means, for research and investigation: imaginativeness, freedom-of-action, evidence in the form of tradition — this itself is the means. When human tradition, as the bearer and carrier of evidence and authenticity — that is, evidence-endowed tradition — becomes able to evidence itself, it is in that very understanding that the evidence of universal orderliness, of undivided society, remains formed. Participating in being evidenced within universal orderliness is itself what keeps evidence universally accessible. It is precisely as a constituent part of universal orderliness that humane education-and-culture is found to become glorious. In human tradition, the glory of universality is found to become meaningful only together with knowledge, education-and-culture. This itself is the meaning of the tradition of evidence. Even so, the general

survey reveals it to be so — universal, that is, acceptance in all humankind. It was found to be extremely easy for every man and woman to take to heart, to test within oneself, and to determine the meaningfulness or meaninglessness of, the whole row of evidence formed by the coexistence-ist method across all the disciplines of practice, study, work, and behavioural orderliness.

The human being's aim is awakening. Its innateness, freedom, and right take the form of knowledge, science, and wisdom, whose evidence is the form of undivided society, universal orderliness through work-behaviour, which is linked to one another as a chain. In this, no link can be separated. The aim, as it stands in the form of tradition — human-aspiration, jeevan-aspiration — cannot possibly be evidenced without coexistence-ist knowledge, wisdom, and science. It can be evidenced only by the coexistence-ist method. Coexistence is no mystery or struggle. In natural form, it is a thing intelligible to, and illumined from, all humankind. Because every human being does indeed want to live and obtain happiness only in mutuality with someone. By this method the glory of coexistence becomes intelligible.

In the sequence of living together, it is the human being who first appears before one. Such a human being remains accepted only in some relationship or other. First the human being, in the form of the mother, and immediately after, in the form of the father; after this, brother-sister, friend, teacher (*guru*), preceptor (*acharya*), elders, and their likenesses, and in the neighbourhood too, similar relationships remain present in the tradition. In the process of living, nothing other than relationship comes to be clear. It is only on the basis of relationships that mutuality, and recognition and carrying-out in the sense of purposes, are found to occur. By this method, carrying-out without recognising, and recognising without carrying-out, are simply not possible. In this it becomes known that purpose is our desired attainment. Forming relationships and carrying them out for the sake of purposes is an inevitable condition; this itself we have given the names responsibility and duty. Together with relationships, responsibility and duty being self-motivated, of their own accord, is natural. What duty means is: making sure what to do, how to do it — this is duty. What is to be obtained, how it is to be obtained, ascertaining this and being propensive toward it, is called responsibility. In every relationship, being sure of the fact of what is to be obtained, and ascertaining how, what, is to be done to obtain it — these are links joined to one another. By the self-motivated method, this comes to be carried out in the greatest number of relationships. Of awakening

alone does such fulfilment become clear. In the deluded condition, the purpose and goal of relationships do not become clear. In deluded tradition, relationships have to be recognised most predominantly on the basis of sensitive tendencies, on the basis of the gathering of convenience, and on the basis of conflict. Human tradition, having endured these very viewpoints from the distant past, has come out desolate in respect of mental well-being.

Whereas, by the coexistence-ist method, the possibility of mental well-being is found to be fitting and accessible for all humankind.

In this same continuity, we are able to recognise all relationships in the sense of meaningfulness, in the sense of purposes, and in the sense of cognisance-replete purpose. Its clear form, in the form of universal orderliness and undivided society; its clear form, human-aspiration and jeevan-aspiration — in the sense of evidences, together with acceptance and together with evaluation, transforming work-behaviour and orderliness and conduct — is itself found to be the fruition of coexistence-ist knowledge, science, and wisdom.

Human-aspiration — the natural evidence of resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, coexistence.

Jeevan-aspiration — is reckoned in the forms of happiness, peace, contentment, joy.

## 3.18 Seer, Doer, Enjoyer (दृष्टा, कर्त्ता, भोक्ता)

In awakened human tradition, fundamental right is evidenced in the forms of seer, doer, and enjoyer. In most countries, spiritualists have made mentality ready to accept God, the Supreme Soul, Brahma, goddesses, and gods. For this, scriptures too were prepared, and, at the root of all religions, the good sayings written in the scriptures, in the form of divine speech, God's speech, or voice-from-the-sky, came to be accepted. This itself came to be regarded as scripture. On the basis of such a belief, the treatise, and, on the basis of the treatise, the method of preaching became prevalent. Such scriptures came to be prepared at a time when the human beings of one country were not capable of forming relations with all countries. When human beings became so capable, one community became ready to study the religious texts of another. In this it became known that the things which we accept according to our own religious text, another religious text does not accept. Then where is this divine speech? In this way religious differences of opinion only became more entangled. It is naturally accepted by all that there should be a religious text; along with this, the self-motivated conception also arises in mentality that there should be no dispute in religion; and if a dispute does occur,

[p. 151]

then what indeed became of religion! Human beings who think with such logical intellect do indeed exist upon this earth too. Despite this, what an undisputed religious text ought to be — this has not become available to us by the idealist method. For materialists, there is no need at all for a religious text. In this way we ordinary human beings kept on being tormented — both by internal contradiction, and, in the conflicts of the outer world, by treachery-rebellion, by exploitation, and by the fear of war, we kept on being afflicted. At the root of all these, we have kept believing only in God's grace, the gods' grace; whenever, at any moment, we obtained relief, obtained happiness, on any issue, we took it to be the grace of God, of a god, or of a guru. In this way we ordinary human beings kept enduring joy and sorrow. Trust in oneself was found neither with those called ordinary, nor with those called special. Whereas the matter of obtaining relief on the basis of trust has kept coming to be recognised in every human being. It is still the same even now. It is in whatever mutualities we are able to trust that we find relief — whether this be a moment, a day, or a year. Taking this itself to be God, a god, or a guru's grace, we keep engaging, for ever and ever, in the work of deceiving ourselves. Whereas it is by force of his own imaginativeness and freedom-of-action alone that the human being has succeeded upon this earth in giving concrete form to his own ideas. If this is not the produce of the human being himself, then whose produce is it? Whether we understand this or not, it is a necessary matter to be thought about. Along with this, it is precisely evidencing mental well-being that is resolution within human tradition.

The matter to which attention needs to be paid here is just this: whatever is reflected before us — is there, at its root, any doer-status or not, is there one who does or not? If, at its root, there is no doer, then the ascription of the status of universal-doer became wrong. If there is no doer, then what would the enjoyer-status be like? If this is not so, then what became of the seer-status? All these questions have kept arising one after another. The answer to these questions is not to be found by the idealist method.

When, by the coexistence-ist method, thinking and reflection began, and decisions began to be recognised, then it became known that whatever is functioning, in coexistence, in the form of the four states, all of it is indeed itself in the self-motivated doer-status. For this reason, activity, in itself, is clearly functioning in the sense of the developmental sequence in the forms of effort, motion, and consequence. In this same sequence, awakening was recognised in the forms of the immortality of consequence, the repose of effort, and the destination of motion. It has become possible to recognise the immortal status of consequence in the sense of development, the repose of effort, and the destination of motion, as awakening and awakening-completeness. In this way the human being himself becomes the knower of the awakening-status,

[p. 152]

the herald (*udghata*), or the unit worthy of evidencing — this becomes intelligible. From all the above study it became clear that, within coexistence — that is, within the pervasive substance — all single substances remain conjoined, and, by force of being endowed with energy and endowed with force, become functional by the self-motivated method; it was found that there is no need at all for anyone to make this happen. It was seen that all compositions, from the very smallest to the very largest, remain functional in themselves. It became intelligible that such functionality itself, in the forms of effort, motion, and consequence, is precisely what came to be known, in the developmental sequence, as the immortality of consequence from the developed status. The testimony of this is precisely the constitutionally complete atom-status, which is itself the conscious status.

In the developmental sequence, the doer-status was clear. In the conscious status, in the sequence of awakening, in the combined form of jeevan and body, the hope, thought, and desire to live, in the animal order and in deluded human tradition, became clear. In making clear the hope to live, wanting and not-wanting came to be evidenced. Along with this, in the form of wanting — that is, together with the wanting to live — the wanting to be happy came to be manifested from the human being. For this, giving concrete form to ideas became possible. From this it becomes entirely clear that the mentality of wanting to be happy manifested itself in the human being in the form of his own desire. On the basis of this, the doer-status having already existed, the expectation of the enjoyer-status arose, the expectation of enjoying happiness arose. For this, the rise of the seer-status became inevitable. Such an establishment of the seer-status is itself the evidence of understanding.

Understanding itself was found to become the seer-status, established as the knower together with the knowledge-element. By this method it is the human being alone who evidences the doer, enjoyer, and seer statuses. The human being is found to remain made glorious only in the combined form of jeevan and body. The glory of the conscious unit is precisely the attainment of the seer- and knower-status. As a result, in the sense of evidencing, evidencing himself within human tradition, the human being is found to be made glorious in the doer- and enjoyer-status. In this way it became clear that the physical, chemical world is in the self-motivated doer-status, while the conscious status, jeevan, together with its being endowed with awakening, was found to be in the form of seer and knower. As seer and knower, the utility, well-utility, and purposeful deployment of the doer-status gives rise to the good fortune of evidencing jeevan-aspiration and human-aspiration. In contrast to this, remaining deluded leads to affliction through problems. As far as the chemical, physical world is concerned, this has become clear on the basis of consequential-fruition within the actual condition, in the developmental sequence. In this way, the enjoyer-status has come to be formed together with the doer-status. Because the form of the consequence is seen in many actual conditions. To see means precisely to understand. Understanding is found to be the entire glory and grandeur present in jeevan. In this way, in the conscious world, the establishment of the seer-status becomes linked

[p. 153]

to this. This became clear as a result of awakening. This itself is the main matter. In the whole of chemical and physical activity, in the animal order, and in the deluded human being, testimony to being doer and enjoyer has forever remained formed. For this very reason, it is precisely together with the seer-status that the human being, as doer, has the arrangement for becoming meaningfully successful — not in the deluded condition. So be it: it is natural for the seer-status to be the glory of awakened tradition alone. Therefore, keeping human tradition intact is, for all of us human beings, the all-auspicious thread, the all-auspicious explanation.
