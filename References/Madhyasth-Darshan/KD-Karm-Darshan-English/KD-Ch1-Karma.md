Chapter One

Karma

By physical, vocal, mental, and by the distinctions of done, caused, and intended, every human being performs karma in nine ways. Every karma has result.

In the human being, physical, vocal, and mental karma are well known across all three times. By the distinctions of done, caused, and intended, these are reckoned in fully nine kinds, which are evident. The human being's field of work is only the natural, the social, and the intellectual, whose desire (*abhīṣṭa*) is happiness. It is neither more nor less than this. Without this, the repose of effort does not manifest in the human being. Therefore, activity together with aspiration is karma itself; motion together with awareness is the complete activity.

The pressure that lies in accepting the unacceptable — that itself is pain (*vedanā*). It is the environment alone that is the pressure-giving fact, and relativity, which is evident. Relativity alone is the influential fact of decline and development.

In every karma, five limbs remain contained: doer, cause, objective, result, and effect. It is only for the fulfilment of necessity that there is contact and relationship. Necessity is itself desire, at whose root remain contained society, sociality, its observance, its upholding, conduct, following, and cultivation.

All the activities carried out, in accordance with darshan, for determining necessities and for the acceptance of following them, occur in the form of desire, which is the manifestation of the awareness found in conscious activity.

From the fulfilment of every desire and necessity, the human being aspires to become happy.

[p. 2]

In every karma, the hope of happiness that exists — this itself, by the realisation-based method, is to be known, in natural form, as happiness, peace, contentment, and joy. It is the balance of desire, karma, and result that is the accomplishment of the desire, whose possibility exists. For intellectual, collective (social), and natural ascent, there is rule and process in the form of a determinate direction — this itself is the human being's desire.

The whole of karma has proven, in the human being, to be both the nourisher and the exploiter of long-hoped-for happiness. This is to be known in three parts: righteous action, unrighteous action, and mixed action. By the distinction of the goal of union and awakening, righteous action; and, in the form of offence and retribution, unrighteous action; and, in the form of enjoyment and retaliation, mixed action, are to be seen. The whole of karma, without exception, is relative to its result.

The result of the whole of karmas is to be known in four forms: moksha, dharma, *kama* (desire), and wealth (*artha*). There is no karma without desire. In the human being, desires are to be known by the distinctions of intense, causal, and subtle. Intense desires descend in the form of activity. Causal desires are of little possibility in the form of activity. Subtle desires are of very little possibility in the form of activity.

Intense desires — that without which one cannot live.

Causal desires — whatever impulses arise by union, coincidence, or happenstance, all of these are causal desires.

Subtle desires — in the human being, truth is some thing, just as dharma, justice, are some thing, for the evidencing of which no clear thought remains.

The whole of means is to be seen by the distinction of internal and external. Internal means are found in the forms of hope, thought, desire, resoluteness (*ṛtambharā*), and the evidences of realisation; external means are found in the forms of body and wealth. This itself is the clear form of material and conscious activity. It is precisely the attaining of the oneness, balance, and harmony of the material and the conscious that is the accomplishment of the desire. Every human being is engaged in effort for the accomplishment of the desire, which is evident.

For the aim (the desire), the surrender of the seeker and the deployment of the means are necessary. The means is itself wealth, which is to be seen in the forms of body, mind, and money. This, while being constituted from work, is also the means for the constitution of karma.

[p. 3]

In every karma, the sequence of experiencing and inference is available. This sequence of karmas will keep going on until realisation. True-experiencing alone is completeness. Factual condition, the objective, and the condition are well known by natural realisation of truth, and therefore the complete meaning of the whole of meaning is realisation itself.

The whole of karma exists only for the acquisition of wealth, whose purpose is realisation. The acquisition, use, proper use, and distribution of the whole of wealth also occur only for the fulfilment of desire. At the root of the fulfilment of desire is realisation alone. This is evident only, with awakening, in the individual and in the whole of humankind. Whatever is in existence is the darshan and knowledge of that very thing. Darshan and knowledge are themselves realisation.

In the human being, happiness is the desired equilibrium (*abhīṣṭa*). It is precisely the deployment of internal and external means for realisation that is enlightenment; otherwise it is non-enlightenment. The possibility of these two conditions is itself the basis of diversity.

It is precisely in accordance with the capacity of internal means (hope, thought, desire, resoluteness, the evidences of realisation) that there is control of external means. The internal totality is itself the conscious. Material-conscious nature is contained within knowledge (Omnipotence), and therefore it is controlled within it; in such equilibrium-control, development and awakening exist all the way to realisation.

In a life full of knowledge, the rise of freedom-from-delusion is seen, and, in its opposite, delusion is to be seen. Knowledge is itself realisation, resolve, desire, thought, and hope, whose complete manifestation is found to be based on the conscious unit's disposition toward awakening and capacity.

Freedom-from-delusion, being a complete capacity, is itself the accomplishment of the desire, because of being capable of doing proper and refined karma. The knowledge that comes to be manifest through the human being is to be known in three kinds: (1) physical, (2) intellectual, and (3) spiritual (coexistence). Their evident form is skill, proficiency, and erudition.

Principally, the practice of material science is from the whole of external means, and the practice of intellectual and spiritual science is from internal means. Material science and psychology are relative knowledge and study. The Spirit (Omnipotence) is absolute (determinate) knowledge. In relativity there is gain, expenditure, decline, development, and awakening. The proper use of the whole of word-powers is knowledge-producing, and wastage is ignorance-producing. Whoever wastes whatever, becomes deprived of it.

[p. 4]

It is precisely as the result of the whole of karma that there is wealth, calamity, good-desire, unrighteous-action, dharma, un-dharma, and the moksha-bondage pair. Wealth, good-desire, dharma, and moksha-bound awakening are happiness in the jeevan programme, and, opposite to this, calamity, unrighteous action, un-dharma, and bondage — this process, and the problem in jeevan, is itself sorrow and pain.

Dharma is all-round resolution. Wealth is body, mind, money. Ultimate wealth is realisation. In the awakened human being, what is meant by good-desire is that sensations remain controlled and evidenced through cognisance. Moksha is freedom from delusion.

"Wealth alone is happiness, righteous action alone is peace, dharma alone is contentment, and moksha alone is supreme joy."

The whole of desire has seven distinctions:

Wealth for the sake of moksha, moksha for the sake of wealth — these are, respectively, the best-of-the-best and the worst-of-the-worst. Wealth for the sake of dharma, dharma for the sake of wealth — these are, respectively, the middling-best and the worst. Wealth for the sake of *kama*, *kama* for the sake of wealth — these are, respectively, best, worst, and middling; and wealth for the sake of wealth, which is middling.

By the distinction of desire comes investigation; by the distinction of investigation comes the destination; by the distinction of the destination comes realisation; by the distinction of realisation comes value; by the distinction of value comes sociality and universal orderliness.

Three kinds of investigative tendency are found in the human being:

(1) truth-investigation, (2) desire-investigation, (3) object-investigation.

Object-investigative tendencies are to be seen in animals too. The remaining two are in the human being alone. For this reason, object-investigative tendencies are not free from offence.

Non-attachment from objects is itself dispassion, which is freedom from vitiations. Non-attachment from desires is itself further-detachment, which is the acceptance and observance of proper use.

[p. 5]

The object-investigative tendency is functional in self-interest; the desire-investigative in others'-interest; and the truth-investigative in ultimate-interest.

Behaviour full of self-interest is base and asocial; behaviour full of others'-interest is middling-best and social; behaviour full of ultimate-interest is best, social, and free. Behaviour full of ultimate-interest is itself the mentality of universal wellbeing.

Goal-based, value-based, and interest-based tendencies are found to exist.

In the human being, activities born of righteous action and unrighteous action, by intellect-based and interest-based unions, are well known.

*Kāsā*, *ākūti*, *medhā* — the intellect born of union; *mati*, *sumati*, *anumati* — the intellect born of righteous action; *amati*, *kumati*, *durmati* — these are the evident forms of the tendency born of unrighteous action.

The evident form of union-born *kāsā* is in the forms of the poet, the sage, the artist; *ākūti* is the divine worker (clearly bringing about comprehensive resolution); the deployment of divine purposefulness is in the form of the divine knower (undeluded); and *medhā*, together with complete knowledge, is the evident form of moksha (freedom from delusion). This itself is manifest and evident in the forms of compassion, grace, and mercy.

Righteous-action-born *mati* is in the forms of accepted (social) karma, conduct, and work-behaviour; *sumati* is righteous action (karma necessary for society), in the forms of conduct and work-behaviour; *anumati* is incomparable karma (worthy of being followed), in the forms of conduct and work-behaviour.

Righteous action is itself right intellect, right tendency, right understanding, right science, sociality, and true resolve.

Unrighteous-action-born *amati* is reckoned in the forms of unaccepted (asocial) karma, conduct, and work-behaviour; *kumati* is reckoned in the forms of vile karma (karma specifically forbidden), conduct, and work-behaviour; *durmati* is reckoned in the forms of wicked karma, conduct, and work-behaviour — such as coveting others' wealth, others' wife or husband, and inflicting pain on others.

[p. 6]

The object-investigative tendency is engrossed in unrighteous action; the desire-investigative intellect in righteous action; the truth-investigative intellect is engrossed, striving, and practising in union — that is, in awakening.

Every human being's natural propensity is evident in the sum of environment, study, and *sanskar*.

The environment is well known by the distinction of nature and the human-made.

Education and orderliness are themselves the evident form of the human-made environment.

The *sanskar* of the whole of humankind is clear in the "triad of investigation" (object-investigation, desire-investigation, truth-investigation).

The reckoning of the natural environment is connected to the balance of the measure of cold, heat, and rainfall found on every land, together with the three states other than the human.

Whatever natural environment is present on any land is based on the quantity of minerals and vegetation found on that land. This is based on the development of that land.

Before the event of the human being of the knowledge-state coming to be situated on any land, it is essential for animals and plants to have become abundant. Before this, it is necessary for there to be water.

It is only within the limits of some maximum-minimum measure of cold, heat, and rainfall, on every land, that animals and the human being have become capable of accomplishing their life-sequence and the programme of jeevan.

The balance of the natural environment is also cooperative for the human being's natural awakening; therefore —

A basis is necessary for balance.

The basis of balance for the whole of nature, without exception, is Omnipotence (*sattā*) alone, which is complete.

[p. 7]

The basis of the balance of every activity is rule, which is complete.

The basis of the balance of every behaviour is justice, which is complete.

The basis of the balance of every thought is resolution, which is complete.

The basis of realisation in every individual is the supreme truth that is coexistence, which is total.

The principal cause of natural and human balance and imbalance is the human being alone: in the deluded condition, the human being is free while performing karma and subject to it while undergoing its result. The awakened human being is free both while performing karma and while undergoing its result. In the awakened condition, the tradition will be one of doing after understanding; in the deluded condition, the tradition will be one of understanding after doing. It is the human being in particular who makes use of natural splendour, which is evident.

To maintain seasonal balance, using the necessary quantity of mineral and plant (forest) wealth in the land while keeping it secure, and, along with this, not creating obstruction in its production-process but being helpful to it — this itself is what is meant by natural law. This is entirely the human being's responsibility.

It is proper to make use of plants (forests) and minerals, whose possibility and sequence of production are clear, only in proportion to that very sequence; otherwise it is natural to become afflicted by natural calamities.

Education and orderliness are the sole means of maintaining collective (social) balance.

Social balance is the tradition of work-behaviour full of contentment with one's own wealth, one's own wife/husband, and compassion. In contrast to this, imbalance is precisely from coveting another's wife, another's husband, another's wealth, and another's pain, which is evident.

[p. 8]

At the root of the individual's thought-balance (intellectual balance) is found the activeness of necessary and unnecessary root tendencies. At the root of the human being's necessary root tendency remains simply *sanskar*-understanding. At the root of unnecessariness, delusion (compulsions) is to be seen.

Necessary root tendencies are evident, in sequence, in the forms of non-accumulation (prosperity), affection, learning, simplicity, and fearlessness (trust in the present); unnecessary root tendencies are evident in the forms of convenience, accumulation, hatred, ignorance, conceit, and fear.

The rule fit for natural balance, social balance, and intellectual balance is itself the necessary rule. This is the "triad of rule."

The decision to know and follow the necessary rules becomes clear from its proper use; the decision of proper use, from development and awakening; the decision of development and awakening, from the understanding and observance of intellectual, social, and natural rules. For the human being, following the necessary rules is an essential process for keeping the chain-sequence of one's own development and awakening unbroken. This itself is also the evident form of human jeevan, the awakening sequence, and the programme of awakened jeevan.

The immersion in, upholding of, and evaluation of cultural values is itself the human-made environment.

It is the *sanskar* contained within the human being that expresses his disposition. Human culture originates only in company with wholesome-*sanskar*-purity. This will keep remaining pure in *sanskar* only until it becomes complete.

The evident form of the completeness of *sanskar* is itself humaneness, supra-humaneness-replete disposition.

The evident form of the accomplishment of wholesome culture is humaneness-replete disposition.

The evident form of a corrupted culture is itself inhumaneness.

[p. 9]

Culture full of sociality becomes clear through humaneness. It is precisely for the nourishment of such humaneness that culture, civilisation, method, and orderliness exist.

These are mutually complementary, because civilisation nourishes culture, method nourishes civilisation, orderliness nourishes method, and culture nourishes orderliness.

In awakened tradition, the humaneness-replete cultural programme is nothing but the display of *sanskars*, because in awakened tradition there is no human being devoid of *sanskar*. Whereas deluded human traditions are reckoned in the form of communities [factions]. This itself is the cause of the whole problem. Problems are not evidence of *sanskar*, because all problems do indeed get resolved.

Visualisation and thought reveal, respectively, art and usefulness.

Thought remaining bound to wholesome *sanskar* is itself awakening.

Humaneness-replete *sanskar* is itself the indicator of universal culture. Below this — that is, for inhumane tendencies — becoming universal is simply not possible.

It is only from the observance of social rules that healthy, universal culture and civilisation arise. As a result, the wholeness of society and its unbrokenness are established.

The human being alone is such a unit as is not satiated only by his own happiness and convenience, but is satiated by understanding-full behaviour and orderliness. Therefore every individual wants the security and proper use of the convenience obtained, which is essential. In this way the human being has been proven to be a social and judicial unit, or is bound to become one.

Sociality is the combined form of intellectuality and physicality.

Physicality is under the control of intellectual capacity, because production and behaviour are not accomplished in the absence of thought.

[p. 10]

The perspectives of pleasant-unpleasant, beneficial-harmful, profit-loss have come to be deployed in physical enterprise and its use, which exist in the human being living through animal consciousness. The perspectives of just-unjust, dharma-undharma, true-untrue have proven decisive in behaviour and conduct within awakened human tradition.

The human being, through animal consciousness, gives rise to many problems. The human being, through consciousness, evidences resolution.

Without the study of good scripture there is no true aspiration and tendency; without true aspiration, no true love; without true love, no true fidelity; without true fidelity, no true establishment; without true establishment, no true conviction; without true conviction, no true realisation; without true realisation, no unveiling of good scripture; and without the unveiling of good scripture, the study of good scripture is neither complete nor meaningful.

It is only in true conviction and experiencing that fidelity is found in humaneness-replete conduct and just behaviour.

Without humaneness-replete conduct and just behaviour, just exchange and just benefit in mutuality are not possible.

Just exchange and benefit are themselves prosperity.

Unjust profit and benefit are themselves evident in the form of sorrow and problems.

Realisation-based enlightenment, as natural jeevan, is itself evident in the forms of humaneness and supra-humaneness.

Without proper use there is no true love, right tendency, righteous action, happiness, peace, contentment, and joy.

The human being is inclined toward true knowledge, toward study fit for the vision of truth, and toward performing karma and conduct, because the possibility and necessity of this exist — which is a lawful life.

[p. 11]

In humaneness, or in the humaneness-replete, every programme is method and orderliness. Its conduct is itself civilisation, and the observance of that tradition is itself culture.

In the human being, sorrow, disquiet, discontent — this itself is duality, and this itself is also the result of duality.

For the human being, the whole of circumstances is constituted only from the karma, worship, and knowledge-worthy capacity accomplished by him. These very circumstances take on the form of a proper or improper environment.

The conduct and behaviour of the awakened human being is itself orderliness, which is the cause of peace.

To whatever extent there is the human being's contact, to that extent there is no absence of responsibility.

In relationship, the observance of responsibility does indeed occur.

In relationship, duty-predominant responsibility, and in contact, responsibility-predominant duty, are present. This itself is sociality.

Through the process of lawful orderliness, together with comprehension of the cause of universally-agreeable peace, resolution, and prosperity, an auspicious environment is constructed. In such a condition, the practice of and engagement with good scripture becomes universally accessible.

Scripture and thought worthy of establishing undivided sociality, a social programme endowed with propagation, refined method-orderliness and law-endowed orderliness, the conduct-endowed individual, and the family worthy of encouraging him — this itself is the evident form of undivided society. This is the human being's abiding aspiration and evidence. This itself is longing, and this too is its possibility.

The human being needs peace and stability in every condition of jeevan. Peace and stability are found in the individual and the family through the practice, contemplation, and conduct of good scripture.

Capable of granting freedom-from-delusion toward the whole, clarifying the direction and sequence of awakening, determining human values in universal form, providing education fit for advancing from humaneness to supra-humaneness —

[p. 12]

it is scripture and the education-system alone that are capable of establishing a life full of peace and stability at every level. Without this, stability and peace in human life are not possible, which is clear.

It is only in the motion toward the sight of happiness, peace, contentment, and its stability (the goal together with the means) that the human being wants to perform the whole of karma.

Because the human being is free while performing karma, it is precisely from the results of karma that he becomes happy or unhappy, which is evident. The whole of karma performed, in humaneness, in the intellectual, social, and natural sphere, is happiness-giving. This same karma, within the bounds of inhumaneness, is sorrow-giving, which is evident.

At present, the human being is reckoned in four kinds.

(1) the meritorious soul, (2) the sinful soul, (3) the happy, (4) the unhappy.

In the human being's five states (individual, family, society, nation, and international), in the policy and manner of dealing with the meritorious soul, the sinful soul, the happy, and the unhappy, their own respective particularities are to be seen. In due sequence: within the bound of the individual, reverence, neutrality, contentment, and compassion; within the bound of the family, pride, disregard, cooperation, and service; within the bound of society, reward, correction, collaboration, and cooperative feeling; within the bound of the nation, honour, punishment (reform), guidance toward resolution, and cooperative feeling; within the international bound, protection, deliverance, promotion, and refinement — a life endowed with a manner-and-policy method full of this feeling, and endowed with law-and-regulation-behaviour-conduct, is successful; otherwise it is unsuccessful. Therefore, as follows:

Without society and sociality, no value or programme is accomplished in human jeevan.

There is no human being devoid of a programme.

The programme is itself karma.

Karma alone is helpful for decline, or for development and awakening.

There is no unit in material-conscious nature that is not in motion toward decline or development.

Knowledge, wisdom, and science alone are the one basis for the completeness of awakening. It is only for becoming endowed with such capacity that there is propensity toward refined karmas. This itself is to be conducted in the human being's five states and four dimensions.

An international order devoid of actual darshan; a programme devoid of resolution and an orderliness devoid of treasury; a society fallen from dharma, devoid of certainty; a family devoid of righteous character; an individual empty of good conduct — these are perpetually anxious.

The evident form of dharma is itself sociality. This itself is culture and civilisation.

It is precisely for the sake of non-disparity in the human being's natural mutuality that *sanskar*, culture, and civilisation are essential. This itself is also their usefulness.

[p. 14]

An international order incapable of holding a mystery-free darshan toward the whole; a (national) order incapable in the process of the protection and promotion of truthfulness; a society incapable of propagating truthfulness; a family incapable of following truthfulness; an individual incapable of truthfulness-replete conduct — these are afflicted by intense distress.

It is precisely from adopting necessities in excess of leisure (possibility) that every kind of ill tendency becomes effective. These occur principally in the form of exploitation, which is harmful to self and other.

The means that is body, mind, and wealth is itself leisure.

It is natural for the means to become less for unrestrained necessity, and for the means to become more in restrained necessities. Wastage is contained within unrestrained necessity.

For the human being, humaneness alone is the thread of restraint.

In the human being, form, strength, intellect, status, wealth, karma, worship, necessity, occasion, and leisure are all fitting.

Assurances that never come into activity, right in excess of merit, scripture devoid of principle, method devoid of basis, conduct devoid of direction — all of these are causes of distress.

The bases of method are reckoned to be of three kinds: (1) truth-basis, (2) karma-basis, and (3) object-basis.

From the laws and regulations that exist on the basis of scripture fit for clarifying truth-basis, the human being has the attainment of happiness, peace, contentment, and joy, which is the thread of glory natural to humaneness and supra-humaneness.

Law-and-regulation and policy based on truthfulness is itself the oneness of internationality. This itself is the unified threading of mutual states and nations, replete with coexistence. This itself is the evident form of a human life freed from individual, communal, and factional-ism.

[p. 15]

In the orderliness-process endowed with law-and-regulation determined on karma-basis, exchange free of self-other profit-loss occurs. This is not free from group sentiment.

In the governance-process endowed with law-and-regulation based on object-basis, self-other profit-loss occurs. In this there is no explanation of society at all. The community alone is its boundary.

Every karma performed with wisdom-full thought, together with the desire for worship, is beneficial at every level.

The capacity for darshan is found to rest on awakening; awakening, on aspiration and conduct; aspiration and conduct, on education and study; education and study, on orderliness; orderliness, on the basis of the capacity for darshan and understanding.

It is only in effort consonant with knowledge, work consonant with effort, result-consequence consonant with work, realisation consonant with result-consequence, and in that which is consonant with realisation, that knowledge has become clear — which is well known.

It is only in order to obtain the three kinds of karma-result that the human being performs work from beginning to end.

The whole of karma is accomplished by the distinction of the goals of self-interest, others'-interest, and ultimate-interest.

In the union of mediating-intellect, *sam*-intellect is found engaged in righteous action. The whole diversity of karma becomes free from a mutually-opposed condition in humaneness. The direct evidence of this is precisely the conduct of the "triad of rule."

In every union and separation, the conviction and deployment of values is found.

From union, sentiment; from sentiment, activity; from activity, union — this itself is the cycle-sequence of relativity and behaviour.

It is precisely the capacity to determine and carry out social values that has made clear personhood full of sociality, together with personhood marked by asociality. This itself is the indicator of the human being's decline and development.

[p. 16]

Activities of sentiment occur mutually by way of the high and the low kind, and it is precisely from these that acquaintance with their development comes about.

A family full of high sentiment, a society full of *sam*-mediating union, an orderliness-system and behaviour full of mediating-*sam* union — this itself is the universal-wellbeing programme.

There is no determination of righteous action without clear capacity for darshan. It is capacity for darshan alone that decides *sam*, *visham*, and mediating, activity-process, and purpose. Along with this, it establishes completeness in conduct, behaviour, orderliness, and the education system.

For behaviour, knowledge, wisdom, and science; for production, wisdom endorsed by knowledge, and knowledge full of science endorsed by wisdom — these are essential. Such knowledge is found to be manifest and put into practice according to the capacity and necessity of every awakened individual.

The determination of practical values occurs only through deliberation.

Deliberations are in accordance with the immortality of jeevan, the mortality of the body, and the rule of behaviour.

Practical values become meaningful in the sense of humaneness. It is on this basis that the triad of rule (intellectual, social, and natural) has been established.

Conduct and observance carried out in the human being's natural mutuality are meaningful by the method of coexistence, and are meaningless, and a problem, by the method of non-coexistence.

The evident form of actual knowledge-capacity is itself humaneness-replete conduct.

The capacity to understand is itself the natural unveiling of knowledge in mutuality.

The capacity to understand is expressibility (*vyanjaniyata*). The unit's root expressibility is nothing but conjointness within Omnipotence. The experiencing of conjointness is itself complete expressibility. It is precisely the qualitative refinement in the conscious unit's expressible capacity that is *sanskar* — this itself is the capacity for darshan and realisation

[p. 17]

— capacity. The darshan of measure and boundary, and realisation in truth, are well known. It is precisely the sequence of expressible capacity that reveals the awakening-sequence and awakening itself.

The human being is present, manifest, in the form of coexistence, as beholder and beheld — this itself is the background of darshan.

For manifestation to be evidenced, it is necessary for the beholder to remain. The beholder alone is established by expressible-capacity.

Nature is a collection of infinite units. This itself is the aggregate of the beheld.

Every beholder is also the beheld for another beholder. Every unit is a beholder in accordance with its own awakening.

The programme and conduct, from beginning to end, of human jeevan are reckoned to be of three kinds: (1) truth-conduct, (2) popular-conduct, and (3) object-conduct. These are, in sequence, in the reckoning of the best, the middling, and the worst.

Humaneness-replete conduct is itself the natural evidence of ascertainment. Ascertainment itself is evidenced in disencumberance (*nivṛtti*). Disencumberance is itself impetus and wisdom; impetus and wisdom are themselves accordance and adherence; accordance and adherence are themselves unveiling; unveiling is itself manifestation; manifestation is itself the evident; the evident is itself evidence; evidence is itself experiencing; experiencing is itself capacity, merit, and worthiness; capacity, merit, and worthiness are themselves state-fullness; state-fullness is itself potency; potency is itself glory; and glory is itself conduct.

The seer-status alone is the counterpart of the capacity for darshan, and behaviour is in accordance with it — which is well known. In the absence of thought, no work-behaviour is accomplished, or becomes evident, through the body. From this it becomes clear that it is thought alone that is at the root of the whole of the activities carried out through the body. The body is not thought. It is the medium for propagating thought. From this conclusion, thought is additional to the body, and this is a conscious activity.

Ideational capacity is situated in the human being's five states.

For this very reason all humankind is equal in capacity.

[p. 18]

For the refinement of ideational capacity, the true path and auspicious yoga-practice are well known. It is precisely qualitative change, through consciousness-development-value education, in *sanskar*, that is universally auspicious. Again, this itself is ideational capacity. This sequence is a complete orderliness right up until it is accomplished through humaneness- and supra-humaneness-replete conduct. It is successful through the observance, adherence, and cultivation of the "triad of rule"; otherwise it is unsuccessful.

Union-worthy practice — the practice of meeting/union. After union, acceptance is to be seen in the form of jeevan-awakening, and deviation in the form of inhumaneness.

Every human being is engaged in effort toward enlightenment. The evident form of enlightenment is itself humaneness-replete conduct. Humaneness and supra-humaneness are themselves glory attained with orderliness, complete.

The programme of jeevan is itself karma. This itself is conduct.

Conduct for prosperity, art, and comprehension is found in the humaneness-replete human being.

Conduct for true comprehension and naturalness is evident in the human being replete with humaneness and divine humaneness, which is their disposition.

For the human being, conduct replete with humaneness and supra-humaneness alone is utterly useful and necessary.

The human being's five states are mutually complementary. Their unified-threading is itself undivided sociality. This comes from the observance of the "triad of rule" being universal to all.

Every discovery and investigation is an individual-based unveiling. It becomes universally accessible through the medium of education and propagation. This itself is the process of universal generalisation.

[p. 19]

Whoever has potency and glory, it is he who has discovery, because before this, clear knowledge of it did not exist at all within the human category. In that time — that is, before the year 2000 — compared with the understanding that remained among human communities, the Madhyasth Darshan is a discovery, an investigation.

Manifestation, in, from, and for coexistence, is itself discovery. The generalisation-process of discovery is itself education; this itself is consciousness-development-value education.

For the human being, however much scope there is for ascent, there is that much scope also for decline. Decline-oriented activity is itself offence or error. The evident form of this itself is sorrow, disquiet, discontent, and the delusion of non-coexistence.

In the chain of decline-oriented jeevan, three causes of offence are to be seen: (1) deficiency, (2) excessive hope, and (3) ignorance. Along with this there are also attachment, hatred, untruth, conceit, fear, indolence, disease, and failure. There is an arrangement for the removal, resolution, and avoidance of these — in sequence, deficiency by production and practice, excessive hope by wisdom, ignorance by knowledge, attachment by dispassion, hatred by affection, untruth by truth, conceit by simplicity, fear by fearlessness, indolence by endeavour, failure by valour and renewed application, disease by medicine-diet and regimen — which is an opportunity for the human being. This itself is necessity.

The non-wastage of scientific capacity is itself wealth not becoming calamity.

The deployment of wisdom is itself the wholeness of society, which is heavenliness.

Science (proficiency, skill) is the fitness for controlling, through quantification, that which is less developed than the human being — which has proven fully useful in the field of production, and partially in behaviour. Whereas wisdom, through the analysis of the self (of the human being), clarifies social values.

[p. 20]

In coexistence, it is knowledge alone that is the beheld, the human being the beholder, and the sight is in accordance with human-ness — this is clear. Therefore, in coexistence, knowledge, in coexistence, behaviour and resolution come to be evidenced, and as a result orderliness comes to be evidenced.

Practical value is itself stable value.

Right up to relationship and contact, the human being is endowed with occasion for behaviour.

There is no contact or relationship devoid of value.

Expectations are contained in every mutuality. This itself is, at root, relativity. This itself is necessity.

It is only on the basis of the knowledge-perceptible and the sight-perceptible that there is the classification and determination of direct-perception, inference, and testimony-activity.

The realisation of the continuity of proximity is itself direct perception; the possibility of the continuity of proximity is itself inference; existence beyond the possibility of proximity is itself testimony-activity.

The infinitude of nature and completeness in Omnipotence are themselves the evidence of existence beyond inference.

By the distinctions of gross, subtle, and causal, there are direct-perception activities.

The direct-perceptibility of the gross by ordinary intellect, of the subtle by special intellect, and of the cause by particular intellect, is well known. On this very basis, the evidence of inference and testimony-activity also remaining in gross, subtle, and causal form is established. Form and property are gross; property and disposition are subtle; disposition and dharma are causal activities. Therefore —

In behavioural-productive karmas, tendencies are found at three levels: (1) independent, (2) imitative, (3) adherent.

The functionality of independent tendencies becomes clear in those perspectives with respect to which these have expertise.

Those who are desirous of becoming experts are found engaged in imitative activity.

[p. 21]

Those in whom the desire for expertise has not been awakened are found to remain engaged in karma and behaviour only through cultivation in adherent activity. These are the indicator of the sum of development and opportunity. Opportunity is principally orderliness and education alone.

Independent-activeness is found accomplished by the capacity for the proximity-experiencing of gross, subtle, and causal activity; imitative-activeness by the capacity for the proximity-experiencing of gross and subtle activity; adherent-activeness by the capacity for experiencing of gross activity alone. This itself is the evident inter-difference in the capacity of expressibility.

In independent activeness, autonomous control-capacity; in imitative capacity, the capacity to be controlled by one's own volition; in adherent activeness, the capacity to be disciplined — these remain contained.

It is only from being engaged in a programme in accordance with universal aspiration that faults are removed in all conditions. This itself is auspiciousness-granting.

It is precisely others' wealth, others' wife/husband, and others' pain that are obstacles to behavioural, social, and material advancement and to awakening.

Attachment, hatred, ignorance, and conceit have proven to be obstructive to intellectual awakening.

Fear is obstructive to the development of the capacity worthy of spiritual experiencing (the experiencing of coexistence).

From the wastage of natural splendour comes seasonal-imbalance, and from that arises distress, which is evident.

From contentment with one's own wealth, one's own wife/husband, and compassionate work-behaviour and conduct comes social happiness and balance; from non-accumulation (prosperity), affection, learning, and simplicity comes intellectual happiness; from fearlessness comes the experiencing of spiritual joy. This itself is the purpose, thought, and experiencing of the physical, the intellectual, and the spiritual — of the individual, and the unified-threading, balance, resolution, and prosperity of individual-family-society-nation-and-international. This itself is universal aspiration for equilibrium.

[p. 22]

Through the whole of karma, the human being has made the wish and the effort to experience establishment in truth, ascendancy in dharma, continuity in justice, and the usefulness of things. For this, truth-oriented, dharma-oriented, justice-oriented, and object-oriented efforts have been made. This is evident.

The control, acquisition, use, and proper use of physical substances; the bearing and upholding of social values, conduct, and continuity; the comprehension and realisation of truthfulness-truth — these themselves are the establishments. Without these the human being is neither satiated nor free from delusion.

The acquisition of wealth in production and service, prosperity in the proper use of wealth, balance in restrained use and proper use, resolution in universal dharma, and realisation and joy in truth-truthfulness are all well known.

The joy of experiencing is itself the eternal establishment; conduct, behaviour, orderliness, method, and education in accordance with dharma and justice are themselves resolution and immortal establishment; object-tendency is found established only by a slight establishment.

Activity, karma, substance, process, result, and powers — these are mutually complementary. Activity is itself substance, substance is itself process, process is itself result, result is itself powers, powers are themselves activity and karma. All of these are to be seen in substance.

Effort, motion, and consequence-fullness belong to activity; its projection to karma; meaningfulness to status; developmental-ness to process; usefulness to result; wave, pressure, and effect clarify the state-fullness of power-fullness. All of these are the projection or the changeable manifestation found within the bounds of substance.

In relative form, material powers, and, in the sense of expectation, conscious powers, are state-bound; and, in absolute form, the Spirit-Omnipotence is complete in state.

Material powers are well known in the forms of heat, light, electricity, attraction, and sound; conscious powers are well known in the forms of hope, thought, desire, resolve, and experiencing. The foundational Omnipotence of material-conscious nature is itself the Spirit, which is absolute Omnipotence.

[p. 23]

In mediating activity, comprehension and experiencing occur within natural Omnipotence.

*Atma* alone is mediating activity, and Omnipotence alone is mediating.

Material powers are principally kinetic; conscious powers are awareness-endowed.

The capacity to accept deficiency, presence, and dissolution is itself sensation. This is, in sequence, pain in deficiency, humane sensation in presence, and address together with the dissolution of deficiency. This itself is right comprehension. This itself is the prior sign of realisation.

For deficiency to be transformed into presence there is application and production. For presence's natural completeness there is conduct, behaviour, and orderliness. The dissolution of deficiency is itself presence.

Presence is itself fundamental distinctiveness, value, and meaning.

The complete meaning of every meaning is realisation alone. Therefore, in the absence of awareness there is no determination of value, presence, and fundamental distinctiveness. It is precisely in the capacity of awareness that the particularity of deciding and deliberating upon the direction, time, quantity, motion, use, destination, union, separation, decline, awakening, propriety, impropriety, and prohibition-of-method of state-fullness lies contained. Until this becomes universally instructive and fit for immersion, there remain doubtfulness, the bounds of dispute, and delusions. As a result, factionalism and confrontation are impending.

Decisive capacity is evident in the forms of cause, property, and calculation; deliberative capacity is evident in the form of the enlightenment that clarifies the immortality of *atma*, the mortality of the body, and the rules of behaviour.

In comparison with material powers, conscious powers are far more capable. The direct evidence of this is that, in the absence of conscious powers, the activity to be accomplished through the human body is not achieved.

Whatever fuel the human being provisions for the body, he expends powers in a greater quantity than that.

[p. 24]

The capacity of the conscious powers, which are reckoned in five forms, is evaluated and experienced only in their own mutuality. The mind (*mun*), endowed with hope, experiences *vritti* endowed with thought. *Vritti*, endowed with thought, beholds the mind endowed with hope. *Vritti*, endowed with thought, experiences *chitta* endowed with desire. *Chitta*, endowed with desire, beholds *vritti* endowed with thought.

*Chitta* endowed with auspicious desire, and *buddhi* endowed with true resolve, is the evidence, within coexistence, of the awakening of the experiencing-full *atma*. The experiencing-full *atma* beholds *buddhi* endowed with resolve. This itself is the aggregate of realisation. This itself is complete awakening.

The more-awakened beholds the darshan of the less-awakened; the less-awakened recognises the more-awakened. Therefore the awakened human being recognises the seeker of awakening, and the seeker recognises the awakened.

The awakened human being places trust in the seeker, and the seeker remains disciplined together with the awakened human being.

It is in human jeevan alone that the particularities connected with the gross, the subtle, and the causal are to be seen. In these three conditions, it is happiness, peace, contentment, and joy alone that are expected.

The human being's gross life is in expectation of happiness and peace; the subtle life, in expectation of peace and contentment; and the causal life is in anticipation of joy and supreme joy — for which this awakening is the natural evidence. It is precisely the all-round programme in accordance with this that is awakening. This itself is also the physical, intellectual, and spiritual attainments. This has become successful only through a programme replete with humaneness and supra-humaneness.

The deployment and disposal of the powers found in material-conscious nature lie only in origination, potency, and dissolution. In a perspective beyond this there is no disposal or deployment of property.

The beginning-to-end aim of the whole of disposal is itself the completeness of awakening.

It is precisely in the unit's natural completeness of awakening that the capacity for address has become manifest.

[p. 25]

Of origination, potency, and dissolution, potency alone is the universally-accepted event. It is only in the completeness of awakening that the establishment of potency-fullness is found. Before this, union, separation, and conjunction are found engaged in behaviour, karma, and pastime within relativity. All these conditions are the indicator of the sequence-succession natural to awakening.

Karma is result-bearing. The deluded human being is subject to it while undergoing the result. It is precisely by force of this universal rule that the human being is bound to become free from delusion with respect to karma and its result-bearing-ness.

It is to be seen that doubtfulness, apprehension, and ignorance occur with respect to karma performed by the human being in the deluded condition and its result — even though these are not desired in, from, or for the human being.

The property deployed in potency is itself mediating; that deployed in dissolution is *visham*; that deployed in origination is *sam*. This itself, in sequence, is ultimate-interest, self-interest, and others'-interest karma. In the form of behaviour, it is evident in the conditions of the undeluded, the deluded, and the deluded-undeluded.

The *sam*, *visham*, and mediating powers are, in sequence, *rajoguna*, *tamoguna*, and *sattvaguna*.

The conduct of a person endowed with *sattvaguna* is, together with behaviour, full of the capacity for wisdom and science, together with values such as compassion, respect, and love.

In a person endowed with *rajoguna*, the proper use of science occurs, together with values such as patience, courage, and good-naturedness.

In a person endowed with *tamoguna*, the wastage of the capacity for science occurs, together with undesirable vitiations such as envy, hatred, conceit, and egotism.

It is precisely conducting oneself in accordance with truth-connected method-orderliness, thought, conduct, behaviour, education, direction, karma, and system that is, in the human being, the observance of the dharma of the individual and the collective.

[p. 26]

It is precisely the human being's observance of his own dharma that is wholeness, universality, and boundlessness, which is, in itself, coexistence, sociality, prosperity, balance, control, restraint, fearlessness, non-disparity, simplicity, and generosity. It is in this that the bearing of social, stable values such as compassion, affection, generosity, pride, respect, tenderness, reverence, love, and gratitude occurs. This itself is also the human being's abiding wish. This itself is the beginning-to-end attainment of healthy sociality.

For the abundance of individuals full of righteous character, the awakened human being's cooperation and encouragement, their proper education and protection, and circumstances favourable to them, are themselves the evident form of world peace. Opposite to this is unrest, which is clear.

It is precisely the observance of the rule of the "triad of behaviour" that is righteous character. This itself is the evident dignity in the human being's five states. This itself is natural fidelity, and it is in this that science and wisdom have been fulfilled in their entirety. As a result, a healthy orderliness-method and education-system become effective.

What is meant by the "triad of behaviour" is physical, vocal, and mental behaviour carried out by the awakened human being.

The whole of consequence, refinement, and change is to be seen in five kinds:

(1) in existence that is coexistence, (2) developmental sequence, (3) development, (4) awakening sequence, (5) completeness of awakening.

The material state, with consequence, is purposeful in the form of complementing the actual condition. The prana state, with consequence, is purposeful in the forms of pulsation, and, with growth, compositional actual-condition and complement. The jeevan state, with change and consequence, together with the hope of living, is purposeful together with the complementing of the actual condition. The human being is reckoned in the knowledge-state. This is a tradition natural to awakening and the completeness of awakening, through refinement. This itself has been found to be evidence in the form of complementing the actual condition.

In a life of enjoyment and excessive-enjoyment-ism, the human being is not capable, and cannot become capable, of making himself healthy and of providing education for the conduct-based awakening of the coming generation. This burning fact is itself the principal cause of the need for a change and refinement of thought. Enjoyment is not the basis of culture.

[p. 27]

In the absence of culture, it is not possible for civilisation, method, and orderliness to be beyond doubt. Therefore this itself is evident in the forms of vexation, anguish, agitation, anger, indignation, conflict, rivalry, terror, fear, and distress. This is not what the human being desires. Therefore he is bound toward progress in the direction of the desired. This itself is the possibility, in the human being, of a change and refinement of thought. Along with this, there is opportunity for qualitative arising in *sanskars*.

The evident form of qualitative arising in *sanskars* is itself the observance of the "triad of rule." By this very method, the capacity to uphold social values, together with restrained tendency, becomes established as natural disposition. What is meant by restrained tendency is the natural evidence of universal orderliness. It has been seen by living according to this method.

This itself is also universal aspiration.

Refined thought is itself wisdom-full science. This itself has been established in the forms of proficiency, skill, and erudition.

Every accomplishment has occurred through application, behaviour, and realisation, which is evident.

In the consequence of enjoyment, the animalistic human and the demonic human are to be seen.

In the consequence of thought, the awakened human and the divine human are evident.

In the divine human there is no possibility of any consequence, refinement, or change, because the divine human is situated at the destination. His continuity alone is what is to come.

It is science and wisdom-full capacity alone that yields the inference of: the form of substance; the property contained in form; the effect contained in property; the disposition contained in effect; the capacity contained in disposition; the motion contained in capacity; the method contained in motion; the orderliness contained in method; the sovereignty contained in orderliness; the pervasiveness contained in sovereignty; the potency contained in pervasiveness; the world contained in potency; and the world contained in Omnipotence. This itself is the attainment of refinability. Inference is to be known prior to evidence.

[p. 28]

Science has been applied both upward and downward, whereas wisdom is the establishment only of upward application. What is meant by science's upward application is this — it is clear that, as upward application, telecommunication becomes accessible; and it became clear that, from downward application, comes the martial system (the mechanical war-system). At the root of pollution on the earth are to be seen mineral coal, mineral oil, and radioactive substances in the form of fuel-residue. In the correct application of science's power in accordance with wisdom, no untoward event such as fear and terror occurs. In the condition of purpose natural to wisdom, no ill effect is established.

The arising or non-arising of wisdom-full capacity is what is found, not the wastage of wisdom-full capacity. Whereas the wastage and the proper use of science-full capacity are both evident. In this way it is established that the control of science is from wisdom alone, and not that the control of science is from science.

Progress toward development is itself upward-facing; compulsion toward decline is downward-facing — this is clear.

Every state of development is linked in a chain to further development beyond it. Therefore every state is bound for further development ahead.

In progress toward development there is the experience of enthusiasm, delight, and joy. Opposite to this, from motion toward decline, there is dejection, discouragement, helplessness, and distress.

Upward-facing science is engrossed in the work of potency through pure *rajoguna* and together with *sattvaguna*, which is the actual fulfilment of science. Along with this, opposite to it, in science, there is science engrossed in destruction and over-indulgence through tainted *rajoguna* and together with *tamoguna*, which is the indicator of the complete misuse of science.

It is the humaneness-replete human being, and awakening-fullness beyond this, that is reckoned in upward-facing life, in which fundamental valuableness such as compassion, simplicity, sacrifice, austerity, benevolence, truth, non-violence, non-stealing, non-possession, and dignity is conducted. This itself is auspicious for self and other.

[p. 29]

For a life steeped in wicked character, being terror-stricken by fear is helplessness, which is the principal cause of pain to self and other. This itself is the fear of the inhumaneness contained within the human being. This itself is the root form of asociality and non-coexistence.

The capacity to hold affection for the human family is itself the continuity of trust and contentment. This itself is also the enthusiasm and impetus for further development.

A relationship devoid of trust is not successful. In a condition devoid of relationship there is no accomplishment of karma.

The observance of every social value has succeeded only through trust.

In just behaviour, resolution-full thought, and a life full of true realisation, there is an utter absence of afflictions — this itself is universal wellbeing.

It is only through the deployment of wisdom-following science that the life of every state of the human being is beautiful in every part. This itself is the karma-tendency and attainment that is beneficial to all humankind — this itself is universal wellbeing.

The karma-tradition endowed with knowledge-sanctioned wisdom and science is itself karma that benefits the world. This itself is the human being's abiding hope, aspiration, necessity, and opportunity.

"May there be universal wellbeing; may there be everlasting wellbeing."
