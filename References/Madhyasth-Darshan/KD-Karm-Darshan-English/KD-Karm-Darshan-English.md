## Alternative (*vikalp*)

1. The study of humans could not be completed through the unstable, uncertain, physicochemical-centric method of science, nor through mystery-based idealistic contemplation. Both these ideologies categorised humans as mere living beings (*jeeva*).

    As an alternative, through the existence-based human-centric contemplation method of Madhyasth Darshan (Co-existentialism), the human being is identified and established in the knowledge order.

    According to Madhyasth Darshan, the human being is the knower, and existence as coexistence is the object to be known (understandable and acceptable) — this is the knowledge of the holistic view (*darshan*). Along with this, the knowledge of *jeevan* and the knowledge of humane conduct, which validate coexistence, are now accessible for study.

    I have presented this existence-based human-centric knowledge — Madhyasth Darshan and Co-existentialism — in the form of treatises (*shastra*) for study.

2. Prior to this existence-based human-centric contemplation, my initiation was in spiritual knowledge and Vedic tradition-based worship and action (*upasana*–*karma*).

3. According to the Vedanta view, "Brahma is truth, the world is an illusion (*maya*)," while also stating that the world of living beings originated from Brahma. In the language of tradition: *upasana* refers to the rituals and ceremonies for becoming deities (Vedic worship); *karma* refers to all actions aimed at attaining heaven; in *Manu Dharma Shastra*, daily duties (*nitya karma*) for the four classes (*varnas*) and four stages of life (*ashramas*); in ritual texts (*karmakanda*), sixteen kinds of rituals from conception (*garbha sanskar*) to death (*mrityu sanskar*). By studying all these, my mind presented a question -

[p. 5]

4. If Brahma is "truth, knowledge, and infinite," how can the world of living beings originated from Brahma be false? In the process of seeking answers to these questions from contemporary Vedic scholars and experts:

    I was assured that the unknown becomes known in the state of *samadhi*. On the basis of scriptural support, I resolved to perform *sadhana*, *samadhi*, and *samyama*. In the states of *sadhana*, *samadhi*, and *samyama*, I attained complete understanding of the entire existence as coexistence through the method of study and realisation, as a result of which this Alternative (*vikalp*) emerged in the form of the treatises of Madhyasth Darshan (Co-existentialism).

5. According to idealistic scriptures and mystery-based God-centric thought and tradition, knowledge is unmanifest and inexpressible. In Madhyasth Darshan, knowledge is manifest and expressible, understandable through study, and verifiable through behaviour, making evidence easily accessible to all.

6. According to materialism, which is based on instability and uncertainty, science is considered to be knowledge, in which rules are treated as human-made. In its alternative, according to the existence-based human-centric contemplation of coexistence, existence is stable, development and awakening are definite, and all rules are natural.

7. Existence is not merely physicochemical; rather, physicochemical entities and *jeevan* entities are present as inseparable in the omnipresent reality. This is the core formula of the treatises of Madhyasth Darshan (Co-existentialism).

8. Where I began this bodily journey, my ancestors were renowned as Vedic scholars (*vedamurti*). In my home and village, the Vedas, and Vedic thought related to Vedanta, Upanishads, and Darshan, were continuously heard as language, sounds, and melodies. In my family tradition, Vedic-aligned worship (*upasana*), adoration (*aradhana*), prayer (*archana*), and libations (*tarpan*) were performed.

[p. 6]

9. In our family tradition, high-caliber scholars were naturally service-oriented and industrious in their practice of behaviour and action. I accepted the industriousness and service-oriented tendencies, but question marks remained in my mind regarding their scholarship.

10. **First question:** If Brahma is truth, how can the world and living beings that originated from Brahma-truth be false?

    **Second question:** How can Brahma alone be the cause of bondage (*bandhan*) and liberation (*moksha*)?

    **Third question:** What is evidence (*pramana*) — is the word the evidence, or the bearer of the word? Is the authoritative statement (*apta-vakya*) the evidence, or its enunciator? Is the scripture the evidence, or its propounder?

    **Fourth question:** In the context of independent India, a constitutional assembly was formed, yet the constitution accepts elected representatives without defining nation, nationality, and national character.

    How can public mandate and representatives be genuine when they arise from vote–money alliances?

    The constitution states secularism, yet explicitly mentions numerous castes, sects, and communities.

    The constitution states equality, yet details reservations and their procedures.

    In this democracy, the election of public representatives is governed by alliances of vote and money. What kind of democracy is this?

11. To find liberation from this web of questions, on the suggestion of contemporary scholars, Vedic experts (*vedamurti*), and honourable sages:

    (1) I accepted *samadhi* as the sole path pointed out for making the unknown known.

    (2) I accepted Amarkantak as a suitable place for *sadhana*.

    (3) I began the practice of *sadhana* in 1950; maturity in *sadhana* came in the 1960s.

    [p. 7]

    (4) In 1970, the state of *samadhi* was attained. In this state of *samadhi*, my expectations, thoughts, and desires remained silent. I realized that in this state, the occurrence of the unknown becoming known was zero. This state lasted twelve (12) to eighteen (18) hours daily.

    Following the sequence of *samadhi*, *dhyana*, and *dharana*, I accepted the self-motivated system of *samyama*. After two years of *samyama*, the validation of *samadhi* was established. The process of accomplishing *samyama from samadhi* also took 12 to 18 hours daily. As a result, I realised the entire existence as coexistence, which was presented in the form of the treatises of Madhyasth Darshan (Co-existentialism).

12. **Coexistence:** The entire insentient and sentient nature is saturated and eternally present in the omnipresent reality.

    **Within coexistence:**

    (1) In the development progression of atoms, "hungry" (unsaturated) and "undigested" (unstable) atoms, and constitutionally complete, satisfied atoms that emerge through evolution, are understood to exist as *jeevan*.

    (2) A constitutionally complete atom is understood to exist as a sentient unit — *jeevan*.

    (3) From hungry and undigested atoms, molecules, and biological cells (*pranakośa*), all physical, chemical, and biological order structures form, and from these atoms and molecules, the earth and other planets are clearly understood to have formed.

13. In existence, on this earth (which is a physical structure), through chemical processes and composition, biological cells formed and flourished as vegetation. Following this, biological cells formed animal bodies, and finally, the human body and tradition were completed and understood.

[p. 8]

14. **Within coexistence:** The human tradition is understood as the combined form of body and *jeevan*.

    In, from, and for coexistence, coexistence was understood to be eternally effective; this itself was understood as the natural progression (*niyatikram*).

15. **Natural-law: Only by the natural method of coexistence:**

    (i) Four orders in existence: material order, biological order, animal order, knowledge order; and

    (ii) Four planes in existence: biological plane, delusion plane, deific plane, divine plane; and

    (iii) Development progression and development; awakening progression and awakening.

    And the eternal glory of the awakening-based human tradition, replete with human-ness, orderliness, and participation in the overall orderliness, was understood. I accepted this as the universal-auspicious formula and recognized the aspiration for universal welfare in all humans. As a result, I have presented before humankind the natural explanation of consciousness-development value education, constitution, conduct, and orderliness.

[p. 9]

## Main points propounded in Madhyasth Darshan

Omnipotence is mediating and omnipresent. Nature in Omnipotence is generative-degenerative and mediating activity, and is limited; therefore Omnipotence is state-complete.

Insentient and sentient nature in Omnipotence is state-dynamic; therefore nature is immersed in Omnipotence — nature is interwoven with Omnipotence. Because nature is saturated in Omnipotence, nature is fully energised; therefore nature is active. Hence nature is effort-, motion-, and result-bearing. As a result, nature itself is manifest in four orders. Therefore, in insentient–sentient nature saturated in Omnipotence, the sentient part affords appropriate opportunities for the capacities of experiencing in the knowledge order, and all four orders are bound to one another in the sense of completeness and wholeness.

Omnipotence is mediating; therefore nature saturated in mediating Omnipotence is controlled and protected. The nucleus (*madhyansh*) found in every atom is mediating activity; therefore generative-degenerative activities and relative forces are controlled and protected.

Infinite activity, or groups of activity, is nature — countable as insentient and sentient. Insentient nature, after completeness of development, attains the sentient stage by the natural-law method. The human being is the combined form of insentient and sentient, and is also a part of nature.

Constitutional completeness in the development progression is, in the awakening progression, activity completeness and conduct completeness in the deluded human. The awakened human, together with less-developed nature, provides nourishment to right-use and usefulness in vocation and behaviour. Behaviour with the human being is a duty to honour those more awakened, and to practise, study, and contemplate for greater awakening.

The deluded human alone is free while doing karma and dependent while experiencing result.

[p. 18]

On this earth the human being is in the awakening progression; he has the opportunity, desire, and possibility of becoming awakened. The chief characteristic of the awakened human is to be helpful to the less developed.

From the material order the biological order develops; from the biological order the animal order develops; and from the animal order the animalistic human of the deluded knowledge order develops. From the animalistic human develops the deluded demonic human; from him the humane human; from him the delusion-free deific human; and from the deific human, development and awakening are complete in the divine human.

The unit of the knowledge order is endowed with holistic-view capacity. The holistic view is in reference to insentient–sentient nature situated in Omnipresence.

Realisation knowledge and the holistic view are complete only in the state free from delusion. Therefore —

Freedom from delusion itself is awakening; awakening itself is enlightenment; enlightenment itself is sovereignty; sovereignty itself is supreme order; supreme order itself is undivided society and universal orderliness.

"The human being alone is primarily helpful in the decline and development of the human being."

*Jñānātmanorvijayate*

Chapter One

Karma

Through the physical, vocal, and mental modes, and through the distinctions of action done, caused, and intended, every human being performs karma in nine ways. Every karma has a result.

Physical, vocal, and mental karma are well known in the human being across all three times. Together with the distinctions of done, caused, and intended, they constitute nine evident kinds. The human field of action encompasses only the natural, the social, and the intellectual, and its desired end (*abhīṣṭa*) is happiness—neither more nor less. Without this, the restfulness of effort does not manifest. Activity together with aspiration is therefore karma; motion together with awareness is complete activity.

The pressure involved in accepting the unacceptable is pain (*vedanā*). The environment is the pressure-giving factor, and relativity is evident. Relativity influences both decline and development.

Every karma contains five limbs: doer, cause, objective, result, and effect. Contact and relationship arise only for the fulfilment of necessity. Necessity is desire, rooted in society and sociality, their observance and upholding, and in conduct, following, and cultivation.

All activities undertaken, in accordance with the holistic view, to determine necessities and accept their observance occur as desire. Desire manifests the awareness present in conscious activity.

Human beings expect the fulfilment of every desire and necessity to bring happiness.

[p. 2]

The hope present in every karma is for happiness. Through the realisation-based method, this is naturally known as happiness, peace, contentment, and joy. The balance of desire, karma, and result makes the accomplishment of desire possible. Human desire seeks intellectual, collective (social), and natural ascent through rules and processes that provide a determinate direction.

For the human being, karma has proved both to nourish and to exploit the happiness long hoped for. It is understood in three parts: righteous action, unrighteous action, and mixed action. Righteous action is directed toward union and awakening; unrighteous action takes the form of offence and retribution; and mixed action takes the form of enjoyment and retaliation. Every karma, without exception, is relative to its result.

The results of karma are understood in four forms: moksha, dharma, *kama* (desire), and wealth (*artha*). There is no karma without desire. Human desires are distinguished as intense, causal, and subtle. Intense desires readily become activity; causal desires have little possibility of doing so; and subtle desires have very little possibility.

Intense desires are those without which one cannot live.

Causal desires are impulses arising from union, coincidence, or happenstance.

Subtle desires concern such matters as truth, dharma, and justice when no clear thought remains for evidencing them.

Means are distinguished as internal and external. Internal means comprise hope, thought, desire, resoluteness (*ṛtambharā*), and the evidences of realisation; external means comprise body and wealth. This is the clear form of insentient and sentient activity. The accomplishment of desire lies in attaining oneness, balance, and harmony between the insentient and the sentient. Every human being is evidently engaged in endeavour toward this accomplishment.

For the aim—the desire—the seeker's dedication and the deployment of means are necessary. Resources (*artha*) are the means, manifest as body, mind, and wealth. Although constituted through work, these resources also serve as the means for constituting karma.

[p. 3]

Every karma carries a sequence of experiencing and inference. This sequence continues until realisation. True experiencing alone is completeness. The existent state, the objective, and the condition become known through the natural realisation of truth; the complete meaning of all meaning is therefore realisation.

The whole of karma exists for the acquisition of wealth, whose purpose is realisation. The acquisition, use, right-use, and distribution of wealth likewise serve the fulfilment of desire. Realisation lies at the root of that fulfilment and becomes evident, with awakening, in both the individual and humankind as a whole. Whatever exists can be viewed and known as it is; this holistic view and knowledge constitute realisation.

For the human being, happiness is the desired equilibrium (*abhīṣṭa*). Deploying internal and external means toward realisation is enlightenment; any other deployment is non-enlightenment. The possibility of these two conditions is the basis of diversity.

External means are controlled according to the capacity of the internal means—hope, thought, desire, resoluteness, and the evidences of realisation. Their internal totality is the conscious. Insentient and sentient nature is contained within knowledge (Omnipotence) and is therefore regulated within it; through such equilibrium and control, development and awakening continue up to realisation.

A life filled with knowledge gives rise to freedom from delusion; its opposite gives rise to delusion. Knowledge comprises realisation, resolve, desire, thought, and hope. Its complete manifestation rests on the conscious unit's capacity and its essential nature toward awakening.

As a complete capacity, freedom from delusion accomplishes desire by enabling proper and refined karma. The knowledge manifested through the human being is of three kinds: (1) physical, (2) intellectual, and (3) spiritual (coexistence). These become evident as skill, proficiency, and scholarliness.

Material science principally employs the external means, whereas intellectual and spiritual science employ the internal means. Material science and psychology are relative knowledge and study; the Spirit (Omnipotence) is absolute (determinate) knowledge. Relativity encompasses gain, expenditure, decline, development, and awakening. The right-use of word-powers produces knowledge, while their wastage produces ignorance. Whatever a person wastes, that person becomes deprived of.

[p. 4]

The results of karma include wealth, calamity, good desire, unrighteous action, dharma, un-dharma, and the pair of moksha and bondage. Within the jeevan programme, wealth, good desire, dharma, and awakening directed toward moksha constitute happiness. Their opposites—calamity, unrighteous action, un-dharma, and bondage—constitute sorrow and pain as process and problem in jeevan.

Dharma is all-round resolution. Resources (*artha*) comprise body, mind, and wealth; the supreme meaning is realisation. In the awakened human being, good desire means that sensations remain controlled and evidenced through cognisance. Moksha is freedom from delusion.

"Wealth alone is happiness, righteous action alone is peace, dharma alone is contentment, and moksha alone is supreme joy."

The whole of desire has seven distinctions:

Wealth for the sake of moksha and moksha for the sake of wealth are, respectively, the best-of-the-best and the worst-of-the-worst. Wealth for the sake of dharma and dharma for the sake of wealth are, respectively, the middling-best and the worst. Wealth for the sake of *kama* and *kama* for the sake of wealth are, respectively, best and worst; wealth for the sake of wealth is middling.

The distinctions of desire give rise to exploration; those of exploration, to destination; those of destination, to realisation; those of realisation, to value; and those of value, to sociality and universal orderliness.

The human being has three kinds of exploratory tendency:

(1) truth-oriented exploration, (2) motive-oriented exploration, (3) instincts-oriented exploration.

Instincts-oriented exploratory tendencies also occur in animals; the other two belong to the human being alone. For this reason, instincts-oriented exploratory tendencies are not free from offence.

Non-attachment to objects is dispassion, or freedom from vitiations. Non-attachment to desires is supreme-detachment, or the acceptance and observance of right-use.

[p. 5]

The instincts-oriented exploratory tendency is functional in self-interest; the motive-oriented exploratory tendency in others'-interest; and the truth-oriented exploratory tendency in ultimate-interest.

Behaviour full of self-interest is base and asocial; behaviour full of others'-interest is middling-best and social; behaviour full of ultimate-interest is best, social, and free. Behaviour full of ultimate-interest is itself the mentality of universal wellbeing.

Tendencies may be goal-based, value-based, or interest-based.

Activities born of righteous and unrighteous action, through intellect-based and interest-based unions, are well known in the human being.

*Kāsā*, *ākūti*, and *medhā* are forms of intellect born of union; *mati*, *sumati*, and *anumati* are forms of intellect born of righteous action; and *amati*, *kumati*, and *durmati* manifest the tendency born of unrighteous action.

Union-born *kāsā* becomes evident in the poet, sage, and artist. *Ākūti* manifests as the divine worker who clearly brings about comprehensive resolution; divine purposefulness is deployed through the divine knower, who is undeluded; and *medhā*, together with complete knowledge, manifests as moksha, or freedom from delusion. These become evident as kindness, grace, and compassion.

Righteous-action-born *mati* takes the form of accepted (social) karma, conduct, and work-behaviour. *Sumati* is righteous action—karma necessary for society—expressed in conduct and work-behaviour. *Anumati* is incomparable karma, worthy of being followed, likewise expressed in conduct and work-behaviour.

Righteous action comprises right intellect, right tendency, right understanding, right science, sociality, and true resolve.

Unrighteous-action-born *amati* is reckoned as unaccepted (asocial) karma, conduct, and work-behaviour. *Kumati* is reckoned as vile karma—karma specifically forbidden—and its conduct and work-behaviour. *Durmati* is reckoned as wicked karma, conduct, and work-behaviour, such as coveting another's wealth or spouse and inflicting pain on others.

[p. 6]

The instincts-oriented exploratory tendency is engrossed in unrighteous action; the motive-oriented exploratory intellect in righteous action; the truth-oriented exploratory intellect is engrossed, striving, and practising in union — that is, in awakening.

Every human being's natural propensity is evident in the sum of environment, study, and *sanskar*.

The environment is distinguished as natural or human-made.

Education and orderliness manifest the human-made environment.

The *sanskar* of humankind becomes clear in the "exploration-trio": instincts-oriented, motive-oriented, and truth-oriented exploration.

The natural environment is assessed through the balance of cold, heat, and rainfall on each land, together with the three orders other than the human.

The natural environment of any land depends on the measure of its minerals and vegetation, which in turn depends on that land's development.

Before human beings of the knowledge order can inhabit any land, animals and plants must be abundant; before they can be abundant, water must be present.

Only within definite minimum and maximum measures of cold, heat, and rainfall can animals and human beings on any land fulfil their life-sequence and the programme of jeevan.

The balance of the natural environment also supports the human being's natural awakening. Therefore:

A basis is necessary for balance.

Capacity filled with wisdom may arise or fail to arise, but it is not wasted. Capacity filled with science, by contrast, may be either wasted or rightly used. Science is therefore governed by wisdom, not by science itself.

Progress toward development is upward-facing; compulsion toward decline is downward-facing.

Every state of development is linked to further development and is therefore directed beyond itself.

Progress toward development brings enthusiasm, delight, and joy; movement toward decline brings dejection, discouragement, helplessness, and distress.

Upward-facing science works toward potency through pure *rajoguna* together with *sattvaguna*; this is science's actual fulfilment. Its opposite is science directed toward destruction and excessive enjoyment through tainted *rajoguna* together with *tamoguna*, indicating the complete misuse of science.

Upward-facing life is expressed by the humane human being and, beyond this, by complete awakening. It conducts fundamental values such as kindness, simplicity, sacrifice, austerity, benevolence, truth, non-violence, non-stealing, non-possession, and dignity, and is auspicious for self and other.

[p. 29]

A life steeped in wicked character becomes helpless under terror and fear, the principal cause of pain to self and other. This is fear of the inhumaneness within the human being and the root form of asociality and non-coexistence.

The capacity to hold affection for the human family sustains trust and contentment and provides enthusiasm and impetus for further development.

A relationship devoid of trust is not successful. In a condition devoid of relationship there is no accomplishment of karma.

Every social value is successfully observed only through trust.

Just behaviour, resolution-filled thought, and a life of true realisation are entirely free from affliction; this is universal wellbeing.

Only the deployment of science guided by wisdom makes every state of human life beautiful in every part. This karma-tendency and attainment benefits all humankind and constitutes universal wellbeing.

A karma-tradition endowed with wisdom and science sanctioned by knowledge benefits the world. It is the human being's abiding hope, aspiration, necessity, and opportunity.

"May there be universal wellbeing; may there be everlasting wellbeing."

[p. 30]

Chapter Two

Upasana (*upāsanā*) – Wisdom

Knowledge: — knowledge of the holistic view of existence, knowledge of jeevan, knowledge of humane conduct

Wisdom: — the immortality of jeevan, the mortality (*nashwaratva*) of the body, the rules of behaviour — together with knowledge — being evidenced in the human goal and the jeevan goal

Human goal: — resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, the natural evidencing of coexistence

Jeevan goal: — happiness, peace, contentment, joy

Upasana is an important part of human jeevan. It is the process of refining and transforming the fundamental tendencies, and this study also transforms *sanskar* and essential nature.

Upasana naturally conceives togetherness attained through proper means, requiring diligence (*pariśram*) and practice. Through practice, diligence, and study, the veracity of the gross, subtle, and causal becomes clear. Research into the relevant matter, the course of destiny, power, magnificence, special powers, and their governing rules—conceived through *anugaman* (the path to realisation)—is thereby established. Research differs across the physical, intellectual, and spiritual domains, and manifests directly as ratiocination, contemplation, resolve, and experiencing.

The gross, subtle, and causal (the Seer) together mean being endowed with the capacity to see, understand, apply, behave, and realise.

[p. 31]

Upasana is of three kinds: *kutastha* (abiding in the immutable), *rupastha* (abiding in form), and *atmastha* (abiding in the self). The process undertaken for complete realisation is *kutastha upasana*. The process undertaken to approach a form endowed with magnificence is *rupastha upasana*. Reflection itself is the process of *atmastha upasana*, to which the human being is bound. The refinement of tendencies is therefore the programme of human jeevan. The aspiration for completeness and the capacity for refinement are present in every state of the human being. The capacity for refinement comprises skill, proficiency, and scholarliness; its completeness is scholarliness. Nothing in knowledge and skill surpasses scholarliness, just as nothing in behaviour and production surpasses proficiency. The capacity for refinement is expressed in production and behaviour. Scholarliness is enlightenment, and enlightenment is education and orderliness. Until one is fully endowed with enlightenment, upasana remains an extremely helpful aid.

Deities (*devi-devata*) are classified into three kinds: terrestrial, cosmic, and celestial. This classification is based on their capacity for movement. The conscious unit is the combined form of subtle and causal activity. This is the jeevan-cloud (*jeevan-punj*); the deities are none other than this. The gross body is controlled by *pranavayu*, subtle activity by chitta, and causal activity by atma. The subtle and causal are never separated; this bears witness to immortality. Their signals become evident only in their operation of the gross body, and this operation is indispensable. In a state without the gross body, subtle activities are carried out solely according to signals from buddhi.

The three kinds of deity are: celestial (moving through limitless expanse), cosmic (moving within the expanse of a single cosmos), and terrestrial (moving only within the environment of a single earth). These correspond, respectively, to the divine self, the deific self, and the elemental self.

Complete conduct together with complete awakening characterises the divine self; alertness together with complete activity characterises the deific self; and intense attachment to objects, accompanied by apprehension and fear, characterises the elemental self. After a change of body, the demonic human and the animalistic human are reckoned as elemental selves; the humane human and the deific human are reckoned as deific selves; and, after relinquishing the body, the divine human is reckoned in the category of the divine self.

Elemental selves are in a state of absolute-oneness (*tadatmya*) with adhibhautik elements, deific selves with adhidaivik facts, and divine selves with the Spirit (*adhyatma*). Accordingly, these three categories are established.

[p. 32]

In the adhibhautik domain, behaviour is dominated by production; in the adhidaivik domain, production is guided by behaviour; and in a life filled with the Spirit, realisation-based thought, behaviour, and production follow in direct sequence. Behaviour and enterprise are therefore restrained and controlled in a life filled with the Spirit, orderly and disciplined in an adhidaivik life, and undisciplined and disorderly in a physical life. Only a life filled with the Spirit integrates the human being's four dimensions.

Absolute-oneness with adhibhautik elements serves the "instincts-quad" (*vishaya-chatushtaya*); absolute-oneness with adhidaivik social and behavioural values serves the "motive-trio" (*eshana-traya*); and the divine human attains absolute-oneness with the Spirit. Attachment, upasana, and dedication are employed, respectively, for these three.

The whole of insentient and sentient nature is saturated in Paramatma (Omnipotence); within this existence, the three kinds of self are therefore bound toward awakening culminating in realisation.

The deluded human is evidently afflicted, excited, or bound by relativity. This relativity remains observable only until the unit becomes free from delusion, its state of supreme excellence.

Control encompasses rule, inspiration, coordination, relativity, effort, motion, result, activity, conduct, union, separation, combination, status, state, and control itself; all are observable.

Upasana of divine selves is learning (*vidya*); upasana of deific selves is sub-learning (*upavidya*); and upasana of elemental selves is base learning (*kshudra vidya*). Upasana of divine selves is undertaken, with awakening, for freedom from delusion together with intellectual resolution and material prosperity; upasana of deific selves for intellectual resolution and material prosperity; and upasana of elemental selves for materiality alone.

Every jeevan is endowed with its own essential nature, which constitutes its standing. No further manifestation of essential nature is possible beyond this.

[p. 33]

Materiality alone has not proved sufficient for the human being, because material substances cannot satisfy the four human dimensions. Until production, behaviour, thought, and experiencing are fully integrated, the human being therefore remains bound to application, endeavour, and practice.

The upasana of divine selves directly yields science filled with wisdom, austerity (firm dedication), determination, decision, and the capacity for resolve. The upasana of deific selves yields partial wisdom, science, patience, courage, affection, trust, renunciation, and control and restraint in the activities of jeevan. The upasana of elemental selves yields science without wisdom, impatience, recklessness, attachment and malice, apprehension, and uncontrolled activities of jeevan. These three upasanas lead, respectively, to alertness-filled watchfulness, awakening-filled watchfulness, and apprehension-filled non-awakening—the delusion-free, semi-deluded, and deluded states.

A body constituted from *rasayanik* (chemical) substances and containing a brain is meat substance; plants provide vegetable substance. Their chemical substances are predominantly similar, yet the formation, mechanisation, and use of the meat-body differ fundamentally from the formation process and use of plant forms, as follows:

In meat-eating decorum, forceful slaughter and exploitation are inherently embedded events.

Within inhumaneness, vegetarian and non-vegetarian human beings differ in decorum, conduct, behaviour, and tendency, yet their purposes remain predominantly similar; the same is evident among animals. Therefore:

[p. 35]

when humaneness becomes available for study and is understood, both non-vegetarians and vegetarians agree in its favour. Once established in dedication, even the non-vegetarian becomes vegetarian. Trust and expectancy are alike in awakened human decorum. This is the point of truth toward which every kind of decorum waits for the establishment of human decorum—a site of possibility for the continuous excellence of awareness.

Humaneness and higher-humaneness make the originality of vegetarianism clear. Human decorum attains oneness—freedom from diversity—only through humaneness. The chief utility of upasana therefore lies in immersion in universal values.

Upasana methods whose resulting decorum establishes class-sentiment do not fully support comprehensive resolution, because class-sentiment ultimately tends toward conflict. Universality is indispensable to freedom from conflict.

All upasanas share a common aim: undivided society, universal orderliness, and universal welfare. Without the aspiration for universal welfare, one's own welfare cannot be accomplished.

Other desirable aims provide only the appearance of welfare, not its realisation. The human being is therefore bound to endeavour toward a programme aligned with universal welfare and to develop the capacity required to follow it.

Only the universal, humane observance of the intellectual, social, and natural "rule-trio" makes freedom from economic and communal class-sentiment possible and actual. All class-sentiment dissolves through this method. Upasana therefore becomes meaningful only through the desired understanding together with awakening.

The human being possesses three powers: activity-power, desire-power, and knowledge-power. These qualifications rest on each unit's capacity for awakening and its actual awakening.

[p. 36]

Upasanas exist only to develop the capacity for awakening, not for decline.

The progress of awakening involves no pain; it is the music or integrality of jeevan. Every kind of pain arises instead from wrongful activity and decline, and these constitute sorrow.

Pain may be intellectual, behavioural, or physical, and is observable as problem, offence, incapacity, and wastage. This is the direct form of non-awakening.

Deficiency in watchfulness and alertness is the cause of all affliction. Watchfulness and alertness manifest only on the basis of the capacity, ability, and receptivity present in the human unit. Therefore:

The awakening of activity-power means putting powers to right-use rather than wasting them through the sense-organs of sound, touch, form, taste, and smell. Within humaneness, the "rule-trio" determines right-use and wastage.

Only human consciousness and higher-human consciousness prevent the wastage of the *antahkarana*'s fundamental tendencies—hope, thought, desire, and resoluteness (*ṛtambharā*). This right-use is the awakening of desire-power.

Right-enlightenment together with complete experiencing provides the capacity to manifest knowledge. This is the awakening, or complete awakening, of knowledge-power. The resulting "awakening-trio" becomes directly evident in humaneness and higher-humaneness. It is the supreme attainment of human jeevan, the utility of empowered upasana, the aspiration of humankind, and universal welfare.

Jeevan-awakening directly manifests as the application of science filled with wisdom. It comprises watchfulness, undivided sociality, enlightenment, freedom from disparity, coexistence, education, method, orderliness, civilisation, culture, intellectual resolution, material prosperity, and the continuity of jeevan-awakening.

[p. 37]

Every human unit is bound to endeavour and upasana until complete awakening. These refine the fundamental tendencies, allowing a distinctive and cultured mentality, the capacity for thought, contemplation, and enlightenment, and complete realisation to manifest. These are the attainments of the best upasana and constitute completeness of study.

One who completely knows a discipline and is practised in its work, behaviour, and realisation remains continuously active in it. The completeness of an activity is therefore its continuity. It neither causes indifference nor makes another indifferent, because activeness exists for the sake of completeness, and completeness is continuity of activity.

Whatever acts for the sake of completeness continues after becoming complete, as seen in complete activity and complete conduct. Completeness cannot be measured by incompleteness, nor the awakened by the unawakened, because the lesser value is contained within the greater, not the greater within the lesser. Every human being can measure what is less developed, can produce together with less-developed nature, and can gain knowledge of what is more developed. The human being is therefore bound to behave with equals. This is the imperative of sociality and the natural glory and dignity of human jeevan. That dignity provides the impetus to behave with one's equal and to practise toward greater awakening; this is real upasana.

Wisdom—the human goal—and jeevan-value—resolution and prosperity—are the direct results of upasana, within which sociality is naturally contained.

Realisation-enlightenment projects as non-accumulation (prosperity), generosity, and kindness. Enriching the natural foundational values of generosity and kindness is the life-essence of sociality and its music; this alone is what the human being thirsts for. Wisdom manifests intellectual resolution and social values through sustaining them.

Wisdom and science are the chief characteristics of indirect knowledge (good-behavioural knowledge). Realisation is its final state; before realisation, only the authority of inference is established. Realisation exists only in the truth of the existent state, the objective, and the state.

[p. 38]

Without indirect knowledge, no discernment of the eternal and non-eternal, proper and improper, just and unjust, righteous and unrighteous, true and false, desirable and undesirable, or seen and unseen is established; nor is the authority of indirect knowledge itself.

Without authoritative knowledge of the eternal and non-eternal, the human being cannot dedicate oneself to one's own-dharma. Human dharma is happiness; happiness is just conduct; and just conduct is the observance of humane conduct and the "rule-trio." This is the human being's own-dharma. The human being is happiness-natured.

No unit is without propriety. Animals do not violate the propriety of essential nature, plants the propriety of property, or substances the propriety of form; this is their dignity. Likewise, resolution-happiness is dharma in the human being, and dharma is propriety. This is human dignity and trust; trust is the direct form of propriety.

"There is no happiness in relationship and connection devoid of trust." No human being is without relationship and connection; hence the imperative of one's own-dharma. Any deficiency of capacity, ability, or receptivity in its observance becomes sorrow, affliction, problem, and non-awakening.

Until one becomes fully complete with fullness in one's own-dharma and with the capacity, ability, and receptivity fit to observe it, there is no absence of upasana in the form of study, for the purpose of acquiring knowledge.

Creed, sect, and class disappear only in the human being's own-dharma. This is the direct dignity of capable upasana: "This is auspicious." Upasana must contain the aim, seeker, and means together; their integrality brings success, and their separation brings failure. The right-use of whatever power and means are available in each state advances awakening; this is upasana.

There is no such power or means which has no deployment or use, because the accumulation of power or means is not practical.

[p. 39]

Self-delight is grounded in wisdom; wisdom-filled buddhi grounds science; science informs art, composition, discernment, and thought; discernment guides tasting and welcoming; and the sentiment-filled mind remains continuously balanced and resolved. Consequently, atma is suffused with supreme bliss, buddhi with joy, chitta with contentment, vritti with peace, and mun with happiness.

The activities of the sense-organs and their field of operation define the boundary of direct knowledge. Within this domain, attainments related to the four objects are its outer limit; nothing beyond them can be attained through it.

The human being is not confined to the domain of objects, because four dimensions are established within the human being.

The human being alone is bound to accomplish production, behaviour, thought, and experiencing. These constitute necessity, occasion, possibility, and orderliness.

Through restraint alone, dignified grandeur manifests in every sphere of human activity, as follows:

Mastery in speaking truth, grounded in enlightenment of truth, ends fear and distrust and gives rise to delight and enthusiasm.

Mastery of non-violence brings freedom from the fear of opposition and gives rise to affection and generosity. Non-violence means not participating in violence, whether done, caused, or intended, and whether physical, vocal, or mental.

Dedication to non-possession destroys pitiableness and baseness and gives rise to patience, fortitude, and generosity.

Establishment in non-stealing destroys cunning and distress and gives rise to satisfaction and contentment. Non-stealing means not committing theft.

[p. 40]

Establishing value (sentiment) toward the world removes disparity between the desired deity and the seeker; the essential nature of that deity thereby enters the seeker.

Through the practice of consistently remembering the meaning of a word—that is, of a mantra—its meaning and essential nature become attainable. Every meaningful word is a mantra.

Greater dedication to awakening eradicates pride and ego and gives rise to learning and simplicity.

Restraining physical sensitivity brings purity of mind, from which mental strength arises.

Destroying attachment to one's own body removes attachment toward the world. Only an intellect filled with a trustworthy aspiration for universal welfare can pour generosity, kindness, grace, and compassion into the world and attain knowledge and realisation of coexistence in the world's foundational Omnipotence.

Without restraint, there can be no refinement of the fundamental intellectual tendencies, no strength of intellect, no realisation of social values, no vision of conscious activity, and no resolution or restraint.

"Gratitude alone is the beginning-to-end basis of restraint."

Ingratitude directly evidences lack of restraint. Gratitude must be cultivated from infancy and adolescence.

Continuous kindness-filled thought remedies miserliness and enables the experiencing of supreme delight.

When the *antahkarana* (mind, vritti, chitta, buddhi) is brought into absolute-resonance with an aim, it becomes intense in that very aim. Thus, by attaining absolute-resonance with water, distress is dispelled, giving rise to the experiencing of satisfaction.

By attaining absolute-resonance with fire, disease is dispelled and health is gained.

By attaining absolute-resonance with air, instability is dispelled and stability is gained.

[p. 41]

By attaining absolute-resonance with light, ignorance is dispelled and knowledge arises.

Contemplation of others' welfare increases enthusiasm and delight.

Benevolence toward others brings fame.

Resolving problems brings glory.

Contemplation of universal welfare develops talent.

The practice of recognising others' virtues destroys one's own vices.

A biased intellect is incapable of grasping the virtues of the opposing side, because it is not free from envy, malice, hatred, neglect, and delusion.

The progression of awakening contains no self-versus-other faction; orderly sequence, rule, and justice are therefore accomplished within it. Non-enlightenment is the cause of problems, and enlightenment—awakening—is their sole remedy. This is the direct dignity of upasana.

Environment is indispensable to upasana, especially the human-made environment of education and orderliness.

Education completed by enlightenment gives personality its direction and magnificence. The orderliness capable of protecting and promoting it is sovereignty.

The generalisation of enlightenment is the direct form of qualitative change. What one person attains through upasana becomes common through education and orderliness.

[p. 42]

Without the perspective of qualitative change, the deluded human being evaluates solely by measure. This gives rise to class struggle and conflict.

Personality is the conduct of the "rule-trio" natural to humane behaviour. Actively contributing to such personality is duty, human endeavour, and enlightenment.

Upasana directly gives the individual personality and duty because it is, by nature, education and orderliness. It is therefore enlightenment; in the human being, enlightenment is education and orderliness.

There is no diversity in truth and truthfulness.

Symbols are multiple and indicate only delusion.

An intellect that mistakes the symbol for the goal gives rise to communal excitation, pride, and ego.

To the extent that it is incomplete, symbol-and-goal-oriented upasana proves secondary and unsocial because it neither clarifies nor attains the human goal. Disparities therefore arise. Such symbols are distinguished by place, caste, language, art, image, composition, statue, memorial, and word.

Intellectuality, not the body, directly manifests human personality, because the intellectual capacity of each individual receives respect or neglect, honour or contempt, and esteem. Intellectuality is therefore the seat of the fundamental tendencies and the capacity to receive and transmit signals. It encompasses necessity, desire, thought, attachment or non-attachment, refinement, effect, readiness, and intellectuality itself.

Natural and capable upasana is an indispensable human programme, establishing the progression from inhumaneness to humaneness and from humaneness to higher-humaneness.

[p. 43]

Within coexistence, natural-recurrence arises through the moment-to-moment unbroken tendency (*anukshan-vikshan-vritti*).

The moment-to-moment unbroken tendency is the disappearance, at every moment, of mediating obstruction.

*Anukshan* means that contemplation of coexistence continues at every moment and authenticity naturally becomes evident in the course of thought. This is natural-recurrence.

Complete knowledge of nature saturated in Omnipotence — the complete holistic view — is the disappearance of mediating obstruction at every moment. It dispels deluded notions of presence and absence and establishes natural standing and state.

In the absence of natural knowledge of nature saturated in Omnipotence, and of realisation-rooted knowledge, fear and temptation sustain all delusion-rooted activity, behaviour, and thought. This causes all affliction.

Time is the duration of activity. Within this duration, imposed thought and desire are unnatural, while non-imposed thought and desire are natural.

Only the human unit possesses the capacity for non-imposition, even during the progression of awakening. Imposition arises from delusion.

To regard what is as more than it is, less than it is, or not to understand it is imposition. This is ignorance, incapacity, and delusion.

Imposition or non-imposition occurs only in relation to the stable state of insentient and sentient nature saturated in Omnipotence, and to the natural completeness of Omnipotence.

Every unit comprises form, property, essential nature, and dharma; this is its functionality. Knowledge of their count, measure, and purpose indicates a capacity dependent upon nature. This is the evidence and the naturalness of that capacity.

All activities are fundamentally observable through the distinction between form and word.

[p. 44]

Without complementarity, there is no advancement in a unit.

Until completeness, the unit remains inclined toward complementarity and usefulness.

Complementarity manifests the characteristics of decline and development in the unit; this is also its chief utility.

The properties beginning with sound that manifest in a unit are relative powers. No unit is without property. Therefore:

Natural aspiration is present in every human being. Naturalness is dharma and manifests as happiness, peace, contentment, and joy. It clarifies conception directly.

Every result of karma has proved capable of either nourishing or exploiting human happiness.

The realisation progression of truth and truthfulness meets no obstruction because its orderliness and the links of awakening are dense. Therefore:

Naturalness is free from imposition. Imposition is deficient or excessive evaluation, and deficient or excessive self-evaluation is unnaturalness.

Self-evaluation occurs within the domain of the "*-tā*-trio".

There is no human being on this earth who does not fall within the domain of the "*-tā*-trio".

There is no separation from dharma, because conception belongs to dharma itself. Its direct form is humane and higher-humane conduct, the chief characteristic of naturalness. Conduct that advocates inhumaneness is therefore unnatural.

Nature is complete within itself; this is duration and is contained within the whole. It is coexistence, natural to completeness within the whole: the ever-presence of completeness and its emergence in the human being through knowledge. This makes awakening imperative.

[p. 45]

Removing unnecessary imaginings from the human intellectual domain qualitatively refines the capacity for the holistic view. This is the combined outcome of qualitative *sanskar*-transformation, education, and the programme of jeevan.

The excellence of the capacity for the holistic view is the moment-to-moment unbroken tendency. This is the capacity of mediating activity, and mediating activity is the Seer.

Mediating activity culminates in complete control of generative and degenerative activity. This capacity is freedom from moment-to-moment mediating obstruction.

Through *sanskar*, the human being engages the capacity for intellectual orderliness, through activity and cessation, within the domain of natural desire and objects.

The intellectual process and orderliness aligned with atma (mediating activity) manifest truth-resolve and a mentality filled with truth-imagination, renowned as completeness in the knowledge of karma and upasana. This capacity manifests deific and divine humaneness; it is complete awakening.

Intellectual orderliness attached to desire manifests humane and deific-human essential nature, together with a corresponding mental environment. This capacity—social consciousness—becomes complete through alertness and watchfulness.

Object-attached tendency and process give rise to inhumane conduct, observable as bestiality and demonic-ness, together with a mentality suited to those conditions. This causes latent and dormant imagination and indicates non-awakening and incomplete watchfulness.

Latent and dormant imaginings are refined only when one becomes inquisitive toward the good (awakening). Indifference toward demonic and bestial tendencies is thereby established, and wisdom arises.

Inquisitiveness toward the good arises through one's own *sanskar*, method-prescribed study, and a favourable environment.

[p. 46]

Method-prescribed study develops skill, proficiency, and scholarliness.

Study and environment form the orderliness capable of *sanskar*-transformation; their qualitative change is inquisitiveness for self-enlightenment.

Self-enlightenment is the chief characteristic of inquisitiveness for truth. Therefore:

Conception is the inclination toward *anugaman* and cultivation, manifested as decorum.

For progress, conception is indispensable.

Conception leads toward awakening and attachment toward decline; they correspond, respectively, to cessation and unnecessary tendency.

Conception is right-wisdom, and right-wisdom is the discernment of truthfulness. At root, it is universal goodness and welfare.

The conception of realisation takes the form of truth-enlightenment, and conception as right-enlightenment is truth-resolve. When projected, it bears fruit as good karma, upasana, and conduct. Its projected value manifests as courage, valour, generosity, kindness, grace, and compassion.

Right-enlightenment occurs only in truth; falsehood gives rise only to imagination and appearance.

Karma joined with baseness, wretchedness, and cruelty is inauspicious.

Self-value brings satisfaction in both activity and cessation. Therefore:

[p. 47]

Karma and upasana free from falsehood, pride, and arrogance, and joined with truth, simplicity, naturalness, and courtesy, give rise to the good.

Continuity in the aspiration for truth gives rise to the conception of the goal. Production, behaviour, thought, and experiencing manifest directly only in knowledge.

Knowledge is complete science sanctioned by wisdom.

Firmness in truth and truthfulness constitutes a jeevan filled with the good.

The experiencing of truth is everyone's desired end (*abhīṣṭa*).

It is the mind alone that operates, through the brain, all the activities accomplished by the body. All the nerves are controlled by the brain.

Mind is the fundamental form of word. Through the brain, mind transmits, operates, and controls waves filled with the sentiments of tasting and welcoming; word lies at their root.

A word that guides movement toward awakening is a mantra. Control is the process that orders the sequence toward attaining the goal. The sentiment or value contained in a word is its meaning. Meaningful words direct movement toward awakening because the meaning of a word is an entity in existence.

The indispensability and usefulness present in sentiment constitute its importance. The definite direction within such indispensability constitutes its firmness, or right resolve. Completeness in right resolve is realisation. These are the cultivated fundamental activities of mind, vritti, chitta, buddhi, and atma, respectively. The meaning of sentiment lies in its being.

Activity-power as *kāsā*, desire-power as *ākūti*, and knowledge-power as *medhā* are the directly manifest divine intellects. Their arising alone evidences mental strength, intellect strength, and atma strength.

[p. 48]

Without the awakening of the powers-trio—desire-power, activity-power, and knowledge-power—renunciation (freedom from delusion) and love cannot be evidenced.

Body-centred jeevan gives rise to beings other than the human; mind-centred, intellect-centred, and atma-centred jeevan give rise to the human being alone.

Without freedom from delusion (renunciation) and a love-filled jeevan, there is no welfare of self and other.

The meaning of welfare is the natural continuity of awakening.

Without karma, upasana, and knowledge that are beneficial to self and other, peace does not become complete.

In a jeevan filled with dispassion, absence itself disappears. Dispassion alone leads to the atma-goal.

The influence of the atma-goal gives rise to self-enlightenment.

Self-enlightenment gives rise to self-illumination.

Self-illumination gives rise to self-trust.

Self-trust brings the decay of ignorance.

The decay of ignorance gives rise to self-standing.

Self-standing gives rise to supreme-detachment.

Supreme-detachment brings the accomplishment of dominion.

The accomplishment of dominion brings supreme opulence and complete satisfaction.

[p. 49]

The attainment of opulence gives rise to natural-recurrence.

Natural-recurrence brings realisation of freedom from delusion.

For the human being, realisation of freedom from delusion is the ultimate human endeavour.

This is complete awakening and the standing of the Seer's plane.

"May there be eternal good, may there be good for all."

## 3.1 Atomic Structure and Molecular Composition (परमाणु संरचना एवं अणु रचना)

Human beings have long desired to understand atomic structure.

By the method of decomposition, the human being used thought, reflection, and application in order to reach the fundamental substance. Even at the end of decomposition, the expectation of some definite fundamental substance remained.

This line of thought led to the following conclusion: if any substance is repeatedly decomposed, a state is eventually reached in which the resulting units function in a self-motivated (*svayamsphurt*) manner. Such a functioning unit is called an atom. Human beings have also sought to understand how it functions.

An atom has one or more particles (*ansh*) at its centre and one or more particles revolving around it in orbits. There is no atomic constituent below this level. The possibility that the number of particles at the centre or in the orbits may increase arises only in human imagination.

Two main questions arose in this connection: (1) How is mutual recognition established among atomic particles? (2) Having recognised one another, why do they enter into orderliness? Further reflection showed coexistence to be the principal cause. Every unit is submerged, saturated, and enclosed in the omnipresent reality (Omnipresence), and is thereby energised. Omnipresence permeates them all. Thus, from the subtlest to the largest unit, being saturated in Omnipresence is the basis of being energised. To be energised is to be forceful and endowed with magnetic force. By virtue of this forcefulness, even the subtle atomic particle has the propensity to recognise others and participate in orderliness. Human beings need to accept mutual recognition as evidence of this strength. Human beings alone possess the innate right to recognise propensity, action, and outcome. When they recognise one another, they present themselves within orderliness. When they err in recognising this meaning, they function and behave within disorderliness, and the problem

[p. 55]

arises. Thus every atomic particle, by virtue of being saturated in Omnipresence, functions within orderliness through mutual recognition. The functional expression of orderliness is definite conduct.

Because every atomic particle is energised, mutual recognition is natural to it; the purpose of that recognition is combined conduct. This was understood as an eternal law. A further formula follows: constitution and organisation arise only through such recognition, and their ever-present meaning is orderliness. It is therefore easy to understand that every complete unit is indivisible within the omnipresent reality, and that this indivisibility is coexistence. Human beings can accept that coexistence is eternally active.

Coexistence itself became the fundamental formula and the basis of orderliness. At the root of every state of motion, coexistence is well established. In this way it became clear that coexistence itself is the basis of the diverse action (activity) and of the conduct of each one of them.

Differences in conduct arise only from differences in the number of functioning particles in atoms. On this basis, human beings have attempted to determine the species of atoms and their number. They have so far proposed 108 species of atoms on earth and continue to hope that another species will be discovered.

On understanding the atom in this way, we humans become clear in an orderliness-centred mindset. As a result, it became natural for the human being to be ready to recognise the innate orderliness within himself. In this progression it became natural for the human being to understand humane conduct. From this it is found that value, character, and morality — their combined expression, communication, and manifestation — have, within the human tradition, the basis of orderliness, from it, and for it.

This fact applies throughout the animal, plant, and mineral orders, in all the species reckoned in solid, liquid, and rarefied forms: the atom is at the root of them all, and jeevan too is an atom. The atom of every species performs definite conduct. The human being, as the combined form of jeevan and body, therefore also wishes to live within orderliness through definite conduct. The grandeur of atomic structure becomes clear only in conduct, and conduct is expressed through its activity.

[p. 56]

Atomic composition also makes clear that atomic particles become evident only in coexistence with one another. A particle that remains separate in the limitless void (Omnipresence) cannot evidence the intent of orderliness. It therefore continues whirling in the limitless void in search of another particle. Ultimately it is absorbed into another atom or forms a definite atom with another atomic particle. Orderliness thus becomes manifest only after an atom is constituted, and conduct becomes manifest through that orderliness. This is evident in physical, chemical, and jeevan activity-processes.

That atomic structures are based upon one or more particles has already become clear; this too has become clear: that the atom of every species is self-actuated. Their conduct is definite.

Every atom consists of a nucleus (*madhyansh*) and particles functioning in orbits. At least as many particles are present at the centre as in the orbits, and there may be more. Here, "orbits" means the paths of particles circling the centre; an atom may have one or several. When there are more than four orbits, there is always the possibility of decrease, and when there are fewer than four, of increase. Such atoms exist as their respective species in various numerical states. Their weight depends on the number of particles inherent in the nucleus. Atoms endowed with inherent weight recognise one another and gather together; this is called a molecule. Molecules occur in solid, liquid, and rarefied forms. Solid compositions are evident in large formations such as the earth. Liquid substance is considered the initial substance of the chemical world. All these are constituted through compounding. Human beings do not contribute to this process; they only study it. Study is necessary because it enables recognition of the innate functional orderliness in these units. The activity-process of biological cells, as an outcome of the composition of the nourishment-element and composition-element, becomes clear only on the basis of chemical substances.

Atoms and molecules alone perform the physical-chemical activity-process. A molecule therefore works continuously within orderliness. Its continuing state of motion is evidence of that orderliness.

[p. 57]

Every atom and molecule is a rung of its own species. What is meant by being a rung is its existent-state, its continuity. Just as two particles are in one existent-state in the form of an atom. This is one step. Their having definite conduct is itself their grandeur. This very having of definite conduct becomes the basis of the further step, and remains so. Atoms and molecules with various numerical particles remain with their definite conduct established. This is a wondrous self-motivated form of orderliness, clear before the human being.

Each physical atom and molecule that participates in chemical activity can be studied as a state of motion. Their combination becomes evident in large compositions such as the earth. This indicates that, before chemical action could begin on earth, all the necessary species of atoms and molecules were brought together by the destiny-borne innate method — the method of coexistence. The propensity and processes of compounding are accomplished only after atoms and molecules of all species are present. The earth itself is well-established testimony to this.

All these manifestations in the material order are based on species of atoms and molecules, and those species depend on the number of atomic particles. Changes in that number continually produce corresponding outcomes. This activity-process is called absorption-displacement (*prasthapan-visthapan*). It occurs in atoms to fulfil a result-oriented intent and, in doing so, also fulfils their complementarity. Before a particle is displaced, its atom remains excited; only this excitation makes displacement possible. The displaced particle is then absorbed into another atom. After displacement, the former atom enters natural-state motion; after absorbing the particle and swelling, the receiving atom also establishes natural-state motion. Every atom, molecule, and molecular composition fulfils definite conduct only through the establishment of natural-state motion. Definite conduct is evidence of orderliness, and the intent of development is contained in this progression.

## 3.2 Development Progression, Development (विकासक्रम, विकास)

The development progression establishes molecules, atoms, and molecular compositions of many species, such as the earth and other planetary globes. These compositions exist in solid, liquid, and rarefied forms, all three of which are well established on earth. Water, as a liquid substance, is the first grandeur of compound action. Through combinations on the earth, water gives rise to acids and alkalis. Once these arise in sufficient measure, cosmic heat and reflection — the basis of mutual recognition — enable nourishment-element and composition-element to form within the water. Their earliest form is called algae (*kaai*). From algae, compositions made of biological cells develop through single-celled, two-celled, and multi-celled forms. This exhibits the development progression in composition. The earth also contains compositions of many sizes in the forms of soil, stone, gems, and metals. Compositions made of biological cells are the grandeur of compound action and are called the plant-order. The plant-order is indivisible from the earth and remains joined to it.

Thus, the combination of diverse substances on earth accomplishes the grandeur of compound action: a festival of forms becoming manifest as distinct from their roots. This is chemical resonance (*rasayanik urmi*); the festival of chemical substances is called resonance.

Every biological cell contains prana sutras, definite measures of the composition-element and nourishment-element, and an inherent composition-method. Prana sutras carry out accretion within themselves and multiply into sutras like themselves. This requires the availability of chemical substances and the conjunction of a definite degree of heat. In the bodies of living beings, including human beings, biological cells form in the marrow-filled fluids within the hollows of bones and are distributed according to the body's needs. The combination of prana sutras also forms many further sutras and cells. Together with chemical substances, these cells preserve their existence as blood and participate in composing the body. In the plant-order, the activity-processes of leaves and roots accumulate sap within the circulation system,

[p. 59]

from which biological cells continue to form. The development-propensity of compositions is thus contained within biological cells. The diversity of present compositions is evidence of this development progression, which has reached the grandeur of the human-body composition on earth.

Biological cells manifest compositions as plants of many sizes — trees, creepers, and shrubs — and further as the bodies of living beings. As biological cells in the plant-order develop, they express changes in the composition-method. These changes become the basis of living-being bodies, extending up to the human body. Four orders are perceptible in nature (*prakriti*): the material order as soil, stone, and so forth; the biological order as trees and plants; and the animal and knowledge orders as the combined form of body and jeevan. The composition progression of biological- and animal-order bodies also includes the sweat-born world. When residues of the plant-order, such as dried leaves, collect and receive water and heat, many worms and insects arise spontaneously. This develops further into the egg-born world, through which propagation is established. The sweat-born world appears among water-, land-, and sky-dwellers. The more powerful egg-born world appears as birds and as large water- and land-dwellers. It leads into the womb-born world, whose composition develops from within the egg-born world. Through the egg-born and womb-born worlds, the analysis and synthesis of chemical substances into the seven metals becomes established up to the composition of the human body.

From the study of the progression of all these compositions and of the human body, this too is learnt: that in the human body the richness of the brain (*medhas*) composition has completely and fully become clear. The evidence of this is precisely that in the human being, imaginativeness, freedom-of-action, and its satisfaction, in the form of knowing, believing, recognising, and carrying-out through the medium of the brain, is a unit worthy of expression. The non-human order is limited up to recognising and carrying-out. The basis of recognising and carrying-out is forcefulness itself. This very energy-fullness, forcefulness, has already become evident, in the living human being, in the form of knowing, believing, recognising, and carrying-out. This is also testimony to the brain system being rich. By the above method the outline of there being development in compositions becomes known. Before this it has already been made clear that

[p. 60]

development occurs only in the atom. A developed atom is constitutionally complete; this conscious unit is jeevan. By keeping the body alive, jeevan evidences understanding.

## 3.3 Development in the Atom (परमाणु में विकास)

The preceding account has clarified the development progression in composition from atoms and molecules through molecule-composed bodies, the plant-order, animal bodies, and the human-body composition. The human body can be studied as the most highly developed composition.

The human body is the most highly developed composition because its brain composition has fully developed. Human beings have long desired to unveil the facts of justice, dharma, and truth. Many applications were attempted, yet the need to develop discernment remained. Idealist and materialist modes of discernment were applied in these attempts, and the human family has already reckoned their results.

The expectation of justice, even today, remains in the human being most of all.

The present account seeks to understand and express existence as coexistence. On this basis, the development progression in compositions becomes clear. The atom is at the root of every composition, and both development progression and development within atoms become intelligible.

Development in an atom means its becoming constitutionally complete. Human beings naturally desire the existent state and grandeur of a result, together with its continuity. We have already understood the decrease and increase of particles in atoms as result. The expectation that a result be imperishable and continuous occurs only in human beings, as imagination, sense, semblance, and conviction; many attempts to realise it are therefore natural. The coexistence-oriented method shows many compositions, states, and circumstances within the development progression, with the atom functioning at their root. An atom engaged in result is constituted into the condition of constitutional accretion through the natural method of result.

[p. 61]

Within the result-progression, atoms can be understood as hungry or undigested. Undigested atoms continually attempt to expel particles from their constitution and are therefore endowed with radiative grandeur. Atoms are always active, and their activity spontaneously produces sound, heat, and electricity. Because they remain saturated in the omnipresent reality, they retain energy-fullness and forcefulness; this forcefulness is reckoned as magnetic force. A radiative atom expresses its undigested condition through the continuing attempt to expel particles. Heat converges internally toward its nucleus, and electricity is also present. Radiativeness becomes manifest through these two processes.

Hungry atoms retain the possibility of absorbing further particles, which is why they are called "hungry." The distinction between undigested and hungry atoms is understood in relation to the satisfied (*tript*) atom, as studied by the human being. A constitutionally satisfied atom attains the jeevan status. In a constitutionally complete atom, strength and power are consequently inexhaustible: however much they are expressed or expended, they do not diminish. Hope, thought, desire, and evidence remain keen however much they are expressed. Every human being can recognise this inexhaustibility within through examination.

The human being alone can study both itself and the whole of existence. This grandeur establishes the human being in the knowledge order. Every man and woman has equal grandeur in that order; this is the formula of mutual equality, and forgetting it is a sign of delusion.

It is thus well established that jeevan is a constitutionally complete atom, functions as a conscious activity-process, and becomes evident through the human body in human tradition. Maintaining this grandeur requires studying, understanding, and evidencing the conditions in which human beings are established or disestablished. The destination of development in the atom is therefore the constitutionally complete status.

[p. 62]

The atom is therefore present at the root of physical, chemical, and jeevan action. Jeevan is a constitutionally complete atom, while an atom participating in physical and chemical action is constitution-oriented. Constitution-orientation entails weight bondage and molecular bondage, whereas the jeevan atom remains free of both. The hope, thought, desire, resolve, and evidence inherent in a human being cannot be weighed or enumerated on a physical scale, which is a means of recognising weight. A physical atom passes through development progression and irreversibly transitions into the constitutionally complete status. In its constitution-oriented status an atom is inert (*jad*); in its constitutionally complete status it is conscious. Development cannot be studied without its progression. Through human tradition, the conscious atom — jeevan — studies and evidences development, the developed status, awakening progression, and awakening. The destiny method thereby forms an orderliness in which awakening is evidenced and human tradition becomes grand. This method is the process, result, and continuity of existence's grandeur as coexistence.

Development in the atom is the constitution-complete status, embodied in the constitutionally complete atom. The human being studies what "is." Study means recognising in existence the objects indicated by words, through remembrance of their meanings in the light of realisation. Awakening is the remembrance of words together with the clarity, in existence, of the objects their meanings indicate. Clarity means realisation within oneself and its evidence in action and behaviour.

Development in the atom means that the atom attains the constitution-complete status, and that the constitutionally complete atom awakens; this is destiny. It becomes evident only within human tradition, for which a body-composition capable of expressing it has already formed. Human beings have applied the body in various ways to evidence this awakening. Yet tradition did not explain the constitutionally complete atom as the intent of atomic development, so the jeevan status could not be studied. That status can be studied only in the light of the awakening that occurs in jeevan; it is therefore natural to discuss it here.

[p. 63]

So long as the constitutionally complete atom, through delusion, takes the body to be jeevan, it cannot understand development in the atom. Within the physical and chemical development progression, jeevan and jeevan-awakening become fully developed in the atom and are evidenced through the human body; awakening thereby becomes evident in human tradition. To evidence awakening is to live justly, with resolution, actuality, reality, and truth. To be evidenced is to be present in tradition. When the five faculties described above are naturally evidenced from generation to generation, the tradition is awakened.

Human grandeur is seen in the universalisation, through education, study, and practice, of research and investigation shown to be useful, well-utilised, and fit for application.

By the above method, however much application has so far occurred in giving form to the imaginary, all of that has been universalised. And however much application has occurred for mental well-being (*manah-svasthata*), it has not been universalised. Whereas the very definition of the human being is: the one who gives form to the imaginary, the one who presents the evidence of mental well-being (the idealist certainly exists).

Development-completeness in the atom is an irreversible-transition event: the atom does not return to its previous state. Freedom from result means freedom from the decrease or increase of particles within the atom.

By the coexistence-oriented method, the human being is grand as the combined form of body and jeevan. Jeevan-awakening is the evidence of mental well-being. It is accomplished within jeevan by the realisation-based method: buddhi receives enlightenment of realisations; chitta contemplates them according to justice, dharma, and truth; vritti weighs them through reflection; and mind (*mann*) tastes their values. Values can be tasted in work and behaviour only when relationships are accepted and recognised; only then are those values carried out.

Relationships bring values; values bring the dedication and surrender of objects; and this dedication brings usefulness, well-utilisation, and fitness for application. This is the grandeur and intent of development in the atom and of awakening. Only the human being is capable of presenting its evidence.

[p. 64]

Awakening's entire form is nothing but knowing, believing, recognising, and carrying out. The material order, the biological order, and the animal order — recognising and carrying out all of these, these units evidence their own conduct in the form of participation in orderliness-with-inherence (*tva*) and in the overall orderliness. Whereas in the human being, along with recognising and carrying out, knowing and believing have remained a necessary process as well. This is evidenced properly only within the human family (*kul*). Knowing and believing, together, attest to the fact of being in the human knowledge order.

In its root form, the atom alone is the root substance of all activity. In the atom, as the outcome of development progression and development, physical, chemical, and jeevan activity occurs. As the constitution-forming atom, the world's grandeur is seen displayed all the way from the material order and the biological order up to the rich brain composition.

The first stage of development in the atom is constitutional completeness, through which jeevan is present as the imperishable activity of a conscious unit.

The jeevan atom's becoming awakened (activity-completeness) is the second stage of development in the atom, and evidencing this awakening within human tradition (conduct-completeness) is the final stage.

In this way, development and awakening in the atom are completed in these three stages — constitutional completeness, activity-completeness, conduct-completeness. This is the meaning of development in the atom.

## 3.4 The Nature of Mental Well-Being (मनःस्वस्थता का स्वरूप)

The study of physical, chemical, and jeevan activities requires a complete study of the human being. Tireless endeavour has been devoted to this issue. Any study of the human context that failed to recognise human-ness (*manavatva*) also left physical and chemical activity incompletely understood. Conversely, the study of physical and chemical activity alone could not complete the understanding of the human being. Thus, however much human beings may know of physical and chemical activity, that knowledge alone does not provide the thread and

[p. 65]

explanation of the human being. This is why people in every country trust physical activity but have not yet come to trust human activity.

Consequently, no determination has yet been reached regarding human-ness, human conduct, mentality, or nature. Governing, educational, religious-cultural, technological, and commercial institutions all acknowledge this indirectly in various ways. This gap creates the need to understand development and awakening.

This point has already been made clear: that it is the atom alone that participates in physical, chemical, and jeevan activity. It has already been made clear that at the root of jeevan activity is the presence of the constitutionally complete atom. Whether it be physical activity, chemical activity, or jeevan activity — it is on the basis of the conduct of each of these three modes that we are able to determine their recognition, the recognition of their diversity, and the recognition of their unity. We determine the recognition of iron on the basis of iron's forms of conduct. In the same way, we recognise soil, stone, metal, donkey, horse, cat, dog, ant, and elephant, each on the basis of their own respective forms of conduct, and, along with this, we are also able to determine what conduct and behaviour humans are to have toward them.

It is clear that, in existence, the human being is the combined form of jeevan activity and physical-chemical activity (the body). The atom, in its constitutionally complete condition, carries out jeevan activity. It is jeevan alone that expresses, communicates, and manifests hope, thought, desire, resolve (resoluteness, *ṛtambharā*), and evidence (realisation). The testing of this has to be carried out by every human being, both within himself and within every other human being. For this reason the human being is an object worthy of study in the human being, by the human being, for the human being.

Mental well-being may be called happiness; it is the realisation and evidence of resolution. In the tradition of behaviour, mental well-being is evidenced as resolution. The human goal is recognised as resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, and coexistence, and every man and woman can realise it. Resolution in realisation is happiness, and happiness is evidenced as resolution in behaviour. Resolution is thereby established as the expression, communication, and manifestation of jeevan activity, mental well-being, and jeevan awakening.

[p. 66]

Mental well-being is a most necessary issue in, from, and for human tradition. Only with mental well-being does the human goal become evident. In the process of the human goal becoming meaningful, the human being becomes ordered within himself, and participates in orderliness.

Human orderliness is not confined to chemical-physical activity because jeevan activity is also present. Every human being is testimony to this activity. Jeevan activity occurs in animals as well, but there it remains confined to hereditary tendencies and actions in accordance with the jeevan-body, and therefore to sensation. Human beings express both cognisance — knowing and believing — and sensitivity — recognising and carrying out. In the animal order only sensitivity becomes evident; in human tradition both cognisance and sensitivity do, and an awakened human being regulates sensation through cognisance. This regulation makes sensation balanced and purposeful and is the fundamental distinctiveness of the awakened, or natural, human being. Purpose is therefore evidence of cognisance.

The definition of the human being is meaningful in the sense of giving concrete form to ideas (*manakar*) and evidencing mental well-being. This is accepted in, from, and for every human being. The human being does indeed want to have a healthy mind (*manas*), and does keep on giving concrete form to ideas. This can be tested in every country and time. In this way it is self-evident that the human being, as the combined manifestation of chemical-physical and jeevan activity, is distinct from other kinds of nature — such as animal nature, plant nature, and mineral nature.

According to coexistentialist thought, according to knowledge, wisdom, and science, it is necessary for all humankind to adopt education-and-culture in the sense of the human goal. In this, it is necessary that humane conduct remain clear in the sense of the human goal, because it is only on the basis of conduct that the fulfilment of the goal of each of these orders becomes intelligible. For instance, in the material order, on the basis of the outcome for the whole substance, one gets to see conduct acting toward the goal in the form of its existent state (*yatha-sthiti*). In the same way, in the biological order and the animal order too, all the units at work are seen acting for the sake of the goal of each of those orders — for example, the biological order is seen making the journey, in the sense of existence together with growth (*pushti*), from seed to tree and from tree to seed. In this

[p. 67]

way the entire animal order is seen acting, together with existence and growth, in the hope of living, by the hereditary method. Having seen such entirely clear conditions, it also remained necessary that human conduct be in the sense of evidencing happiness together with existence, growth, and hope — and it is for this very reason that the evidencing of mental well-being remained natural.

Human conduct combines value, character, and ethics. Values are evaluated on the basis of fundamental distinctiveness. For human beings, accepting relationships, carrying them out, and maintaining their continuity constitutes such a distinctiveness. Recognising relationships, carrying out and evaluating their values, and attaining mutual satisfaction together express value and fundamental distinctiveness. Character, the second aspect of conduct, consists in fidelity to one's own wealth, wife, or husband, and in kindness in work and behaviour; together these establish universal human character. Ethics, the third aspect, comprises moral policy and the state's economic policy. Every awakened man and woman evidences ethics by protecting and rightly using the wealth of body, mind, and money. The methods of protection are called statecraft, those of right-use moral policy, and production beyond the family's needs economic policy.

By the coexistentialist method, every human being can participate with human-ness in orderliness and the overall orderliness. This requires accepting through study that the self combines chemical, physical, and jeevan activity, and keeping in view the grandeur and necessity of jeevan activity within human tradition. Only then is the human being ready for research. The natural method of awakening directs this readiness toward universal wellbeing: all humankind expresses resolution, every family becomes resolved and happy through understanding, and prosperity, fearlessness, and coexistence are brought to bear. This is evidence of universal wellbeing, because resolution and prosperity enable human beings to realise happiness and peace. Contentment and joy, together with resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, and coexistence, are meaningful in themselves. When the human goal becomes meaningful, the jeevan goal — happiness, peace, contentment, and joy — also becomes meaningful. The jeevan

[p. 68]

goal and the human goal become universally available through study and teaching. Human tradition then provides the thread and explanation for recognising oneself and the whole of humankind: as understanding it is the thread, and as evidence it is the explanation. The natural method of destiny comprises development progression, development, awakening progression, and the tradition of natural evidence for awakening. It is also expressed through the combination of physical-chemical composition, the body, and jeevan activity.

The human goal is to evidence resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, and coexistence naturally. On that basis, the jeevan goal is mental well-being, evidenced as meaningful happiness, peace, contentment, and joy. Human education and *sanskar* have always been necessary for this purpose, and their success is the good fortune of the human family.

### The Meaningful Evidence of the Human Family and of Chemical-Physical Activity

Within existence as coexistence, the whole subject matter of education consists of chemical, physical, and jeevan activity. The activity, behaviour, thought, and understanding of human tradition are indivisible within these. Every human being must study in order to understand. Understanding is evidenced within oneself as knowing, believing, recognising, and fulfilling; study is meaningful in precisely this sense.

All units in existence are discernible as chemical, physical, or jeevan activity. From the constitution-forming atom through molecules and molecular bodies to biological cells, the plant order, and the animal order, each is intelligible through its own determinate conduct while remaining within its orderliness. The earth is the greatest such evidence: it functions as an orderliness, and upon it physico-chemical and jeevan activities are manifest as the material, biological, animal, and knowledge orders. What greater testimony could there be? On the basis of the determinate activities of the material, biological, and animal orders, the human being can trust in his own place within orderliness.

[p. 69]

Through the self-motivated method, the human being can understand the thread and explanation for living within orderliness by the natural method of existence-as-coexistence.

The coexistentialist method makes it possible to recognise all humankind as one undivided society (*akhand samaj*). Complete understanding of this society's mentality as knowledge, science, and wisdom keeps the weave of universal orderliness clear. Human beings become meaningful within orderliness only in the context of such universal wellbeing. Universal orderliness is therefore the human family's ultimate grandeur, thread, and explanation. With human awareness, participation in undivided society and universal orderliness becomes self-motivated, because by the coexistentialist method the human being recognises himself.

In the conduct of humankind, dharma predominates; in animal species, essential nature predominates; in plants, property predominates; in material things, form predominates. It has already become necessary that human dharma, in itself, evidence resolution on the basis of happiness. All-round, resolution-complete education-and-culture is itself the tradition and the source for this. This is awakened tradition.

## 3.5 Coexistence Is Stable; Development and Awakening Are Certain

Existence-as-coexistence is stable, and within coexistence development and awakening are certain. Human beings on earth have reached a condition in which this fact can be studied. The awakened human being alone is the seer, doer, and enjoyer of universal wellbeing, and the bearer and carrier of the knowledge, science, and wisdom rooted in education-and-culture. Through coexistentialist philosophy, knowledge, science, and wisdom, every human being can evidence himself within orderliness and participate in the overall orderliness as universal wellbeing unfolds. This is the reach and manifest form of the programme of universal wellbeing. Study is the sole source of universal knowledge, science, and wisdom. Complete knowledge comprises knowledge of the holistic view of existence-as-coexistence, knowledge of jeevan as conscious nature, knowledge of activity and behaviour as humane conduct, and knowledge of participation in the overall orderliness. Existence-as-coexistence is studied in four orders and four categories (*pad*). At its root, within the omnipresent reality, every single substance

[p. 70]

remains submerged, saturated, and enclosed within the whole. This is the ever-present nature of coexistence. Human beings evidence motion on the basis of awakening. Thus every eternally established unit in existence-as-coexistence is present as state–motion. State is expressed as strength and motion as power, and strength and power are indivisible.

This indivisibility of state–motion is also evident in human jeevan activities. Realisation (*anubhav*) is state, and evidence is motion. In buddhi, the enlightenment (*bodh*) of reality, actuality, and truth is state, while the resolve (*sankalp*) to express it is motion. Enlightenment manifests realisation, and evidence communicates it so that others may realise it. In chitta, contemplation as the direct recognition (*sakshatkar*) of justice, dharma, and truth is state, while picturing it through visualisation is motion. In vritti, deliberation (*tulan*) on justice, dharma, and truth is the innate state; analysing and communicating that deliberation is thought-motion. Analysis accepts the essence as value. Tasting that value is the state of mind (*mun*), while selecting the manner and method of making it meaningful is motion. Human beings are therefore present in state–motion in every respect. Once endowed with understanding, they naturally evidence it. Until then, acquiring the knowledge, wisdom, and science needed for evidence is the meaning of education and instruction; the coexistential education sequence is appropriate for this purpose.

[p. 85]

To understand coexistential knowledge, wisdom, and science is to attain understanding. The idealist and materialist modes of living and thought did not yield a method of universal wellbeing. Its source is coexistential knowledge, science as thought, and wisdom. These can be understood and communicated. Each person can live in the state–motion of understanding, help others to live, and incline them toward evidence. Understanding is disseminated when all humankind evidences this state–motion within universal and undivided-society relationships. Participation in these steps is evidence. Such realisation-based evidence is universal and can only be confirmed by living it.

The human wants to be adept in the discipline of science. In becoming adept, the hindrance in application and practice so far has come down to just these points. First, no trace of the ultimate truth could be found; second, the root measure (*mool matra*), the source of energy, and the goal could not be ascertained; third — the study of the human could not take place; fourth — the purpose of existence could not become clear. In this manner, even after applying science with such long and wide dedication, all those wishing to live clearly ended up frustrated. On this issue it was first settled that whatever claims come before science by the materialist method or by the idealist method — that is, whatever question marks arise — the attempt be made to solve them on the basis of the economic, social, and political poles. But on this basis no logically consistent and purpose-consistent work-behaviour, orderliness, or tradition according to any meaningful view could be established. Compared with this, such a desire is being found in every man and woman who desires wellbeing — in other words, who desires universal wellbeing. On the other side, the matter of becoming frustrated has become clear. Even among those who become frustrated, many people must surely be desiring universal wellbeing. If one proceeds to reason consistently by the method of desiring wellbeing, universal wellbeing alone is the pole. Now here, taking the desire for universal wellbeing as the acceptance of all humankind, or the acceptance of the majority, this treatise has been carried forward in the sequence of thinking, understanding, and presenting evidences further.

Whatever methods have so far been called science — in them, the conception and evidence for clarifying the following points have been from their own standpoint. The standpoint of science is committed to struggle. On this basis, the whole of nature other than the human is

[p. 86]

for the human and for human use. Among humans, it is then the most powerful who is deemed fit to use it — the one who beats, plunders, murders, terrorises, and slaughters others. One may ask whether science has ever explicitly declared this. It has not done so in a formal document. Yet writings composed as personal opinion in the name of science contain claims of this kind.

The central difficulty is uncertainty and instability: science is treated as independent, while the scientist is not. Scientific writings themselves confirm this stance. Yet responsibility belongs neither to a book nor to an instrument, but to the human being. The belief arose that whatever an instrument demonstrates repeatedly is true, even if it is not the ultimate truth. The instrument thereby became the principal evidence, and human responsibility was displaced onto it. An instrument cannot reason; it merely repeats its operation. Repetition was consequently taken as the basis of trust and assurance, distancing human beings from responsibility even though they make and use the instruments.

Observation and testing show that every human favours responsibility, justice, and orderliness. At the root of all three, every human favours understanding.

One further point must be made. By definition, we humans give concrete shape to ideas (*manakar* — the mind's forms), yet prevalent education has been unable to evidence mental well-being. Mental well-being is essential to being human. In evidencing this essentiality, we have examined within ourselves and established that the human being is happy through the coexistential method. In every discipline, we become happy only by living with resolution. Living with resolution becomes meaningful through understanding, and understanding is accomplished through the complete standpoint of coexistence. As seen earlier, understanding has its eternal source in three disciplines. The study so far has shown that understanding is not evidenced by idealism, materialism, or logic alone. By the proposed coexistential

[p. 87]

method alone, by the realisation-based method, every man and woman can evidence all-round resolution with humane conduct, in the form of understanding, honesty, responsibility, and participation. They can realise happiness for ever and ever within themselves, because being endowed with universal resolution is itself the very first rung of understanding. This is indeed present in every awakened human. For this very reason, the possibility of being happy for ever and ever also remains contained therein. On this very basis, the need to be evidenced persists. Thus, it became possible for every understanding human to be responsible and to be benevolent in, from, and for the tradition. The meaning of benevolence (*upkar*) too is clear. In the sense of awakening: to make understood what has been understood, to have done what one has done, to teach what one has learnt — all this being resolution-bringing, benevolence consequently becomes meaningful. In this manner it has become clear how the human can be benevolent. It has also become clear how all of us — those regarding themselves as understanding and those regarding themselves as ignorant — created problems. Along with this it has also become clear that the world of science, in its mentality and in its works of enlightening, in the course of arriving very far from responsibilities, tried to recognise the human as being like a machine, and in this failed utterly.

Nothing remains unclear by the coexistence method. Things can be understood, and communicating what has been understood is a self-motivated activity. The basis of the manifest and unmanifest thereby becomes clear. What a human being can evidence, communicate, and live is manifest; what was regarded as understood but could not be communicated was called unmanifest. Yet existence-as-coexistence is eternally present and manifest; nothing in it is unmanifest.

## 3.9 Measure

Every substance exists as a single unit and expresses actuality. In each substance, actuality is orderliness-with-inherence (*tva*). Form, property, essential nature, and dharma are mutually and indivisibly present and are expressed together with measure. Their expression, communication, and manifestation are rooted in coexistence. Every unit in coexistence is submerged, saturated, and enclosed within the omnipresent reality, which permeates it transparently. Saturation in Omnipresence energises each unit and endows it with magnetic force.

Each and every atom alone is the root measure. Such numerous atoms, in the form of molecules; numerous molecules, in the form of molecular composition; such compositions, in the form of matter and the biological order — this too has already been made clear before. In every species, atoms constituted from two particles up to many particles have made their own respective forms of conduct clear.

The atom is the root measure because a defined unit is itself an orderliness and participates in the overall orderliness. Even a unit not yet within orderliness — such as an atomic particle or a deluded human being — retains the tendency to participate in it. A particle by itself cannot evidence orderliness or express determinate necessity. It is therefore inclined to participate in an atom: it is absorbed into an existing atom or joins other particles that mutually recognise one another and function as an atom. Their resulting determinate conduct is orderliness. Atomic particles thus recognise and fulfil one another within the atom.

Just as an atomic particle does not exist in orderliness without recognising other particles, so too the human being cannot live in orderliness at all without recognising other human beings. There is one and only one thread for recognising the human being — human-ness. Human-ness is nothing but human consciousness, by which all humankind can be recognised in the form of the human being. Human-ness, in its own nature, is nothing but the nature of the definition of the human being. In the sense of mental well-being

[p. 89]

itself, the coexistentialist method enables even science to analyse, determine, and elaborate the thread of mental well-being. This determination is necessary in relation to the human goal. Human beings must firmly recognise humane necessity, whose certainty is evidenced through resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, and coexistence. Humane conduct becomes clear only in the process of evidencing this goal. Determining humane necessity indicates knowing and believing; together they provide the thread and explanation of evidenced conduct.

It is natural that the principle of measure too, at its root, remains animated by these very meanings. Therefore, as more than one particle gathers together and functions — that is, conducts itself — the many species of atoms remain recognisable on the basis of their conduct. The human being recognises this; this becomes the grand, dignified condition of the human being's own natural fitness. At the root of this remains simply the tinge of knowing and believing. In the same way, in the compositions constituted from biological cells too, the composition of the biological cells remains determinate. The evidence of this is precisely the composition of the plants of many species — in all of these, remaining animated from seed to tree, and from tree to seed — that is, impulses from one seed to the tree, and impulses from the tree to the seed, remain contained in that very seed-tree. In these two kinds of impulse together, the necessity of the trees of that species has been made clear. In this way we human beings recognise any substance in a firm form; the basis of this remains precisely firm necessity. In the same way, the human being has become assured about the conduct of the ant up to the elephant, of the dog up to the cat, and of the cow, the tiger, the hen, the insect, and so on. Along with these, the human being has also adopted the necessary method. This necessary method appears, by way of indirect statement, to have been accomplished all the way to the world other than the human. When, in that same sequence, human beings come to the position of recognising other human beings, we then feel doubtfulness — whether we are managing to recognise them or not. For this doubtfulness, disputes remain formed in human tradition by methods relating to caste, ideology, sect, position, and money. As a result, treachery, rebellion, exploitation, and war are found effective. What is meant by being effective is that these accidents keep on occurring.

Even if the human being were to consider himself as mere measure, would the human being's necessity thereby become clear? Even on thinking of oneself as mere measure, it still seems that we human beings have not been able to determine our own necessity, and it is for this very reason that necessity has remained uncertain,

[p. 90]

and even on the basis of necessity we have not been able to recognise the universality of orderliness, its poles. One pole of human-ness is precisely the human being's own necessity, which becomes evident in behaviour, in the work of production, as participation in orderliness with resolution.

Human necessity is entirely clear: resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, and coexistence. Its evidence is successful humane conduct in the form of value, character, and ethics; this has been tested through application. Neither the idealist nor the materialist method establishes it. The coexistential method therefore makes the human goal and humane conduct accessible to study. Their universal success throughout humankind is the glory of an awakened human tradition. Human beings continually seek within themselves to evidence value, character, and ethics. Why should this not be pursued as a human necessity? Any person can make such a resolve. The need for it is especially apparent at a certain age. Predominantly between twelve or fourteen and twenty, most people expect justice in society, orderliness aligned with everyone, and happiness for all. Attachment to one faction or another gradually stunts these expectations. People then regard only the outlook of their own sect or class as right and proper. The result is mutual treachery, rebellion, exploitation, and war.

Coexistential study makes it possible to affirm that, at some age, all human beings wish well for the greatest number — that is, they wish for universal well-being. At present, the same person who at one age wants happiness for all or most people may later become absorbed in gathering conveniences for self and family. Balancing these tendencies requires consciousness-development value education according to Madhyasth Darshan — in other words, the universalisation of coexistential education. The coexistential method must be adopted to bring all four orders to a point of balance.

[p. 91]

Every substance in its subtle atomic form is a root measure. As compositions, substances are available to us as units of many sizes and as minerals; our future remains bound to these attainments. The results of today's conduct and work-behaviour, including conduct within disorderliness, must be undergone even if they appear only years later. We must therefore remain understanding in the present and diligent in preserving a tradition of understanding. This is the meaning of the human being's state–motion.

State-motion, in every measure, is a natural activity. It is only in the combined form of state and motion that every activity is recognised. The conduct of a substance is recognised; in motion, conduct remains as outcome in state. This is the form of the existent state. The thread and the explanation of the existent state remain contained in this very thing. For example, hardness in iron is a physical necessity. It is only in its state-motion that the evidence of this comes about. To make use of iron is precisely what is meant by its capacity for motion. It is only when the human being, bringing his own power and strength to bear, tests iron, that trust in iron comes about. In this the human being has already succeeded considerably.

Many substances have been tested and inspected, yielding certainties on which numerous technologies operate. People undertake technical education in order to participate in these technologies, and many succeed in doing so. Yet success in technology has not evidenced mental well-being. For that, recognising the human being is primary. Such recognition occurs only through living with resolution and prosperity and evidencing justice, which consists of relational value, evaluation, and mutual satisfaction.

Without oneself carrying out humane conduct, recognising humane conduct in another human being simply does not come about. This can be tested in the following way. One person carries out humane conduct, and another also does so; if, in the mutuality of these two, work-behaviour is assessed, and applied research is also assessed, then of humane conduct

[p. 92]

evaluation occurs in their mutuality, because evaluation is possible only between persons. If one person carries out humane conduct and the other does not, the former trusts himself while the latter does not. If neither does, they may agree about some vices and partly agree or disagree about criminal thoughts and acts, but no conclusion emerges.

These situations show that an awakened human being preserves identity through conduct grounded in inner orderliness. This is the ascertainment of the existent state. The material, biological, and animal orders have already been ascertained by trustworthy methods, and human beings have relied on them to attain success, chiefly in accumulating conveniences, though these have not reached everyone. Undivided society becomes possible only when human beings maturely evidence their own necessity. Once the thread of undivided society becomes meaningful, universal orderliness can be explained; this is the meaningful and effective form of wisdom and science. The study must therefore address the human being, human-ness, humane conduct, undivided society, and the thread and explanation of universal orderliness. Every human being's purpose begins with firmness in conduct, proceeds to evidencing conduct within orderliness, and culminates in participation in universal orderliness.

At present, while recognising everyone else, the human being is most backward in the discipline of recognising the human being. This deficiency can be made up through work-behaviour filled with knowledge, wisdom, and science. This means that, in the units of the three states, participation, together with their own inherence, in orderliness and in the overall orderliness, has already become evident. It is precisely as an outcome of this that the human being has succeeded in giving concrete form to ideas. Iron's remaining unfailing in its own orderliness; soil, stone, gem, metal — all remain unfailing, each in its own conduct. On this very basis the human being has been able to enrich, together with these, the technological system relating to his social ambitions, or production. If all these were to become mutually quarrelsome, given to disagreement, opposition, and rebellion, like the human being, what would happen then? If, in the same way,

[p. 93]

the rest of nature too were not to maintain stability and certainty in its conduct, what would happen? No human being would have the possibility of giving concrete form to ideas. It became a meaningful form for the human being that the three states, by the sequential method, each remain manifest in the form of determinate conduct according to their own existent state. But the human being, in order to be meaningful while living together with the rest of nature, has simply not managed to evidence mental well-being. Without evidencing this, it is not at all possible for the human being to be complementary with all of non-human nature. Not only this — without the human being being complementary with the human being, being complementary with the rest of nature does not come about at all.

From the coexistentialist viewpoint, the activity, process, and meaningfulness of science become assured, because, by the coexistentialist method, the purpose of science is to determine the direction for the human goal to become meaningful. Since the human goal is resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, and coexistence, it is precisely the determining of the direction, and of the methods of teaching, for this to become meaningful in all humankind that is the purpose of science and wisdom.

Science has its importance by way of this method of determining such a goal. By this method both the viewpoint and the work of wisdom and science became clear. This also makes clear the intent that, in favour of human conduct becoming determinate —

- science should become meaningful in the sense of wisdom;
- wisdom and science should become meaningful in the sense of knowledge;
- knowledge, science, and wisdom should become meaningful in the sense of coexistence;
- knowledge should be in the sense of coexistence.

This is the meaning of realisation-based coexistentialist knowledge, science, and wisdom.

Measure, in its root form, is recognised as a unit that is itself an orderliness, and that participates in orderliness. Such a unit begins in the form of the constitution of more than one particle, whose shape (result) is determinate. On the side of the determinacy of shape (result), a great many examples have already been presented. Now here it is necessary to become clear about form, property, essential nature, and dharma. Every form is clear in the forms of shape, volume, and bulk. What is meant by shape is however it remains constituted, together with however much

[p. 94]

space it remains spread out over, and functions — that is, remains in state. As one might think about a tradition, into however much space it remains active. In the same way, further, the compositions constructed from numerous molecules, whether very large or small — the shape they remain formed in, however much space they occupy — it is on this basis that the recognition of form comes about. Of this, what shape is, how the shape is, is a matter of construction; the meaning of volume is enclosure (spread). What is meant by enclosure is remaining bounded on every side within the omnipresent reality; by another method it is also in terms of length, breadth, and height. By these methods we are able to recognise shape; every such shape, or unit, remains reflected on every side. The unit remains reflected in the units situated on every side of it, which are illumined by it. In this way the method of mutual recognition remains formed. There is no need to construct the method of recognition. By this method, every substance remains reflected upon every other substance. Every substance is luminous.

On thinking by the above method, this question can arise: is light not needed in order to recognise one another? — as sunlight is available upon the earth. The answer to this is just this: whatever light we human beings recognise at present, it is only on the basis of recognising light through the eyes. Whereas every substance is indeed luminous. The reason for this is just this: in every atom, activity in the form of effort, motion, and result remains present. In the outcome of motion, sound, heat, electricity, and wave remain manifest. At the root of activity, being energised and being magnetic-force-full remain self-established. The glory of the unit endowed with these four kinds of force keeps itself illumined in the forms of existing-ness and luminousness — that is, they remain illumined. From this we come to understand that every substance is luminous. The testimony of luminousness has already become entirely clear as being, in the form of reflection, present on every side of the unit. That reflection alone is the basis for recognising one another — this fact has already become understood. For this: place any substance in any one room; if we sit down naturally on every side, that substance does indeed come within the sight of all of us. By another method: our eyes, according to the construction of the visual organ, cannot see anything in a dark room without more light. There is no motion at all in the reflection of substances placed in the room. This is understood by this method: owls, which are active by night, and animals with similar eyes, do indeed recognise the substances they need. Just as owls and bats do recognise fruits, and the deer recognises grass. Whereas the human being does not recognise. Merely from the human being's not recognising, the substance does not itself

[p. 95]

become an error in presenting itself, nor is it the case that there is no one else who recognises it either. It can be considered that the elephant, the ant, and the owl too do see it. By the method of investigation the conclusion emerges that each of their eyes remain formed according to their own respective necessities. In the same way the basis of the five sensations remains formed, because the arrangement for recognising something through the eye, something through the nose, something through the tongue, something through the skin, something through the ear, is found formed in the living human being. It can be thought that a similar arrangement will surely be present in all living creatures. Just as the frog's skin remains formed as it is, the feathers of many birds remain formed in different ways, and the cow's hide remains formed of its own kind — in all of these, tactility remains formed differently according to each one's respective necessity. In this way it remained natural for the tactility of the human body to remain distinct. We can accept, from physical knowledge, that the composition of the human body can express all five kinds of sensation with excellence, and can communicate tendencies. This good fortune makes clear the sense of equality present in every kind of human body. By way of indirect statement, sensation is present in all living beings. In this way it became clear what activity occurs, together with measure, in the animal order and the knowledge order. Every composition is one measure; in these measures, diversity of form is a matter for study in the forms of shape and volume; diversity of property in the forms of motion, work, and tendency; diversity of essential nature in being different across the four orders; diversity of dharma in being distinct across all four orders. This matter has already been made clear before as well. The material order is endowed with the dharma of existence, whose destruction is not possible. The biological order is endowed, together with existence, with the dharma of growth (*pushti*) — the evidence of this is the tradition of one seed transforming into many. It is by the logic of seed-and-tree alone that the whole of the biological order exists. The animal order, together with existence and growth, is endowed with the dharma of hope. Every animal wants to live; to live means to express sensations. There is nothing more than this. In the knowledge order, the human being, together with existence and growth, is endowed with the dharma of happiness — this too has been made clear along with this. Resolution alone is the evidential form of happiness. All-round resolution is what the human being expresses in the process of evidencing his own understanding. This expression, communicated, reaches, in, from, and for every human being, unto study, comprehension, realisation, and evidence, and is accepted. From this it also becomes known that the human being, in coexistence, expresses all-round resolution only by the realisation-based method, from which it is natural for the human family to be benefited. What is meant by the benefit of the human family is being within human awakened tradition, human universal orderliness, human undivided society, and, at the very least, human family orderliness. In family orderliness too, the human being, for the human being, or for the family,

[p. 96]

is also motivational, and by this method resolution remains bound up with benefit. Or, by the method of benefit, resolution remains bound up. In this way, in the human being, it is precisely understanding that is meant by seeing; understanding alone is seeing.

When the human being is treated as one measure, its purpose becomes intelligible. Animals can likewise be studied as measures complementary to human beings, the plant-order, and the earth. Their excreta and respiration increase the earth's fertility and complement the biological order. Animals also remove many diseases of the plant-order: forests without animals show more disease among trees and plants, while those with many animals show less. The same complementarity is visible in crops, where weeds, insects, birds, and especially honeybees are beneficial; where these are absent, crops diminish, destructive insects increase, and new diseases arise. The earth's adornment therefore deserves attention. It has arisen through complementarity and usefulness. The biological order appeared only after the material order, with its various species of atoms, had been established on earth; each order is complementary to the other. As the biological order develops, fertility increases, and greater fertility advances the biological order by increasing excellence among shrubs and plants. This is qualitative change (*gunatmak parivartan*), or development. The biological order is useful to the animal order, and the animal order complements it. Male and female cells first arise within the biological order; in the animal order, female and male body-compositions become mutually distinct. Thus, however far cell-compositions in the biological order

[p. 97]

advanced, they were insufficient for the animal order and required further development. Distinct male and female body-compositions consequently became manifest in animals. The biological order also provides their food, making complementarity and usefulness entirely clear among the material, biological, and animal orders. After biological cells attained satisfaction, their further composition-method enabled them to participate in animal body-compositions. In some animal bodies this incorporated the seven metals (elements) and produced the most enriched brain composition. Such a body provided the natural system in which biological cells could join through reproduction and compose the human body. This composition could arise from various animal bodies in different geographical environments, as indicated by the body-compositions of humans of many races. Each race may therefore trace its fixed point (*dhruv bindu*) to the accomplishment of the human body from a basic animal body, with a characteristic colour accompanying it. This provides a conjecture about the beginning of human lineages through innumerable generations, linking them to the development progression. Humans of diverse racial colours then secured their traditions in their respective geographical conditions, just as other animal traditions remain secure in their own colours and forms. As the most developed body-composition, the human body is the most useful for jeevan. Human beings seek to sharpen cognisance in the process of evidencing it. Their study is consequently evidenced as knowledge, wisdom, and science. From the coexistentialist viewpoint, fullness in these disciplines and participation with human-ness in orderliness and the overall orderliness are necessary and inevitable, and make it possible to explain what human beings have wished to know but left unknown.

By the above method it became clear that, of the whole of measures found in the four orders in existence, each single measure is itself an orderliness together with its inherence, and is a participation in the overall orderliness. This study has been presented for the human being to be meaningful precisely in the sense of participation, together with human-ness, in orderliness and in the overall orderliness.

In the discipline of measure, the study of form has already become clear for all four orders, in relation to form, property, essential nature, and dharma. Now it is necessary to become clear about property and essential nature. Essential nature is seen to be different across the four orders. In the material order, essential nature is seen in the form of the activity of combination-decomposition, or combination with one thing and decomposition from another. In the biological order, essential nature is found in the forms of vitalising (*sarakta*) and devitalising (*marakta*). Vitalising is in the sense of being abundant in oneself, and, secondly, in the sense of being useful for the animal order. Devitalising means the activity of creating obstruction in making one's own seed-arrangement abundant, and the condition of being disease- and death-causing for the animal order. In the animal order, essential nature has been recognised in the forms of the cruel and the non-cruel. Non-cruel means not killing any other living beings, not eating flesh, and continuing to live on plant foods. In this way both the cruel and the non-cruel become clear. In another language these are known as the carnivorous and the herbivorous; in a third language they can also be called the violent and the non-violent. The essential nature of the knowledge order is fortitude, valour, generosity, kindness, grace, and compassion. These themselves become clear as the human essential nature. Human-opposing essential nature is reckoned in the forms of baseness, wretchedness, and cruelty, together with covetousness of others' wealth, others' wife/husband, and inflicting pain on others. Those who remain in these tendencies are reckoned among the animalistic human and the demonic human. Both of these are human-opposing. There remains the possibility that those with human-opposing essential nature may gradually be transformed into humane essential nature. It is on this very basis that meaningful education is proposed — in other language, coexistentialist education is proposed.

What is meant by fortitude is remaining entirely clear about, and evidenced in respect of, human justice. Study and tradition alone are the sole basis for becoming entirely clear. Tradition is the bearer and carrier of the work of study; study keeps on occurring in the present. What is meant by valour is precisely this: remaining committed in faith and in behaviour to justice oneself, and, while remaining evidenced, dedicating and surrendering one's own body, mind, and wealth so that justice becomes accessible to people. This too becomes meaningful only by the method of education, and by the method of education for the populace. Generosity's

[p. 99]

meaning is precisely using the resources of body, mind, and wealth available within oneself through the methods of usefulness, right-use, and purposefulness. Usefulness means deploying these resources for bodily nourishment, protection, and the movement of society. Right-use means dedicating and surrendering objects while participating in undivided society. Purposefulness means participating in universal orderliness by the self-motivated, self-governing method.

Kindness means making substances available according to worthiness (*patrata*). Grace means enabling worthiness in relation to the available substance when commensurate worthiness is absent. Compassion means establishing both substance and worthiness when neither is present. Through kindness, grace, and compassion, the human being becomes glorious as the human, the deific human, and the divine human. Such glory is highly useful for the country, the earth, and the human being.

The principle of measure, in summary form, becomes intelligible in this way: the substance existing in the form of each single unit is a unit-measure. In the unit, form, property, essential nature, and dharma remain present in an indivisible form. Each and every atom alone is the root measure. That the root measure is the atom has become clear for this reason: that the unit which is defined is one that is itself an orderliness and participates in the overall orderliness. Where a unit exists but does not participate in orderliness, even in such units a tendency for participation in orderliness is nonetheless found — as with an atomic particle, or a deluded human being. A particle, in itself, is not able to evidence orderliness — that is, it is not able to express a determinate necessity — and therefore every atomic particle is propensive in the sense of participating in an atom and, as a result, evidencing orderliness. It is precisely determinate conduct that becomes known as orderliness. From this it becomes known that atomic particles recognise one another and carry each other out. The human being's recognising the human being is, even now, a necessity. There is one and only one thread for recognising the human being — human-ness; it is necessary only in the sense of the human goal. The human goal is resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, coexistence. It is only in the process of evidencing the human goal that we are able to make humane conduct clear. The evidence of this is in the forms of value, character, and ethics. To succeed at this universally, or in all humankind — or for it to be so — is the glory of awakened human tradition. On this very basis, every human being becomes, in himself, an orderliness together with human-ness, and comes to participate in the overall

orderliness — participates in undivided society, in universal orderliness. ## 3.10 Property, Essential nature, Dharma (गुण, स्वभाव, धर्म)

Property consists of generative, degenerative, and mediating activities and tendencies, and is assessed by their results. Creative and combinative activity is called generative tendency. Activity and tendency that decompose or destroy what has been manifested are called degenerative. The activities and tendency that maintain compositions formed through union and combination are called mediating. Every activity manifests as state and motion, and every activity has a tendency at its root. In every unit, propensity is dynamic and becomes manifest through mutual recognition; this can be recognised in every order, plane, and existent state. Every unit has a tendency to recognise. Recognising and fulfilling give rise to propensity, development progression, development, and awakening progression; knowing gives rise to awakening. On this basis, each unit's determinate necessity becomes clear as inherence (*tva*). Necessary propensity leads to necessary recognition and then to mutual recognition. Such recognition occurs only within orderliness together with inherence. Through mutual recognition, the units cease to be mysterious; nothing about them need remain unknown. Yet a question may arise during study: to what extent must every individual thing be tested? How long, for example, should we continue testing the sea drop by drop?

How long should we test atom after atom, or unit after unit in the biological, animal, and knowledge orders? Such a method can never reach the far shore. Every existent state, order, and plane is instead linked to the next like the rungs of a staircase. In the material order's development progression, physical substances from the smallest to the largest pass through many existent states and establish orderliness by expressing form, property, essential nature, and dharma: shape, volume, and mass as form; generative, degenerative, and mediating as property; combination-decomposition,

[p. 101]

for essential nature; and existence, for dharma — we have already understood these. From this it becomes known that merely from testing one drop of the ocean we come to understand the water of the entire ocean. It is in this very way that the method of study is able to become meaningful.

By the above method, the study of the biological order too has already been understood: namely, shape, volume, and bulk, for form; generative, degenerative, and mediating, for property; vitalising and devitalising, for essential nature; and, together with existence, growth, for dharma. Beyond this, in the animal order, this has been evidenced in some animals as jeevan and body being in a combined form. In the elaboration of this: the presence of jeevan is evidenced in those animals endowed with a body composed of the seven metals (elements), an enriched brain system, and the capacity to receive the human being's directive signals. In such animals, jeevan too has shape, volume, and bulk; the body too has these — however exceedingly subtle jeevan may be. In jeevan too, the generative, degenerative, and mediating property remains at work. In the human being, jeevan predominates. For this very reason, the body's generative, degenerative, and mediating property becomes secondary. In animals, essential nature and dharma are in a combined form. Cruelty and non-cruelty become clear in the combined form of body and jeevan. The hope of living, too — dharma — becomes clear in combined form. The whole of the animal order is explained by the hereditary method. In the same way, further, the elaboration of the human being's generative, degenerative, and mediating properties too becomes possible. In the human being too, property is most predominantly on the side of the mediating, which is expressed in the form of one's own glory. So long as the human being remains deluded, he regards the being of the self as paramount, and regards others as secondary. It is precisely the manifestation of this very tendency that has, from the distant past, become clear in the forms of treachery, rebellion, exploitation, and war. In this too, it is only the acceptance of the glory of the self that remains. The glory of the self is held to be good. Only with awakening does one come to understand the fact of the glory of the self existing together with the good of all; through delusion one remains unaware of this. When faith is placed in this, one becomes awakened, and then one becomes free from degenerative properties. So long as one is not free from degenerative properties, frustration, despair, and torment remain in effect. This is what is called suffering in human tradition. The awakened human being's property is, for ever and ever, mediating. Its universal form is: keeping awakened tradition intact, keeping universal orderliness intact, and, through study, keeping the thread of undivided society evidenced. This becomes manifest in the form of keeping justice evidenced within the family. In studying all these facts, an entirely clear outlook of goodness, resolution, and truth becomes evidenced for the whole of humankind. This alone is what is necessary. It is by this very traditional method that the whole of study becomes intelligible. It is through humane properties alone that the human being's fundamental distinctiveness becomes mutually intelligible. In the same way, the units present in every state, in every category, keep on being evidenced in expressed form together with their respective property, essential nature, and dharma-form

[p. 102]

itself. Across every order and plane other than the human, each unit is an orderliness together with its inherence. As a unit, the human too must awaken to the point where cognisance governs sensitivity. To be human means to know, believe, recognise, and fulfil the insentient and sentient (*jad-chaitanya*) nature present throughout the planes and orders of coexistence. In this way, wholeness and universality in the human, communality among animals, genus and species among plants, and the many existent states of the material order — differentiated by the number of particles in the development progression — are evidenced. Although existence is diverse, the arrangement of quantity across these orders and planes, through which the human family is recognised, is a subject for study within the thread of coexistence.

## 3.11 Strength–Power (State–Motion) (बल-शक्ति (स्थिति-गति))

Historically, humans have made many conjectures about strength (*bal*) and power (*shakti*). With the advent of modern science, applications were made to draw further conclusions about power. Yet science has reached no complete conclusion, nor has it produced anything for humanness; this merits reflection. Science has attempted to identify pressure as force, and motion, waves, and flow as power. Examples have been presented as electrical waves and magnetic force, sound waves and pressure, and the waves and pressure generated by flow. These studies have, however, denied the mediation (*madhyasthiyakaran*) at work in all these phenomena. They are based only on excited motion. This raises the question: where are strength and power when motion is in its natural state?

From the viewpoint of Madhyasth Darshan, strength is manifest in state and power in motion. Power is introduced and recognised as a mechanical process when one unit manifests an effect upon another; it is recognised on the basis of state. Humans have many suitable (*samicheen*) — that is, nearby and available — ways to test this principle. Consider a stone: when we lift it, our apprehension of the conjunction between the stone and ourselves is called weight. Weight, of its own

[p. 103]

accord, evidences the formula (*sutra*) of coexistence with the earth. Every human being clearly perceives weight in this way. Take an empty pot, fill it with water, soil, potatoes, or grain, and lift it: what is felt in lifting it is called weight. As one who gives concrete form to ideas (*manakar*), and as imaginative and free in action, the human being considered how weight could be measured and found an answer. Methods of weighing and measuring were established, followed by instruments capable of weighing and measuring ever-larger things, including the loads carried by trucks and trains. Everyone sees this daily. The entire phenomenon of weight is the arrangement (*vinyas*) through which coexistence with the earth is evidenced; it is a principle. In this way, weight is animated by coexistence. Every unit, forceful in state, evidences and displays power in motion.

In this sequence it comes into understanding that every object maintains its own orderliness within a definite good distance. For example, in one atom more than one atomic particle, remaining at a definite distance, manifests orderliness with inherence (*tva*) in the form of conduct, of which the human is the seer. If, within such a definite good distance, the distance increases, then the mediating activity working in the atom in the form of the nucleus (*madhyansh*) applies its force and calls the particles near. If an atomic particle begins to come close to the nucleus active at the centre, then too the nucleus, applying its force, fixes the particle at the good distance. This is the controlling activity that takes place in the atom. By these very activities, control over mutual distance remains. When atoms are in the form of molecules, it is on the basis of the nuclei that mutual attraction-force is exercised, on the basis of magnetic force-fullness. As a result, one atom joining with another atom is called a molecule, together with molecular bond. It is natural for such molecules to remain endowed with weight. Because of this very weight, the propensity remains for one molecule to join with another molecule while remaining within a definite good distance. From such propensities, atoms, molecules, and large material objects formed of molecules — bodies (*pind*) — stand presented before the human, such as this earth. All objects joined to the earth, together with this earth, up to the earth's atmosphere, evidence coexistence — solid with solid, liquid with liquid, rarefied with rarefied. The arrangements which the earth and the objects that are limbs of the earth make for this, we name falling from above, running, flying, and so on. From above, only all those things fall down which are in the form of solid and liquid.

[p. 104]

The arrangement that a liquid object makes in its eagerness to evidence coexistence with liquid is called rain or flow. In the same way, the solid object that falls from above — that is the arrangement occurring with the earth, which happens in the effect of that object's evidencing coexistence with the earth. To this very arrangement the name of gravitation has been given. In reality this is nothing but the arrangement of a solid object's evidencing coexistence with a solid object. This is the respectful acceptance. In gravitation, the notion of small and big keeps running. In the form of coexistence it has been seen that each one, together with its environment, is complete. In this there simply is no small or big. This is confirmed by the fact that each one is orderliness together with its inherence. Both these principles are seen being fulfilled in the combined outcome of natural-state motion and definite conduct. In this sequence, each object being a constituent limb of this earth, honouring coexistence with the earth is, as a result, a natural propensity. The arrangement of this propensity remains bounded up to the atmosphere of this earth. This earth too, together with its environment, is complete. However many limbs and organs are contained in the completeness, all these remain bounded with the complete. 'Bounded' means evidencing a definite intent through a definite arrangement. Such definite intent is precisely the evidencing of coexistence — solid with solid, liquid with liquid, rarefied with rarefied. Whatever activity occurs in the course of evidencing coexistence, we call it arrangement. Its outcome is nothing but the evidence of coexistence. By this method the understanding that coexistence itself is existence becomes complete.

In relativity there is apprehension of small and big. Completeness slips out of sight. Only in completeness can each one be respected, be evaluated. It is the human alone who recognizes such evaluation and the standing of respect. When the human evidences each one in coexistence together with completeness, in such a situation it is found natural for the human to give attention toward his own completeness. Contrary to this, by the relative method attention goes toward small and big. Considering oneself big becomes a necessity. To keep that intact, keeping the other small also becomes a necessity. In this sequence treachery, revolt, exploitation, starvation, bombardment — all these deeds get done. When there is dialogue about them, their impropriety is accepted. In this manner the human becomes self-contradictory. Such a situation we address in the language of 'belonging neither to home nor to shore'. From this very thing indeterminism gets prepared. As a result the human falls prey to every kind of frustration or

[p. 105]

excitation. He moves away from natural-state motion. As a result, asociality and disorder come to hand. Unanticipated events in the form of disorder take place. This has proved entirely unnecessary for the human race. Therefore, as its alternative, it became necessary to be endowed with natural-state motion, state, and — along with certainty — hopefulness with definite plan, and evidence. This, by the method of coexistence, is at hand for all humankind.

The central formula is the evidencing of coexistence itself. It is natural to evaluate coexistence in every work-arrangement, propensity, endeavour, activity, behaviour, and result. The human being alone performs this evaluation.

Human beings have long awaited their own evidence through the coexistential, or truth, method. To fulfil this expectation, study of the coexistential activity-system proved sufficient for human beings to evidence humaneness in behaviour. Its unveiling was therefore necessary. Coexistence alone is the ultimate truth.

State-motion is the combined form of pressure and flow. Pressure may remain without visible flow: a stone rests upon the earth, and the water in a pond shows no flow. A river, by contrast, visibly flows, and its pressure is evident. Water flows down a slope that ultimately joins the sea; by the natural method, or the method of destiny (*niyati*), the water of rivers therefore reaches the sea. Water pressure and flow can each be converted into another kind of pressure and flow, with pressure as the principal cause of change. Steam under definite pressure, for example, can operate particular machines and produce magnetic electricity. Magnetism is thus evident as pressure and the electric wave as flow. One kind of force can become the agent of another force and flow. Human tradition similarly learned to operate machines by heating — that is, burning — oily substances. Such machines perform many kinds of work, including movement on land, water, and air. Oil-combustion also operates machines that generate electricity, producing electromagnetic flow and pressure. In the same way, heat released from radiant (radioactive) materials can operate electricity-transmitting machines;

[p. 106]

human beings have learned to operate these as well. Their discharges and residues — from minerals, coal, oil, and radiant materials — have become causes of pollution, as studied earlier. As alternatives, attention has been directed toward the flow-force of water and must also be directed toward wind, ocean waves, and solar heat for obtaining magnetic electric current. Only then can human beings be freed from a gravely criminal, polluting tradition opposed to destiny, development, awakening, humanity, and the earth's environment. Examining state-motion by the method of existence — that is, ascertaining it through testing, observation, and survey — shows that every unit expresses strength in state and power in motion. This is equally true of units from the smallest to the largest. As established earlier, every unit endowed with state-motion is itself an orderliness and participates in orderliness. **Strength in state evidences orderliness in oneself; power in motion evidences participation in the overall orderliness.** Every unit is energised and forceful because it remains saturated in the omnipresent reality. The atom lies at the root of every unit and functions in physical, chemical, and jeevan activity. Jeevan atoms are free from molecular and weight bonds but, while deluded, remain bound by hope, thought, and desire. All other atoms participating in physicochemical activity remain joined by molecular and weight bonds, through which small and large structures form. The atom is the smallest structure. Atoms manifest grandeur as molecules and molecular structures, all animated by the formula of coexistence. More than one particle in an atom, more than one atom in a molecule, and more than one molecule in a molecular structure are all evidence of this formula. Structures are either physical or chemical, and their mutual complementarity and usefulness are clear. Molecules of every kind continually express both state and motion; these activities and expressions have always been present and never cease. The human being is therefore a unit fit to study them and must evidence orderliness in state and participation in the overall orderliness in motion.

[p. 107]

Only then is the evidence of mental wellbeing (*manah svasthata*) presented in tradition. Without mental well-being, all-round resolution in the human is simply not possible. Therefore mental well-being is for the human an utterly essential or supremely necessary attainment.

In this sequence, understanding naturally enables the human being to manifest mental well-being as all-round resolution. This grandeur — endowment with resolution — succeeds in evidencing prosperity, fearlessness, and coexistence. Such success is the succour and *pran* in, from, and for the human tradition. *Tran* means state; *pran* means motion. Inspiration in motion, orderliness evidenced in the environment or sphere of effect, and being an orderliness oneself in state together form meaningful grandeur. Human beings naturally hope to maintain such grandeur. In the atom likewise, strength in state and power in motion establish a sphere of effect. A good mutual distance preserves the spheres of effect of both units. Every unit that maintains its originality within mutuality through such effects is under control.

A good mutual distance means that each unit's sphere of effect remains formed and secure. This sphere of effect, as well as state, shape, volume, and mass, exists only in the omnipresent reality. Shape is present only together with activity. In every atom, activity takes the form of effort (*śram*), motion, and result. **Effort is the grandeur of an existent state; motion is the grandeur of spreading effect; result is the grandeur of a different existent state. Every result is thus arranged in a quantitative and qualitative chain.** Results may be fixed (*dhruva*) or long-enduring, distinctions that become clear in assessing the stability and instability of existent states. Threaded by the formula of coexistence, every existent state is meaningful through complementarity and useful complementarity. The meaningfulness of all things thus becomes intelligible. A molecule has the same history: molecules join with other molecules to form a body while maintaining field control in relation to each atom's sphere of effect. Human beings contribute nothing to this process. Through complementarity and usefulness, these activities evidence meaningfulness up to awakening and its continuity. Every stage remains joined to the others. Physical activity cannot be separated from chemical activity, chemical activity from the activity of living beings, or physicochemical activity

[p. 108]

apart from the activity of the human, no work or behaviour is accomplished, nor can it be accomplished. All these remain inseparably joined with the human. They keep honouring their bounds indeed. For honouring the bounds with these, the human needs to be informed.

All other-than-human nature is useful to the human being, yet the human failure to complement it has become the basis of every problem-creating propensity. This failure also obstructed the use and right-use of attainments produced through giving concrete shape to ideas, because mental well-being remained neglected. Its critical necessity is therefore clear in the present. Mental well-being is universally attainable only by the coexistential method; this is the clear possibility of universal good. Human beings become capable of evidencing mental well-being only after becoming endowed with resolution. The completeness of understanding is itself mental well-being, and understanding is the spontaneous acceptance of coexistence. Complete knowledge thus takes the form of acceptance, while every human being retains the right to accept or reject.

The grandeur of state-motion is present in the earth, the solar system (*saur vyuh*), many solar systems, each galaxy, and all galaxies. Study makes everything from the atom to the earth accessible as evidence in its own state-motion. Beyond this direct study, the state-motion of other planetary globes is accepted, as is that of a galaxy through the activity of its many solar systems. The omnipresent reality is accepted together with them. Within this vast field of acceptance, studying one's own necessities as evidence is itself a human necessity, and the extent of such study depends on that necessity. The vastness of knowing and believing has already been accepted in relation to Omnipresence and the infinite. On this basis, studying one locale or one unit provides assurance about the form, property, essential nature, and dharma of all units of that kind. By testing one drop of seawater, for example, we accept what the water of the whole sea is like. But who is prepared to measure how much water it contains? We measure according to our needs, and human necessity is limited. Examining the activity of measuring shows that it

[p. 109]

consists only of addition and subtraction; measuring and weighing apply the sequence 1, 2, 3. As one side gains, another decreases. Bringing addition and subtraction together reveals only the existent state: it is exactly what and how much it is. Such an existent state is neither more nor less and neither decreases nor increases; existence itself therefore neither decreases nor increases. The limits of testing accordingly depend on human necessity. Through addition and subtraction, human tradition has accepted the meanings of minus infinity and plus infinity. The infinite has thus been imagined or accepted as what cannot, to some extent, be enumerated. Until now, apprehension of the omnipresent reality has not been prevalent. Once it is apprehended, the nature of infinite objects becomes intelligible. Study as understanding — recognition that the infinite is inseparable in Omnipresence — thereby becomes the basis of all-round resolution. Every unit is present with its state and motion. 'The present' (*vartaman*) means continuing to be current; to be current is to remain active; and to remain active is to make conduct clear. Clear conduct means orderliness together with inherence in state and participation in the overall orderliness in motion. The earth participates in one solar system, the group of planetary globes joined with the sun. Every planetary globe acts ceaselessly in its own state-motion. Without interfering with one another, they mutually recognise one another, perform determinate conduct, and participate in the orderliness of the solar system.

State and motion are therefore both present, and the earth's state-motion becomes accessible to study. One solar system, arranging its activity with many others at a definite good distance, displays the grandeur of solar-system inherence. Scientists now call such an array of numerous solar systems, or planets, a galaxy. There are many galaxies, and by maintaining good mutual distance they participate in the larger orderliness.

## 3.12 Projection–Reflection (परावर्तन-प्रत्यावर्तन)

On the issue of state-motion and strength-power, the matter has already become clear with units from the smallest of the small to the biggest of the big, and that too in all planes (*pad*) and stages. Now we give attention to knowledge, thought, decision, evidence, result related to projection-reflection, upon knowledge being the witness.

[p. 110]

Human beings seek to accomplish every activity in relation to a destination (*gamyasthali*). When a person begins to walk, the destination must be before them; if there is only walking and no destination, what would we call it? Every person can consider this. The same applies to ants, elephants, animals, birds, and other living creatures: each moves in relation to a destination, as shown by its arrival at some definite place. Even crocodiles swimming in water occupy definite, determined places. Some things occur everywhere on earth, others only here and there, and still others in very small areas. Air surrounds the earth; sunlight reaches one side and not the other; a river flows far and wide, while a mountain, shrub, or plant remains in one place. Humans and birds live on this same earth. People once thought that only birds travelled great distances; now human beings too roam the whole earth. Yet even while roaming, each person remains in some place. Being somewhere is inevitable and is joined with state-motion. There is no motion without state; motion is evidenced only together with a positive state. Human state-motion is clear: moving from one place to another displays both being and relocation. Projection and reflection proceed along with them.

We recognise projection and reflection within ourselves through recognising and enabling recognition, understanding and enabling understanding, and learning and teaching. Projection-reflection is clearest in understanding and making understood: through reflection we understand, and in making understood we project. Human activities are physical, verbal, or mental and may be done (*krit*), caused (*karit*), or intended (*anumodit*). Projection lies at the root of all these, and reflection at the root of projection; within the grandeur of reflection, projection proceeds by an interwoven method. Projection becomes meaningful only in relation to a person who wishes to understand — only then does making the understood understood fulfil its purpose. Projection must therefore be consistent with sequence, work, behaviour, result, and purpose. It becomes meaningful through production in work, justice in behaviour, human-aspiration in result, and, in purpose,

[p. 111]

on the basis of the jeevan goal. The acceptance that these — that is, production, justice, human-aspiration, jeevan-aspiration — become meaningful is spontaneous in every human. This point has become clear earlier.

Every human being becoming endowed with understanding is an ordinary, common, and most desired event. Once understanding is present, all the other links join naturally. Together with understanding, the result must become a source of satisfaction. The result, the evidence of understanding, and the meaningfulness of all human work, behaviour, and orderliness arise through the fulfilment of human-aspiration. Humane orderliness, humane education, thinking, and thought likewise become meaningful only through its fulfilment. The central point is that when human-aspiration is fulfilled, jeevan-aspiration is fulfilled as a matter of course.

Projection has already been described as making the understood understood, teaching the learned, and getting the done done. Beyond these is intention (*anumodan*), which becomes meaningful through the same three senses. Human beings are projected through the methods of done, caused, and intended action. What is projected is the human being's fundamental form: endowment with knowledge, wisdom, and science. It is evidenced through those three methods. In this sequence of projection, the human being is evaluated within undivided society and universal orderliness. Undivided society recognises human necessities and establishes formula-orderliness; a third method provides explanation of meaning. The human being's place, in the sense of human necessity, from family orderliness to universal orderliness, has been clarified earlier. In this sequence, human meaning is evidenced and becomes meaningful. Complete knowledge, wisdom, and science therefore extend from formula to explanation. The seed of coexistence is fulfilled as aspiration, as the formula of knowledge-wisdom-science, and as the explanation of work, behaviour, and orderliness. This is the evidence of an awakened human tradition and makes the human being's spontaneous awakened propensity clear. Every work and behaviour undertaken as evidence is projection. Projection thereby fulfils its goal and intent, making the universally good, beautiful, and resolution-filled expectation meaningful.

[p. 112]

Happiness is the intent. The human goal is resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, and evidence lived in coexistence; the expectation is universality and undividedness. Intent, goal, and expectation become meaningful through complementarity. When the goal is fulfilled, resolution is realised as happiness; resolution and prosperity as peace; resolution, prosperity, and fearlessness as contentment; and resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, and coexistence as bliss. In this sequence, realisation (*anubhav*) occurs in coexistence and in undivided society and universal orderliness; freedom is realised in production beyond necessity, and resolution in every activity. These are recognised as happiness, peace, contentment, and bliss. Every person can test this against their own realisation. We verified the result only after living it; satisfaction lies in universalising it among people.

For every person, the realisation-oriented method — study, research, testing, and observation — evidences human-aspiration through realisation and, in projection, through a realisation-based system. Study, enlightenment (*bodh*), the realisation-oriented method, and the realisation-based system thus enable living in accordance with human-aspiration. This is the fundamental aim of study. As the number of people living by this method grows, undivided society and universal orderliness are evidenced, followed by resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, and coexistence; every awakened person can then be recognised. Every man and woman wants to awaken. Universalising education fit for awakening is therefore the only means. Humane education treats every issue necessary in, from, and for the human being as an object of study.

In this manner the human tradition does remain projected in the form of becoming successively more awakened. In reflection it does remain drenched in happiness, peace, contentment, bliss. Human-aspiration does get evidenced in itself by the work-behaviour-orderliness method. This alone is what living means. That is, work, goal, and purpose being evidence, and their continuity remaining — this alone is what living means.

From within the human race, knowledge, science, wisdom is evidenced. This alone is the form of undisputed innateness (*svatva*). In the projection of such innateness, on the basis of the assessment and acceptance of meaningfulness alone, assessment keeps forming in innateness; being evidenced from within ourselves toward continuous excellence does keep coming about. Once evidenced, a chain of excellence forms in the evidence method. This becomes the formula of firmness in reflection. In the human, only by the evidence method does meaningful capital, by the reflection method, keep growing continuously sharper and increasing

[p. 113]

indeed. Increase means that evidence in one matter today is joined by evidence in two matters tomorrow and three thereafter. Reflection preserves these attainments in enlightenment and realisation as acceptance, and this accumulated acceptance becomes the basis for further projection. Realisation is established in enlightenment as resolution, which can then be evidenced through projection. Reflection therefore makes projection a continuing source for realisation. This is the formula and explanation for evidencing awakening: every reflection is a formula of realisation, and every projection explains that formula. **Through reflection, the human being evidences knowledge, science, and wisdom; through projection, participation in work, behaviour, and orderliness.** This is awakened tradition, and maintaining it from generation to generation is every human being's duty and obligation.

Projection–reflection activity can now be studied in considerable detail. Within tradition, consciousness-development value education-sanskar is an interwoven activity: teaching occurs through projection and study through reflection. Teaching is heard articulation (*shruti*), or language. Study aims to enlighten the reality (*vastu*) in existence that language signifies; this is reflection. By the realisation-based method, enlightenment of every reality in coexistence can be projected. Study therefore follows a realisation-oriented procedure, and teaching is its corresponding projection. Enlightenment of meaning and realisation are the outcomes of study. Reflecting enlightened meaning into realisation is natural because every meaning refers to a reality in existence. Without the light of realisation, study cannot enlighten reality. Every such enlightenment attained thus far has been evidenced in realisation and presented for study.

The complete study of the human being as reality-enlightenment was not possible within materialism, because chemical-physical phenomena account only for the body. Every human being exists as the combined form of jeevan and body and is present through tradition. The body is one principal basis of tradition, which proceeds from generation to generation. Yet body tradition is limited to formation and deformation, whereas in human tradition jeevan and

[p. 114]

body evidence a combined superior status. That is, it makes clear glories greater than the body. Where there is body and jeevan is not, in such a situation there is no human. Therefore in the designation 'human' there is acceptance only of the combined form of jeevan and body. This too has already come into the human's understanding. It is the body that is living and lifeless; lifelessness is simply the separation of jeevan. Livingness itself is the proper expression, the manifestation, of jeevan and body. In this sequence, recognizing human satisfaction and jeevan satisfaction has been a necessity. With human satisfaction, jeevan satisfaction does become meaningful. The study of this fact has become clear before. Human satisfaction being in the form of the goal — human jeevan is evidenced becoming meaningful. In this very sequence, for study, the human adopted the realisation-oriented procedure by the realisation-based method. Its clarification and mention is what is here. Attention to jeevan satisfaction by the human's spontaneous method is necessary for this very reason: that only with the deployment of jeevan strength and power do body protection, nourishment, and societal motion (*samaj gati*) become clear. In the situation of jeevan not being there, the protection and nourishment of the body cannot occur. On the contrary, such a body is declared a dead body and is consigned. In this way we come to this conclusion: body, nourishment, protection are accomplished only with livingness. Therefore, as much as we are able to know, we are able to desire less than that; as much as we are able to desire, we are able to do less than that; as much as we are able to do, we are able to consume less than that. The result becomes known: as far as the limit of consuming goes, it is the body alone. For the nourishment and protection of the body, the use of things has been recognized; and the activity-processes of things useful for societal motion have also been recognized. The tele-communication machines and instruments that are employed or are useful for societal motion are given the name telecommunication, in which far-travel, far-seeing (television), and far-hearing are included.

In the awakened human tradition, the use, right-use, and purposeful methods of things get accomplished and get evidenced. Usefulness — that is, the limit of a thing's usefulness — becomes clear in the form of family-based necessity. Production more than necessity has become possible in the human tradition for this reason: that whatever we produce, however much we produce, we are not able to consume that much. Therefore the formula of production more than necessity becomes clear. This production more than necessity became spontaneous by this method: that there is inexhaustible power and strength in jeevan; production work being accomplished in the combined form of jeevan and body;

## 3.15 Time (काल)

Human tradition has long considered knowledge, knower, and object of knowledge through the viewpoints of *Adhyatma-vada*, *Adhidaivika-vada*, and *Adhibhautika-vada*, collectively called idealism (*adarshvad*). Their common ground is mystery: what lies beyond the physical, divinity, and spirituality are all treated as mysterious, making both the basis and destination of idealism mystery. Differences of opinion consequently arise. Many applications and practices were attempted within these disciplines, from exercise, means, yoga, and meditation to worship, recitation, and prayer. Their promised outcomes were moksha and heaven, both themselves mysterious, and these were called the destination.

The basis too became a mystery in this way. It is from mystery alone, in mystery alone — from the void, from Brahma — that all this creation, sustenance, and dissolution occurred; or, from some substance beyond physical activity, creation, sustenance, and dissolution occurred. In this way the beginning was from mystery, and the end too was from mystery. The end, an auspicious mystery, was from heaven and moksha. Auspicious, because heaven and moksha bring joy; mystery, because its ending occurs in mystery. According to idealism, which is the combined intent of these three kinds — regarding the scene, the seer, and the worldview: according to the Brahma-doctrinaires, Brahma alone is the seer, Brahma alone, according to his own resolve, is the scene, and Brahma alone is the worldview by which it is beheld. In the same way the god-centred ones too say — as one gets to read according to the documents — that, according to them, three are propounded: Shakti-knowledge, Shiva-knowledge, and Vishnu-knowledge. In Shakti alone is the entirety of the scene, creation, sustenance, dissolution. In the same way, in all three, this is god-dependent. All that is seen exists precisely because it is, in the form of the scene, the resolve of the gods; the gods alone are the seer, and the one who beholds too is the god. In the same way, according to *Adhibhautika-vada* too, it has been said that there is some mysterious seer, scene, and worldview more powerful than the physical. In this same sequence, a statement has also been made regarding knowledge, knower, and object of knowledge. Brahma alone, or the god, is himself, in the forms of knowledge, knower, and object of knowledge, concentration, the one who concentrates, and object of concentration — all of these. In the forms of effect, cause, and doer too, they are ready to accept God or the god. This has been the tenor of the voice up to now.

[p. 147]

Materialist thought holds that differences in composition produce differences in consciousness, yet no evidence of this is found. Physical substance alone was taken to be the source of consciousness, but testing stone and soil reveals nothing of the kind.

From the coexistentialist viewpoint, study of the human being as the combined form of jeevan and body makes intelligible the status of jeevan as conscious. Jeevan, as conscious nature, awakens through awakening progression; this awakening must be evidenced in human tradition. The conjunction of body and jeevan — coexistence — is necessary in human tradition. Ten activities are accomplished in jeevan: realisation and evidence; the enlightenment of realisation and resolve; the direct recognition and imaging of resolve; the comparison of images and their analysis; and the tasting and selection of that analysis. These ten activities become evident after jeevan awakens. So long as the body is taken to be jeevan, only four and a half of these activities can be accomplished; the remaining five and a half cannot be evidenced. This crisis torments the human being. Internal contradiction arises because the powers of jeevan cannot be evidenced, while the delusion of taking the body to be jeevan causes problems and leaves the human being agitated and beset by external affliction. Treachery, exploitation, and war have consequently continued to dominate the human family, or have been regarded as unavoidable. It is worth asking what more degraded picture the human family could present, even as human beings continue to regard this very condition as glory.

The coexistentialist method brings within reach the study of jeevan and body, of coexistence, of jeevan's indivisibility in coexistence, and of all physical and chemical activities occurring within coexistence. It also makes accessible the study of the activities through which chemical and physical substances, the entire plant and animal orders, and human body-compositions are formed. Because the brain system is the most highly developed composition in the human body, the freedom of action and imaginativeness of jeevan are expressed through thought, reflection, activity, and results; those results, in turn, prompt further thought and reflection. Inquiry and investigation are thus directed toward the point at which imaginativeness and freedom of action are satisfied, because we seek freedom from treachery, rebellion, exploitation, and war. This aspiration is presumably accepted by most well-intentioned people. The need for coexistentialist study therefore remains.

[p. 148]

Coexistentialist thinking enables precise study of knowledge, knower, and object of knowledge. Jeevan is the knower. Knowledge comprises the holistic view of coexistence, knowledge of jeevan, and knowledge of humane conduct. The object of knowledge is to make human-aspiration and jeevan-aspiration meaningful through awakening by evidencing the human and jeevan goals within undivided society and universal orderliness.

Seer, scene, and worldview are explained in the same way: jeevan is the seer, existence-as-coexistence is the scene, and the philosophy of existence is the worldview.

Concentration, the one who concentrates, and its object follow the same sequence. Awakening is the object, concentration is accomplished through understanding, and the human being who evidences understanding is the one who concentrates, thereby recognising jeevan.

Doer, effect, and cause are likewise clear. Jeevan is the doer; the human being and the establishment of the deific and divine-human statuses are the effect; and ever-present existence-as-coexistence is the cause.

Aim, means, and seeker are also purposefully identified. The human being is the seeker and jeevan-awakening is the aim. Awakening establishes human innateness, freedom, and right as understanding, and understanding as knowledge, science, and wisdom; this is the evidence of awakening. Every individual can examine the meaningfulness of this aim. Imaginativeness, freedom of action, and evidence embodied in tradition are the means of research and investigation. When human tradition bears and carries authenticity as evidence, understanding forms the evidence of undivided society and universal orderliness. Participation in universal orderliness keeps that evidence universally accessible. As a constituent part of this orderliness, humane education-and-culture becomes glorious, and universality within human tradition becomes meaningful through knowledge and education-and-culture. This is the meaning of the tradition of evidence. A general

[p. 149]

survey reveals it to be so — universal, that is, acceptance in all humankind. It was extremely easy for every man and woman to take to heart, to test within oneself, and to determine the meaningfulness or meaninglessness of, the whole row of evidence formed by the coexistentialist method across all the disciplines of practice, study, work, and behavioural orderliness.

The human being's aim is awakening. Its innateness, freedom, and right take the form of knowledge, science, and wisdom, whose evidence is the form of undivided society, universal orderliness through work-behaviour, which is linked to one another as a chain. In this, no link can be separated. The aim, as it stands in the form of tradition — human-aspiration, jeevan-aspiration — cannot possibly be evidenced without coexistentialist knowledge, wisdom, and science. It can be evidenced only by the coexistentialist method. Coexistence is no mystery or struggle. In natural form, it is a thing intelligible to, and illumined from, all humankind. Because every human being does indeed want to live and obtain happiness only in mutuality with someone. By this method the glory of coexistence becomes intelligible.

In living together, another human being first appears through relationship: as mother, then father, followed by brother, sister, friend, teacher (*guru*), preceptor (*acharya*), elders, neighbours, and similar relations maintained in tradition. Living discloses relationships, and through them mutual recognition and carrying-out according to purpose. Neither carrying-out without recognition nor recognition without carrying-out is possible. Purpose is the desired attainment for which relationships are formed and fulfilled; this gives rise to responsibility and duty. Both arise naturally with relationship. Duty determines what is to be done and how; responsibility determines what is to be attained and inclines one toward attaining it. In every relationship, these determinations are joined and are carried out by the self-motivated method. Only through awakening

[p. 150]

does such fulfilment become clear. In delusion, the purposes and goals of relationships remain obscure, and relationships are governed chiefly by sensation, the accumulation of convenience, and conflict. Having long endured these viewpoints, human tradition has become desolate in respect of mental well-being.

Whereas, by the coexistentialist method, the possibility of mental well-being is fitting and accessible for all humankind.

By the coexistentialist method, all relationships can be recognised through meaningful purposes informed by cognisance. Universal orderliness and undivided society provide their social form; human-aspiration and jeevan-aspiration provide their goal. Their evidence, acceptance, and evaluation transform work, behaviour, orderliness, and conduct. This is the outcome of coexistentialist knowledge, science, and wisdom.

Human-aspiration — the natural evidence of resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, coexistence.

Jeevan-aspiration — is reckoned in the forms of happiness, peace, contentment, joy.

## 3.18 Seer, Doer, Enjoyer

In awakened human tradition, fundamental right is evidenced through the seer, doer, and enjoyer statuses. In most countries, spiritual traditions prepared people to accept God, the Supreme Soul, Brahma, goddesses, and gods. Scriptures were composed, and their teachings were accepted as divine speech, God's speech, or a voice from the sky. Treatises based on this belief gave rise to methods of preaching. These scriptures arose when people in one country had little contact with other countries. Once such contact became possible, communities began to study one another's religious texts and found that teachings accepted in one were rejected in another. This deepened religious disagreements and raised the question of where divine speech was to be found. People naturally expect a religious text to be free of dispute; if dispute does arise,

[p. 151]

what remains of religion? Yet the idealist method has not provided an undisputed religious text, while materialism sees no need for one. Ordinary human beings have consequently remained tormented by internal contradiction and afflicted in the external world by treachery, rebellion, exploitation, and fear of war. Relief or happiness has been attributed to the grace of God, a god, or a guru, while people continue to endure joy and sorrow without trust in themselves. Yet every person recognises that trust within mutuality brings relief, whether for a moment, a day, or a year. Treating that relief solely as divine or a guru's grace obscures the role of human imaginativeness and freedom of action, through which people have given concrete form to their ideas. This deserves reflection. Within human tradition, resolution is the evidence of mental well-being.

The matter to which attention needs to be paid here is just this: whatever is reflected before us — is there, at its root, any doer-status or not, is there one who does or not? If, at its root, there is no doer, then the ascription of the status of universal-doer became wrong. If there is no doer, then what would the enjoyer-status be like? If this is not so, then what became of the seer-status? All these questions have kept arising one after another. The answer to these questions is not to be found by the idealist method.

When, by the coexistentialist method, thinking and reflection began, and decisions began to be recognised, then it became known that whatever is functioning, in coexistence, in the form of the four orders, all of it is indeed itself in the self-motivated doer-status. For this reason, activity, in itself, is clearly functioning in the sense of the development progression in the forms of effort, motion, and result. In this same sequence, awakening was recognised in the forms of the immortality of result, the restfulness of effort, and the destination of motion. It has become possible to recognise the immortal status of result in the sense of development, the restfulness of effort, and the destination of motion, as awakening and awakening-completeness. In this way the human being himself becomes the knower of the awakening-status,

[p. 152]

the herald (*udghata*), and the unit capable of evidencing it. The preceding study shows that all units are conjoined within coexistence — the omnipresent reality — and function by the self-motivated method because they are energised and forceful; no external agent is required. Every composition, from the smallest to the largest, is functional in itself. This functionality as effort, motion, and result culminates in the development progression as the immortality of result in the developed status. The constitutionally complete atom, which is the conscious status, is its testimony.

The doer-status is clear in the development progression. In the conscious status and awakening progression, the combined form of jeevan and body expresses hope, thought, and desire to live, as seen in the animal order and deluded human tradition. The hope to live brings wanting and not-wanting into evidence; together with it, human beings express the desire to be happy and give concrete form to ideas. The desire for happiness thus manifests as the human being's own mentality. Because the doer-status is already present, the expectation of the enjoyer-status — of enjoying happiness — arises, and with it the seer-status becomes necessary. Establishing the seer-status is evidence of understanding.

Understanding establishes the seer-status and, together with knowledge, the knower-status. The human being alone evidences the doer, enjoyer, and seer statuses, and becomes glorious as the combined form of jeevan and body. The conscious unit's grandeur lies in attaining the seer- and knower-status; by evidencing itself within human tradition, it also becomes glorious in the doer- and enjoyer-status. The physical-chemical world occupies the self-motivated doer-status, while awakened jeevan is seer and knower. From this position, the use, right-use, and purposeful deployment of the doer-status enable jeevan-aspiration and human-aspiration to be evidenced; remaining deluded instead brings affliction. In the physical-chemical world, the doer-status becomes clear from results within the existent state and development progression, and the enjoyer-status forms together with it because results appear in many existent states. To see is to understand, and understanding is the complete grandeur present in jeevan. In the conscious world, the seer-status is therefore linked

[p. 153]

to this. This became clear as a result of awakening. This is the main matter. In the whole of chemical and physical activity, in the animal order, and in the deluded human being, testimony to being doer and enjoyer has forever remained formed. For this very reason, it is precisely together with the seer-status that the human being, as doer, has the arrangement for becoming meaningfully successful — not in the deluded condition. So be it: it is natural for the seer-status to be the glory of awakened tradition alone. Therefore, keeping human tradition intact is, for all of us human beings, the all-auspicious thread, the all-auspicious explanation.
