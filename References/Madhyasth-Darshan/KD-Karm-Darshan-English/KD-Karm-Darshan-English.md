## Alternative (*vikalp*)

1. The study of humans could not be completed through the unstable, uncertain, physicochemical-centric method of science, nor through mystery-based idealistic contemplation. Both these ideologies categorised humans as mere living beings (*jeeva*).

    As an alternative, through the existence-based human-centric contemplation method of Madhyasth Darshan (Co-existentialism), the human being is identified and established in the knowledge order.

    According to Madhyasth Darshan, the human being is the knower, and existence as coexistence is the object to be known (understandable and acceptable) — this is the knowledge of the holistic view (*darshan*). Along with this, the knowledge of *jeevan* and the knowledge of humane conduct, which validate coexistence, are now accessible for study.

    I have presented this existence-based human-centric knowledge — Madhyasth Darshan and Co-existentialism — in the form of treatises (*shastra*) for study.

2. Prior to this existence-based human-centric contemplation, my initiation was in spiritual knowledge and Vedic tradition-based worship and action (*upasana*–*karma*).

3. According to the Vedanta view, "Brahma is truth, the world is an illusion (*maya*)," while also stating that the world of living beings originated from Brahma. In the language of tradition: *upasana* refers to the rituals and ceremonies for becoming deities (Vedic worship); *karma* refers to all actions aimed at attaining heaven; in *Manu Dharma Shastra*, daily duties (*nitya karma*) for the four classes (*varnas*) and four stages of life (*ashramas*); in ritual texts (*karmakanda*), sixteen kinds of rituals from conception (*garbha sanskar*) to death (*mrityu sanskar*).

[p. 5]

4. If Brahma is "truth, knowledge, and infinite," how can the world of living beings originated from Brahma be false? In the process of seeking answers to these questions from contemporary Vedic scholars and experts:

    I was assured that the unknown becomes known in the state of *samadhi*. On the basis of scriptural support, I resolved to perform *sadhana*, *samadhi*, and *samyama*. In the states of *sadhana*, *samadhi*, and *samyama*, I attained complete understanding of the entire existence as coexistence through the method of study and realization, as a result of which this Alternative (*vikalp*) emerged in the form of the treatises of Madhyasth Darshan (Co-existentialism).

5. According to idealistic scriptures and mystery-based God-centric thought and tradition, knowledge is unmanifest and inexpressible. In Madhyasth Darshan, knowledge is manifest and expressible, understandable through study, and verifiable through behaviour, making evidence easily accessible to all.

6. According to materialism, which is based on instability and uncertainty, science is considered to be knowledge, in which rules are treated as human-made. In its alternative, according to the existence-based human-centric contemplation of coexistence, existence is stable, development and awakening are definite, and all rules are natural.

7. Existence is not merely physicochemical; rather, physicochemical entities and *jeevan* entities are present as inseparable in the omnipresent space. This is the core formula of the treatises of Madhyasth Darshan (Co-existentialism).

8. Where I began this bodily journey, my ancestors were renowned as Vedic scholars (*vedamurti*). In my home and village, the Vedas, and Vedic thought related to Vedanta, Upanishads, and Darshan, were continuously heard as language, sounds, and melodies. In my family tradition, Vedic-aligned worship (*upasana*), adoration (*aradhana*), prayer (*archana*), and libations (*tarpan*) were performed.

[p. 6]

9. In our family tradition, high-caliber scholars were naturally service-oriented and industrious in their practice of behaviour and action. I accepted the industriousness and service-oriented tendencies, but question marks remained in my mind regarding their scholarship.

10. **First question:** If Brahma is truth, how can the world and living beings that originated from Brahma-truth be false?

    **Second question:** How can Brahma alone be the cause of bondage (*bandhan*) and liberation (*moksha*)?

    **Third question:** What is evidence (*pramana*) — is the word the evidence, or the bearer of the word? Is the authoritative statement (*apta-vakya*) the evidence, or its enunciator? Is the scripture the evidence, or its propounder?

    **Fourth question:** In the context of independent India, a constitutional assembly was formed, yet the constitution accepts elected representatives without defining nation, nationality, and national character.

    How can public mandate and representatives be genuine when they arise from vote–money alliances?

    The constitution states secularism, yet explicitly mentions numerous castes, sects, and communities.

    The constitution states equality, yet details reservations and their procedures.

    In this democracy, the election of public representatives is governed by alliances of vote and money. What kind of democracy is this?

11. To find liberation from this web of questions, on the suggestion of contemporary scholars, Vedic experts (*vedamurti*), and honourable sages:

    (1) I accepted *samadhi* as the sole path pointed out for making the unknown known.

    (2) I accepted Amarkantak as a suitable place for *sadhana*.

    (3) I began the practice of *sadhana* in 1950; maturity in *sadhana* came in the 1960s.

[p. 7]

    (4) In 1970, the state of *samadhi* was attained. In this state of *samadhi*, my expectations, thoughts, and desires remained silent. I realized that in this state, the occurrence of the unknown becoming known was zero. This state lasted twelve (12) to eighteen (18) hours daily.

    Following the sequence of *samadhi*, *dhyana*, and *dharana*, I accepted the self-motivated system of *samyama*. After two years of *samyama*, the validation of *samadhi* was established. The process of accomplishing *samyama from samadhi* also took 12 to 18 hours daily. As a result, the entire existence was experienced by me as coexistence, which was presented in the form of the treatises of Madhyasth Darshan (Co-existentialism).

12. **Coexistence:** The entire insentient and sentient nature is saturated and eternally present in the omnipresent space.

    **Within coexistence:**

    (1) In the developmental sequence of atoms, "hungry" (unsaturated) and "undigested" (unstable) atoms, and constitutionally complete, satisfied atoms that emerge through evolution, are understood to exist as *jeevan*.

    (2) A constitutionally complete atom is understood to exist as a sentient unit — *jeevan*.

    (3) From hungry and undigested atoms, molecules, and biological cells (*pranakośa*), all physical, chemical, and prana-state structures form, and from these atoms and molecules, the earth and other planets are clearly understood to have formed.

13. In existence, on this earth (which is a physical structure), through chemical processes and composition, biological cells formed and flourished as vegetation. Following this, biological cells formed animal bodies, and finally, the human body and tradition were completed and understood.

14. **Within coexistence:** The human tradition is understood as the combined form of body and *jeevan*.

[p. 8]

15. In, from, and for coexistence, coexistence was understood to be eternally effective; this itself was understood as the natural sequence (*niyatikram*).

[p. 9]

16. **Natural-law method — only by the natural method of coexistence:**

    (i) Four orders in existence: material order, prana order, animal order, knowledge order; and

    (ii) Four stages in existence: prana-stage, delusion-stage, divine-human stage, godly-human stage; and

    (iii) Development progression and development; awakening progression and awakening.

    Similarly, the awakening-based human tradition, replete with human-ness, orderliness, and participation in the overall orderliness, is understood as an eternal glory. I accepted this as the universal-auspicious formula and recognized the aspiration for universal welfare in all humans. As a result, I have presented before humankind the natural explanation of consciousness-development value education, constitution, conduct, and orderliness.

May the earth be heaven, may humans be divine,

May dharma succeed, may there be eternal auspiciousness.

— A. Nagraj

## Main points propounded in Madhyasth Darshan

Omnipotence is mediating and all-pervasive. Nature in Omnipotence is balanced–unbalanced and mediating activity, and is limited; therefore Omnipotence is state-complete.

Insentient–sentient nature in Omnipotence is state-dynamic; therefore nature is immersed in Omnipotence — nature is interwoven with Omnipotence. Because nature is saturated in Omnipotence, nature is fully energy-endowed; therefore nature is active. Hence nature is effort-, motion-, and result-bearing. As a result, nature itself is manifest in four orders. Therefore, in insentient–sentient nature saturated in Omnipotence, the sentient part affords appropriate opportunities for the capacities of experiencing in the knowledge order, and all four orders are bound to one another in the sense of completeness and wholeness.

Omnipotence is mediating; therefore nature saturated in mediating Omnipotence is controlled and protected. The nucleus (*madhyansh*) found in every atom is mediating activity; therefore balanced–unbalanced activities and relative forces are controlled and protected.

Infinite activity, or groups of activity, is nature — countable as insentient and sentient. Insentient nature, after completeness of development, attains the sentient stage by the natural-law method. The human being is the combined form of insentient and sentient, and is also a part of nature.

Constitutional completeness in the developmental sequence is, in the awakening sequence, activity completeness and conduct completeness in the deluded human. The awakened human, together with less-developed nature, nurtures right use and usefulness in occupation and behaviour. Behaviour with the human being is a duty to honour those more awakened, and to practise, study, and contemplate for greater awakening.

The deluded human alone is free while doing karma and dependent while experiencing result.

[p. 18]

On this earth the human being is in the awakening sequence; he has the opportunity, desire, and possibility of becoming awakened. The chief characteristic of the awakened human is to be helpful to the less developed.

From the material order the prana order develops; from the prana order the animal order develops; and from the animal order the animal-human of the deluded knowledge order develops. From the animal-human develops the deluded demonic human; from him the deluded–undeluded human; from him the undeluded divine human; and from the undeluded divine human, development and awakening are complete in the godly human.

The unit of the knowledge order is endowed with holistic-view capacity. The holistic view is in reference to insentient–sentient nature situated in the all-pervasive.

Realisation knowledge and the holistic view are complete only in the state free from delusion. Therefore —

Freedom from delusion itself is awakening; awakening itself is enlightenment; enlightenment itself is sovereignty; sovereignty itself is lordly existence; lordly existence itself is undivided society and universal orderliness.

"The human being alone is primarily helpful in the decline and development of the human being."

Chapter One

Karma

By physical, vocal, mental, and by the distinctions of done, caused, and intended, every human being performs karma in nine ways. Every karma has result.

In the human being, physical, vocal, and mental karma are well known across all three times. By the distinctions of done, caused, and intended, these are reckoned in fully nine kinds, which are evident. The human being's field of work is only the natural, the social, and the intellectual, whose desire (*abhīṣṭa*) is happiness. It is neither more nor less than this. Without this, the repose of effort does not manifest in the human being. Therefore, activity together with aspiration is karma itself; motion together with awareness is the complete activity.

The pressure that lies in accepting the unacceptable — that itself is pain (*vedanā*). It is the environment alone that is the pressure-giving fact, and relativity, which is evident. Relativity alone is the influential fact of decline and development.

In every karma, five limbs remain contained: doer, cause, objective, result, and effect. It is only for the fulfilment of necessity that there is contact and relationship. Necessity is itself desire, at whose root remain contained society, sociality, its observance, its upholding, conduct, following, and cultivation.

All the activities carried out, in accordance with the holistic view, for determining necessities and for the acceptance of following them, occur in the form of desire, which is the manifestation of the awareness found in conscious activity.

From the fulfilment of every desire and necessity, the human being aspires to become happy.

[p. 2]

In every karma, the hope of happiness that exists — this itself, by the realisation-based method, is to be known, in natural form, as happiness, peace, contentment, and joy. It is the balance of desire, karma, and result that is the accomplishment of the desire, whose possibility exists. For intellectual, collective (social), and natural ascent, there is rule and process in the form of a determinate direction — this itself is the human being's desire.

The whole of karma has proven, in the human being, to be both the nourisher and the exploiter of long-hoped-for happiness. This is to be known in three parts: righteous action, unrighteous action, and mixed action. By the distinction of the goal of union and awakening, righteous action; and, in the form of offence and retribution, unrighteous action; and, in the form of enjoyment and retaliation, mixed action, are to be seen. The whole of karma, without exception, is relative to its result.

The result of the whole of karmas is to be known in four forms: moksha, dharma, *kama* (desire), and wealth (*artha*). There is no karma without desire. In the human being, desires are to be known by the distinctions of intense, causal, and subtle. Intense desires descend in the form of activity. Causal desires are of little possibility in the form of activity. Subtle desires are of very little possibility in the form of activity.

Intense desires — that without which one cannot live.

Causal desires — whatever impulses arise by union, coincidence, or happenstance, all of these are causal desires.

Subtle desires — in the human being, truth is some thing, just as dharma, justice, are some thing, for the evidencing of which no clear thought remains.

The whole of means is to be seen by the distinction of internal and external. Internal means are found in the forms of hope, thought, desire, resoluteness (*ṛtambharā*), and the evidences of realisation; external means are found in the forms of body and wealth. This itself is the clear form of material and conscious activity. It is precisely the attaining of the oneness, balance, and harmony of the material and the conscious that is the accomplishment of the desire. Every human being is engaged in effort for the accomplishment of the desire, which is evident.

For the aim (the desire), the surrender of the seeker and the deployment of the means are necessary. The means is itself wealth, which is to be seen in the forms of body, mind, and money. This, while being constituted from work, is also the means for the constitution of karma.

[p. 3]

In every karma, the sequence of experiencing and inference is available. This sequence of karmas will keep going on until realisation. True-experiencing alone is completeness. Factual condition, the objective, and the condition are well known by natural realisation of truth, and therefore the complete meaning of the whole of meaning is realisation itself.

The whole of karma exists only for the acquisition of wealth, whose purpose is realisation. The acquisition, use, proper use, and distribution of the whole of wealth also occur only for the fulfilment of desire. At the root of the fulfilment of desire is realisation alone. This is evident only, with awakening, in the individual and in the whole of humankind. Whatever is in existence is the holistic view and knowledge of that very thing. The holistic view and knowledge are themselves realisation.

In the human being, happiness is the desired equilibrium (*abhīṣṭa*). It is precisely the deployment of internal and external means for realisation that is enlightenment; otherwise it is non-enlightenment. The possibility of these two conditions is itself the basis of diversity.

It is precisely in accordance with the capacity of internal means (hope, thought, desire, resoluteness, the evidences of realisation) that there is control of external means. The internal totality is itself the conscious. Material-conscious nature is contained within knowledge (Omnipotence), and therefore it is controlled within it; in such equilibrium-control, development and awakening exist all the way to realisation.

In a life full of knowledge, the rise of freedom-from-delusion is seen, and, in its opposite, delusion is to be seen. Knowledge is itself realisation, resolve, desire, thought, and hope, whose complete manifestation is found to be based on the conscious unit's essential nature toward awakening and capacity.

Freedom-from-delusion, being a complete capacity, is itself the accomplishment of the desire, because of being capable of doing proper and refined karma. The knowledge that comes to be manifest through the human being is to be known in three kinds: (1) physical, (2) intellectual, and (3) spiritual (coexistence). Their evident form is skill, proficiency, and erudition.

Principally, the practice of material science is from the whole of external means, and the practice of intellectual and spiritual science is from internal means. Material science and psychology are relative knowledge and study. The Spirit (Omnipotence) is absolute (determinate) knowledge. In relativity there is gain, expenditure, decline, development, and awakening. The proper use of the whole of word-powers is knowledge-producing, and wastage is ignorance-producing. Whoever wastes whatever, becomes deprived of it.

[p. 4]

It is precisely as the result of the whole of karma that there is wealth, calamity, good-desire, unrighteous-action, dharma, un-dharma, and the moksha-bondage pair. Wealth, good-desire, dharma, and moksha-bound awakening are happiness in the jeevan programme, and, opposite to this, calamity, unrighteous action, un-dharma, and bondage — this process, and the problem in jeevan, is itself sorrow and pain.

Dharma is all-round resolution. Wealth is body, mind, money. Ultimate wealth is realisation. In the awakened human being, what is meant by good-desire is that sensations remain controlled and evidenced through cognisance. Moksha is freedom from delusion.

"Wealth alone is happiness, righteous action alone is peace, dharma alone is contentment, and moksha alone is supreme joy."

The whole of desire has seven distinctions:

Wealth for the sake of moksha, moksha for the sake of wealth — these are, respectively, the best-of-the-best and the worst-of-the-worst. Wealth for the sake of dharma, dharma for the sake of wealth — these are, respectively, the middling-best and the worst. Wealth for the sake of *kama*, *kama* for the sake of wealth — these are, respectively, best, worst, and middling; and wealth for the sake of wealth, which is middling.

By the distinction of desire comes investigation; by the distinction of investigation comes the destination; by the distinction of the destination comes realisation; by the distinction of realisation comes value; by the distinction of value comes sociality and universal orderliness.

Three kinds of investigative tendency are found in the human being:

(1) truth-investigation, (2) desire-investigation, (3) object-investigation.

Object-investigative tendencies are to be seen in animals too. The remaining two are in the human being alone. For this reason, object-investigative tendencies are not free from offence.

Non-attachment from objects is itself dispassion, which is freedom from vitiations. Non-attachment from desires is itself further-detachment, which is the acceptance and observance of proper use.

[p. 5]

The object-investigative tendency is functional in self-interest; the desire-investigative in others'-interest; and the truth-investigative in ultimate-interest.

Behaviour full of self-interest is base and asocial; behaviour full of others'-interest is middling-best and social; behaviour full of ultimate-interest is best, social, and free. Behaviour full of ultimate-interest is itself the mentality of universal wellbeing.

Goal-based, value-based, and interest-based tendencies are found to exist.

In the human being, activities born of righteous action and unrighteous action, by intellect-based and interest-based unions, are well known.

*Kāsā*, *ākūti*, *medhā* — the intellect born of union; *mati*, *sumati*, *anumati* — the intellect born of righteous action; *amati*, *kumati*, *durmati* — these are the evident forms of the tendency born of unrighteous action.

The evident form of union-born *kāsā* is in the forms of the poet, the sage, the artist; *ākūti* is the divine worker (clearly bringing about comprehensive resolution); the deployment of divine purposefulness is in the form of the divine knower (undeluded); and *medhā*, together with complete knowledge, is the evident form of moksha (freedom from delusion). This itself is manifest and evident in the forms of compassion, grace, and mercy.

Righteous-action-born *mati* is in the forms of accepted (social) karma, conduct, and work-behaviour; *sumati* is righteous action (karma necessary for society), in the forms of conduct and work-behaviour; *anumati* is incomparable karma (worthy of being followed), in the forms of conduct and work-behaviour.

Righteous action is itself right intellect, right tendency, right understanding, right science, sociality, and true resolve.

The awakened human being places trust in the seeker, and the seeker remains disciplined together with the awakened human being.

It is in human jeevan alone that the particularities connected with the gross, the subtle, and the causal are to be seen. In these three conditions, it is happiness, peace, contentment, and joy alone that are expected.

The human being's gross life is in expectation of happiness and peace; the subtle life, in expectation of peace and contentment; and the causal life is in anticipation of joy and supreme joy — for which this awakening is the natural evidence. It is precisely the all-round programme in accordance with this that is awakening. This itself is also the physical, intellectual, and spiritual attainments. This has become successful only through a humane and supra-humane programme.

The deployment and disposal of the powers found in material-conscious nature lie only in origination, potency, and dissolution. In a perspective beyond this there is no disposal or deployment of property.

The beginning-to-end aim of the whole of disposal is itself the completeness of awakening.

It is precisely in the unit's natural completeness of awakening that the capacity for address has become manifest.

[p. 25]

Of origination, potency, and dissolution, potency alone is the universally-accepted event. It is only in the completeness of awakening that the establishment of potency-fullness is found. Before this, union, separation, and conjunction are found engaged in behaviour, karma, and pastime within relativity. All these conditions are the indicator of the sequence-succession natural to awakening.

Karma is result-bearing. The deluded human being is subject to it while undergoing the result. It is precisely by force of this universal rule that the human being is bound to become free from delusion with respect to karma and its result-bearing-ness.

It is to be seen that doubtfulness, apprehension, and ignorance occur with respect to karma performed by the human being in the deluded condition and its result — even though these are not desired in, from, or for the human being.

The property deployed in potency is itself mediating; that deployed in dissolution is *visham*; that deployed in origination is *sam*. This itself, in sequence, is ultimate-interest, self-interest, and others'-interest karma. In the form of behaviour, it is evident in the conditions of the undeluded, the deluded, and the deluded-undeluded.

The *sam*, *visham*, and mediating powers are, in sequence, *rajoguna*, *tamoguna*, and *sattvaguna*.

The conduct of a person endowed with *sattvaguna* is, together with behaviour, full of the capacity for wisdom and science, together with values such as compassion, respect, and love.

In a person endowed with *rajoguna*, the proper use of science occurs, together with values such as patience, courage, and good-naturedness.

In a person endowed with *tamoguna*, the wastage of the capacity for science occurs, together with undesirable vitiations such as envy, hatred, conceit, and egotism.

It is precisely conducting oneself in accordance with truth-connected method-orderliness, thought, conduct, behaviour, education, direction, karma, and system that is, in the human being, the observance of the dharma of the individual and the collective.

[p. 26]

It is precisely the human being's observance of his own dharma that is wholeness, universality, and boundlessness, which is, in itself, coexistence, sociality, prosperity, balance, control, restraint, fearlessness, non-disparity, simplicity, and generosity. It is in this that the bearing of social, stable values such as compassion, affection, generosity, pride, respect, tenderness, reverence, love, and gratitude occurs. This itself is also the human being's abiding wish. This itself is the beginning-to-end attainment of healthy sociality.

For the abundance of individuals full of righteous character, the awakened human being's cooperation and encouragement, their proper education and protection, and circumstances favourable to them, are themselves the evident form of world peace. Opposite to this is unrest, which is clear.

It is precisely the observance of the rule of the "triad of behaviour" that is righteous character. This itself is the evident dignity in the human being's five states. This itself is natural fidelity, and it is in this that science and wisdom have been fulfilled in their entirety. As a result, a healthy orderliness-method and education-system become effective.

What is meant by the "triad of behaviour" is physical, vocal, and mental behaviour carried out by the awakened human being.

The whole of result, refinement, and change is to be seen in five kinds:

(1) in existence that is coexistence, (2) developmental sequence, (3) development, (4) awakening sequence, (5) completeness of awakening.

The material state, with result, is purposeful in the form of complementing the actual condition. The prana state, with result, is purposeful in the forms of pulsation, and, with growth, compositional actual-condition and complement. The jeevan state, with change and result, together with the hope of living, is purposeful together with the complementing of the actual condition. The human being is reckoned in the knowledge-state. This is a tradition natural to awakening and the completeness of awakening, through refinement. This itself has been found to be evidence in the form of complementing the actual condition.

In a life of enjoyment and excessive-enjoyment-ism, the human being is not capable, and cannot become capable, of making himself healthy and of providing education for the conduct-based awakening of the coming generation. This burning fact is itself the principal cause of the need for a change and refinement of thought. Enjoyment is not the basis of culture.

[p. 27]

In the absence of culture, it is not possible for civilisation, method, and orderliness to be beyond doubt. Therefore this itself is evident in the forms of vexation, anguish, agitation, anger, indignation, conflict, rivalry, terror, fear, and distress. This is not what the human being desires. Therefore he is bound toward progress in the direction of the desired. This itself is the possibility, in the human being, of a change and refinement of thought. Along with this, there is opportunity for qualitative arising in *sanskars*.

The evident form of qualitative arising in *sanskars* is itself the observance of the "triad of rule." By this very method, the capacity to uphold social values, together with restrained tendency, becomes established as natural essential nature. What is meant by restrained tendency is the natural evidence of universal orderliness. It has been seen by living according to this method.

This itself is also universal aspiration.

Refined thought is itself wisdom-full science. This itself has been established in the forms of proficiency, skill, and erudition.

Every accomplishment has occurred through application, behaviour, and realisation, which is evident.

In the result of enjoyment, the animalistic human and the demonic human are to be seen.

In the result of thought, the awakened human and the divine human are evident.

In the divine human there is no possibility of any result, refinement, or change, because the divine human is situated at the destination. His continuity alone is what is to come.

It is science and wisdom-full capacity alone that yields the inference of: the form of substance; the property contained in form; the effect contained in property; the essential nature contained in effect; the capacity contained in essential nature; the motion contained in capacity; the method contained in motion; the orderliness contained in method; the sovereignty contained in orderliness; the pervasiveness contained in sovereignty; the potency contained in pervasiveness; the world contained in potency; and the world contained in Omnipotence. This itself is the attainment of refinability. Inference is to be known prior to evidence.

[p. 28]

Science has been applied both upward and downward, whereas wisdom is the establishment only of upward application. What is meant by science's upward application is this — it is clear that, as upward application, telecommunication becomes accessible; and it became clear that, from downward application, comes the martial system (the mechanical war-system). At the root of pollution on the earth are to be seen mineral coal, mineral oil, and radioactive substances in the form of fuel-residue. In the correct application of science's power in accordance with wisdom, no untoward event such as fear and terror occurs. In the condition of purpose natural to wisdom, no ill effect is established.

The arising or non-arising of wisdom-full capacity is what is found, not the wastage of wisdom-full capacity. Whereas the wastage and the proper use of science-full capacity are both evident. In this way it is established that the control of science is from wisdom alone, and not that the control of science is from science.

Progress toward development is itself upward-facing; compulsion toward decline is downward-facing — this is clear.

Every state of development is linked in a chain to further development beyond it. Therefore every state is bound for further development ahead.

In progress toward development there is the experience of enthusiasm, delight, and joy. Opposite to this, from motion toward decline, there is dejection, discouragement, helplessness, and distress.

Upward-facing science is engrossed in the work of potency through pure *rajoguna* and together with *sattvaguna*, which is the actual fulfilment of science. Along with this, opposite to it, in science, there is science engrossed in destruction and over-indulgence through tainted *rajoguna* and together with *tamoguna*, which is the indicator of the complete misuse of science.

It is the humane human being, and awakening-fullness beyond this, that is reckoned in upward-facing life, in which fundamental valuableness such as compassion, simplicity, sacrifice, austerity, benevolence, truth, non-violence, non-stealing, non-possession, and dignity is conducted. This itself is auspicious for self and other.

[p. 29]

For a life steeped in wicked character, being terror-stricken by fear is helplessness, which is the principal cause of pain to self and other. This itself is the fear of the inhumaneness contained within the human being. This itself is the root form of asociality and non-coexistence.

The capacity to hold affection for the human family is itself the continuity of trust and contentment. This itself is also the enthusiasm and impetus for further development.

A relationship devoid of trust is not successful. In a condition devoid of relationship there is no accomplishment of karma.

The observance of every social value has succeeded only through trust.

In just behaviour, resolution-full thought, and a life full of true realisation, there is an utter absence of afflictions — this itself is universal wellbeing.

It is only through the deployment of wisdom-following science that the life of every state of the human being is beautiful in every part. This itself is the karma-tendency and attainment that is beneficial to all humankind — this itself is universal wellbeing.

The karma-tradition endowed with knowledge-sanctioned wisdom and science is itself karma that benefits the world. This itself is the human being's abiding hope, aspiration, necessity, and opportunity.

"May there be universal wellbeing; may there be everlasting wellbeing."

[p. 30]

Chapter Two

Upasana (*upāsanā*) – Wisdom

Knowledge: — knowledge of the holistic view of existence, knowledge of jeevan, knowledge of humane conduct

Wisdom: — the immortality of jeevan, the perishability (*nashwaratva*) of the body, the rules of behaviour — together with knowledge — being evidenced in the human goal and the jeevan goal

Human goal: — resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, the natural evidencing of coexistence

Jeevan goal: — happiness, peace, contentment, joy

Upasana is an important part of human jeevan. Upasana itself is the process of refining and transforming the root tendencies. This very study is also the transformation of sanskar and essential nature.

Attaining togetherness by proper means is itself upasana's natural conception, for which there is diligence (refined effort) and practice. It is through practice and diligence alone that the veracity of the gross, the subtle, and the causal becomes clear through study. Through this, research concerning the relevant matter, the course of destiny, power, magnificence, and special powers, and the rules connected with them — conceived by way of *anugaman* (the path to realisation) — becomes well established. Research differs by the physical, the intellectual, and the spiritual. The process of research is directly manifest in the form of ratiocination, contemplation, resolve, and experiencing.

The meaning of the gross, the subtle, and the causal (the Seer) is: being endowed with the capacity to see, understand, apply, behave, and experience.

[p. 31]

Upasanas occur by the distinction of *kutastha* (abiding in the immutable), *rupastha* (abiding in form), and *atmastha* (abiding in the self). The process undertaken for complete realisation is *kutastha upasana*. The process undertaken for proximity to a form together with magnificence is *rupastha upasana*. The process of reflection itself is *atmastha upasana*, to which the human being is bound. Therefore, the refinement of tendencies is itself the programme of human jeevan. The expectation of completeness and of refinement-capacity is found in every state of the human being. Refinement-capacity itself is skill, proficiency, and scholarliness. Completeness itself is scholarliness. There is no knowledge and skill greater than scholarliness, and no behaviour and production greater than proficiency. Refinement-capacity is found in production and behaviour. Scholarliness itself is enlightenment; enlightenment itself is education and orderliness. Until one becomes fully complete with enlightenment, upasana is an extremely helpful aid.

Gods and goddesses (*devi-devata*) are to be reckoned of three kinds: earth-located, space-located, and divine-located. This is based on their range of movement. The conscious unit is the combined form of subtle and causal activity. This itself is the jeevan-cluster (*jeevan-punj*). This itself is gods and goddesses. The gross body is found controlled by the life-breath (*pran vayu*); subtle activity, by chitta; and causal activity, by atma. There is no separation of the subtle and the causal — this itself is the witness of immortality. The indication of the subtle and the causal becomes evident only in operating the gross body, and this is also indispensable. In the state without a gross body, subtle activities are accomplished only according to the indication of buddhi.

The three kinds of deity are: heaven-located (moving in unlimited space), space-located (moving in the space of a single cosmos), and earth-located (moving only within the environment of a single land). These, in order, are the divine soul, the deific soul, and the elemental soul.

Because of conduct-completeness and being endowed with complete awakening, [there is] the divine soul; because of being endowed with alertness accompanied by activity-completeness, [there is] the deific soul; and because of intense attachment to objects together with apprehension and fear, [there is] the elemental soul. It is the demonic human and the animalistic human who, upon a change of body, are reckoned as elemental souls; it is the humane human and the deific human who, upon a change of body, are reckoned as deific souls; and it is the divine human who, after relinquishing the body, is reckoned in the category of the divine soul.

Elemental souls are in a state of absolute-oneness (*tadatmya*) with adhibhautik elements, deific souls with adhidaivik facts, and divine souls with the Spirit (*adhyatma*). Therefore, three categories are found to be established.

[p. 32]

In the adhibhautik domain, production-predominant behaviour [prevails]; in the adhidaivik domain, behaviour-predominant production; in a life filled with the Spirit, realisation-based thought, behaviour, and production occur in direct sequence. Therefore, behaviour and enterprise are found to be restrained and controlled in a life filled with the Spirit; orderly and disciplined in an adhidaivik life; and undisciplined and disorderly in a physical life. Therefore, it is only in a life filled with the Spirit that the integrality of the human being's four dimensions is found.

For the sake of the "instincts-quad" (*vishaya-chatushtaya*), [absolute-oneness is found] in adhibhautik elements; for the sake of the "triad of desires" (*eshana-traya*), in adhidaivik (social and behavioural) values; and the divine human is found in a state of absolute-oneness with the Spirit — for which, respectively, attachment, upasana, and dedication are employed.

The whole of insentient-sentient nature is saturated in Paramatma (Omnipotence); therefore these [three kinds of soul] are bound to awakening, culminating in realisation, in "this" [existence].

The deluded human is afflicted, excited, or bound by relativity — this is directly evident. This relativity remains observable only until the unit becomes free from delusion, because the free-from-delusion state is itself its supreme excellence.

Control itself is rule, inspiration, coordination, relativity, effort, motion, result, activity, conduct, union, separation, combination, status, state, and control — all of which is observable.

Upasana of the divine souls is itself learning (*vidya*); upasana of the deific souls is itself sub-learning (*upavidya*); and upasana of the elemental souls is itself base learning (*kshudra vidya*). It is well known that upasana of divine souls is undertaken, with awakening, for freedom-from-delusion together with intellectual resolution and material prosperity; upasana of deific souls is for intellectual resolution and material prosperity; and upasana of elemental souls is only for materiality.

All jeevans are endowed with their own respective essential nature. This itself is their standing. Beyond this, no further manifestation of essential nature is possible from them.

[p. 33]

Materiality alone has not proven sufficient for the human being, because the four dimensions found in the human being are not satiated by material substances alone. Therefore, until the human being becomes fully complete with the integrality of production, behaviour, thought, and experiencing, he remains bound to experimentation, endeavour, and practice.

In the upasana of divine souls, wisdom-filled science, austerity (firm dedication), determination, decision, and the capacity for resolve become available — this itself is the direct result of their upasana. From the upasana of deific souls, half-wisdom, science, patience, courage, affection, trust, renunciation, and control and restraint in the activities of jeevan are found — these are the direct attainments of deific upasana. From the upasana of elemental souls, unwise science, impatience, recklessness, attachment and aversion, apprehension, and uncontrolled activities of jeevan are found. It is as the result of these three upasanas, respectively, that one becomes observable in states of alertness-filled watchfulness, awakening-filled watchfulness, and apprehension-filled non-awakening. These are, respectively, the delusion-free, the semi-deluded, and the deluded states.

Rasayanik (chemical) substances' constituted body formation, in which medhas is contained, is itself the meat substance, and among plants, the vegetable substance is available. In both of these, chemical substance is found to be predominantly similar. Even while such similarity of chemical substance remains, there is a fundamental difference between meat-body-formation-mechanisation and its use, and the formation-process and use of the plant-image. In meat-eating decorum, forceful slaughter and exploitation are inherently embedded events.

Within the domain of inhumaneness, even while a state of diversity persists in decorum, conduct, behaviour, and tendency between the vegetarian and the non-vegetarian human being, predominantly a similarity is found in the state of purpose — this is also evident among animals. Therefore this fact is clear:

[p. 35]

when humaneness becomes studiable and comes to be understood, both the non-vegetarian and the vegetarian come to agree in favour of humaneness. When they become established-in-dedication, even the non-vegetarian becomes vegetarian. In awakened human decorum, trust and expectancy are also alike. This itself is the one point of truth where every kind of decorum stands in abiding wait for the maintenance of human decorum. This itself is a site of possibility, which is the interval for the continuous excellence of awareness.

In humaneness and higher-humaneness, the originality of vegetarianism becomes clear. The oneness (freedom from diversity) of decorum in the human being is found to be established only through humaneness. Therefore, the chief utility in upasana lies in the immersion in universal values.

Those upasana methods through which resulting decorums establish class-sentiment in the human being are not fully helpful for comprehensive resolution, because class-sentiment, in the final analysis, is oriented only toward conflict. For the state of being free from conflict, universality is an indispensable fact.

At the root of all upasanas there is a similarity of aim: it is undivided society, universal orderliness. It is only universal welfare. Because, without the aspiration for universal welfare, one's own welfare cannot be accomplished.

Other desirable desires only give the appearance of a state of welfare, not realisation. Therefore, the human being is bound to make effort toward a programme in conformity with the aspiration for universal welfare, and up to the capacity worthy of following it.

It is only through the universal, humane method of conducting the "triad of rule" — intellectual, social, and natural — that there is the possibility of, and actual freedom from, economic and communal class-sentiments. In this alone does all class-sentiment dissolve. Therefore, upasana becomes meaningful only through the desired understanding, together with awakening — which is itself awakening.

In the human being, the powers are activity-power, desire-power, and knowledge-power alone — these are its qualifications. Qualifications are found to be based on each unit's capacity-for-awakening and on [its] awakening.

[p. 36]

Upasanas exist only for capacity-for-awakening, not for decline.

There is no pain in the progress of the process of awakening-progression. This itself is the music (integrality) of jeevan. In contrast, it is only in [wrong] activity-process and decline that every kind of pain exists — these are sorrow.

There are intellectual, behavioural, and physical pains. These pains are observable, respectively, in the form of problem, offence, incapacity, and wastage. This itself is the direct form of non-awakening.

It is only deficiency in watchfulness and alertness that is the cause of all affliction. Watchfulness and alertness are seen to manifest only on the basis of the capacity, competence, and worthiness found in the human unit. Therefore —

For powers not to be wasted through the sense-organs of sound, touch, form, taste, and smell, but rather to be well spent — this itself is the awakening of activity-power. The determination of good-use and wastage lies, within the domain of humaneness, in the form of the "triad of rule".

The non-wasting of the *antahkarana*'s (the inner instrument's) root tendencies — namely hope, thought, desire, and resoluteness (*ṛtambharā*) — occurs only by the method of human consciousness and higher-human consciousness, and this itself is good-use. This itself is the awakening of desire-power.

Right-enlightenment together with the completeness of experiencing is itself the capacity for the manifestation of knowledge. This itself is the awakening — or the complete awakening — of knowledge-power. This "triad of awakening" is directly evident in humaneness and higher-humaneness. This itself is the supreme-culmination attainment of human jeevan. This itself is the utility of empowered upasana; this itself is the aspiration of the whole of humankind. This itself is universal welfare.

The direct form of jeevan-awakening is itself the application of wisdom-filled science. This itself is watchfulness, undivided sociality, enlightenment, freedom-from-disparity, coexistence, education, method, orderliness, civilisation, culture, intellectual resolution, material prosperity, and the continuity of jeevan-awakening.

[p. 37]

Every human unit is bound to effort and upasana up to complete awakening. As a result of this, the refinement of root tendencies occurs, on account of which distinctive and cultured mentality, and the capacity for thought-contemplation-enlightenment, and the completeness of realisation, become manifest. These are the attainments of the best upasana. This itself is study-completeness.

Whoever is a complete knower of a discipline, and is practised in its work, behaviour, and realisation, is found to possess continuous activeness in it. Hence, the completeness of an activity is the continuity of that activity. Therefore, it does not prove to be a cause for indifference, or for making another indifferent — because activeness exists for the sake of completeness. Completeness is the continuity of activity.

Whatever is active for the sake of completeness has continuity after becoming complete, as with activity-completeness and conduct-completeness. Therefore, the measurement of completeness by the yardstick of incompleteness, or the measurement of the awakened by the unawakened, is not possible, because the lesser value is contained within the greater value, not the greater value within the lesser. This is directly evident in the human being. For example, it is directly evident that every human being has succeeded in measuring what is less developed than himself. Concomitant with this, it is evident that the human being produces along with less-developed nature, and has also gained knowledge of what is more [developed]. Therefore he is bound to behave with his equal. This itself is the imperativeness of sociality. This itself is the natural glory and dignity of human jeevan. This dignity itself is the impetus for behaving with one's equal, and for practising toward greater awakening. This itself is real upasana.

Wisdom — that is, the human goal — and jeevan-value — that is, resolution [and] prosperity — is itself the direct result of upasana, in which sociality remains naturally contained.

The projection of realisation-enlightenment is itself non-accumulation (prosperity), generosity, and compassion. It is the enrichment of the natural foundational values of generosity and compassion that is the life-essence of sociality. This itself is social music. It is for this alone that the human being thirsts. Wisdom alone manifests intellectual resolution and social values through their maintenance.

Wisdom and science are themselves the chief characteristic of indirect knowledge (good-behavioural knowledge). Realisation alone is the final state of indirect knowledge. Prior to this, only the authority of inference is established. Realisation exists only in the truth of the factual condition, the objective, and the state.

[p. 38]

Without indirect knowledge, [the discernment of] the eternal and the non-eternal, the proper and improper, the just and unjust, the righteous and unrighteous, the true and false, the desirable and undesirable, the seen and unseen, and the authority of indirect knowledge itself, is not established.

Without the authority-of-knowledge of the eternal-and-non-eternal, dedication toward one's own-dharma is not found in the human being. Human dharma is itself happiness; happiness is itself just conduct; just conduct is itself the observance of humane [conduct] and of the "triad of rule". This itself is the human being's own-dharma. The human being is happiness-natured.

There is no unit without propriety. Just as, in animals, essential nature-propriety; in plants, property-propriety; and in substances, form-propriety, is not violated. This itself is their dignity. In the same way, in the human being, resolution-happiness is itself dharma; dharma is itself propriety. This itself is their dignity and trust. The direct form of propriety is itself trust.

"There is no happiness in relationship and connection devoid of trust." There is no human being devoid of relationship and connection. This itself is the imperativeness for one's own-dharma. Whatever incapacity, incompetence, and unworthiness there is in its observance — that itself is sorrow, affliction, problem, and non-awakening.

Until one becomes fully complete with fullness in one's own-dharma and with the capacity, competence, and worthiness fit to observe it, there is no absence of upasana in the form of study, for the purpose of acquiring knowledge.

It is in the human being's own-dharma alone that creed, sect, and class disappear. This itself is the direct dignity of capable upasana. "This itself is auspicious." It is indispensable for all three of these — the aim, the seeker, and the means — to remain contained within upasana. The integrality of these alone is the success of upasana; otherwise, it is failure. The proper use of whatever power and means are obtained in every state — that itself is its advance awakening. This itself is upasana.

There is no such power or means which has no deployment or use, because the accumulation of power or means is not practical.

[p. 39]

In self-delight: wisdom; in wisdom-filled buddhi: rooted science; in science-filled art, composition, discernment, and thought; in discernment-based tasting and welcoming; in emotion-filled mind — [all of these] are continuously balanced and resolved. As a result of this, it becomes well established that there is an inundation of supreme-bliss in atma, an inundation of joy in buddhi, an inundation of contentment in chitta, an inundation of peace in vritti, and an inundation of happiness in mun.

The activities of the sense-organs, and the field of operation of the sense-organs — this itself is the boundary of direct knowledge. In this transaction, the four-object domain's attainments are its outer limits. Beyond this, there are no further attainments in it.

The human being is not confined within the boundary of objects, because the four dimensions are well established in the human being.

The human being alone is bound to become endowed with production, behaviour, thought, and experiencing. This itself is necessity, occasion, possibility, and orderliness.

It is only with restraint that dignity-filled grandeur manifests from the activities carried out by the human being in every context. As follows —

From practising the speaking of truth together with truth-enlightenment, there arises the cessation of fear and distrust, and the arising of delight and enthusiasm.

From gaining mastery over non-violence, there arises freedom from the fear of opposition, and the arising of affection and generosity. Not participating in violence by the distinctions of done, caused, and intended, [and by] the physical, vocal, and mental — [this equals] non-violence.

From dedication to non-possession, there is the destruction of pitiableness and baseness, and the arising of patience, fortitude, and generosity.

From the establishment of non-stealing, there is the destruction of cunning and distress, and the arising of satisfaction and contentment. The meaning of non-stealing is not committing theft.

[p. 40]

From the establishment of value (sentiment) toward the world, there is the absence of disparity between the desired [deity] and the seeker. Along with this, the essential nature of that deity enters [the seeker].

From the practice of remembering, in identical form, the meaning of a word — that is, the meaning of a mantra — its meaning and essential nature become attainable. Every meaningful word is a mantra.

From devoting oneself more in awakening, there is the eradication of pride and ego, and the arising of learning and simplicity.

Restraining physical sensitivity leads to purity of mind; from purity of mind, the gain of mental strength.

From the destruction of attachment to one's own body, attachment toward the world is removed. It is only through an intellect filled with the trustworthy aspiration in the form of universal-welfare that there occurs the outpouring of generosity, compassion, grace, and mercy toward the world, and knowledge and realisation of coexistence in the world's foundational Omnipotence.

Without restraint, the refinement of intellectual root-tendencies, intellect-strength, the realisation of social values, the vision of conscious activity, resolution, and restraint [itself] are not accomplished.

## 3.1 Atomic Structure and Molecular Composition (परमाणु संरचना एवं अणु रचना)

[p. 53]

The desire to recognise atomic structure has been found in the human being from the distant past.

By the method of decomposition, the human being applied thought, reflection, and experiment in order to reach the fundamental substance. Even at the end of decomposition, the expectation of some definite fundamental substance remained.

Experimenting with the above mindset, the human being arrived at this conclusion: that if we keep on decomposing any substance whatsoever, we will reach a state in which the decomposed units function in a self-motivated (*svayamsphurt*) manner — this is understood. Such a functioning unit we call the atom. The desire to understand how all this functions has also remained in the human being.

The atom has, at its middle, one or more particles (*ansh*), and, revolving on all sides in its orbits, one or more particles. Below this, the matter of an atom does not come to be. That the number of particles in the middle or in the orbit might increase — this arises in human imagination alone.

In this connection, two main questions came to attention — 1. How could recognition come to be established in the mutuality of the atomic particles? 2. After recognition, why did they enter into orderliness? On carrying this line of thought further, it was understood that coexistence itself is its principal cause, because the omnipresent substance (Omnipresence) is fully immersed, soaked, and enveloping within every single object, in energy. The omnipresent substance is all-permeating within all of these. In this way it became clear that, from the subtlest to the largest unit, being saturated within the omnipresent is the basis of remaining energy-endowed. Being energy-endowed itself is understood to be being force-endowed and being endowed with magnetic force. It is by virtue of this force-endowment that in the subtle atomic particle there is the propensity to recognise mutuality and to participate in orderliness. For the human being it is necessary to accept the recognition of one by another as evidence of this force. The human being alone is endowed with the innate right to recognise propensity, action, and fruition. In this very progression, when human beings recognise one another, there they present themselves in the form of orderliness itself. Wherever they have committed error in recognising this meaning, functioning and behaving in the form of disorderliness, the cause of the problem

[p. 55]

comes to be. In this way it is understood that every atomic particle (on the basis of being saturated within the omnipresent) is functioning in orderliness precisely as a result of recognising one another. The functional form of orderliness is definite conduct itself.

In the above manner, as a result of every atomic particle too being energy-endowed, recognising one another is natural for it; the purpose of this recognising is to conduct itself in a combined form — this was understood as an eternal law. From this a further formula also emerges: that on the basis of such recognising alone does constitution-organisation come to be, and constitution-organisation comes to be evidenced in the sense of orderliness, being the ever-present. In this way, that within the omnipresent substance every single entire object is indivisible, and that this is understood in the form of coexistence, has become easy to comprehend. In this progression, that coexistence is eternally influenced — this is acceptable to us humans.

Coexistence itself became the fundamental formula and the basis of orderliness. At the root of every state of motion, coexistence is well established. In this way it became clear that coexistence itself is the basis of the diverse action (activity) and of the conduct of each one of them.

It was found that difference of conduct comes to be only from difference in the number of functioning particles in atoms. On the basis of such difference of conduct alone, the human being made the attempt to determine the species of atoms and the number of species. Human beings have so far laid claim to there being 108 species of atoms on this earth, and hold the hope of discovering the atom of a further species.

On understanding the atom in this way, we humans become clear in an orderliness-centred mindset. As a result, it became natural for the human being to be ready to recognise the innate orderliness within himself. In this progression it became natural for the human being to understand humane conduct. From this it is found that value, character, and morality — their combined expression, communication, and manifestation — have, within the human tradition, the basis of orderliness, from it, and for it.

By virtue of this very fact, in the animal-order, the plant-order, and the mineral-order — in as many species as are reckoned in solid, liquid, and rarefied forms — at the root of all of them is the atom itself, and jeevan too is the atom. The atom of every species performs definite conduct. Therefore the human being too (who is the combined form of jeevan and body) wishes to live in orderliness with definite conduct. It became natural that the glory of atomic structure comes to be clear only in conduct, and that conduct comes to be in the form of its own activity.

[p. 56]

In the context of atomic composition, this too becomes clear: that atomic particles come to be evidenced only in the coexistence of one another. An atomic particle that remains separate in the limitless void (the omnipresent) does not remain evidencing the intent that is in the form of orderliness; therefore, functioning in a whirling form in the limitless void, it remains in search of another particle. Ultimately, it becomes absorbed into some other atom, or forms a definite atom together with another atomic particle. From this it is learnt that only after the constitution of the atom does the manifestation of orderliness come to be; in the course of orderliness being manifested, the manifestation of conduct comes to be. This itself is clear as the physical, chemical, and jeevan activity-processes.

That atomic structures are based upon one or more particles has already become clear; this too has become clear: that the atom of every species is self-actuated. Their conduct is definite.

The composition of every atom comes to be understood in the form of the nucleus (*madhyansh*) and the particles functioning in the orbits. However many particles are functioning in the orbits, that many particles are also present in the middle; more than that is possible. "Orbits" means the particles circling on all sides of the middle. Such orbits are one or more than one. When these orbits become more than four, the possibility of decreasing, and when fewer than four, of increasing, always-always remains. Such atoms remain present in the forms of their respective species, in various numerical states. Weight remains dependent on the number of nuclei inherent in these atoms. In this very form, atoms endowed with the inherent-weight recognise one another and gather together — this we call the molecule. Such molecules are found in solid, liquid, and rarefied forms. Solid compositions have already become clear as the forms of large-large compositions like the earth. Liquid substance is considered the initial substance of the chemical world. All these come to be composed by the compound method. In this the human being has no contribution; the human being only studies it. Study is necessary precisely because through it, recognising the innate functional orderliness with these becomes possible. It is only on the basis of chemical substances that the activity-process of the prana cells, in the fruition of the composition-of-nourishment-element and composition-element, comes to be clear.

At the root of the physical-chemical activity-process, it is the atom and the molecule alone that come to be recognised as performing the activity. In this way the molecule, in its own form, works continuously precisely in the form of orderliness. The evidence of working in the form of orderliness is that the onward state of motion in the sequence of orderliness comes to be clear (from its being the present).

[p. 57]

Every atom and molecule is found to be a rung of its own species. What is meant by being a rung is its existent-state, its continuity. Just as two particles are in one existent-state in the form of an atom. This itself is one step. Their having definite conduct is itself their grandeur. This very having of definite conduct becomes the basis of the further step, and remains so. Atoms and molecules with various numerical particles remain with their definite conduct established. This itself is a wondrous self-motivated form of orderliness, clear before the human being.

Such physical molecule-atoms, participating in the chemical activity-process, are amenable to study, each in the form of a state of motion. The well-established combining of all these molecules-atoms comes to be clear in the form of large-large compositions like the earth. This is indicative of the fact that however many species' atoms and molecules are necessary before chemical action begins on the earth, that has come to be accomplished by the destiny-borne innate method itself. What is meant by the destiny-borne innate method is the coexistence method itself. Only after there comes to be endowment with the atoms-molecules of all species do the compound propensity and processes come to be accomplished. On this earth the testimony to this is well established.

That all the above manifestations, in the material state, are based upon molecule-atom species, and that the species of molecule-atoms are dependent on the number of atomic particles — this is well established. The changing of the number of particles, and, as a result of the change, the result — such results remain continuously coming to be. The name of this activity-process is absorption-displacement (*prasthapan-visthapan*). In atoms, absorption-displacement remains occurring in order to fulfil the result-oriented intent. In this the intent of the process of being complementary to one another also comes to be accomplished. Before some particle is displaced from any atom, that atom is found to remain excited; it is only as a result of being excited that being displaced from some atom becomes possible. That particle becomes absorbed into some atom. The name of this action is absorption. In this way, after being displaced, the displaced atom comes to be in natural-state motion; the emplacing atom, after absorbing the absorbed particle, having welled up, comes to be in the establishment of natural-state motion. Only in the establishment of natural-state motion does every atom, molecule, and molecular composition come to be clear as accomplishing definite conduct. Definite conduct itself is the evidence of orderliness. In this progression the intent of development remains contained.

In this very progression electricity too remains present. It is as a result of these two processes that radiativeness remains manifest.

In hungry atoms, the possibility of absorbing some more particles into themselves remains present. This is precisely why they have the name "hungry" atoms. Being undigested and hungry in atoms is understood precisely in the sense of the satisfied (*tript*) atoms (the human being is the one who studies it). ## 3.10 Property, Essential nature, Dharma (गुण, स्वभाव, धर्म)

Property, in its own nature, is generative, degenerative, and mediating activity and tendencies. It is assessed on the basis of the result of these. Creative, combinative activity is indicated by the name generative tendency. Decomposition contrary to the work of manifestation, and destructive activity and tendency, is called degenerative. ## 3.11 Force–Power (State–Motion) (बल-शक्ति (स्थिति-गति))

[p. 102]

After the arrival of the age of modern science, experiments were made to draw further conclusions about power. That no complete conclusion has yet emerged from the discipline of science, and that nothing has emerged for humanness, is a point worth considering. In this context, through the discipline of science there has been an attempt to recognize pressure as force, and to recognize motion, wave, and flow as power. This means that in the course of evidencing effect upon another, the introduction and recognition of power in the form of mechanical process occurs. It comes to be recognized on the basis of there being states. Many methods for testing this fact or principle are at hand (*samicheen*) before the human. 'At hand' means being nearby, being available. [p. 103]

This very thing — the situation of the experience of this kind of weight occurring in every human — is clear. Every human, on the basis of being in himself one who gives concrete form to ideas (*manakar*), and on the basis of being imaginative and free in action, thought about how weight might be measured or weighed, and found the answer to it. The method of weighing was settled. The method of measuring was settled. In this way weight is found to be animated by coexistence. Every unit, endowed with force in state, evidences and displays power in motion.

In this sequence it comes into understanding that every object maintains its own orderliness within a definite good distance. This is the controlling activity that takes place in the atom. By these very activities, control over mutual distance remains. When atoms are in the form of molecules, it is on the basis of the nuclei that mutual attraction-force is exercised, on the basis of endowment with magnetic force. Because of this very weight, the propensity remains for one molecule to join with another molecule while remaining within a definite good distance. From such propensities, atoms, molecules, and large material objects formed of molecules — bodies (*pind*) — stand presented before the human, such as this earth. In relativity there is apprehension of small and big. Completeness slips out of sight. Only in completeness can each one be respected, be evaluated. When the human evidences each one in coexistence together with completeness, in such a situation it is found natural for the human to give attention toward his own completeness. ## 3.12 Projection–Reflection (परावर्तन-प्रत्यावर्तन)

Regarding what used to be thought of in the forms of aim, means, and seeker: from the coexistence-ist viewpoint, the method of recognising the seeker is found to be very easy, and bound up with purpose. From the coexistence-ist viewpoint, the seeker comes to be recognised in the form of the human being. As the aim, jeevan-awakening becomes accessible. It is awakening alone that comes to be recognised in the forms of the human being's innateness, freedom, and right. Such innateness, freedom, and right, in the form of understanding, and such understanding, in the form of knowledge, science, and wisdom — again, innateness, freedom, and right are found to be made glorious. This itself is the evidence of awakening. In this way, how meaningful the aim is — that is, how meaningful awakening as the aim is — every individual can consider. As the means, for research and investigation: imaginativeness, freedom-of-action, evidence in the form of tradition — this itself is the means. When human tradition, as the bearer and carrier of evidence and authenticity — that is, evidence-endowed tradition — becomes able to evidence itself, it is in that very understanding that the evidence of universal orderliness, of undivided society, remains formed. Participating in being evidenced within universal orderliness is itself what keeps evidence universally accessible. It is precisely as a constituent part of universal orderliness that humane education-and-culture is found to become glorious. In human tradition, the glory of universality is found to become meaningful only together with knowledge, education-and-culture. This itself is the meaning of the tradition of evidence. Even so, the general

[p. 149]

survey reveals it to be so — universal, that is, acceptance in all humankind. It was found to be extremely easy for every man and woman to take to heart, to test within oneself, and to determine the meaningfulness or meaninglessness of, the whole row of evidence formed by the coexistence-ist method across all the disciplines of practice, study, work, and behavioural orderliness.

The human being's aim is awakening. Its innateness, freedom, and right take the form of knowledge, science, and wisdom, whose evidence is the form of undivided society, universal orderliness through work-behaviour, which is linked to one another as a chain. In this, no link can be separated. The aim, as it stands in the form of tradition — human-aspiration, jeevan-aspiration — cannot possibly be evidenced without coexistence-ist knowledge, wisdom, and science. It can be evidenced only by the coexistence-ist method. Coexistence is no mystery or struggle. In natural form, it is a thing intelligible to, and illumined from, all humankind. Because every human being does indeed want to live and obtain happiness only in mutuality with someone. By this method the glory of coexistence becomes intelligible.

In the sequence of living together, it is the human being who first appears before one. Such a human being remains accepted only in some relationship or other. First the human being, in the form of the mother, and immediately after, in the form of the father; after this, brother-sister, friend, teacher (*guru*), preceptor (*acharya*), elders, and their likenesses, and in the neighbourhood too, similar relationships remain present in the tradition. In the process of living, nothing other than relationship comes to be clear. It is only on the basis of relationships that mutuality, and recognition and carrying-out in the sense of purposes, are found to occur. By this method, carrying-out without recognising, and recognising without carrying-out, are simply not possible. In this it becomes known that purpose is our desired attainment. Forming relationships and carrying them out for the sake of purposes is an inevitable condition; this itself we have given the names responsibility and duty. Together with relationships, responsibility and duty being self-motivated, of their own accord, is natural. What duty means is: making sure what to do, how to do it — this is duty. What is to be obtained, how it is to be obtained, ascertaining this and being propensive toward it, is called responsibility. In every relationship, being sure of the fact of what is to be obtained, and ascertaining how, what, is to be done to obtain it — these are links joined to one another. By the self-motivated method, this comes to be carried out in the greatest number of relationships. Of awakening

[p. 150]

alone does such fulfilment become clear. In the deluded condition, the purpose and goal of relationships do not become clear. In deluded tradition, relationships have to be recognised most predominantly on the basis of sensitive tendencies, on the basis of the gathering of convenience, and on the basis of conflict. Human tradition, having endured these very viewpoints from the distant past, has come out desolate in respect of mental well-being.

Whereas, by the coexistence-ist method, the possibility of mental well-being is found to be fitting and accessible for all humankind.

In this same continuity, we are able to recognise all relationships in the sense of meaningfulness, in the sense of purposes, and in the sense of purpose filled with cognisance. Its clear form, in the form of universal orderliness and undivided society; its clear form, human-aspiration and jeevan-aspiration — in the sense of evidences, together with acceptance and together with evaluation, transforming work-behaviour and orderliness and conduct — is itself found to be the fruition of coexistence-ist knowledge, science, and wisdom.

Human-aspiration — the natural evidence of resolution, prosperity, fearlessness, coexistence.

Jeevan-aspiration — is reckoned in the forms of happiness, peace, contentment, joy.

## 3.18 Seer, Doer, Enjoyer (दृष्टा, कर्त्ता, भोक्ता)

In awakened human tradition, fundamental right is evidenced in the forms of seer, doer, and enjoyer. In most countries, spiritualists have made mentality ready to accept God, the Supreme Soul, Brahma, goddesses, and gods. For this, scriptures too were prepared, and, at the root of all religions, the good sayings written in the scriptures, in the form of divine speech, God's speech, or voice-from-the-sky, came to be accepted. This itself came to be regarded as scripture. On the basis of such a belief, the treatise, and, on the basis of the treatise, the method of preaching became prevalent. Such scriptures came to be prepared at a time when the human beings of one country were not capable of forming relations with all countries. When human beings became so capable, one community became ready to study the religious texts of another. In this it became known that the things which we accept according to our own religious text, another religious text does not accept. Then where is this divine speech? In this way religious differences of opinion only became more entangled. It is naturally accepted by all that there should be a religious text; along with this, the self-motivated conception also arises in mentality that there should be no dispute in religion; and if a dispute does occur,

[p. 151]

then what indeed became of religion! Human beings who think with such logical intellect do indeed exist upon this earth too. Despite this, what an undisputed religious text ought to be — this has not become available to us by the idealist method. For materialists, there is no need at all for a religious text. In this way we ordinary human beings kept on being tormented — both by internal contradiction, and, in the conflicts of the outer world, by treachery-rebellion, by exploitation, and by the fear of war, we kept on being afflicted. At the root of all these, we have kept believing only in God's grace, the gods' grace; whenever, at any moment, we obtained relief, obtained happiness, on any issue, we took it to be the grace of God, of a god, or of a guru. In this way we ordinary human beings kept enduring joy and sorrow. Trust in oneself was found neither with those called ordinary, nor with those called special. Whereas the matter of obtaining relief on the basis of trust has kept coming to be recognised in every human being. It is still the same even now. It is in whatever mutualities we are able to trust that we find relief — whether this be a moment, a day, or a year. Taking this itself to be God, a god, or a guru's grace, we keep engaging, for ever and ever, in the work of deceiving ourselves. Whereas it is by force of his own imaginativeness and freedom-of-action alone that the human being has succeeded upon this earth in giving concrete form to his own ideas. If this is not the produce of the human being himself, then whose produce is it? Whether we understand this or not, it is a necessary matter to be thought about. Along with this, it is precisely evidencing mental well-being that is resolution within human tradition.

The matter to which attention needs to be paid here is just this: whatever is reflected before us — is there, at its root, any doer-status or not, is there one who does or not? If, at its root, there is no doer, then the ascription of the status of universal-doer became wrong. If there is no doer, then what would the enjoyer-status be like? If this is not so, then what became of the seer-status? All these questions have kept arising one after another. The answer to these questions is not to be found by the idealist method.

When, by the coexistence-ist method, thinking and reflection began, and decisions began to be recognised, then it became known that whatever is functioning, in coexistence, in the form of the four states, all of it is indeed itself in the self-motivated doer-status. For this reason, activity, in itself, is clearly functioning in the sense of the developmental sequence in the forms of effort, motion, and result. In this same sequence, awakening was recognised in the forms of the immortality of result, the repose of effort, and the destination of motion. It has become possible to recognise the immortal status of result in the sense of development, the repose of effort, and the destination of motion, as awakening and awakening-completeness. In this way the human being himself becomes the knower of the awakening-status,

[p. 152]

the herald (*udghata*), or the unit worthy of evidencing — this becomes intelligible. From all the above study it became clear that, within coexistence — that is, within the pervasive substance — all single substances remain conjoined, and, by force of being endowed with energy and endowed with force, become functional by the self-motivated method; it was found that there is no need at all for anyone to make this happen. It was seen that all compositions, from the very smallest to the very largest, remain functional in themselves. It became intelligible that such functionality itself, in the forms of effort, motion, and result, is precisely what came to be known, in the developmental sequence, as the immortality of result from the developed status. The testimony of this is precisely the constitutionally complete atom-status, which is itself the conscious status.

In the developmental sequence, the doer-status was clear. In the conscious status, in the sequence of awakening, in the combined form of jeevan and body, the hope, thought, and desire to live, in the animal order and in deluded human tradition, became clear. In making clear the hope to live, wanting and not-wanting came to be evidenced. Along with this, in the form of wanting — that is, together with the wanting to live — the wanting to be happy came to be manifested from the human being. For this, giving concrete form to ideas became possible. From this it becomes entirely clear that the mentality of wanting to be happy manifested itself in the human being in the form of his own desire. On the basis of this, the doer-status having already existed, the expectation of the enjoyer-status arose, the expectation of enjoying happiness arose. For this, the rise of the seer-status became inevitable. Such an establishment of the seer-status is itself the evidence of understanding.

Understanding itself was found to become the seer-status, established as the knower together with the knowledge-element. By this method it is the human being alone who evidences the doer, enjoyer, and seer statuses. The human being is found to remain made glorious only in the combined form of jeevan and body. The glory of the conscious unit is precisely the attainment of the seer- and knower-status. As a result, in the sense of evidencing, evidencing himself within human tradition, the human being is found to be made glorious in the doer- and enjoyer-status. In this way it became clear that the physical, chemical world is in the self-motivated doer-status, while the conscious status, jeevan, together with its being endowed with awakening, was found to be in the form of seer and knower. As seer and knower, the utility, well-utility, and purposeful deployment of the doer-status gives rise to the good fortune of evidencing jeevan-aspiration and human-aspiration. In contrast to this, remaining deluded leads to affliction through problems. As far as the chemical, physical world is concerned, this has become clear on the basis of consequential-fruition within the actual condition, in the developmental sequence. In this way, the enjoyer-status has come to be formed together with the doer-status. Because the form of the result is seen in many actual conditions. To see means precisely to understand. Understanding is found to be the entire glory and grandeur present in jeevan. In this way, in the conscious world, the establishment of the seer-status becomes linked

[p. 153]

to this. This became clear as a result of awakening. This itself is the main matter. In the whole of chemical and physical activity, in the animal order, and in the deluded human being, testimony to being doer and enjoyer has forever remained formed. For this very reason, it is precisely together with the seer-status that the human being, as doer, has the arrangement for becoming meaningfully successful — not in the deluded condition. So be it: it is natural for the seer-status to be the glory of awakened tradition alone. Therefore, keeping human tradition intact is, for all of us human beings, the all-auspicious thread, the all-auspicious explanation.
