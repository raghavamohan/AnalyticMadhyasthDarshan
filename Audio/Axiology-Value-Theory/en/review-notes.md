## Editorial choices

Keeps one running meal example to connect realisation, thought, behaviour, and work. Names the value families without reading the full enumeration. Describes ordering through its practical direction while preserving the need for worked decisions under scarcity.

Revision 2 kept the meal for a child as the series' one food example (it was removed from the Human and Knowledge episodes), rewrote the stated question to match the Start here card, corrected the count and content of the five value groups, and added fear and temptation as non-values (§1.7). Paid for by tightening the ordering and comparison paragraphs. The optional evaluative-error taxonomy (§1.7: iron for gold, rope for snake) was drafted and dropped for length. No study follow-up is required; the child-nourishment example is §1.4's own.

Revision 3 reconciles the script with the September 20 study. The original
introduction represented the five groups and justice well, but largely omitted
how values become established and sustained. The new care-and-learning paragraph
connects enduring responsibility, changing means, meaningful practice,
self-evaluation, and continuity. The harmony explanation gives inward fulfilment
content beyond a list of pleasant-sounding states, and the object-values sentence
explains aesthetic value. The meal, care/generosity pair, price/usefulness
distinction, qualitative ordering, fair comparisons, and open questions remain.
Fewer examples in the taxonomy and shorter repeated explanations create space
without extending the intended recording length. Detailed faculty tables,
labour-exchange rules, and the formal appendix remain for the full study.
