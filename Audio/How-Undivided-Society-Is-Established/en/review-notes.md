## Editorial choices

Uses a neighbourhood as an entry point, then follows the study from person and family to institutions. Keeps the four goals and five dimensions because they make the social proposal concrete. The ending closes the five-stage path without implying an additional stage.

Revision 2 sharpened the neighbourhood so that it shows difference hardening into division, added the negative definition of undivided society, expanded the diagnosis of division into the study's causal chain (§§3.1, 3.3), added the prosperity/non-accumulation consequence (§5.2), the justice/legality distinction (§2.2), and the closing reversal (§6). Paid for by compressing the five-dimension list to names, tightening the comparison and transition paragraphs, and removing two summary sentences ("Internal contradiction and external conflict feed each other"; "Each goal places demands on how the others are pursued") whose content the surrounding sentences already carry.

Revision 3 preserves that central argument and makes its institutional links
easier to hear. It names the relationship between undivided society and universal
orderliness, explains the four goals as mutually supporting, and connects shared
family deliberation to wider councils and public dialogue. Returning to the water
source gives the five dimensions a concrete setting. The diagnosis of division
remains attributed to the darshan and concerns reducing fulfilment to bodily
satisfaction and security; it is not an empirical claim that every person with
a materialist philosophy behaves that way. The comparison distinguishes science's
methods from a complete account of human ends. Transition practices and safeguards
remain the study's proposals for examination, with no claim of success at scale.
The shorter ending retains education across generations and closes all five stages.
